\section{Magnitude and Unit Vectors}
\label{sec:magnitude-unit}

The magnitude, or Euclidean norm, measures the length of a vector. For
\[
\mathbf A=A_x\mathbf e_x+A_y\mathbf e_y+A_z\mathbf e_z,
\]
its magnitude is
\[
\boxed{\lVert\mathbf A\rVert=\sqrt{A_x^2+A_y^2+A_z^2}}.
\]
This is the three-dimensional Pythagorean theorem and may also be written as
\[
\lVert\mathbf A\rVert=\sqrt{\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf A}.
\]

\subsection{Unit Vectors and Direction}

A unit vector has magnitude one. Every nonzero vector can be separated into magnitude and direction:
\[
\mathbf A=\lVert\mathbf A\rVert\,\widehat{\mathbf A},
\qquad
\widehat{\mathbf A}=\frac{\mathbf A}{\lVert\mathbf A\rVert}.
\]
Normalization removes the scale of a vector while preserving its direction.

\begin{workedexample}
For $\mathbf A=2\mathbf e_x-3\mathbf e_y+6\mathbf e_z$,
\[
\lVert\mathbf A\rVert=\sqrt{4+9+36}=7,
\]
so
\[
\widehat{\mathbf A}=\frac{2}{7}\mathbf e_x-\frac{3}{7}\mathbf e_y+\frac{6}{7}\mathbf e_z.
\]
A direct calculation confirms that $\lVert\widehat{\mathbf A}\rVert=1$.
\end{workedexample}

\begin{propertybox}[Triangle inequality]
For any vectors $\mathbf A$ and $\mathbf B$,
\[
\lVert\mathbf A+\mathbf B\rVert\leq \lVert\mathbf A\rVert+\lVert\mathbf B\rVert.
\]
Geometrically, the direct path between two points cannot be longer than a broken path through an intermediate point.
\end{propertybox}

\begin{warningbox}
The expression $\mathbf A/\lVert\mathbf A\rVert$ is undefined for the zero vector. The zero vector has no unique direction.
\end{warningbox}

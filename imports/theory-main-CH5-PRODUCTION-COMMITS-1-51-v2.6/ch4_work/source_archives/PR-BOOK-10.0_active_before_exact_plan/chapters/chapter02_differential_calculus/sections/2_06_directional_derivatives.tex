\section{Directional Derivatives}
\label{sec:directional-derivatives}
\subsection{Introduction}
The gradient identifies the direction of greatest increase, but physical questions often ask for the rate of change along a prescribed direction.
\subsection{Definition}
For a differentiable scalar field $f$ and a unit vector $\hat{\mathbf u}$,
\[
D_{\hat{\mathbf u}}f=\nabla f\cdot\hat{\mathbf u}.
\]
\subsection{Geometric Interpretation}
The directional derivative is the projection of the gradient onto the chosen direction. It is maximal parallel to $\nabla f$, zero tangent to a level surface, and minimal in the opposite direction.
\subsection{Applications}
Directional derivatives describe temperature change along a path, potential change along a displacement, and optimization under a chosen search direction.
\subsection{Looking Ahead}
The same local language now extends from scalar fields to vector fields through divergence and curl.

# Editorial Style Guide

## Editorial philosophy

The book should combine mathematical rigor, physical intuition, historical context, and visual explanation without sacrificing clarity.

## Standard chapter flow

1. Chapter title and roadmap.
2. Learning objectives.
3. Historical and physical motivation.
4. Definitions and notation.
5. Main derivations.
6. Physical interpretation.
7. Worked examples.
8. Common mistakes.
9. Applications and connections.
10. Summary and looking ahead.
11. Exercises.

## Mathematical style

- Define every symbol before first use.
- State assumptions explicitly.
- Present important results in displayed equations.
- Use equation numbers only for equations referenced later or central results.
- Separate derivation from interpretation.
- Avoid unexplained algebraic jumps.

## Prose style

- Prefer complete, direct sentences.
- Introduce intuition before heavy formalism when possible.
- Avoid unnecessary repetition.
- Explain what an equation means physically after deriving it.
- Use consistent terminology across chapters.

## Figures and tables

- Every figure requires a meaningful caption.
- Figures must be referenced in the prose.
- Labels and symbols must match the text.
- Prefer vector graphics and TikZ for mathematical diagrams.
- Tables should summarize, compare, or organize—not merely decorate.

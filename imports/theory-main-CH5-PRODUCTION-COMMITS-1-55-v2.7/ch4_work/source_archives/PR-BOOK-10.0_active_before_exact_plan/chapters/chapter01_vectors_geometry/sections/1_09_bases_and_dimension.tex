\section{Bases and Dimension}
\label{sec:bases-dimension}
\subsection{Introduction}
A basis is a minimal complete set of independent vectors from which every vector in the space can be constructed.
\subsection{Definition}
A set $\{\mathbf e_i\}$ is a basis when it is linearly independent and spans the space. The number of basis vectors is the dimension.
\subsection{Coordinates as Components}
For a basis $\{\mathbf e_i\}$,
\[
\mathbf v=\sum_i v^i\mathbf e_i.
\]
The components $v^i$ depend on the basis, while the geometric vector $\mathbf v$ does not.
\subsection{Connections to Physics}
Changing basis is central to rotations, normal modes, spin measurements, and transformations between position and momentum representations.

\section{Linear Independence}
\label{sec:linear-independence}
\subsection{Introduction}
A collection of vectors is linearly independent when none of its members can be reconstructed from the others. This concept separates genuinely new directions from redundant descriptions.
\subsection{Mathematical Definition}
Vectors $\mathbf v_1,\ldots,\mathbf v_n$ are linearly independent if
\[
a_1\mathbf v_1+\cdots+a_n\mathbf v_n=\mathbf 0
\]
implies $a_1=\cdots=a_n=0$.
\subsection{Geometric Interpretation}
In two dimensions, two nonparallel vectors are independent. In three dimensions, three vectors are independent when they do not lie in one plane.
\subsection{Looking Ahead}
Linear independence leads directly to bases, dimension, eigenvectors, and the state spaces of quantum mechanics.

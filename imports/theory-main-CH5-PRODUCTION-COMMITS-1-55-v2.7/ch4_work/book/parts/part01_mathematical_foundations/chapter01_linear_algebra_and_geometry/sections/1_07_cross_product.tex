\section{Cross Product}
\label{sec:cross-product}

The dot product measures alignment. The cross product instead constructs a vector perpendicular to the plane of two vectors and therefore encodes orientation, area, and rotational effect.

\subsection{Geometric Definition}

For vectors $\mathbf A$ and $\mathbf B$ separated by an angle $\theta$,
\[
\boxed{\mathbf A\times\mathbf B=\lVert\mathbf A\rVert\lVert\mathbf B\rVert\sin\theta\,\widehat{\mathbf n}},
\]
where $\widehat{\mathbf n}$ is perpendicular to both vectors. Its magnitude is the area of the parallelogram spanned by $\mathbf A$ and $\mathbf B$.

\begin{rulebox}[Right-hand rule]
Curl the fingers of the right hand from $\mathbf A$ toward $\mathbf B$ through the smaller angle. The thumb points in the direction of $\mathbf A\times\mathbf B$.
\end{rulebox}

\subsection{Algebraic Form}

For an orthonormal right-handed Cartesian basis,
\[
\mathbf e_x\times\mathbf e_y=\mathbf e_z,\qquad
\mathbf e_y\times\mathbf e_z=\mathbf e_x,\qquad
\mathbf e_z\times\mathbf e_x=\mathbf e_y.
\]
Reversing the order reverses the sign:
\[
\mathbf A\times\mathbf B=-\mathbf B\times\mathbf A.
\]
For components,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbf A\times\mathbf B=
\begin{pmatrix}
A_yB_z-A_zB_y\\
A_zB_x-A_xB_z\\
A_xB_y-A_yB_x
\end{pmatrix}}
\]
or, as a mnemonic,
\[
\mathbf A\times\mathbf B=
\begin{vmatrix}
\mathbf e_x&\mathbf e_y&\mathbf e_z\\
A_x&A_y&A_z\\
B_x&B_y&B_z
\end{vmatrix}.
\]

\begin{propertybox}[Orthogonality]
The cross product is orthogonal to both original vectors:
\[
(\mathbf A\times\mathbf B)\cdot\mathbf A=0,
\qquad
(\mathbf A\times\mathbf B)\cdot\mathbf B=0.
\]
It vanishes when the vectors are parallel or when either vector is zero.
\end{propertybox}

\subsection{Physical Applications}

Cross products appear whenever orientation or rotation matters:
\[
\boldsymbol\tau=\mathbf r\times\mathbf F,
\qquad
\mathbf L=\mathbf r\times\mathbf p,
\qquad
\mathbf F=q\,\mathbf v\times\mathbf B,
\qquad
\mathbf S=\mathbf E\times\mathbf H.
\]
These represent torque, angular momentum, magnetic force, and electromagnetic energy flow.

\begin{workedexample}
For $\mathbf A=(2,1,0)$ and $\mathbf B=(1,3,4)$,
\[
\mathbf A\times\mathbf B=(4,-8,5).
\]
Indeed,
\[
(4,-8,5)\cdot(2,1,0)=0,
\qquad
(4,-8,5)\cdot(1,3,4)=0.
\]
Its magnitude $\sqrt{105}$ is the area of the parallelogram generated by the two vectors.
\end{workedexample}

\begin{warningbox}
The cross product is not commutative. Interchanging the vectors changes the sign and therefore reverses the direction.
\end{warningbox}

\section{Summary and Looking Ahead}
\label{sec:ch5-summary}

Complex numbers extend the real line into a plane in which multiplication becomes scaling plus rotation. Euler's formula turns oscillation into exponential evolution, while complex differentiability imposes the Cauchy--Riemann equations and links analytic functions to harmonic fields. Cauchy's theorem and integral formula show that holomorphic functions are globally constrained by local analyticity and boundary data. Taylor and Laurent series classify local behavior; residues convert contour integrals into finite sums of local coefficients.

\begin{summarybox}
\begin{itemize}
\item $z=x+iy=re^{i\theta}$ unifies Cartesian geometry, magnitude, and phase.
\item $e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta$ makes rotations and oscillations algebraic.
\item Holomorphic functions satisfy the Cauchy--Riemann equations and are infinitely differentiable.
\item Cauchy's integral formula reconstructs interior values from boundary data.
\item Laurent series distinguish removable singularities, poles, and essential singularities.
\item The residue theorem evaluates contour integrals through local singular data.
\item Complex methods organize phasors, waves, quantum amplitudes, vortices, Landau-level states, and Green functions.
\end{itemize}
\end{summarybox}

The next chapter develops linear algebra over real and complex vector spaces, then extends finite-dimensional geometry toward inner-product spaces and Hilbert spaces. Complex phase will become the scalar foundation for quantum states, unitary transformations, spectral theory, and the operator formulation of quantum mechanics.

\section{Comprehensive Graded Exercise Bank}
\label{sec:ch5-exercises}

The following 120 exercises progress from algebraic fluency to analyticity, contour methods, residues, and physical applications. Unless otherwise stated, contours are positively oriented, functions have the regularity required by the indicated theorem, and principal branches are used only when explicitly specified.

\subsection*{Tier I: Foundations (30 problems)}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]
\item Compute $(3-2i)+(1+5i)$ and $(3-2i)(1+5i)$.
\item Express $1/(2-i)$ in Cartesian form.
\item Find $\Re z$, $\Im z$, $\bar z$, and $|z|$ for $z=-3+4i$.
\item Prove $z\bar z=|z|^2$.
\item Show $|zw|=|z||w|$.
\item Verify the triangle inequality for $z=1+2i$ and $w=2-i$.
\item Write $-1+i\sqrt3$ in polar and exponential form.
\item Determine all arguments of $-i$.
\item Convert $4e^{5\pi i/6}$ to Cartesian form.
\item Compute $(1+i)^{12}$ using polar form.
\item Find all cube roots of $8i$.
\item Plot the sixth roots of unity and identify their polygon.
\item Use de Moivre's theorem to derive formulas for $\cos3\theta$ and $\sin3\theta$.
\item Show multiplication by $i$ rotates every point through $\pi/2$.
\item Describe geometrically the map $w=(2-i)z$.
\item Find the fixed point of $w=az+b$ when $a\neq1$.
\item Find the image of the real axis under $w=z+2i$.
\item Find the image of the circle $|z|=2$ under $w=3z-1$.
\item Determine the image of the ray $\arg z=\pi/4$ under $w=z^2$.
\item Determine the preimages of $w=1$ under $w=z^4$.
\item Compute $e^{2+i\pi/3}$.
\item Show $e^{z+2\pi i}=e^z$.
\item Express $\cos z$ and $\sin z$ using exponentials.
\item Evaluate $\cos(iy)$ and $\sin(iy)$.
\item Show $\cosh z=(e^z+e^{-z})/2$ and relate $\cos(iz)$ to $\cosh z$.
\item Solve $e^z=1$ for all complex $z$.
\item Find the principal value of $\operatorname{Log}(-1-i)$.
\item Explain why a continuous argument cannot be defined on all of $\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$.
\item Compute the phasor sum $3e^{i\pi/6}+2e^{-i\pi/3}$.
\item Represent $A\cos(\omega t+\phi)$ as the real part of a complex exponential.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Tier II: Analyticity and mappings (30 problems)}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]
\item Determine whether $f(z)=z^2+3z$ is complex differentiable and compute $f'(z)$.
\item Test $f(z)=\bar z$ for complex differentiability at an arbitrary point.
\item Test $f(z)=|z|^2$ and identify any point where it is complex differentiable.
\item Verify the Cauchy--Riemann equations for $f(z)=e^z$.
\item Verify the Cauchy--Riemann equations for $f(z)=\sin z$.
\item Show that $u=x^2-y^2$ is harmonic and find a harmonic conjugate.
\item Find an analytic function whose real part is $u=e^x\cos y$.
\item Find an analytic function whose imaginary part is $v=2xy$.
\item Determine whether $u=x^3-3xy^2$ is harmonic.
\item Recover $f(z)$ from $u=x^3-3xy^2$.
\item Show that the real and imaginary parts of a holomorphic function are orthogonal where their gradients are nonzero.
\item Explain geometrically why a nonzero complex derivative acts locally as scaling and rotation.
\item Find the derivative of $f(z)=1/z$ and describe its local scaling factor.
\item Find the critical points of $f(z)=z^3-3z$.
\item Find the image of horizontal and vertical grid lines under $w=e^z$.
\item Describe the image of the right half-plane under $w=z^2$.
\item Show inversion maps circles through the origin to lines not through the origin.
\item Find the inverse of $w=(z-1)/(z+1)$.
\item Determine where $w=(z-i)/(z+i)$ sends the real axis.
\item Find a Möbius map sending $0,1,\infty$ to $-1,0,1$.
\item Parameterize the line segment from $1$ to $2+i$.
\item Parameterize the circle $|z|=R$ counterclockwise.
\item Evaluate $\int_\gamma z\,dz$ along a line segment from $0$ to $1+i$.
\item Evaluate $\int_\gamma \bar z\,dz$ around the unit circle.
\item Show $\int_\gamma z^n\,dz=0$ around a closed contour for $n\ge0$.
\item Evaluate $\oint_{|z|=2}dz/z$ directly by parameterization.
\item Compute the winding number of $\gamma(t)=2e^{3it}$ about the origin.
\item Compare two paths from $0$ to $1+i$ for the integral of $2z\,dz$.
\item Explain why path independence is equivalent to existence of a primitive on a suitable domain.
\item Use a complex potential to obtain equipotential and stream lines for $F(z)=z^2$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Tier III: Cauchy theory, series, and residues (30 problems)}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]
\item Use Cauchy's theorem to evaluate $\oint_C(z^4+2z)\,dz$.
\item Use Cauchy's integral formula to evaluate $\oint_{|z|=2}e^z/(z-1)\,dz$.
\item Evaluate $\oint_{|z|=3}\cos z/(z-i)^3\,dz$.
\item Derive Cauchy's estimate $|f^{(n)}(z_0)|\le n!M/R^n$.
\item Use Cauchy's estimates to prove that a bounded entire function is constant.
\item Expand $1/(1-z)$ as a Taylor series and state its disk of convergence.
\item Find the Taylor series of $\operatorname{Log}(1+z)$ about zero.
\item Find the Laurent series of $1/[z(z-1)]$ for $0<|z|<1$.
\item Find the Laurent series of $1/[z(z-1)]$ for $|z|>1$.
\item Classify the singularity of $\sin z/z$ at zero.
\item Classify the singularity of $1/\sin z$ at zero.
\item Classify the singularity of $e^{1/z}$ at zero.
\item Compute $\operatorname{Res}(e^z/z^3;0)$.
\item Compute $\operatorname{Res}((z+1)/(z^2+1);i)$.
\item Compute all residues of $1/(z^3-z)$.
\item Evaluate $\oint_{|z|=2}dz/(z^2+1)$.
\item Evaluate $\oint_C z^2/(z^3-1)\,dz$ when $C$ encloses all three roots.
\item Use residues to evaluate $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx/(x^2+a^2)$ for $a>0$.
\item Use residues to evaluate $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx/(x^2+a^2)^2$.
\item Evaluate $\int_0^{2\pi}d\theta/(a+b\cos\theta)$ for $a>|b|$ using $z=e^{i\theta}$.
\item Show the residue at infinity equals minus the sum of finite residues.
\item Prove the argument principle for a meromorphic function at an outline level.
\item Use Rouch'e's theorem to count zeros of $z^5+10z+1$ in a suitable circle.
\item Construct a branch of $\sqrt z$ on the slit plane $\mathbb C\setminus(-\infty,0]$.
\item Compute the jump of the principal logarithm across its branch cut.
\item Explain monodromy for analytic continuation of $\sqrt z$ around the origin.
\item Show a nonconstant holomorphic map is locally conformal wherever $f'
eq0$.
\item Map the upper half-plane to the unit disk with a Möbius transformation.
\item Use the maximum-modulus principle to show a holomorphic function attaining an interior maximum is constant.
\item Prove uniqueness of analytic continuation on a connected domain.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Tier IV: Physics and computational challenges (30 problems)}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]
\item Use complex amplitudes to solve $x''+\omega_0^2x=F_0\cos\omega t$ away from resonance.
\item Derive the impedance of a series RC circuit.
\item Derive the impedance of a series RLC circuit and locate resonance.
\item Compute the phase lag between current and voltage in an RL circuit.
\item Represent two coherent waves by phasors and derive the interference intensity.
\item Derive the beat envelope from two nearby complex frequencies.
\item Represent a circularly polarized electromagnetic wave using a complex vector amplitude.
\item Show how time averaging gives $\langle E^2
angle=|E_0|^2/2$ for a harmonic field.
\item Construct the complex potential for a uniform two-dimensional flow.
\item Construct the complex potential of a point source and identify its branch point.
\item Combine uniform flow and a vortex and sketch streamlines.
\item Use a conformal map to transform a simple electrostatic boundary problem.
\item Normalize the wavefunction $\psi(x)=Ae^{-\alpha x^2}$ and discuss the role of a global phase.
\item Show that relative phase controls interference between two quantum amplitudes.
\item Verify that unitary time evolution preserves the norm of a state amplitude.
\item Write a two-level quantum state on the Bloch sphere using complex amplitudes.
\item For $\psi(z)=z^m e^{-|z|^2/(4\ell_B^2)}$, locate the zeros and their multiplicities.
\item Show that lowest-Landau-level polynomial factors are analytic in $z$ in one magnetic-field convention.
\item Explain how a zero of a wavefunction produces phase winding.
\item Compute the circulation of the phase around a simple zero.
\item Relate the winding number of a complex order parameter to vortex charge.
\item Derive the two-dimensional Green function from the real part of $\operatorname{Log} z$.
\item Use residues to invert a simple rational Fourier transform.
\item Identify retarded and advanced pole prescriptions for a simple propagator.
\item Explain why Wick rotation is an analytic continuation rather than an ordinary rotation in real time.
\item Use contour closure to evaluate a damped causal response integral.
\item Analyze how branch cuts encode continuum spectra in a Green function.
\item Construct a numerical domain-coloring plot for a wavefunction with two vortices.
\item Numerically verify a contour integral is invariant under a deformation that crosses no singularity.
\item Write a short report tracing one complex-analysis idea from geometry through computation to a physical application.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Selected guidance}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.9cm,style=nextline]
\item[Foundation 11.] Write $8i=8e^{i(\pi/2+2\pi k)}$ and divide every allowed argument by three.
\item[Foundation 20.] Solve $z^4=1$ as $z=e^{i\pi k/2}$.
\item[Intermediate 7.] Use $u_x=v_y$ and $u_y=-v_x$, then integrate one partial derivative and determine the remaining one-variable function.
\item[Intermediate 18.] Solve algebraically for $z$; Möbius inverses are again Möbius maps.
\item[Intermediate 24.] On $z=e^{it}$, use $\bar z=e^{-it}$ and $dz=ie^{it}dt$.
\item[Advanced 8.] Rewrite by partial fractions, then choose the geometric-series form valid in the specified annulus.
\item[Advanced 13.] Expand $e^z$ and identify the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ after division by $z^3$.
\item[Advanced 18.] Close the contour in the upper half-plane and include the pole at $ia$.
\item[Physics 17.] The polynomial factor gives an $m$-fold zero at the origin; the Gaussian controls localization rather than winding.
\item[Physics 23.] The sign of the imaginary displacement of a pole determines whether the Fourier contour produces a causal response.
\end{description}

\subsection{Diagrams and charts for Problems 9--10}

The identity
\[
\langle a,\partial x\rangle=\langle \delta a,x\rangle
\]
can be understood as a relative Stokes principle: evaluating a class on the boundary of a relative cycle is the same as evaluating its connecting cohomology class on the relative cycle itself.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/fig_pairing_boundary_disk_sphere.png}
    \caption{Pairing and boundary for the pair \((D^{k+1},S^k)\).}
    \label{fig:pairing-boundary-disk-sphere}
\end{figure}

For the annulus
\[
X=S^1\times[0,1],
\qquad
A=\partial X,
\]
the boundary of the relative fundamental class is
\[
\partial[X,A]=[S^1\times\{1\}]-[S^1\times\{0\}],
\]
and the connecting map measures the difference of the two boundary values:
\[
\delta(m,n)=m-n.
\]

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/fig_annulus_connecting_map.png}
    \caption{The annulus example: the connecting homomorphism detects the obstruction to extending boundary data.}
    \label{fig:annulus-connecting-map}
\end{figure}

For the coefficient sequence
\[
0\to\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\times 2}\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/2\to0,
\]
the Bockstein homomorphism records the divided boundary of an integral lift. In \(\mathbb{RP}^2\), one has
\[
\partial e^2=2e^1,
\qquad
\beta([e^2])=[e^1].
\]

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/fig_bockstein_rp2.png}
    \caption{The Bockstein homomorphism for \(\mathbb{RP}^2\): the mod \(2\) top cell detects the integral torsion class below.}
    \label{fig:bockstein-rp2}
\end{figure}

Finally, the parity of \(n\) explains why the converse statement holds for even \(n\), but fails for odd \(n\).

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/fig_even_vs_odd_rpn.png}
    \caption{Even versus odd real projective spaces. Mod \(2\) homology cannot distinguish \(+1\) from \(-1\) on the top integral class when \(n\) is odd.}
    \label{fig:even-vs-odd-projective-spaces}
\end{figure}

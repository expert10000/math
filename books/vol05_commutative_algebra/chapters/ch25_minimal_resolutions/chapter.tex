\chapter{Minimal Resolutions}
\label{ch:v25}

Minimal resolutions remove redundant free summands. Over local rings, minimality is detected by differentials whose matrix entries lie in the maximal ideal, making Betti numbers intrinsic.

\item use residue-field tensoring to read off minimal generators or Betti numbers;
\item distinguish homological length, projective dimension, and resolution length;
\end{itemize}

\begin{remark}[Minimality is controlled by units, not numerical size]
The local minimality criterion is algebraic.  For a local ring
\((A,\mathfrak m)\), a differential matrix in a minimal free resolution has
all entries in \(\mathfrak m\).  Equivalently, no entry that is a unit of
\(A\) may be cancelled as a rank-one contractible summand.

This is not a magnitude test.  In a local polynomial ring such as
\(k[x,y]_{(x,y)}\), the element \(x\) lies in the maximal ideal and is not a
unit, while \(1+x\) is a unit because its residue modulo \((x,y)\) is \(1\).
There is no meaningful floating-point threshold separating these two
algebraic cases.

A computational minimal-resolution workflow should therefore verify
minimality by exact reduction modulo the maximal ideal (or by the appropriate
positive-degree criterion in the graded setting).  If cancellations are
performed, every row and column operation used to split a unit entry must be
invertible over the coefficient ring.  Betti numbers become certified only
after the resulting complex is verified exact and minimal in the stated
algebraic category.
\end{remark}

\section{Core structural results}

\begin{theorem}[Local minimality criterion]\label{thm:v25-01}
A free resolution $F_\bullet$ over a local ring is minimal iff $d(F_n)\subseteq\mathfrak mF_{n-1}$ for all $n$.
\begin{proof}
If a matrix entry is a unit, elementary basis changes split off an identity map between rank-one free summands, producing a contractible piece. Conversely, if all entries lie in the maximal ideal, no such unit cancellation is possible.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Betti numbers from Tor]\label{thm:v25-02}
For a minimal free resolution of a finite module over a local ring with residue field $k$, $\operatorname{Tor}_n^A(M,k)\cong F_n\otimes_A k$.
\begin{proof}
Tensor the minimal resolution with $k$. Every differential becomes zero because its entries lie in $\mathfrak m$, so homology in degree $n$ is the entire vector space $F_n\otimes k$.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness of minimal resolutions]\label{thm:v25-03}
Minimal free resolutions of a finite module over a local ring are isomorphic as complexes.
\begin{proof}
Comparison maps in both directions lift the identity of the module. Modulo the maximal ideal they are inverse isomorphisms on Tor, hence degreewise isomorphisms by Nakayama's lemma.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{exercise}[Unit detection in a local ring]\label{exr:v25-exact-unit-01}
Let
\[
A=k[x,y]_{(x,y)},
\qquad
\mathfrak m=(x,y)A.
\]
Decide which of the elements
\[
x,\qquad 1+x,\qquad x+y^2
\]
are units.  Explain what this implies if one of these elements appears as an
entry of a differential matrix in a candidate minimal free resolution.
\end{exercise}
\begin{hint}
In this local ring an element is a unit exactly when its residue modulo
\(\mathfrak m\) is nonzero.
\end{hint}
\begin{solution}
Modulo \(\mathfrak m\),
\[
x\mapsto0,\qquad 1+x\mapsto1,\qquad x+y^2\mapsto0.
\]
Thus \(1+x\) is a unit, while \(x\) and \(x+y^2\) are nonunits.  A differential
matrix containing \(1+x\) cannot be minimal: exact row and column operations
can split off a rank-one contractible summand.  Entries \(x\) and \(x+y^2\)
do not trigger such a unit cancellation.  Their numerical magnitudes at
chosen points are irrelevant to the local-algebra criterion.
\end{solution}

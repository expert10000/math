\chapter{Minimal Resolutions}
\label{ch:v25}

Minimal resolutions remove redundant free summands. Over local rings, minimality is detected by differentials whose matrix entries lie in the maximal ideal, making Betti numbers intrinsic.

\section*{Learning goals}
After this chapter, the reader should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
\item identify minimal free resolutions over local or graded rings using the chapter's minimality criterion;
\item compute Betti numbers from ranks of modules in a minimal resolution;
\item detect and remove contractible or unit-entry summands from nonminimal resolutions;
\item compare minimal resolutions up to isomorphism in settings where uniqueness holds;
\item use residue-field tensoring to read off minimal generators or Betti numbers;
\item distinguish homological length, projective dimension, and resolution length;
\end{itemize}

\section{Minimal free resolutions}
A free resolution is minimal when no contractible free summand can be removed.

\section{Local criterion}
Over a local ring $(A,\mathfrak m)$, a finite free resolution is minimal iff each differential has image contained in $\mathfrak m$ times the previous free module.

Over a local ring $(R,\mathfrak m)$, a finite free resolution is minimal when every differential matrix has entries in $\mathfrak m$. Reducing modulo $\mathfrak m$ then makes every differential zero.
\end{example}

Minimal differentials become zero after tensoring with the residue field.

\section{Betti numbers}
Ranks of the free modules in a minimal resolution are invariants of the module.

\section{Nakayama mechanism}
Minimal generating sets are detected after reduction modulo $\mathfrak m$.

\begin{example}[Cancellation of a unit]\label{ex:v25-expand-02}
If a differential matrix contains a unit entry, suitable row and column operations split off a contractible summand $R\xrightarrow{\cong}R$. Removing it shortens the resolution, so a minimal resolution cannot contain such a unit.
\end{example}

\section{Uniqueness}
Minimal free resolutions over local rings are unique up to chain isomorphism.

\section{Projective dimension}
The last nonzero module in a minimal finite resolution detects projective dimension.

\begin{example}[Residue field over a regular local ring]\label{ex:v25-expand-03}
For $R=k[x,y]_{(x,y)}$, the Koszul complex on $x,y$ gives a minimal free resolution of the residue field $k$. Its Betti numbers are $1,2,1$.
\end{example}

Over standard graded rings, minimality is similarly controlled by positive-degree entries.

\section{Computational significance}
Minimal resolutions expose generators, first relations, and higher relations without algebraic cancellation.

\section{Core structural results}

\begin{theorem}[Local minimality criterion]\label{thm:v25-01}
A free resolution $F_\bullet$ over a local ring is minimal iff $d(F_n)\subseteq\mathfrak mF_{n-1}$ for all $n$.
\begin{proof}
If a matrix entry is a unit, elementary basis changes split off an identity map between rank-one free summands, producing a contractible piece. Conversely, if all entries lie in the maximal ideal, no such unit cancellation is possible.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Betti numbers from Tor]\label{thm:v25-02}
For a minimal free resolution of a finite module over a local ring with residue field $k$, $\operatorname{Tor}_n^A(M,k)\cong F_n\otimes_A k$.
\begin{proof}
Tensor the minimal resolution with $k$. Every differential becomes zero because its entries lie in $\mathfrak m$, so homology in degree $n$ is the entire vector space $F_n\otimes k$.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness of minimal resolutions]\label{thm:v25-03}
Minimal free resolutions of a finite module over a local ring are isomorphic as complexes.
\begin{proof}
Comparison maps in both directions lift the identity of the module. Modulo the maximal ideal they are inverse isomorphisms on Tor, hence degreewise isomorphisms by Nakayama's lemma.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

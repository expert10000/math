\chapter{Hom and Finitely Presented Modules}
\label{ch:v12}

Hom and finite presentation control maps out of modules and the stability of algebraic data under limits and base change. Finitely presented modules are those described by finitely many generators and finitely many relations.

\section*{Learning goals}
After this chapter, the reader should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
\item compute Hom modules for finitely generated free and cyclic modules;
\item distinguish finitely generated from finitely presented modules by explicit presentations;
\item use finite presentation to justify commuting Hom with filtered colimits or localization in the chapter's setting;
\item construct presentation matrices for modules and interpret generators and relations;
\item apply the Hom universal property and naturality in concrete calculations;
\item identify which finiteness hypothesis is needed in a proposed Hom/base-change statement;
\end{itemize}

\section{Contravariance and covariance}
Hom is contravariant in its first argument and covariant in its second.

A map $\mathbb Z/n\to M$ is determined by the image of $1$, which must be killed by $n$. Hence $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Z/n,M)\cong M[n]$.
\end{example}

\section{Left exactness}
$\operatorname{Hom}_A(M,-)$ preserves kernels but not generally surjections.

\section{Finite generation}
A module is finitely generated if a finite free module surjects onto it.

\section{Finite presentation}
A module is finitely presented if it is the cokernel of a map between finite free modules.

\begin{example}[Presentation matrix]\label{ex:v12-expand-02}
The cokernel of $\operatorname{diag}(x,y):R^2\to R^2$ is $R/(x)\oplus R/(y)$. The diagonal matrix records two independent relations.
\end{example}

\section{Matrix viewpoint}
Maps between finite free modules are matrices, so finite presentations translate module questions into matrix algebra.

\begin{remark}[A presentation matrix inherits its coefficient ring]
A matrix in this chapter represents a homomorphism of free \(A\)-modules.
Its algebraic meaning therefore depends on the ring \(A\), not only on its
displayed entries.  Operations that are reversible over a field need not be
reversible over a general ring, because row or column operations preserve the
presented module only when the corresponding change-of-basis matrix is
invertible over \(A\).

For example, the \(1\times1\) matrix \((2)\) represents very different maps:
\[
\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\cdot2}\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\mathbb Q\xrightarrow{\cdot2}\mathbb Q,
\qquad
\mathbb F_2\xrightarrow{\cdot0}\mathbb F_2.
\]
Over \(\mathbb Z\) the map is injective but not surjective; over \(\mathbb Q\)
it is an isomorphism; over \(\mathbb F_2\) it is zero.  Thus a numerical
``rank'' attached to the array of coefficients cannot replace the module
question over the stated ring.

Computational work with presentations should therefore record the coefficient
ring, the row/column convention, and the exact transformations performed.
When the coefficients are intended to be exact integers, rationals, finite
field elements, or polynomials, floating-point approximations should be used
only as heuristics and not as certificates of kernel, cokernel, rank, or
isomorphism statements.
\end{remark}

\section{Core structural results}

\begin{theorem}[Finite presentation criterion]\label{thm:v12-01}
$M$ is finitely presented iff there exists an exact sequence $A^m\to A^n\to M\to0$ with finite $m,n$.
\begin{proof}
This is the definition translated into generators and relations: the second map chooses finitely many generators and the image of the first supplies finitely many generators for all relations.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Hom is left exact]\label{thm:v12-02}
For $0\to N'\to N\to N''$, the sequence $0\to\operatorname{Hom}(M,N')\to\operatorname{Hom}(M,N)\to\operatorname{Hom}(M,N'')$ is exact.
\begin{proof}
A map into $N$ lands in $N'$ exactly when its composite with $N\to N''$ is zero. Injectivity follows because $N'\to N$ is injective.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Localization of Hom for finitely presented modules]\label{thm:v12-03}
If $M$ is finitely presented, then $S^{-1}\operatorname{Hom}_A(M,N)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{S^{-1}A}(S^{-1}M,S^{-1}N)$.
\begin{proof}
Choose a finite presentation of $M$, apply Hom to it, localize the resulting kernel description, and use exactness of localization together with the evident formula for finite free modules.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{exercise}[The same matrix over three coefficient rings]\label{exr:v12-exact-ring-01}
Consider the presentation matrix \(A=(2)\).

\begin{enumerate}
\item Describe the kernel and cokernel of
\(\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{A}\mathbb Z\).
\item Repeat over \(\mathbb Q\).
\item Repeat over \(\mathbb F_2\).
\end{enumerate}

Explain why the answer cannot be recovered from a ring-independent
floating-point rank calculation.
\end{exercise}
\begin{hint}
Over \(\mathbb Z\), multiplication by \(2\) is injective but \(2\) is not a
unit.  Over \(\mathbb Q\), \(2\) is a unit.  In characteristic \(2\), the
entry becomes zero.
\end{hint}
\begin{solution}
Over \(\mathbb Z\),
\[
\ker A=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{coker}A\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]
Over \(\mathbb Q\), multiplication by \(2\) is invertible, so both kernel and
cokernel vanish.  Over \(\mathbb F_2\), the map is zero, hence
\[
\ker A\cong\mathbb F_2,
\qquad
\operatorname{coker}A\cong\mathbb F_2.
\]
The array contains the same written integer, but its algebraic behavior is
controlled by the coefficient ring.  A floating-point rank calculation sees
only an approximation to a real matrix and therefore does not encode these
module-theoretic distinctions.
\end{solution}

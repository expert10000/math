\chapter{Derived-Functor Viewpoint}
\label{ch:v28}

Derived functors organize Tor, Ext, and similar constructions as systematic corrections to nonexact functors. Resolutions make these corrections computable, while long exact sequences expose their functorial structure.

\section*{Learning goals}
After this chapter, the reader should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
\item identify which failure of exactness a derived functor is measuring;
\item construct left derived functors from projective or flat resolutions and right derived functors from injective resolutions;
\item explain Tor as derived tensor and Ext as derived Hom;
\item derive long exact sequences from short exact sequences of complexes or resolved objects;
\item compare different resolutions using chain-homotopy and universality principles;
\item connect derived-functor computations to later geometric uses such as sheaf cohomology and intersection theory;
\end{itemize}

\section{Exact and derived behavior}
Left or right exact functors lose information at kernels or cokernels; derived functors measure that failure in higher degrees.

\section{Left derived functors}
Projective resolutions compute left derived functors of right exact functors in the module categories considered here. For tensor product, flat resolutions may also be used because flat modules are tensor-acyclic.

% BEGIN VOL05-EXPANSION V28-example-01
\begin{example}[Deriving a right-exact functor]\label{ex:v28-expand-01}
Tensor product is right exact but may fail on kernels. Replacing an input by a projective resolution and taking homology produces its left derived functors, the Tor groups.
\end{example}
% END VOL05-EXPANSION V28-example-01
\section{Right derived functors}
Injective resolutions produce right derived functors of left exact functors.

\section{Tor as derived tensor}
$\operatorname{Tor}_n$ is the left derived functor of tensor product.

\section{Ext as derived Hom}
For fixed first argument \(M\), \(\operatorname{Ext}_A^n(M,-)\) is the right derived functor of the left-exact functor \(\operatorname{Hom}_A(M,-)\) and may be computed using an injective resolution of the second variable. Equivalently, for fixed second argument \(N\), \(\operatorname{Ext}_A^n(-,N)\) may be computed from a projective resolution of the first variable.

% BEGIN VOL05-EXPANSION V28-example-02
\begin{example}[Deriving a left-exact functor]\label{ex:v28-expand-02}
Hom in the second variable is left exact. Applying it to a projective resolution in the first variable and taking cohomology produces Ext, which records the missing higher exactness.
\end{example}
% END VOL05-EXPANSION V28-example-02
\section{Universality}
Derived functors are characterized by effaceability and universal delta-functor properties.

\section{Long exact sequences}
Connecting morphisms package the failure of exactness into functorial long exact sequences.

% BEGIN VOL05-EXPANSION V28-example-03
\begin{example}[Long exact sequence]\label{ex:v28-expand-03}
A short exact sequence of modules induces long exact sequences in Tor or Ext. The connecting morphisms propagate the failure of exactness into neighboring derived degrees.
\end{example}
% END VOL05-EXPANSION V28-example-03
\section{Acyclic resolutions}
One may compute derived functors using suitable classes of acyclic objects, not only projectives or injectives.

\section{Derived-category preview}
Complexes and quasi-isomorphisms provide a setting in which derived functors become ordinary functors after localization.

\section{Algebra-geometry bridge}
Derived tensor and derived Hom underpin intersection theory, deformation theory, and sheaf cohomology.

\section{Core structural results}

\begin{theorem}[Tor is the left derived tensor functor]\label{thm:v28-01}
For fixed $N$, the homology of a projective resolution tensor $N$ gives the left derived functors of $-\otimes_A N$.
\begin{proof}
Applying \(-\otimes_A N\) to a projective resolution produces a chain complex whose homology is independent of the chosen projective resolution by the comparison theorem and chain homotopy. Degree zero recovers ordinary tensor, and the higher homology groups are the derived functors \(\operatorname{Tor}_n^A(-,N)\).
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Ext is a derived Hom functor]\label{thm:v28-02}
For fixed $N$, cohomology of $\operatorname{Hom}(P_\bullet,N)$ gives derived functors extending $\operatorname{Hom}(-,N)$ contravariantly in the first variable.
\begin{proof}
Projective resolutions replace the first variable by Hom-acyclic objects. Comparison and homotopy yield functorial well-defined cohomology, and degree zero recovers Hom.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Derived functors produce long exact sequences]\label{thm:v28-03}
A short exact sequence in the appropriate variable induces a natural long exact sequence of derived functors.
\begin{proof}
Resolve compatibly or apply the functor to a short exact sequence of suitable resolutions. The resulting short exact sequence of complexes yields the standard long exact sequence in homology or cohomology via connecting morphisms.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

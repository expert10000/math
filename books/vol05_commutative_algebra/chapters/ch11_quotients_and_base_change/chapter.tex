\chapter{Quotients and Base Change}
\label{ch:v11}

Quotients and base change describe how algebraic relations behave after changing the scalar ring. Tensor products are the mechanism that transports modules, presentations, and exact sequences to the new base.

\section*{Learning goals}
After this chapter, the reader should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
\item compute tensor products after quotienting rings or modules;
\item derive standard base-change isomorphisms from universal properties;
\item compare extension and restriction of scalars along a ring homomorphism;
\item track ideals and modules through quotient and base-change constructions;
\item identify when tensoring preserves or destroys injectivity in quotient sequences;
\item use localization as a special case of base change to simplify calculations;
\end{itemize}

\section{Scalar extension}
For $A\to B$, the base-changed module is $B\otimes_A M$.

\section{Restriction of scalars}
Every $B$-module is an $A$-module by composing scalar actions with $A\to B$.

\section{Quotient base change}
$B\otimes_A(A/I)$ is naturally $B/IB$.

\section{Submodule caution}
Base change need not preserve injections unless the new scalar module is flat.

\section{Presentations}
Base changing a presentation replaces its matrices by the same matrices with entries mapped into $B$.

\section{Localization as base change}
Localization is scalar extension along $A\to S^{-1}A$.

\section{Residue-field fibers}
$M\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ measures the fiber of a module at a prime.

\section{Iterated base change}
For $A\to B\to C$, there is a canonical isomorphism $C\otimes_B(B\otimes_A M)\cong C\otimes_A M$.

\section{Compatibility with quotients}
Cokernels commute with base change because tensor is right exact.

\section{Core structural results}

\begin{theorem}[Quotient under base change]\label{thm:v11-01}
For $A\to B$ and ideal $I\subset A$, $B\otimes_A A/I\cong B/IB$.
\begin{proof}
Apply the quotient tensor identity to the $A$-module $B$; the submodule $IB$ is generated by images of elements of $I$.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Transitivity of base change]\label{thm:v11-02}
For $A\to B\to C$ and an $A$-module $M$, $C\otimes_B(B\otimes_A M)\cong C\otimes_A M$.
\begin{proof}
Send $c\otimes(b\otimes m)$ to $cb\otimes m$. Balancedness gives a well-defined map, and the inverse sends $c\otimes m$ to $c\otimes(1\otimes m)$.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Base change of a cokernel]\label{thm:v11-03}
If $M'\to M\to Q\to0$ is exact, then $B\otimes_A M'\to B\otimes_A M\to B\otimes_A Q\to0$ is exact.
\begin{proof}
This is the right exactness of tensor product with the $A$-module $B$.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

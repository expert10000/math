\chapter{Flat Modules}
\label{ch:v14}

Flat modules are those whose tensor product functor preserves injections and hence all finite exact sequences. Flatness is the homological condition behind well-behaved base change.

\section*{Learning goals}
After this chapter, the reader should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
\item test flatness using preservation of injective maps or exact sequences under tensor product;
\item prove that free and projective modules are flat;
\item verify that localization is flat as a module over the original ring;
\item use ideal criteria for flatness in finitely generated situations where developed;
\item construct examples of nonflat modules from torsion or failed injectivity after tensoring;
\item distinguish flatness from projectivity and freeness by structural consequences and examples;
\end{itemize}

\section{Definition of flatness}
An $A$-module $F$ is flat if $-\otimes_A F$ is exact.

\section{Injection test}
Because tensor is always right exact, flatness is equivalent to preserving injections.

\section{Free and projective modules}
Free modules are flat, and direct summands of flat modules are flat; hence projectives are flat.

\section{Localization}
Every localization $S^{-1}A$ is flat over $A$.

\section{Filtered colimits}
Filtered colimits of flat modules are flat.

\section{Ideal criterion}
Flatness can be tested by injectivity of $I\otimes F\to F$ for finitely generated ideals under standard hypotheses.

\section{Tor preview}
$\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(-,F)$ measures the failure of flatness.

\section{Faithful flatness}
A flat module is faithfully flat when tensoring also detects nonzero modules.

\section{Base change}
Flat scalar extension preserves short exact sequences and scheme-theoretic fiber behavior.

\section{Examples and failures}
Quotient modules are generally not flat; localization provides the basic positive example.

\section{Core structural results}

\begin{theorem}[Free implies flat]\label{thm:v14-01}
Every free $A$-module is flat.
\begin{proof}
Tensoring with a direct sum of copies of $A$ gives the corresponding direct sum of the original sequence. Direct sums preserve exactness in modules.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Projective implies flat]\label{thm:v14-02}
Every projective module is flat.
\begin{proof}
A projective module is a direct summand of a free module. Tensoring with the corresponding direct-sum decomposition shows the tensor functor for the projective summand is a direct summand of an exact functor and hence exact.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Localization is flat]\label{thm:v14-03}
$S^{-1}A$ is flat as an $A$-module.
\begin{proof}
Tensoring with $S^{-1}A$ is localization of modules, and localization is exact.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

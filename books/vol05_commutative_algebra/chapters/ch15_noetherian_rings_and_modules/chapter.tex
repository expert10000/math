\chapter{Noetherian Rings and Modules}
\label{ch:v15}

Noetherian conditions impose finiteness on ideals and submodules. They are stable under quotients and finite-type polynomial extension and support induction arguments throughout algebraic geometry.

\section*{Learning goals}
After this chapter, the reader should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
\item verify Noetherianity using ascending chains or finite generation of ideals/submodules;
\item apply Hilbert basis-type arguments to polynomial extensions where established;
\item prove that submodules and quotients of finitely generated modules over Noetherian rings retain finiteness;
\item construct stabilization arguments from ascending chains;
\item compute finite generating sets extracted from chain conditions;
\item distinguish Noetherian hypotheses from merely finitely generated individual ideals or modules;
\end{itemize}

\section{ACC on submodules}
A module is Noetherian when every ascending chain of submodules stabilizes.

\section{Finite-generation criterion}
A module is Noetherian iff every submodule is finitely generated.

\section{Noetherian rings}
A ring is Noetherian when it is Noetherian as a module over itself, equivalently every ideal is finitely generated.

\section{Exact sequences}
In a short exact sequence, the middle module is Noetherian iff both ends are Noetherian.

\section{Finite modules}
Finite modules over a Noetherian ring are Noetherian.

\section{Quotients}
Quotients of Noetherian rings and modules are Noetherian.

\section{Hilbert basis theorem}
If $A$ is Noetherian, then $A[x]$ is Noetherian.

\section{Finite-type algebras}
Iterating Hilbert's theorem shows finitely generated $A$-algebras are Noetherian.

\section{Noetherian induction}
Properties can be proved by assuming all strictly larger subobjects satisfy them.

\section{Examples}
Fields, PID-like rings, polynomial rings over fields, and many coordinate rings are Noetherian.

\section{Core structural results}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-generation criterion]\label{thm:v15-01}
An $A$-module is Noetherian iff every submodule is finitely generated.
\begin{proof}
If every submodule is finite, the union of an ascending chain is finitely generated and therefore contained in one stage. Conversely, if a submodule were not finitely generated, repeatedly adjoining new elements would produce a strictly increasing chain.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Exact-sequence criterion]\label{thm:v15-02}
For $0\to M'\to M\to M''\to0$, $M$ is Noetherian iff $M'$ and $M''$ are Noetherian.
\begin{proof}
Submodules of $M'$ and images in $M''$ control any chain in $M$. For the converse, submodules and quotients of a Noetherian module are Noetherian.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Hilbert basis theorem]\label{thm:v15-03}
If $A$ is Noetherian, then $A[x]$ is Noetherian.
\begin{proof}
For an ideal $I\subset A[x]$, leading coefficients form an ideal of $A$ and stabilize under finite generation. Choose finitely many polynomials controlling high-degree leading terms and finitely many lower-degree remainders; together they generate $I$.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

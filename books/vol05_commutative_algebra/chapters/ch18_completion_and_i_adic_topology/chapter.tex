\chapter{Completion and I-Adic Topology}
\label{ch:v18}

I-adic topology and completion encode infinite approximation by powers of an ideal. Completion packages compatible residue classes modulo $I^n$ as elements of the inverse-limit object \(\widehat M\) (or \(\widehat A\)); such an element need not come from an element of the original module or ring. Completion is fundamental for local algebra and formal geometry.

\section*{Learning goals}
After this chapter, the reader should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
\item construct inverse systems of quotients defining I-adic completion;
\item compute elementary completions such as power-series or p-adic-type examples;
\item verify separatedness by analyzing intersections of powers of an ideal;
\item compare a module with its completion through the canonical map;
\item use completeness to solve compatible systems of congruences;
\item distinguish completion from localization and from ordinary product constructions;
\end{itemize}

\begin{remark}[\(I\)-adic precision is exact algebraic precision]
An approximation modulo \(I^n\) is not a floating-point approximation.  The
statement
\[
a\equiv b\pmod{I^n}
\]
means exactly that \(a-b\in I^n\).  No norm, decimal tolerance, or rounding
threshold is involved.

For example, in \(k[[x]]\), knowing a series modulo \(x^n\) determines its
coefficients of degrees \(0,\ldots,n-1\) exactly.  Increasing \(n\) reveals
additional exact coefficients while preserving all earlier ones.  Likewise,
a \(p\)-adic approximation modulo \(p^n\) records an exact congruence class,
not an estimate with real absolute error about \(p^{-n}\).

Computational completion should therefore record the algebraic precision
level \(I^n\) and verify compatibility:
\[
a_{n+1}\bmod I^n=a_n.
\]
If arithmetic is additionally implemented with machine floating point, that
implementation error is a separate layer and must not be confused with the
\(I\)-adic filtration.  Algebraic precision and floating-point precision are
different notions even when both are informally described as
``approximation.''
\end{remark}

\section{Core structural results}

\begin{theorem}[Inverse-limit description]\label{thm:v18-01}
The I-adic completion of $M$ is the inverse limit $\varprojlim M/I^nM$.
\begin{proof}
A Cauchy-compatible system is exactly a sequence of residue classes whose class modulo $I^nM$ agrees with every later approximation. This is the defining inverse-limit condition.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Completion map kernel]\label{thm:v18-02}
The kernel of the natural map $M\to\widehat M$ is $\bigcap_{n\ge1}I^nM$.
\begin{proof}
An element maps to zero in the inverse limit exactly when it is zero in every quotient $M/I^nM$, equivalently when it lies in every $I^nM$.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Power-series completion]\label{thm:v18-03}
The $(x)$-adic completion of $k[x]$ is canonically $k[[x]]$.
\begin{proof}
A compatible sequence modulo $x^n$ uniquely specifies all coefficients of a formal power series, and truncating a power series gives a compatible residue system.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{exercise}[Compatible formal-power-series truncations]\label{exr:v18-exact-iadic-01}
In \(k[[x]]\), consider
\[
a_3=1+2x+3x^2\pmod{x^3}
\]
and
\[
a_5=1+2x+3x^2+4x^3+5x^4\pmod{x^5}.
\]

\begin{enumerate}
\item Verify that the two approximations are compatible.
\item State exactly what information \(a_5\) determines.
\item Explain why the notation ``precision \(x^5\)'' is not a decimal error
tolerance.
\end{enumerate}
\end{exercise}
\begin{hint}
Reduce \(a_5\) modulo \(x^3\).
\end{hint}
\begin{solution}
Reduction gives
\[
a_5\bmod x^3
=
1+2x+3x^2
=
a_3,
\]
so the truncations are compatible.  The class modulo \(x^5\) determines
exactly the coefficients of \(1,x,x^2,x^3,x^4\); coefficients of degree at
least \(5\) remain unspecified.

The modulus \(x^5\) describes an exact congruence class in the \(x\)-adic
filtration.  It is not a statement that a real or complex numerical error has
magnitude below some decimal threshold.
\end{solution}

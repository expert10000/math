\chapter{Free and Projective Modules}
\label{ch:v13}

Free modules have bases, while projective modules are direct summands of free modules. Projectivity is exactly the lifting property that makes Hom out of the module preserve surjections.

\section*{Learning goals}
After this chapter, the reader should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
\item recognize free modules from explicit bases and coordinate decompositions;
\item test projectivity using lifting properties, direct summands, or idempotent matrices;
\item prove that free modules are projective and that direct summands of projective modules are projective;
\item construct projective modules from idempotents and splitting exact sequences;
\item distinguish projective modules from free modules by examples where available;
\item localize projective modules and analyze finite projectivity through local criteria;
\end{itemize}

\section{Free modules}
A free module is a direct sum of copies of the ring with a distinguished basis.

\section{Rank}
Over suitable rings, finite free modules have a well-defined rank; locally, projective modules acquire ranks prime by prime.

\begin{example}[Idempotent projective]\label{ex:v13-expand-01}
If $e^2=e$, then $R=eR\oplus(1-e)R$. Thus $eR$ is a direct summand of the free module $R$ and is projective.
\end{example}

\section{Projective modules}
A module is projective if maps to quotients lift across surjections.

\section{Direct summands}
Projective modules are exactly direct summands of free modules.

\section{Idempotent matrices}
Finite projective modules arise as images of idempotent endomorphisms of finite free modules.

\begin{example}[Splitting criterion]\label{ex:v13-expand-02}
A module $P$ is projective exactly when every surjection onto $P$ splits. Applying this to a free surjection shows that projectives are precisely direct summands of free modules.
\end{example}

\section{Splitting exact sequences}
A surjection onto a projective module splits.

\section{Hom exactness}
$P$ is projective iff $\operatorname{Hom}(P,-)$ is exact.

A finitely generated projective module becomes free after localization at a prime. Its local rank is the algebraic analogue of vector-bundle rank.
\end{example}

\section{Localization}
Localization preserves projectivity, and finite projective modules are locally free.

\section{Examples}
Free modules, ideals generated by idempotents, and vector bundles over affine rings illustrate projectivity.

\section{Core structural results}

\begin{theorem}[Projective summand criterion]\label{thm:v13-01}
An $A$-module $P$ is projective iff it is a direct summand of a free module.
\begin{proof}
Choose a free module $F$ surjecting onto $P$. If $P$ is projective, the surjection splits, so $F\cong P\oplus K$. Conversely direct summands of free modules inherit the lifting property.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Splitting criterion]\label{thm:v13-02}
A module $P$ is projective iff every short exact sequence $0\to K\to M\to P\to0$ splits.
\begin{proof}
Projectivity lifts the identity map of $P$ through the surjection $M\to P$, producing a section. Conversely apply the splitting property to pullbacks of arbitrary surjections.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Hom exactness criterion]\label{thm:v13-03}
$P$ is projective iff $\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,-)$ is an exact functor.
\begin{proof}
Hom is always left exact. Exactness on surjections is precisely the lifting property defining projectivity.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

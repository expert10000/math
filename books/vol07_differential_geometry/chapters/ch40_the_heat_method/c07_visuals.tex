% C07 conceptual visual: heat method pipeline.
\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\begin{tikzpicture}[>=Stealth,node distance=0.8cm]
  \node[draw,rounded corners,align=center] (src) {source\\$\delta_s$};
  \node[draw,rounded corners,right=of src,align=center] (heat) {diffuse\\$(M+tL)u=M\delta_s$};
  \node[draw,rounded corners,right=of heat,align=center] (grad) {normalize\\$X=-\nabla u/\|\nabla u\|$};
  \node[draw,rounded corners,right=of grad,align=center] (pois) {integrate\\$L\phi=b$};
  \node[draw,rounded corners,right=of pois,align=center] (dist) {normalize\\distance field};
  \draw[->] (src) -- (heat);
  \draw[->] (heat) -- (grad);
  \draw[->] (grad) -- (pois);
  \draw[->] (pois) -- (dist);
\end{tikzpicture}
\caption{The heat method is a three-stage geometric pipeline, not a direct reading of
heat magnitude. Diffusion supplies a direction field, normalization removes magnitude,
and a Poisson solve reconstructs the scalar field whose gradient best matches that direction.}
\label{fig:vii40-c07-heat-pipeline}
\end{figure}

Smooth sign & $\Delta=\operatorname{div}\nabla$ is nonpositive on a closed manifold.\\
Discrete sign & $L$ is the positive-semidefinite stiffness matrix representing $-\Delta$ weakly.\\
Heat time & $t=mh^2$ ties diffusion length to mesh scale.\\
Poisson gauge & On a connected closed mesh, $L\mathbf 1=0$; pin or normalize the additive constant.\\
Boundary & Neumann and Dirichlet conditions solve different geometric problems and must be chosen deliberately.\\

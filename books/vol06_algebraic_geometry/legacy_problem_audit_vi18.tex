\section*{Audit rule}
The Volume VI invariant is
\[
\boxed{\text{every substantive legacy problem}\longrightarrow\text{exactly one canonical destination}.}
\]
VI/18 was therefore audited from both the migration atlas and the volume-wide problem ledger
before the canonical Problem set was written.

\section*{Migration scopes assigned to VI/18}
The source migration atlas assigns three exercise/problem scopes directly to VI/18:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{theory-of-algebraic-geometry-3.tex}, descendants of
\texttt{affine scheme|Spec A} (VI-SCOPE-029);
\item the same file, descendants of \texttt{D(f)|Spec A\_f} (VI-SCOPE-031);
\item the commutative-algebra source \texttt{theory-of-commutative-algebra-13.tex};
its affine-scheme/structure-sheaf descendants form VI-SCOPE-068.
\end{enumerate}
The ledger adds an important fourth recovery target outside those direct selectors:
AG6 Problem 6 is a multi-property problem whose non-affineness subproblem had already been
split and reserved for VI/18.  This is precisely why the volume ledger is required in
addition to chapter-local selectors.

\section*{Cross-source variants}
The affine/structure-sheaf section of \texttt{theory-of-commutative-algebra-13.tex} supplies
examples such as distinguished opens, localizations, stalks, and regular functions.  Its
relevant VI/18 scope contains no additional numbered Problem or Exercise whose unique center
is affine schemes.  Those examples are merged into the regular and advanced example banks,
not promoted to false legacy Problems.

The AG3 affine-scheme and basic-open theory streams are likewise merged into the chapter
around the two recovered corpus items.  Repeated explanatory statements of localization are
treated as variants of AG3 Problem 11, not as additional \emph{legacy} Problems.

\section*{Canonical derivative problems and non-duplication}
The canonical chapter intentionally contains several pedagogical problems that reuse consequences
of already-accounted legacy material.  These are not additional corpus migrations and therefore do
not receive independent legacy-ledger rows:
\begin{itemize}
\item VI.18.P4 cites the principal-open basis theorem already preserved from AG1 Problem 1 in
VI.08.P1, then adds the affine conclusion using VI.18.P1, so the earlier proof is not re-migrated;
\item VI.18.P14 and VI.18.P15 are computational applications of the distinguished-open/localization
dictionary centered in VI.18.P1;
\item VI.18.P19 is an explicit transitivity variant of VI.18.P1, using
$(A_f)_{g/1}\cong A_{fg}$;
\item VI.18.P20 is a conceptual reinforcement of the non-affineness result VI.18.P2.
\end{itemize}
Thus the uniqueness invariant concerns \emph{legacy provenance}: AG3 Problem 11 is consumed once,
as VI.18.P1, and AG6 Problem 6.4 is consumed once, as VI.18.P2.  Canonical derivative exercises
and problems may reuse those theorems provided their provenance explicitly records the reuse.

\section*{No-loss status after VI/18}
The ledger is updated as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item AG3-P11 $\to$ VI.18.P1, status \texttt{MIGRATED\_DRAFT};
\item AG6-P6d $\to$ VI.18.P2, status \texttt{MIGRATED\_DRAFT};
\item VI-SCOPE-029, VI-SCOPE-031, and VI-SCOPE-068 receive the VI/18 instance-audit marker;
\item AG3-P12, AG3-P13, and AG3-P14 remain \texttt{RESERVED} for VI/19, VI/21, and VI/20;
\item the other subparts of AG6 Problem 6 remain tracked at their own destinations, so
marking AG6-P6d complete does not incorrectly mark the entire legacy Problem 6 complete.
\end{itemize}

Thus no substantive affine-scheme corpus problem is silently dropped, duplicated, or consumed
by the wrong canonical chapter.

\section*{Audit invariant}
The Volume VI invariant remains
\[
\boxed{\text{every substantive legacy problem}\longrightarrow\text{exactly one canonical destination}.}
\]
For VI/19 this means distinguishing the single numbered legacy universal-property problem
from the many useful canonical consequences that naturally repeat pieces of its proof.

\section*{Nearby affine-morphism variants}
The T03 affine-morphism theory stream contains statements that are mathematical components or
consequences of AG3 Problem 12: a ring map $A\to B$ gives $\Spec B\to\Spec A$; principal
opens pull back by $D(a)\mapsto D(\varphi(a))$; localization gives the sheaf map; stalk maps
are local; and composition is contravariant.  These are merged into the chapter theory and the
full VI.19.P1 dossier rather than promoted to additional legacy Problems.

The neighboring VI/18 localization theorem AG3 Problem 11 is used only by citation when the
morphism induced by $A\to A_f$ is identified with the inclusion $D(f)\hookrightarrow\Spec A$.
It is not re-migrated.  Likewise, quotient-map examples are kept at the level of affine morphisms
and underlying closed subsets; the closed-subscheme theorem itself remains reserved for VI/22.

\section*{Canonical derivative problems and non-duplication}
Several canonical problems intentionally reuse the universal-property machinery:
\begin{itemize}
\item VI.19.P2 and P3 isolate the explicit $\Spec(\varphi)$ construction and functoriality;
\item VI.19.P4 is the affine anti-equivalence specialization;
\item VI.19.P7 combines VI.18.P1 with the new morphism language for localization;
\item VI.19.P10 specializes the universal property to maps into the affine line;
\item VI.19.P13 studies the topological closure of the image of a spectrum morphism;
\item VI.19.P15 and P16 are quotient/localization factorization criteria;
\item VI.19.P17 shows why the sheaf component cannot be discarded;
\item VI.19.P19 extracts the point-recovery uniqueness step;
\item VI.19.P20 records naturality explicitly.
\end{itemize}
None of these is a second migration of AG3 Problem 12.  Their provenance labels identify them
as canonical derivatives or applications of VI.19.P1.

\section*{Boundary audit}
The chapter deliberately does not consume later legacy centers:
\begin{itemize}
\item AG3-P13 remains \texttt{RESERVED} for VI/21; no proof here asserts the final-object
property of $\Spec\mathbb Z$ or develops structure morphisms as a canonical problem;
\item AG3-P14 remains \texttt{RESERVED} for VI/20; no general gluing theorem is proved here;
\item closed-subscheme language is deferred to VI/22 even though quotient-induced affine
morphisms are computed;
\item fiber products and base change remain reserved for VI/23--VI/24.
\end{itemize}

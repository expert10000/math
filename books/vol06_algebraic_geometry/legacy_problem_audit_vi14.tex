\section*{Volume-wide preservation invariant}
Beginning with VI/14, Volume VI uses two permanent editorial ledgers:
\begin{itemize}
\item the Volume VI legacy-problem ledger, which tracks substantive
legacy problem instances and their unique canonical destinations;
\item the Volume VI problem-source coverage ledger, which records every
problem, exercise, or solution migration selector assigned anywhere in Volume VI and therefore
prevents a source stream from disappearing merely because its problems occur in another file
or under another numbering scheme.
\end{itemize}
The invariant is
\[
\boxed{\text{every substantive legacy problem}\longrightarrow
\text{exactly one verified canonical destination}.}
\]
Allowed dispositions are \textsc{include}, \textsc{merge}, \textsc{move}, \textsc{split},
and \textsc{archive-duplicate}.  A problem is archived only when a comparison establishes
that it contains no unique mathematics beyond the retained version.

\section*{Duplicate and variant audit}
The repository duplicate map classifies the \texttt{merged\_exercises*} family as
source-only historical exercise snapshots.  The representative base, full-v2, and latest
corrected snapshots were inspected in addition to the migration selectors; they contain
analysis exercises rather than an additional associated-sheaf problem.  They therefore do
not create a second VI/14 problem instance.  This is recorded in the global ledger rather
than silently assumed.

No separate numbered sheafification problem was found in AG2.  Its occurrences of the word
``sheafification'' belong to image, cokernel, and quotient constructions and remain routed to
VI/15.  Therefore the corpus audit yields one substantive numbered legacy sheafification
problem: AG1 Exercise 4.  It is VI.14.P1 and is not shortened.

\section*{Canonical VI/14 problem inventory}
The chapter contains twenty Problem Dossiers.  VI.14.P1 is the preserved multi-part legacy
problem.  P2--P20 are new synthesis or legacy-derived problems used to deepen the chapter;
they are explicitly labeled as such in their provenance paragraphs and do not pretend to be
additional legacy problems.  Their presence is pedagogical, not a quota.

The canonical inventory covers unit failure of injectivity and surjectivity, bounded and
locally polynomial sheafifications, uniqueness, functoriality, stalk maps, the two-plus
construction, equivalence of constructions, restriction to opens, basis extension, constant
presheaves, adjunction, local equivalences, idempotence, finite discrete spaces, principal
open localization, and preservation of coproducts.

\section*{No-loss conclusion}
For the VI/14 topic, every migration selector with problem/exercise descendants has been
accounted for and every nearby numbered problem has a recorded destination.  AG1 Exercise 4
is fully migrated; AG1 Exercises 5--6 and AG2 Problems 7--9 remain explicitly reserved for
their later canonical chapters.  Thus no substantive legacy problem is deleted, hidden in an
example, or consumed merely to keep a preferred chapter size.

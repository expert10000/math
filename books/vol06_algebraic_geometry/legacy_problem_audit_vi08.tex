\section*{Audit rule}
Every substantive legacy problem is retained exactly once in the canonical series.  A source
item is either included here, merged into an already retained canonical item, or explicitly
routed to the chapter where its mathematics belongs.  No problem is discarded merely to
keep a target problem count.

\section*{Primary legacy source: Theory of Algebraic Geometry 1}
The legacy file contains three large opening problems followed by theory sections on spectra,
principal opens, localization, points, and connectedness.  The canonical destinations are:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.11\textwidth}p{0.46\textwidth}p{0.27\textwidth}}
\toprule
\textbf{Legacy} & \textbf{Mathematical content} & \textbf{Canonical destination}\\
\midrule
Problem 1 & $D(f)$ form a basis; $D(f)=\varnothing$ iff $f$ is nilpotent & VI.08.P1 --- included here\\
Problem 2 & survey of $\Spec\RR[x]$, $\Spec\CC[x,y]$, $\Spec\ZZ[x]$, $\Spec\FF_p[x]$ & VI.07.P1 --- already retained\\
Problem 3 & disconnected spectrum, orthogonal idempotents, product rings & VI/05 --- already retained as connectedness/idempotent dossier\\

\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The same legacy file has an explicit \emph{Basic Opens $D(f)$} section and a separate
localization interpretation.  Both are primary theory sources for VI/08.  They supply the
basis theorem, $D(fg)=D(f)\cap D(g)$, the localization homeomorphism
$\Spec A_f\cong D(f)$, and the empty-open/nilpotence criterion.  The later generic/closed
point material is reserved for VI/09, the systematic nilpotent/reduced discussion for
VI/10, and local rings/residue fields for VI/11.

\section*{Boundary check against later algebraic-geometry sources}
A later legacy source also discusses $D(f)\cong\Spec A_f$ inside the construction of affine
schemes.  In the migration architecture that material belongs to VI/18 because there it is
used as part of the structure-sheaf/affine-scheme package.  VI/08 proves the topological and
one-element localization theorem now; VI/18 may cite it rather than duplicate the full
problem corpus.

\section*{Canonical VI/08 problem inventory}
VI/08 contains thirteen Problem dossiers.  P1 is the primary recovered legacy problem.
P2--P13 are new canonical synthesis problems that expand the legacy theory without replacing
or hiding legacy material:
\begin{enumerate}
\item P1 legacy principal-open basis and nilpotence criterion;
\item P2 radical criterion for inclusion/equality;
\item P3 general principal-open cover criterion;
\item P4 quasi-compactness of principal opens;
\item P5 $\Spec A_f\cong D(f)$;
\item P6 arithmetic principal opens in $\Spec\ZZ$;
\item P7 nested localization;
\item P8 quotients and principal localization;
\item P9 functorial preimages of principal opens;
\item P10 finite-cover/unit-ideal certificates;
\item P11 crossing axes and removal of components;
\item P12 product rings;
\item P13 dual numbers and topological invisibility of nilpotents.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Audit conclusion}
The only named legacy opening problem whose primary mathematical home is VI/08 is legacy
Problem 1, and it is retained in full as VI.08.P1.  The other named problems from the same
source are already assigned to VI/07 and VI/05.  Practice exercises and later mixed topics
from the comparison source are explicitly routed rather than duplicated.  Thus the
reconstruction preserves the global invariant:
\[
\boxed{\text{every substantive legacy problem has exactly one canonical destination}.}
\]

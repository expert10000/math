\subsection*{Challenge 1: Explicit splitting idempotents}
\begin{solution}
Use the isomorphism
\[
\Phi:\mathbb C\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb C\times\mathbb C,
\qquad \Phi(z\otimes w)=(zw,z\overline w).
\]
We want preimages of $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$.  Put $q=i\otimes i$.  Since
\[
\Phi(q)=(i^2,i\overline i)=(-1,1),
\]
we have
\[
e_+=\frac12(1\otimes1-q),\qquad e_-=\frac12(1\otimes1+q),
\]
with $\Phi(e_+)=(1,0)$ and $\Phi(e_-)=(0,1)$.  Directly, $q^2=(i^2)\otimes(i^2)=1$, so
\[
e_+^2=\frac14(1-2q+q^2)=\frac12(1-q)=e_+,
\]
and similarly $e_-^2=e_-$.  Also $e_+e_-=\frac14(1-q^2)=0$ and $e_++e_-=1$.
Thus the tensor product decomposes as $e_+R\times e_-R$, where $R=\mathbb C\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$.  On spectra these orthogonal idempotents cut out the two open-and-closed complex points.
\end{solution}

\subsection*{Challenge 2: Finite separable splitting}
\begin{solution}
Let $L/k$ be finite separable and choose a primitive element $\alpha$, so
$L\cong k[t]/(m(t))$ with $m$ separable.  Over an algebraic closure $\overline k$,
\[
L\otimes_k\overline k\cong\overline k[t]/(m(t)).
\]
Because $m$ is separable, it has distinct roots $\sigma(\alpha)$ indexed by the $k$-embeddings
$\sigma:L\hookrightarrow\overline k$.  Hence
\[
m(t)=\prod_{\sigma}(t-\sigma(\alpha))
\]
with pairwise comaximal linear factors.  The Chinese remainder theorem gives
\[
L\otimes_k\overline k
\cong
\prod_{\sigma:L\hookrightarrow\overline k}\overline k.
\]
This description is independent of the chosen primitive element: intrinsically the factor indexed by $\sigma$ is obtained from the multiplication map $L\otimes_k\overline k\to\overline k$, $x\otimes a\mapsto\sigma(x)a$.  Geometrically,
\[
(\Spec L)_{\overline k}
\cong\coprod_{\sigma:L\hookrightarrow\overline k}\Spec\overline k.
\]
Thus a separable point splits into one geometric point for each embedding into the algebraic closure.
\end{solution}

\subsection*{Challenge 3: Inseparable thickening}
\begin{solution}
Let $k=\mathbb F_p(s^p)$ and $L=\mathbb F_p(s)$.  Writing the second copy with a variable $u$ gives
\[
L\otimes_kL\cong L[u]/(u^p-s^p).
\]
In characteristic $p$, $u^p-s^p=(u-s)^p$.  With $\eta=u-s$ we obtain
\[
L\otimes_kL\cong L[\eta]/(\eta^p).
\]
This is a local Artinian ring with unique prime ideal $(\eta)$, residue field $L$, and nilradical $(\eta)$.  Its powers form the chain
\[
(\eta)\supset(\eta^2)\supset\cdots\supset(\eta^{p-1})\supset(\eta^p)=0.
\]
Therefore the spectrum has only one underlying topological point, but the structure sheaf contains a nilpotent infinitesimal thickening of length $p$ over $L$.  Both input schemes $\Spec L$ are reduced; the nilpotents arise because the minimal polynomial becomes a repeated $p$th power after the purely inseparable base change.  This is precisely the obstruction that separability avoids in Challenge 2.
\end{solution}

\subsection*{Challenge 4: Base change and scheme-theoretic intersection}
\begin{solution}
Let $X=\Spec B$ be an affine $A$-scheme and let $Z_1,Z_2$ be defined by ideals $I,J\subseteq B$.  Their scheme-theoretic intersection is
\[
Z_1\cap_X Z_2=\Spec B/(I+J).
\]
After base change along $A\to A'$, put $B'=B\otimes_AA'$.  Then
\[
(Z_1\cap_XZ_2)_{A'}
=\Spec\bigl((B/(I+J))\otimes_AA'\bigr)
\cong\Spec\bigl(B'/(I+J)B'\bigr).
\]
On the other hand,
\[
(Z_1)_{A'}=V(IB')\subseteq\Spec B',\qquad
(Z_2)_{A'}=V(JB')\subseteq\Spec B'.
\]
Their scheme-theoretic intersection is defined by the sum
\[
IB'+JB'=(I+J)B'.
\]
Hence
\[
(Z_1\cap_XZ_2)_{A'}\cong (Z_1)_{A'}\cap_{X_{A'}}(Z_2)_{A'}.
\]
Categorically this is also an instance of products commuting with base change, since a scheme-theoretic intersection of closed subschemes is their fiber product over $X$.
\end{solution}

\subsection*{Challenge 5: Stability/descent audit}
\begin{solution}
The following table separates statements proved directly in VI/24 from properties for which no arbitrary-base-change assertion is available here.
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{p{0.23\textwidth}p{0.22\textwidth}p{0.43\textwidth}}
\raggedright\textbf{Property} & \raggedright\textbf{Arbitrary base change?} & \raggedright\textbf{Reason / warning}\tabularnewline\hline
\raggedright isomorphism & \raggedright yes & \raggedright Base-change the inverse; functoriality preserves both identity composites.\tabularnewline
\raggedright open immersion & \raggedright yes & \raggedright Affine-locally, localization commutes with tensor product.\tabularnewline
\raggedright closed immersion & \raggedright yes & \raggedright Affine-locally, surjections remain surjective by right exactness.\tabularnewline
\raggedright monomorphism & \raggedright yes & \raggedright Pullback uniqueness preserves left-cancellability.\tabularnewline
\raggedright connectedness of the total scheme & \raggedright no & \raggedright $\Spec\mathbb C$ over $\mathbb R$ becomes two points over $\mathbb C$.\tabularnewline
\raggedright irreducibility of the total scheme & \raggedright no & \raggedright The same example becomes a union of two proper closed components.\tabularnewline
\raggedright reducedness & \raggedright no in general & \raggedright Purely inseparable scalar extension can create nilpotents.\tabularnewline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The first four rows are \emph{stability} assertions: if a morphism has the property before base change, its pullback has the property afterward.  A \emph{descent} assertion runs in the opposite logical direction: from a property after some cover or extension, infer the property before base change.  Such statements need additional hypotheses (for example faithful flatness in many contexts) and are not proved in VI/24.  Likewise later chapters distinguish geometric connectedness, geometric irreducibility, and geometric reducedness precisely because ordinary connectedness, irreducibility, and reducedness can change under field extension.
\end{solution}

\subsection*{VI.24.P14: Geometric interpretation of $\mathbb C/\mathbb R$ splitting}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi24-p14}
Explain why P1 shows that connectedness and irreducibility can change under field extension.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This dossier isolates a reusable piece of the base-change calculus and records both its categorical and affine content.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Start from the Cartesian universal property.  When the schemes are affine, translate the square contravariantly into tensor products, quotients, or localizations, and then translate the resulting ring statement back into geometry.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Identify the relevant pullback or scalar-extension ring.
\item Use the appropriate universal property or exactness statement.
\item Check that the canonical maps are the expected projections.
\item State the geometric consequence, not only the ring isomorphism.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
As an $\mathbb R$-scheme, $X=\Spec\mathbb C$ has one point.  A one-point space is both connected and irreducible, and $\mathbb C$ is a field, so the scheme is reduced as well.  P1 computes
\[
X_{\mathbb C}=\Spec(\mathbb C\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C)
\cong\Spec(\mathbb C\times\mathbb C).
\]
The spectrum of a product is the disjoint union of the spectra, hence
\[
X_{\mathbb C}\cong\Spec\mathbb C\amalg\Spec\mathbb C.
\]
This has two open-and-closed points, so it is disconnected.  It is also reducible because it is the union of two proper closed subsets.  Thus connectedness and irreducibility of the total scheme need not survive field extension.  The example also clarifies the correct invariant language: one later distinguishes geometric connectedness and geometric irreducibility precisely by asking what happens after field extension.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The base-changed object is forced by a universal property; tensor products make that universal property computable on affine charts.

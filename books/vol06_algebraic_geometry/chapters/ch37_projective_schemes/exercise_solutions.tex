% VI37 detailed exercise solutions -- VI/24+ proof-depth standard.

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-01
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E1.}
Ordinary localization commutes with quotient:
\[
(S/I)_f\cong S_f/IS_f.
\]
All maps preserve the grading because \(I\) and \(f\) are homogeneous. Taking degree-zero parts yields
\[
(S/I)_{(f)}
=
((S/I)_f)_0
\cong
(S_f/IS_f)_0
\cong
(S_f)_0/(IS_f)_0.
\]
By definition \((S_f)_0=S_{(f)}\) and \((IS_f)_0=I_{(f)}\). Hence
\[
\boxed{(S/I)_{(f)}\cong S_{(f)}/I_{(f)}}.
\]

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-02
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E2.}
A prime of \(S/I\) is of the form \(\mathfrak p/I\) with \(\mathfrak p\supseteq I\). Homogeneity is preserved under this correspondence. The irrelevant ideal of \(S/I\) is
\[
(S_++I)/I.
\]
Thus \(\mathfrak p/I\) belongs to \(\Proj(S/I)\) exactly when
\[
S_+\not\subseteq\mathfrak p.
\]
Therefore the projectively relevant primes of the quotient are precisely the points
\[
\mathfrak p\in\Proj S
\]
containing \(I\), namely
\[
\boxed{V_+(I)}.
\]

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-03
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E3.}
The quotient is
\[
k[x_0,x_1,x_2]/(x_2)\cong k[x_0,x_1]
\]
with the standard grading. Hence
\[
\boxed{\Proj k[x_0,x_1,x_2]/(x_2)\cong\mathbb P^1_k}.
\]
It is the coordinate line \(x_2=0\subseteq\mathbb P^2_k\).

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-04
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E4.}
Let
\[
F=XZ-Y^2.
\]
On \(X\neq0\), set \(y=Y/X\), \(z=Z/X\). Dividing by \(X^2\) gives
\[
z-y^2=0.
\]
On \(Y\neq0\), set \(x=X/Y\), \(z=Z/Y\). Dividing by \(Y^2\) gives
\[
xz-1=0.
\]
On \(Z\neq0\), set \(x=X/Z\), \(y=Y/Z\). Dividing by \(Z^2\) gives
\[
x-y^2=0.
\]
Thus the three affine chart equations are
\[
\boxed{z=y^2,\qquad xz=1,\qquad x=y^2}.
\]

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-05
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E5.}
Let
\[
S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n].
\]
Then \(\dim S=n+1\). If \(F\neq0\), every minimal prime over \((F)\) has height one by the principal ideal theorem, since \(S\) is a domain and \(F\) is nonzero. Therefore every irreducible component of
\[
\Spec S/(F)
\]
has dimension \(n\). For a nonempty standard graded cone, passing to Proj lowers the dimension by one, so each projective component has dimension
\[
\boxed{n-1}.
\]

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-06
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E6.}
The two line ideals are
\[
I=(x_0),\qquad J=(x_1).
\]
Scheme-theoretic intersection is defined by \(I+J\):
\[
I+J=(x_0,x_1).
\]
Hence
\[
X\cap Y
=
\Proj k[x_0,x_1,x_2]/(x_0,x_1)
\cong
\Proj k[x_2]
\cong
\Spec k.
\]
It is the reduced point
\[
\boxed{[0:0:1]}.
\]

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-07
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E7.}
In the UFD \(k[x_0,x_1,\ldots]\), the elements \(x_0,x_1\) are relatively prime. If \(f\in(x_0)\cap(x_1)\), then both \(x_0\mid f\) and \(x_1\mid f\), so \(x_0x_1\mid f\). Thus
\[
(x_0)\cap(x_1)=(x_0x_1).
\]
Therefore the scheme-theoretic union of the two coordinate hyperplanes is
\[
\boxed{\Proj S/(x_0x_1)}.
\]

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-08
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E8.}
The radical of \((x_0^2)\) is \((x_0)\), so both schemes have underlying closed set \(V_+(x_0)\). But on a chart \(x_j\neq0\), \(j\neq0\), put
\[
u=x_0/x_j.
\]
For the doubled hyperplane the local ring contains the nonzero nilpotent class satisfying
\[
u^2=0.
\]
For the reduced hyperplane, \(u=0\). Hence the structure sheaves differ:
\[
\boxed{\Proj S/(x_0^2)\neq\Proj S/(x_0)}
\]
as schemes, although their topological spaces agree.

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-09
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E9.}
Let
\[
\mathfrak m=(x_0,x_1,x_2),\qquad I=x_0\mathfrak m.
\]
Since
\[
\mathfrak m x_0\subseteq I,
\]
we have \(x_0\in I^{\mathrm{sat}}\), hence
\[
(x_0)\subseteq I^{\mathrm{sat}}.
\]
Conversely \(I\subseteq(x_0)\). The ideal \((x_0)\) is saturated: if \(\mathfrak m^r g\subseteq(x_0)\), then in particular \(x_1^r g\in(x_0)\); since \(x_0\) and \(x_1\) are relatively prime, \(x_0\mid g\). Therefore
\[
I^{\mathrm{sat}}\subseteq(x_0),
\]
so
\[
\boxed{I^{\mathrm{sat}}=(x_0)}.
\]

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-10
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E10.}
If \(S_+^r\subseteq I\) and \(\mathfrak p\supseteq I\) is prime, then
\[
S_+^r\subseteq\mathfrak p.
\]
For every homogeneous \(f\in S_+\), \(f^r\in\mathfrak p\), so \(f\in\mathfrak p\). Hence
\[
S_+\subseteq\mathfrak p.
\]
No prime containing \(I\) satisfies the Proj condition. Thus
\[
\boxed{\Proj(S/I)=\varnothing}.
\]

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-11
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E11.}
Assume \(\Proj(S/I)=\varnothing\). Then every homogeneous prime containing \(I\) contains \(S_+\), so
\[
S_+\subseteq\sqrt I.
\]
The ideal \(S_+=(x_0,\ldots,x_n)\) is finitely generated. For each \(x_i\), choose \(r_i\) with \(x_i^{r_i}\in I\). Set
\[
N=(r_0-1)+\cdots+(r_n-1)+1.
\]
Every monomial of degree \(N\) is divisible by some \(x_i^{r_i}\), hence lies in \(I\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{S_+^N\subseteq I}.
\]

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-12
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E12.}
Using \(x=x_1/x_0\), \(y=x_2/x_0\), the equation \(y-x^2\) homogenizes to
\[
\boxed{x_0x_2-x_1^2}.
\]
At infinity \(x_0=0\), so \(x_1=0\). The only projective point is
\[
\boxed{[0:0:1]}.
\]

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-13
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E13.}
The equation \(xy-1\) homogenizes to
\[
\boxed{x_1x_2-x_0^2}.
\]
At infinity \(x_0=0\), so \(x_1x_2=0\). Hence the two points at infinity are
\[
\boxed{[0:1:0],\qquad[0:0:1]}.
\]

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-14
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E14.}
The homogeneous coordinate ring of the conic is
\[
A=k[X,Y,Z]/(XZ-Y^2).
\]
Its affine cone is
\[
\boxed{C(X)=\Spec A}.
\]
The irrelevant ideal
\[
A_+=(X,Y,Z)
\]
defines the cone vertex, namely the origin of \(\mathbb A^3\).

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-15
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E15.}
If \(A\) is reduced, every localization \(A_f\) is reduced. The degree-zero chart ring
\[
A_{(f)}=(A_f)_0
\]
is a subring of the reduced ring \(A_f\), hence is reduced. Standard opens cover \(\Proj A\), so every affine chart is reduced. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\Proj A\text{ is reduced}}.
\]

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-16
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E16.}
If \(A\) is a domain, every localization \(A_f\) is a domain, and so is the subring \(A_{(f)}\). Hence every nonempty standard chart is integral. Since \(A\) itself has the homogeneous prime \((0)\), the point \((0)\) belongs to every nonempty \(D_+(f)\); thus all nonempty standard charts meet at the generic point. Their union is therefore irreducible and reduced. Hence
\[
\boxed{\Proj A\text{ is integral}}
\]
when it is nonempty.

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-17
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E17.}
Let
\[
A=k[x_0,x_1,z]/(z^2,x_0z,x_1z).
\]
On \(D_+(x_0)\), \(x_0\) is invertible. The relation \(x_0z=0\) therefore implies
\[
z=0
\]
in \(A_{x_0}\), hence in \(A_{(x_0)}\). Similarly \(z=0\) on \(D_+(x_1)\). These two opens cover \(\Proj A\), because \(z\) is nilpotent and hence lies in every prime. Thus the nilpotent class \(z\) is projectively invisible.

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-18
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E18.}
For
\[
A=k[x_0,x_1,x_2]/(x_0x_1),
\]
the minimal homogeneous primes are
\[
(x_0),\qquad(x_1).
\]
Neither contains the irrelevant ideal. Hence both contribute projective components:
\[
V_+(x_0)\cong\mathbb P^1,\qquad
V_+(x_1)\cong\mathbb P^1.
\]
Therefore \(X\) is the union of the two coordinate lines.

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-19
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E19.}
The point \([1:0:0]\) has ideal
\[
I_1=(x_1,x_2),
\]
and \([0:1:0]\) has ideal
\[
I_2=(x_0,x_2).
\]
Their union is defined by the intersection
\[
I_1\cap I_2.
\]
A direct calculation gives
\[
I_1\cap I_2=(x_2,x_0x_1).
\]
Hence one homogeneous defining ideal is
\[
\boxed{(x_2,x_0x_1)}.
\]

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-20
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E20.}
On the chart \(x_0\neq0\), set
\[
u=x_1/x_0,\qquad v=x_2/x_0.
\]
The homogeneous ideal \((x_1,x_2)^2\) dehomogenizes to
\[
(u,v)^2=(u^2,uv,v^2).
\]
Hence near \([1:0:0]\) the scheme is
\[
\boxed{\Spec k[u,v]/(u,v)^2}.
\]
It has one underlying point and local \(k\)-vector-space basis
\[
1,u,v,
\]
so its length is \(3\).

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-21
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E21.}
The standard presentation of \(\mathbb P^1\) is
\[
\mathbb P^1=\Proj k[s,t].
\]
Its quadratic Veronese subring is
\[
k[s,t]^{(2)}
=
k[s^2,st,t^2]
\cong
k[X,Y,Z]/(XZ-Y^2).
\]
VI/35 proves
\[
\Proj k[s,t]\cong\Proj k[s,t]^{(2)}.
\]
Thus the two graded rings give isomorphic projective schemes, even though the graded rings themselves are different. The homogeneous coordinate ring therefore depends on the chosen projective embedding.

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-22
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E22.}
If \(g\in I^{\mathrm{sat}}\), choose \(r\) with
\[
S_+^r g\subseteq I.
\]
Then in particular
\[
x_i^r g\in I.
\]
After localizing at \(x_i\), the element \(x_i^r\) is a unit, so
\[
g\in IS_{x_i}.
\]
Hence
\[
I^{\mathrm{sat}}S_{x_i}\subseteq IS_{x_i}.
\]
The reverse inclusion follows from \(I\subseteq I^{\mathrm{sat}}\). Thus
\[
\boxed{IS_{x_i}=I^{\mathrm{sat}}S_{x_i}}.
\]

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-23
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E23.}
Let \(I_{\overline Y}\) be the saturated homogeneous ideal of the projective closure. The hyperplane at infinity is defined by \((x_0)\). Scheme-theoretic intersection is obtained by adding ideals:
\[
\boxed{
\overline Y\cap H_\infty
=
\Proj S/(I_{\overline Y}+(x_0)).
}
\]
Thus one may compute chart equations by setting \(x_0=0\), but only after the correct closure ideal has been obtained. If one merely substitutes into unsaturated homogenized generators, spurious components or embedded structure may survive.

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-24
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E24.}
On a chart \(x_j\neq0\), \(j\neq0\), write
\[
u=x_0/x_j.
\]
For the quotient by \((x_0^2)\), the local equation is
\[
u^2=0.
\]
The class \(u\) is nilpotent and generally nonzero. For the quotient by \((x_0)\), one instead has
\[
u=0.
\]
Passing to the radical therefore kills precisely the nilpotent normal direction represented by \(u\).

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-25
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E25.}
It is enough to prove
\[
I^{\mathrm{sat}}\subseteq J^{\mathrm{sat}}
\]
and then argue symmetrically. Let \(g\in I^{\mathrm{sat}}\) be homogeneous. Since
\[
IS_{x_i}=JS_{x_i}
\]
for every \(i\), and \(g\in I^{\mathrm{sat}}S_{x_i}=IS_{x_i}\), there exists \(n_i\ge0\) such that
\[
x_i^{n_i}g\in J.
\]
Set
\[
N=(n_0-1)_++\cdots+(n_n-1)_++1,
\]
where terms with \(n_i=0\) may be omitted. Choosing all \(n_i\ge1\) causes no loss. Then every monomial of degree \(N\) is divisible by some \(x_i^{n_i}\). Hence
\[
S_+^Ng\subseteq J,
\]
so \(g\in J^{\mathrm{sat}}\). Thus
\[
\boxed{I^{\mathrm{sat}}=J^{\mathrm{sat}}}.
\]

% VI37-DETAILED-EXERCISE-26
\paragraph{Solution VI.37.E26.}
Let
\[
f_1=x^2-y,\qquad
f_2=x^3-xy-x.
\]
Since
\[
f_2-xf_1=-x,
\]
we have \(x\in J\). Then
\[
y=x^2-f_1\in J,
\]
so
\[
J=(x,y).
\]

Homogenize using \(z\):
\[
F_1=X^2-YZ,
\]
\[
F_2=X^3-XYZ-XZ^2.
\]
Let
\[
H=(F_1,F_2).
\]
Then
\[
F_2-XF_1=-XZ^2,
\]
so
\[
XZ^2\in H.
\]
Hence \(X\in H:Z^\infty\). From \(F_1=X^2-YZ\) and \(X\in H:Z^\infty\), we get \(YZ\in H:Z^\infty\), so \(Y\in H:Z^\infty\). Therefore
\[
(X,Y)\subseteq H:Z^\infty.
\]
Conversely both \(F_1,F_2\) lie in \((X,Y)\), and \((X,Y)\) is \(Z\)-saturated. Thus
\[
\boxed{H:Z^\infty=(X,Y)}.
\]
The naive generators carry an extra scheme contribution at \(z=0\); saturation removes it and leaves the projective closure of the affine origin.

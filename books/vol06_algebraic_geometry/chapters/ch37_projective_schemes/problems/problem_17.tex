\begin{problem}[VI.37.P17: Projective closure of the cusp]\label{prob:vi37-17}
Let
\[
Y=V(y^2-x^3)\subseteq\mathbb A^2_k.
\]
Compute its projective closure in \(\mathbb P^2_k\), determine its point at infinity, and write the affine equation on the chart \(y\neq0\).

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical VI/37 extension.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Use homogeneous coordinates
\[
[x_0:x_1:x_2]
\]
with
\[
x=x_1/x_0,\qquad y=x_2/x_0.
\]
The affine polynomial has degree three, so its homogenization is
\[
\boxed{
F=x_0x_2^2-x_1^3.
}
\]
The projective closure is
\[
\overline Y=V_+(F).
\]

At infinity \(x_0=0\), the equation becomes
\[
-x_1^3=0,
\]
so \(x_1=0\), and therefore the unique point at infinity is
\[
\boxed{[0:0:1]}.
\]

On the chart \(x_2\neq0\), set
\[
u=x_0/x_2,\qquad v=x_1/x_2.
\]
Dividing \(F\) by \(x_2^3\) gives
\[
u-v^3=0.
\]
Thus this chart of the closure is
\[
\boxed{\Spec k[u,v]/(u-v^3)\cong\mathbb A^1_k}.
\]
The singularity of the cusp lies on the original affine chart, not at its point at infinity.
\end{solution}
\fi

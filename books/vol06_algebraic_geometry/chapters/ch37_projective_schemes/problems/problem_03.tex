\begin{problem}[VI.37.P3: Intersection multiplicity preview]\label{prob:vi37-03}
Inside \(\mathbb P^2\), intersect the line \(Y=0\) with the conic \(Y^2-XZ=0\). Determine the scheme-theoretic intersection.
\begin{solution}
Add the ideals:
\[
  (Y)+(Y^2-XZ)=(Y,XZ).
\]
Thus
\[
  X\cap L=\Proj\bigl(k[X,Y,Z]/(Y,XZ)\bigr),
\]
the union of the two coordinate points \([1:0:0]\) and \([0:0:1]\).  This example is reduced; tangential intersections can instead produce nonreduced zero-dimensional schemes.
\end{solution}
\end{problem}

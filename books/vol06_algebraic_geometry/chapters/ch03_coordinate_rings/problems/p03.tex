\subsection*{Problem VI.03.P3 --- Different embeddings, the same coordinate ring}
\label{prob:vi03-p3}
\paragraph{Problem.}
Assume \(k\) is infinite.  Let \(L=\mathbb A_k^1\) and \(P=V(y-x^2)\subseteq\mathbb A_k^2\).
Construct an explicit isomorphism \(k[P]\cong k[L]\).  Identify which coordinate
functions on \(P\) correspond to \(t\) and \(t^2\) on \(L\).
\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.} It separates intrinsic algebra from extrinsic coordinates.
\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.} Use the relation \(\bar y=\bar x^2\).
\paragraph{Guided hints.} Send \(\bar x\mapsto t\), \(\bar y\mapsto t^2\), and build the inverse from \(t\mapsto\bar x\).
\paragraph{Solution.}
The homomorphism
\[
\Phi:k[x,y]/(y-x^2)\to k[t],\qquad \bar x\mapsto t,\quad\bar y\mapsto t^2
\]
is well-defined.  The map \(\Psi:k[t]\to k[x,y]/(y-x^2)\), \(t\mapsto\bar x\), is its
inverse because \(\bar y=\bar x^2\).  Thus the coordinate rings are isomorphic; \(x|_P\)
corresponds to \(t\), while \(y|_P\) corresponds to \(t^2\).
\paragraph{What happened mathematically.} The second generator in the plane presentation is redundant once the relation is imposed.
\paragraph{Extensions.} Generalize to graphs \(V(y-p(x))\).
\paragraph{Provenance.} New synthesis problem based on the chapter's repeated warning that presentations are not intrinsic.
\fi

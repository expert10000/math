% Problem dossier: VI.03.P1
\subsection*{Problem VI.03.P1 --- The monomial curve \((t^3,t^4,t^5)\)}
\label{prob:vi03-p1}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Assume \(k\) is infinite and let
\[
X=\set{(t^3,t^4,t^5):t\in k}\subseteq\mathbb A_k^3.
\]
Prove that
\[
I(X)=(y^2-xz,\ x^3-yz,\ z^2-x^2y).
\]
Show moreover that this ideal cannot be generated by only two polynomials.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the first genuinely nontrivial presentation problem in the affine chapters.  A
one-parameter set in three-space can require three defining relations, and the coordinate
ring naturally appears as a monomial subalgebra of \(k[t]\).

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Map \(k[x,y,z]\) to \(k[t]\) by \(x\mapsto t^3\), \(y\mapsto t^4\), \(z\mapsto t^5\).
For generation, reduce monomials using the three displayed relations.  For minimality, work
at the origin with \(\mathfrak m=(x,y,z)\) and compare quadratic initial terms modulo
\(\mathfrak m I\).

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\textbf{Hint 1.} The three displayed polynomials vanish after the substitution
\((x,y,z)=(t^3,t^4,t^5)\).

\textbf{Hint 2.} Modulo those relations, every monomial can be reduced to a linear
combination of
\[
x^a,\qquad x^a y,\qquad x^a z\qquad(a\ge0).
\]

\textbf{Hint 3.} Their images are \(t^{3a}\), \(t^{3a+4}\), \(t^{3a+5}\), whose exponents
lie in distinct residue classes modulo \(3\).

\textbf{Hint 4.} If two generators sufficed, \(I/\mathfrak m I\) would have dimension at
most two.  Inspect the quadratic parts of the three displayed generators.

\paragraph{Solution.}
Let
\[
\phi:k[x,y,z]\to k[t],\qquad
x\mapsto t^3,\ y\mapsto t^4,\ z\mapsto t^5.
\]
Its image is \(k[t^3,t^4,t^5]\), the coordinate algebra generated by the three coordinate
functions on the parametrized curve.  Because \(k\) is infinite, a polynomial lies in \(I(X)\)
exactly when its substituted polynomial in \(t\) vanishes for infinitely many values of \(t\), hence
is the zero polynomial.  Therefore \(I(X)=\ker\phi\).  Put
\[
f_1=y^2-xz,\qquad f_2=x^3-yz,\qquad f_3=z^2-x^2y,
\]
and \(J=(f_1,f_2,f_3)\).  Direct substitution shows \(J\subseteq\ker\phi\).

In \(A=k[x,y,z]/J\), use
\[
y^2=xz,\qquad yz=x^3,\qquad z^2=x^2y.
\]
Repeatedly applying these relations reduces every monomial to a linear combination of
\[
x^a,\quad x^a y,\quad x^a z.
\]
Under the induced map \(A\to k[t]\), these monomials go to
\[
t^{3a},\qquad t^{3a+4},\qquad t^{3a+5}.
\]
The three families have exponents congruent to \(0,1,2\pmod3\), respectively, and within
each family the exponents are distinct.  Therefore their images are linearly independent.
Hence the induced map \(A\to k[t]\) is injective.  Thus \(J=\ker\phi=I(X)\).

For minimality, let \(\mathfrak m=(x,y,z)\).  Since \(I(X)\subseteq\mathfrak m^2\), one has
\(\mathfrak m I(X)\subseteq\mathfrak m^3\).  The quadratic initial parts of \(f_1,f_2,f_3\)
are
\[
y^2-xz,\qquad -yz,\qquad z^2.
\]
These are linearly independent in \(\mathfrak m^2/\mathfrak m^3\).  Hence the classes of
\(f_1,f_2,f_3\) are linearly independent in \(I(X)/\mathfrak m I(X)\), so
\[
\dim_k I(X)/\mathfrak m I(X)\ge3.
\]
Any generating set of \(I(X)\) maps to a spanning set of this quotient, so at least three
generators are necessary.

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The geometry is controlled by the numerical semigroup generated by \(3,4,5\).  The three
relations are exactly the first collisions among exponent sums, while the minimal-generator
argument detects three independent quadratic directions in the ideal at the origin.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Identify \(k[X]\) explicitly with the subalgebra \(k[t^3,t^4,t^5]\subseteq k[t]\).
\item Compare with the cusp ring \(k[t^2,t^3]\).
\item For \((t^m,t^{m+1},\dots)\), search for relations by matching exponent sums.
\end{enumerate}

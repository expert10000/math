\subsection*{Problem VI.03.P4 --- The affine line and hyperbola are distinguished by units}
\label{prob:vi03-p4}
\paragraph{Problem.}
Assume \(k\) is infinite.  Prove
\[
k[t]^\times=k^\times,
\qquad
k[t,t^{-1}]^\times=\set{ct^m:c\in k^\times,\ m\in\mathbb Z}.
\]
Deduce that \(k[t]\not\cong k[t,t^{-1}]\) as \(k\)-algebras, and hence that the affine
line and hyperbola \(V(xy-1)\) cannot have isomorphic coordinate rings.
\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.} A simple intrinsic ring invariant can distinguish spaces that are both one-dimensional and parametrized by one variable.
\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.} Use degrees in \(k[t]\), and lowest/highest exponents in Laurent polynomials.
\paragraph{Guided hints.} If \(fg=1\) in \(k[t]\), compare degrees.  For Laurent polynomials, factor out the smallest exponent and compare ordinary polynomial units.
\paragraph{Solution.}
If \(fg=1\) in \(k[t]\), then \(\deg f+\deg g=0\), so both are nonzero constants.  Hence
\(k[t]^\times=k^\times\).  For a nonzero Laurent polynomial write \(f=t^r p(t)\) where
\(p\in k[t]\) and \(p(0)\neq0\).  If \(f\) is a unit, its inverse has the form \(t^s q(t)\)
with \(q(0)\neq0\), so \(t^{r+s}p(t)q(t)=1\).  Therefore \(p,q\) are constants and
\(r+s=0\).  Thus the units are exactly \(ct^m\).  The quotient of the unit group by
\(k^\times\) is trivial for \(k[t]\) but isomorphic to \(\mathbb Z\) for the Laurent ring,
so the rings cannot be isomorphic as \(k\)-algebras.
\paragraph{What happened mathematically.} Inverting one coordinate changes the intrinsic function algebra in a detectable way.
\paragraph{Extensions.} Compute units in \(k[t_1^{\pm1},\dots,t_r^{\pm1}]\).
\paragraph{Provenance.} New synthesis problem; it upgrades the routine hyperbola computation into an intrinsic comparison.
\fi

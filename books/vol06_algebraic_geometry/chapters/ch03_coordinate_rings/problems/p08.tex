% Problem dossier: VI.03.P8
\subsection*{Problem VI.03.P8 --- The cusp as \(k[t^2,t^3]\)}
\label{prob:vi03-p8}
\paragraph{Problem.}
Assume \(k\) is infinite and let \(C=V(y^2-x^3)\subseteq\mathbb A_k^2\).  Prove that
\[
k[C]\cong k[t^2,t^3]\subseteq k[t]
\]
under \(x\mapsto t^2\), \(y\mapsto t^3\).  Show that every element of this subalgebra is a
linear combination of \(1\) and monomials \(t^m\) with \(m\ge2\).
\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.} Monomial subalgebras provide a concrete way to see singular curves through their coordinate rings without yet invoking local algebra.
\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.} Reduce powers of \(y\) modulo \(y^2-x^3\).
\paragraph{Guided hints.} Every class can be written \(A(x)+yB(x)\); substitute \(x=t^2\), \(y=t^3\).
\paragraph{Solution.}
The parametrization \(t\mapsto(t^2,t^3)\) is onto \(C\): for a nonzero point use
\(t=y/x\), and the origin comes from \(t=0\).  Since \(k\) is infinite, the vanishing ideal
is the kernel of substitution \(x\mapsto t^2,y\mapsto t^3\).  Every polynomial is congruent modulo \((y^2-x^3)\) to \(A(x)+yB(x)\).  Under the
substitution this becomes \(A(t^2)+t^3B(t^2)\), so the image is exactly
\(k[t^2,t^3]\).  If such an expression maps to zero, the even exponents from \(A(t^2)\)
and odd exponents at least \(3\) from \(t^3B(t^2)\) cannot cancel, hence both \(A\) and
\(B\) are zero.  Thus the map is injective.  The semigroup generated by \(2\) and \(3\) is
\(\{0,2,3,4,5,\dots\}\), proving the final description.
\paragraph{What happened mathematically.} The missing exponent \(1\) records that the parameter \(t\) is not itself a polynomial function generated by the embedded coordinates.
\paragraph{Extensions.} Compare with the semigroup \(\langle3,4,5\rangle\) in Problem VI.03.P1.
\paragraph{Provenance.} New synthesis problem complementing the recovered monomial-curve problem.
\fi

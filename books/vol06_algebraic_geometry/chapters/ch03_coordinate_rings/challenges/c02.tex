\subsection*{Challenge VI.03.C2 --- Polynomial functions on \(\mathbb F_q^n\)}
\label{chall:vi03-c2}
\paragraph{Challenge.}
Prove
\[
I(\mathbb F_q^n)=(x_1^q-x_1,\dots,x_n^q-x_n)
\]
and deduce that every function \(\mathbb F_q^n\to\mathbb F_q\) is represented uniquely by a
polynomial whose degree in each variable is \(<q\).
\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Orientation.} Reduce one variable at a time; then use induction on \(n\) to prove a reduced polynomial vanishing everywhere is zero.
\paragraph{Guided hints.} Regard the reduced polynomial as a polynomial in \(x_n\) with coefficients in the first \(n-1\) variables and evaluate at all \(q\) values of \(x_n\).
\paragraph{Solution.}
Division by the monic polynomials \(x_i^q-x_i\) yields a unique remainder \(R\) with degree
less than \(q\) in each variable.  If \(R\) vanishes on all of \(\mathbb F_q^n\), write
\(R=\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}R_j(x_1,\dots,x_{n-1})x_n^j\).  For each fixed point in the first
\(n-1\) coordinates, this is a one-variable polynomial of degree below \(q\) with \(q\)
roots, hence all its coefficients vanish at that point.  By induction, all \(R_j\) are zero.
Thus the displayed ideal is the full vanishing ideal.  The reduced monomials number \(q^n\),
matching the dimension of the function algebra \(\mathbb F_q^{\mathbb F_q^n}\); evaluation
therefore identifies the quotient with all functions, and the reduced representative is
unique.
\paragraph{Extension.} Use CRT to express the ring as a product of \(q^n\) copies of \(\mathbb F_q\).
\paragraph{Provenance.} New challenge extending Problem VI.03.P7.
\fi

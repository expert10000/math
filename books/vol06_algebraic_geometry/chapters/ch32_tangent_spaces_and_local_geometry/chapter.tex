\chapter{Local Dimension, Regularity, and Singularities}\label{ch:vi32-local-geometry}

VI/29--VI/31 measured prime chains globally and relatively.  VI/32 now fixes a point $x\in X$ and asks what the scheme
looks like infinitesimally near that point.  The local ring $\OO_{X,x}$ supplies two basic numerical invariants:
\[
 \dim\OO_{X,x}
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 \dim_{\kappa(x)}\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2.
\]
The first counts prime chains inside the local ring; the second counts independent first-order directions.  Their
comparison detects regularity.  For a Noetherian local ring, the second number is always at least the first, and equality
is exactly the definition of a regular local ring.  This turns singularity detection into a concrete comparison between
Krull dimension and linear algebra.

\section*{Learning goals}
By the end of the chapter, the reader should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
\item compute the local-ring dimension $\dim\OO_{X,x}$ from an affine chart and identify it with prime height;
\item prove the closed-point local-dimension theorem for varieties and recognize its failure for general schemes;
\item compute the cotangent space $\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2$ and the embedding dimension;
\item define the Zariski tangent space as the dual of the cotangent space and compute it from linearized equations;
\item define regular local rings, regular points, and singular points;
\item apply the Jacobian matrix to curves and hypersurfaces at rational points;
\item distinguish a smooth-looking tangent direction from genuine regularity, especially for cusps, nodes, crossings,
  and nilpotent thickenings.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Conceptual roadmap}
At one point $x$, the chapter follows the chain
\[
 x\in X
 \longrightarrow
 (\OO_{X,x},\mathfrak m_x,\kappa(x))
 \longrightarrow
 \mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2
 \longrightarrow
 T_xX
 \longrightarrow
 \text{regular or singular}.
\]
The local ring retains all algebra visible in arbitrarily small neighborhoods.  Passing to $\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2$
throws away quadratic and higher-order terms and keeps only first-order behavior.  Regularity says that this first-order
model has exactly as many independent directions as the local Krull dimension predicts.

\section{Local-ring dimension at a point}\label{sec:vi32-local-dimension}
\begin{definition}[Local-ring dimension]\label{def:vi32-local-dim}
For a scheme $X$ and a point $x\in X$, define
\[
 \dim_x^{\mathrm{loc}}X:=\dim\OO_{X,x}.
\]
In this volume, ``local dimension at $x$'' means this local-ring dimension unless another convention is stated explicitly.
\end{definition}
If $x$ corresponds to $\mathfrak p\in\Spec A$ in an affine chart, then
\[
 \OO_{X,x}\cong A_{\mathfrak p},
 \qquad
 \dim\OO_{X,x}=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p).
\]
Thus local dimension counts chains of generalizations ending at the chosen point.  At a generic point of an irreducible
component it is $0$; at a closed point of an $n$-dimensional variety it is $n$.

\section{Closed points of varieties}\label{sec:vi32-closed-variety}
Let $X$ be a variety over $k$ and let $x\in X$ be closed. Choose an affine neighborhood $U=\Spec A$ of $x$, with maximal
ideal $\mathfrak m$.  Since $A$ is a finitely generated $k$-domain, the dimension formula gives
\[
 \operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak m)+\operatorname{trdeg}_k\kappa(x)=\dim A.
\]
Zariski's lemma makes $\kappa(x)/k$ algebraic, so the transcendence degree term is $0$. Hence
\begin{theorem}[Closed-point local dimension for varieties]\label{thm:vi32-closed-variety}
If $X$ is a variety over $k$ and $x\in X$ is closed, then
\[
 \boxed{\dim\OO_{X,x}=\dim X.}
\]
\end{theorem}
This is the remaining local-dimension statement from the AG8 Problem 2 stream.
\input{chapters/ch32_tangent_spaces_and_local_geometry/figures/figure_02}

\section{Why the closed-point formula can fail}\label{sec:vi32-counterexample}
The preceding theorem is not a formal fact about arbitrary schemes. Let
\[
 X=\Spec k\sqcup\mathbb A^1_k
\]
and let $x$ be the unique point of the $\Spec k$ component. Then $x$ is closed and
\[
 \OO_{X,x}\cong k,
 \qquad
 \dim\OO_{X,x}=0,
 \qquad
 \dim X=1.
\]
Thus the equality at closed points fails once components of different dimensions are allowed.  This realizes the
remaining part-(a) counterexample requirement of AG8 Problem 3 and completes the Problem 3 routing begun in VI/30 and
VI/31.
\input{chapters/ch32_tangent_spaces_and_local_geometry/figures/figure_03}

\section{The maximal ideal and the cotangent space}\label{sec:vi32-cotangent}
The local ring $(\OO_{X,x},\mathfrak m_x)$ distinguishes functions vanishing at $x$ from units.  Modulo
$\mathfrak m_x^2$, products of two vanishing functions disappear, leaving their linear parts.
\begin{definition}[Cotangent space and embedding dimension]\label{def:vi32-embed-dim}
The cotangent space at $x$ is the $\kappa(x)$-vector space
\[
 \mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2.
\]
Its dimension
\[
 \operatorname{edim}_xX:=\dim_{\kappa(x)}\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2
\]
is the embedding dimension at $x$.
\end{definition}
For a Noetherian local ring, Nakayama's lemma identifies this number with the minimal number of generators of the maximal
ideal.
\input{chapters/ch32_tangent_spaces_and_local_geometry/figures/figure_04}

\section{Zariski tangent space}\label{sec:vi32-tangent}
\begin{definition}[Zariski tangent space]\label{def:vi32-zariski-tangent}
The Zariski tangent space at $x$ is
\[
 T_xX:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\kappa(x)}(\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2,\kappa(x)).
\]
Hence $\dim_{\kappa(x)}T_xX=\operatorname{edim}_xX$.
\end{definition}
At a $k$-rational point of a $k$-scheme, these tangent vectors can be viewed as first-order maps from the dual numbers
$\Spec k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ whose closed point maps to $x$.  The dual-number interpretation explains why the
tangent space sees first-order infinitesimal directions rather than only ordinary nearby points.
\input{chapters/ch32_tangent_spaces_and_local_geometry/figures/figure_05}

\section{Regular local rings and regular points}\label{sec:vi32-regular}
A basic theorem of Noetherian local algebra gives
\[
 \dim R\leq\dim_\kappa\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2
\]
for every Noetherian local ring $(R,\mathfrak m,\kappa)$.
\begin{definition}[Regular local ring]\label{def:vi32-regular-local}
A Noetherian local ring $(R,\mathfrak m,\kappa)$ is regular if
\[
 \dim R=\dim_\kappa\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2.
\]
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[Regular and singular points]\label{def:vi32-regular-point}
A locally Noetherian scheme $X$ is regular at $x$ if $\OO_{X,x}$ is a regular local ring. Otherwise $x$ is singular.
The scheme is regular if it is regular at every point.
\end{definition}
Thus the numerical test is
\[
 \boxed{x\text{ regular}\iff \operatorname{edim}_xX=\dim\OO_{X,x}.}
\]
\input{chapters/ch32_tangent_spaces_and_local_geometry/figures/figure_06}

\section{Affine equations and the Jacobian matrix}\label{sec:vi32-jacobian}
Let
\[
 X=V(f_1,\ldots,f_r)\subseteq\mathbb A^n_k
\]
and let $a\in X(k)$.  Write $J(a)$ for the $r\times n$ matrix
\[
 J(a)=\left(\frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_j}(a)\right).
\]
Linearizing the equations at $a$ gives
\begin{proposition}[Tangent space from the Jacobian]\label{prop:vi32-jacobian-tangent}
For a $k$-rational point $a$,
\[
 T_aX\cong\ker\bigl(J(a):k^n\to k^r\bigr),
 \qquad
 \dim_k T_aX=n-\operatorname{rank}J(a).
\]
\end{proposition}
This formula computes the tangent space directly.  Regularity still requires comparison with the local Krull dimension;
Jacobian rank alone is not a substitute for knowing the dimension of the local scheme.
\input{chapters/ch32_tangent_spaces_and_local_geometry/figures/figure_07}

\section{Hypersurfaces}\label{sec:vi32-hypersurface}
For a nonzero nonconstant polynomial $f\in k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, every component of the hypersurface $V(f)$ has dimension
$n-1$. At a $k$-rational point $a\in V(f)$,
\[
 T_aV(f)=\left\{v\in k^n:\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_j}(a)v_j=0\right\}.
\]
Consequently, in this hypersurface setting,
\begin{corollary}[Hypersurface regularity test]\label{cor:vi32-hypersurface}
The point $a$ is regular if at least one partial derivative of $f$ is nonzero at $a$.  If all partial derivatives vanish,
then the tangent space has dimension $n>n-1$ and $a$ is singular.
\end{corollary}
\input{chapters/ch32_tangent_spaces_and_local_geometry/figures/figure_08}

\section{Cusp, node, and crossing}\label{sec:vi32-cusp-node}
Assume the relevant characteristics are excluded when factorization or derivatives require it.
For the cusp
\[
 C: y^2=x^3,
\]
the origin has local dimension $1$, while both first derivatives vanish there, so $\dim T_0C=2$.  The lowest-degree
part is $y^2$, giving one tangent direction with multiplicity two.

For the node
\[
 N:y^2=x^2(x+1),
\]
the origin also has local dimension $1$ and two-dimensional Zariski tangent space, but the lowest-degree part
$y^2-x^2=(y-x)(y+x)$ exhibits two distinct tangent directions.  Both points are singular, but their first nonzero
homogeneous terms distinguish the geometry beyond the mere dimension of the tangent space.

For the crossing $xy=0$, the origin is again singular with local dimension $1$ and tangent dimension $2$, now because
two irreducible components meet.
\input{chapters/ch32_tangent_spaces_and_local_geometry/figures/figure_09}

\section{A recurring multiple-lens example: the cusp}\label{sec:vi32-cusp-lenses}
The cusp now supports several viewpoints developed across the volume:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{lens} & \text{cusp data}\\ \hline
\text{equation} & y^2-x^3=0\\
\text{coordinate ring} & k[x,y]/(y^2-x^3)\cong k[t^2,t^3]\\
\text{function field} & k(t)\\
\text{normalization} & \Spec k[t]\to\Spec k[t^2,t^3]\\
\text{dimension} & 1\\
\text{local ring at cusp} & k[t^2,t^3]_{(t^2,t^3)}\\
\text{embedding dimension} & 2\\
\text{regularity} & \text{singular, since }2>1
\end{array}
\]
This is the first full ``same geometry, multiple lenses'' panel in the dimension arc.

\section{Common pitfalls}\label{sec:vi32-pitfalls}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Local-ring dimension is not the dimension of the closure of the point.}  For a prime $\mathfrak p$ in an
affine scheme, $\dim\OO_{X,x}=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)$, whereas $\dim\overline{\{x\}}=\dim A/\mathfrak p$ in the
finite-type variety setting.
\item \textbf{Terminology varies.}  Some references use ``dimension at $x$'' for an infimum of neighborhood dimensions.
This chapter explicitly uses the local-ring convention $\dim\OO_{X,x}$.
\item \textbf{Tangent dimension is not local dimension.}  Equality characterizes regularity; a larger tangent space is a
signal of singularity.
\item \textbf{A tangent line is not enough to classify a singularity.}  The cusp and node both have two-dimensional
Zariski tangent spaces as plane curves, but their tangent cones differ.
\item \textbf{Regular, normal, reduced, and smooth are different adjectives.}  This chapter treats regularity.  Their
systematic comparison belongs to later property and morphism chapters.
\end{itemize}

\section{Boundaries of VI/32}\label{sec:vi32-boundaries}
VI/32 closes the dimension/local-geometry arc by introducing local-ring dimension, cotangent and tangent spaces,
embedding dimension, regularity, singularity, and elementary Jacobian calculations.  It does not yet develop Kähler
differentials, smooth morphisms, étale morphisms, completions, tangent cones in full generality, Serre's conditions, or
divisor theory.  Those require later structural chapters.

\section{Chapter synthesis}\label{sec:vi32-synthesis}
Local geometry compresses the neighborhood of a point into the local ring and then linearizes its maximal ideal.  The
Krull dimension $\dim\OO_{X,x}$ measures specialization depth, while $\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2$ measures independent
first-order directions.  A Noetherian point is regular exactly when these numbers agree.  The Jacobian matrix makes the
comparison computable for affine equations, while the cusp, node, crossing, and dual numbers show how singularity can
arise from ramification, branching, reducibility, or nilpotent structure.  With VI/32, Part VI has moved from global
prime chains to a pointwise diagnostic of geometric regularity.

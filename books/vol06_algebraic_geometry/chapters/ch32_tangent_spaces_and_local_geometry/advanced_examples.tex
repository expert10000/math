\begin{example}[Advanced 1: Embedding dimension via Nakayama]\label{ex:vi32-adv-01}
For a Noetherian local ring $(R,\mathfrak m,\kappa)$, a set generates $\mathfrak m$ minimally exactly when its classes form a basis of $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$. Thus embedding dimension is the minimal number of local parameters needed at first order.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced 2: Regular parameters]\label{ex:vi32-adv-02}
In a $d$-dimensional regular local ring, any minimal generating set of $\mathfrak m$ has exactly $d$ elements. Such a set is called a regular system of parameters.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced 3: Tangent space under redundant presentations]\label{ex:vi32-adv-03}
Adding a redundant defining equation does not change $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$ or the intrinsic tangent space, although it adds a row to a chosen Jacobian matrix.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced 4: Tangent cone of the cusp]\label{ex:vi32-adv-04}
For $y^2-x^3$, the first nonzero homogeneous part is $y^2$. The tangent cone is the doubled line $y=0$, refining the information that the Zariski tangent space itself is two-dimensional.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced 5: Tangent cone of the node]\label{ex:vi32-adv-05}
For $y^2-x^2-x^3$, the lowest homogeneous part is $y^2-x^2$. In characteristic not $2$ it factors as $(y-x)(y+x)$ and displays two branches.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced 6: Regular implies reduced locally]\label{ex:vi32-adv-06}
A regular local ring is a domain, hence reduced. Therefore a nonreduced local scheme cannot be regular at a point where nilpotents survive.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced 7: A normal singularity warning]\label{ex:vi32-adv-07}
Normality and regularity are not equivalent in dimension at least two. Later chapters may use the quadric cone as a standard normal-but-singular example after the needed normality criterion has been established.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced 8: Regular curves and normality]\label{ex:vi32-adv-08}
For Noetherian integral curves, a regular local ring at a closed point is a DVR. Conversely, a one-dimensional Noetherian normal local domain is a DVR, so normal integral curves are regular.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced 9: Base field and tangent spaces]\label{ex:vi32-adv-09}
At a non-rational point, the intrinsic cotangent space is naturally a vector space over $\kappa(x)$, not necessarily over the ground field $k$. Relative tangent spaces over $k$ require the corresponding differential formalism.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced 10: Closed-point equality uses finite type]\label{ex:vi32-adv-10}
The proof $\dim\OO_{X,x}=\dim X$ at closed points uses that maximal ideals of finitely generated $k$-domains have residue fields algebraic over $k$ and full height. It is not a theorem for arbitrary integral Noetherian schemes.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced 11: Singular locus versus one singular point]\label{ex:vi32-adv-11}
Computing one Jacobian rank identifies the behavior at one point only. Determining the whole singular locus means solving the defining equations together with the appropriate rank-drop conditions.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced 12: Products of regular affine spaces]\label{ex:vi32-adv-12}
At a rational point of $\mathbb A^m\times\mathbb A^n$, cotangent spaces split as a direct sum, so embedding dimensions add. This is the infinitesimal counterpart of product dimension.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced 13: Completion preview]\label{ex:vi32-adv-13}
Passing from a Noetherian local ring to its adic completion preserves dimension and regularity. This powerful local replacement is stated only as a preview here; completions are not developed in VI/32.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced 14: Smoothness boundary]\label{ex:vi32-adv-14}
For finite-type schemes over a perfect field, regularity and geometric smoothness agree at rational points under the standard hypotheses. Over imperfect fields, regular need not imply smooth over the base. Smooth morphisms are therefore deferred.
\end{example}

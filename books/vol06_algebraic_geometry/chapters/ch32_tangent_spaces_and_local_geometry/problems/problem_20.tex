\begin{problem}[VI.32.P20: Legacy DG4 Problem 13 --- unions of three lines]\label{prob:vi32-20}
Assume \(k\) is algebraically closed and \(\operatorname{char}k\neq2\). Compare
\[
X=V(xy,xz,yz)\subset\mathbb A^3_k
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
Y=V(uv(u-v))\subset\mathbb A^2_k.
\]
Both are unions of three affine lines meeting at one point. Prove that \(X\) and \(Y\) are not isomorphic by comparing their Zariski tangent spaces at the common intersection point.

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical recovery of legacy DG4 Problem 13, whose tangent-space invariant was reserved for VI/32. The two line-union models are the running examples already established in VI/05.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
First identify the geometry. For
\[
X=V(xy,xz,yz),
\]
a point has at most one nonzero coordinate, so \(X\) is the union of the three coordinate axes in \(\mathbb A^3\).
For
\[
Y=V(uv(u-v)),
\]
the three irreducible components are the lines
\[
u=0,\qquad v=0,\qquad u=v
\]
in \(\mathbb A^2\). In both schemes, the origin is the unique point lying on all three irreducible components.

Now compute tangent spaces. The defining equations of \(X\) are
\[
xy,\quad xz,\quad yz.
\]
All have degree \(2\), hence every linear term vanishes at the origin. Equivalently, the Jacobian matrix at \(0\) is
the zero \(3\times3\) matrix. Therefore
\[
T_0X=k^3,
\qquad
\dim_kT_0X=3.
\]

For \(Y\), the defining polynomial
\[
f(u,v)=uv(u-v)
\]
has degree \(3\), so its gradient also vanishes at the origin. Thus
\[
T_0Y=k^2,
\qquad
\dim_kT_0Y=2.
\]

Suppose there were an isomorphism
\[
\varphi:X\to Y.
\]
An isomorphism permutes irreducible components and preserves their incidence. Since the origin is the unique point
where all three components meet, necessarily
\[
\varphi(0)=0.
\]
The induced isomorphism of local rings
\[
\mathcal O_{Y,0}\cong\mathcal O_{X,0}
\]
identifies maximal ideals and their squares, hence induces an isomorphism of cotangent spaces and therefore of
Zariski tangent spaces. But their dimensions are \(2\) and \(3\), contradiction.

Thus
\[
\boxed{X\not\cong Y}.
\]
This example shows why tangent dimension is a genuine intrinsic invariant even when component counts and incidence
pictures agree.
\end{solution}

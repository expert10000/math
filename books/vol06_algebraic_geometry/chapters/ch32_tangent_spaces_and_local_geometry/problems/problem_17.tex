\begin{problem}[VI.32.P17: Regularity is intrinsic]\label{prob:vi32-17}
Explain why regularity does not depend on a chosen affine embedding of \(X\).

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical VI/32 synthesis.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Regularity at \(x\) is defined using only the local ring
\[
R=\mathcal O_{X,x}
\]
and its maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m_x\):
\[
x\text{ regular}
\iff
\dim R
=
\dim_{\kappa(x)}\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2.
\]
Both \(R\) and \(\mathfrak m_x\) are intrinsic to the locally ringed space \(X\). An isomorphism of neighborhoods of
\(x\), or any different affine presentation of the same scheme, produces an isomorphic local ring and therefore
the same Krull dimension and cotangent-space dimension.

An affine embedding
\[
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb A^n
\]
may produce a particular list of equations and a particular Jacobian matrix. A second embedding may have a
different ambient dimension and a different matrix. Nevertheless both Jacobian computations recover the same
intrinsic vector space
\[
T_xX
=
\operatorname{Hom}_{\kappa(x)}
(\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2,\kappa(x)).
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{\text{regularity is intrinsic and independent of the chosen embedding}.}
\]
\end{solution}
\fi

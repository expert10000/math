\begin{problem}[VI.32.P7: Cusp singularity]\label{prob:vi32-07}
Assume $\operatorname{char}k\ne2,3$. Prove the origin of $y^2=x^3$ is singular.

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical VI/32 synthesis.
\begin{solution}
The curve is a hypersurface, so its local dimension is $1$. The gradient $(-3x^2,2y)$ vanishes at the origin, so the tangent space is all of $k^2$, of dimension $2$. Since $2>1$, the local ring is not regular.
\end{solution}
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[VI.32.P13: Hypersurface criterion]\label{prob:vi32-13}
Let \(f\ne0\) be nonconstant and \(a\in V(f)(k)\). Prove that a nonzero gradient at \(a\) implies regularity.

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical VI/32 synthesis.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Let
\[
X=V(f)\subseteq\mathbb A^n_k.
\]
A nonzero nonconstant hypersurface has every irreducible component of codimension \(1\), so at a point on such a
component the local dimension is \(n-1\).

At the rational point \(a\), the Jacobian is the single row
\[
\nabla f(a).
\]
If
\[
\nabla f(a)\ne0,
\]
then its rank is \(1\). Therefore
\[
\dim_kT_aX=n-1.
\]
The local dimension is also \(n-1\), so
\[
\dim\mathcal O_{X,a}
=
\dim_kT_aX.
\]
By the regular-local numerical criterion, \(a\) is regular:
\[
\boxed{\nabla f(a)\ne0\Longrightarrow a\text{ regular on }V(f)}.
\]

Conversely, if every partial derivative vanishes, the tangent space has dimension \(n\), strictly larger than the
hypersurface local dimension \(n-1\), so the point is singular. Thus for this hypersurface setting the gradient test
is exact.
\end{solution}
\fi

\begin{problem}[VI.32.P18: Regularity and reduction]\label{prob:vi32-18}
Give an example showing that reduction can change regularity even though it does not change Krull dimension.

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical VI/32 synthesis.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Take
\[
X=\Spec k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\]
The unique local ring has Krull dimension \(0\), because the ring is Artinian. Its maximal ideal is
\[
\mathfrak m=(\varepsilon),
\qquad
\mathfrak m^2=0,
\]
so
\[
\dim_k\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2=1.
\]
Thus \(X\) is singular.

The reduction is
\[
X_{\mathrm{red}}
=
\Spec k.
\]
Its local ring is the field \(k\), whose Krull dimension and embedding dimension are both \(0\). Therefore
\(X_{\mathrm{red}}\) is regular.

Yet reduction is a homeomorphism on underlying spaces and preserves all prime chains, so
\[
\dim X_{\mathrm{red}}=\dim X=0.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{\text{reduction preserves Krull dimension but need not preserve regularity}.}
\]
The missing information is the first-order nilpotent structure.  It is recorded by
\[
\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2.
\]
\end{solution}
\fi

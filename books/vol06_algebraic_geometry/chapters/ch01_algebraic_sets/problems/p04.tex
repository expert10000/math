\subsection*{Problem VI.01.P4 --- What fails over a non-algebraically-closed field?}
\label{prob:vi01-p4}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Work in \(\mathbb R[x]\) with
\[
I=(x^2+1).
\]
Show that \(I\) is radical but
\[
I(V_{\mathbb R}(I))=\mathbb R[x].
\]
Then repeat the calculation over \(\mathbb C\) and explain why the outcome changes.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
The inclusion \(\sqrt I\subseteq I(V(I))\) is elementary, but equality requires hypotheses.
This problem isolates exactly why algebraic closedness matters in the Nullstellensatz.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
An ideal generated by an irreducible polynomial in \(\mathbb R[x]\) is maximal.  Also,
by definition every polynomial vanishes on the empty set.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\textbf{Hint 1.} Determine \(V_{\mathbb R}(x^2+1)\).

\textbf{Hint 2.} What is \(I(\varnothing)\)?

\textbf{Hint 3.} Over \(\mathbb C\), factor \(x^2+1\) and compute the ideal of the two
points.

\paragraph{Solution.}
The polynomial \(x^2+1\) has no real root, so
\[
V_{\mathbb R}(I)=\varnothing.
\]
The polynomial is irreducible over \(\mathbb R\), hence \(I\) is maximal and therefore
prime and radical.  But every polynomial vanishes vacuously on the empty set, so
\[
I(V_{\mathbb R}(I))=I(\varnothing)=\mathbb R[x].
\]
Thus
\[
\sqrt I=I\subsetneq I(V_{\mathbb R}(I)).
\]

Over \(\mathbb C\),
\[
x^2+1=(x-i)(x+i),
\]
so
\[
V_{\mathbb C}(I)=\{i,-i\}.
\]
A polynomial vanishes at both points exactly when it is divisible by both \(x-i\) and
\(x+i\); since these factors are coprime,
\[
I(\{i,-i\})=((x-i)(x+i))=(x^2+1)=I.
\]
Hence the desired equality holds after passing to the algebraically closed field.

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Real affine space does not contain enough points to detect the ideal \((x^2+1)\).  Complex
affine space supplies the missing roots and restores the expected algebra--geometry match.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Repeat the example with \(x^2+y^2+1\) over \(\mathbb R\).
\item Find a nonempty real zero set for which \(I(V(I))\) still strictly contains \(\sqrt I\).
\item Compare real algebraic geometry's real Nullstellensatz with the classical theorem.
\end{enumerate}

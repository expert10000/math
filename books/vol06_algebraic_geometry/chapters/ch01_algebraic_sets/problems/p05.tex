% Problem dossier: VI.01.P5
\subsection*{Problem VI.01.P5 --- Finite point sets in affine space}
\label{prob:vi01-p5}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Let
\[
F=\{p^{(1)},\dots,p^{(r)}\}\subseteq\mathbb A_k^n
\]
be a finite set of distinct points.  For
\(p=(a_1,\dots,a_n)\), put
\[
\mathfrak m_p=(x_1-a_1,\dots,x_n-a_n).
\]
Prove
\[
\{p\}=V(\mathfrak m_p),
\qquad
F=V\!\left(\prod_{p\in F}\mathfrak m_p\right),
\]
and
\[
I(F)=\bigcap_{p\in F}\mathfrak m_p.
\]

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This gives a complete algebraic description of finite point clouds and is the finite-set
prototype for decomposing geometry into pieces.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the finite-union identity for \(V\), and use the definition of \(I(F)\) point by point.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\textbf{Hint 1.} The equations \(x_i-a_i=0\) force all coordinates of a point.

\textbf{Hint 2.} Apply \(V(IJ)=V(I)\cup V(J)\) repeatedly.

\textbf{Hint 3.} A polynomial vanishes on all of \(F\) exactly when it belongs to every
single-point vanishing ideal.

\paragraph{Solution.}
The common zero locus of
\[
x_1-a_1,\dots,x_n-a_n
\]
is exactly the point \(p\), hence \(V(\mathfrak m_p)=\{p\}\).  Repeated use of the union
formula gives
\[
V\!\left(\prod_{p\in F}\mathfrak m_p\right)
=\bigcup_{p\in F}V(\mathfrak m_p)
=F.
\]
Finally,
\[
f\in I(F)
\]
means precisely that \(f(p)=0\) for every \(p\in F\), which is equivalent to
\[
f\in\mathfrak m_p\quad\text{for every }p\in F.
\]
Therefore
\[
I(F)=\bigcap_{p\in F}\mathfrak m_p.
\]

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A finite union on the geometric side becomes an intersection of vanishing ideals and can
also be represented by a product ideal before taking the zero locus.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Prove that the ideals \(\mathfrak m_p\) and \(\mathfrak m_q\) are comaximal for
      distinct points \(p\neq q\).
\item Deduce a Chinese-remainder decomposition for \(k[x_1,\dots,x_n]/I(F)\).
\item Construct explicit polynomial indicator functions for the points; Challenge VI.01.C2
      develops this fully.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical problem; finite point sets occur repeatedly in the legacy notes, but this
formulation is designed specifically for the VI/01 algebraic-set toolkit.

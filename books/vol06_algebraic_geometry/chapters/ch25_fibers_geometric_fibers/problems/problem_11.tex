\subsection*{VI.25.P11: A real conic after geometric extension}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi25-p11}
Study
\[
X=\Spec\mathbb R[x,y]/(x^2+y^2)
\]
before and after scalar extension to \(\mathbb C\).
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This example shows that the component structure of a positive-dimensional scheme can be hidden over the ground field even when the underlying real point set is misleadingly small.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
First use irreducibility of \(x^2+y^2\) over \(\mathbb R\).  Then factor it over \(\mathbb C\).

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Over \(\mathbb R\), \(x^2+y^2\) has no nonconstant linear factor.
\item Over \(\mathbb C\), \(x^2+y^2=(x+iy)(x-iy)\).
\item The two complex factors are distinct, so the complexification is reduced but reducible.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Over \(\mathbb R\), the polynomial \(x^2+y^2\) is irreducible in the UFD \(\mathbb R[x,y]\).  Hence it is prime, so
\[
\mathbb R[x,y]/(x^2+y^2)
\]
is an integral domain.  Therefore \(X\) is reduced and irreducible.  Notice that its set of real rational points consists only of the origin, but the scheme itself has dimension \(1\); rational points alone do not describe its geometry.

After scalar extension,
\[
X_{\mathbb C}
=
\Spec\mathbb C[x,y]/(x^2+y^2).
\]
Now
\[
x^2+y^2=(x+iy)(x-iy),
\]
with two distinct linear factors.  Thus
\[
X_{\mathbb C}
=
V(x+iy)\cup V(x-iy),
\]
the union of two complex affine lines meeting at the origin.  Since the product is squarefree, the complexification remains reduced, but it is reducible.  Hence \(X\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb R\) but not geometrically irreducible.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Geometric fibers reveal components that may not be defined over the original residue field.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
This phenomenon generalizes from conics to varieties whose irreducible components are permuted by the Galois group of the ground field.

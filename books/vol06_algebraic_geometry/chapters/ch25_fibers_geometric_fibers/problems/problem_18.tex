\subsection*{VI.25.P18: Fiber of a product family}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi25-p18}
For \(X\to S\) and \(Y\to S\), prove
\[
(X\times_SY)_s\cong X_s\times_{\kappa(s)}Y_s.
\]
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This identity lets fiberwise questions about products be reduced to products of the individual fibers.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
View taking the fiber as base change along \(\Spec\kappa(s)\to S\), then use compatibility of products with base change.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Expand the left side as \((X\times_SY)\times_S\Spec\kappa(s)\).
\item Reassociate the pullbacks.
\item Recognize the two factors \(X_s\) and \(Y_s\).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
By definition,
\[
(X\times_SY)_s
=
(X\times_SY)\times_S\Spec\kappa(s).
\]
Products over \(S\) commute with base change, so there is a canonical isomorphism
\[
(X\times_SY)\times_S\Spec\kappa(s)
\cong
\bigl(X\times_S\Spec\kappa(s)\bigr)
\times_{\Spec\kappa(s)}
\bigl(Y\times_S\Spec\kappa(s)\bigr).
\]
The two factors on the right are precisely \(X_s\) and \(Y_s\).  Therefore
\[
\boxed{
(X\times_SY)_s
\cong
X_s\times_{\Spec\kappa(s)}Y_s
}.
\]
One can also see the result directly from the universal property: a test scheme over \(\kappa(s)\) maps to either side exactly when it maps compatibly to both \(X\) and \(Y\) with base point fixed at \(s\).
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Taking a fiber is a base-change functor, so it preserves fiber products.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
The same argument works for any finite diagram built from pullbacks.

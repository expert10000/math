\subsection*{VI.25.P14: Fiber of an open immersion}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi25-p14}
For
\[
D(f)\hookrightarrow\Spec A,
\]
compute the fiber over \(\mathfrak p\).
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the open-subscheme analogue of P13 and turns membership in a principal open into a fiber calculation.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Compute \(A_f\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p)\) and ask whether the image of \(f\) is already invertible or becomes zero.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item If \(f\notin\mathfrak p\), its image in \(\kappa(\mathfrak p)\) is nonzero.
\item If \(f\in\mathfrak p\), localization tries to invert zero.
\item Translate the two ring outcomes into spectra.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
The fiber ring is
\[
A_f\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p).
\]
If \(f\notin\mathfrak p\), then the image of \(f\) in \(\kappa(\mathfrak p)\) is nonzero and hence invertible.  The universal property of localization therefore gives
\[
A_f\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p)\cong\kappa(\mathfrak p).
\]
If \(f\in\mathfrak p\), the image of \(f\) in the residue field is \(0\), while the localization \(A_f\) requires \(f\) to be invertible.  Thus the tensor product forces \(0\) to be invertible, so it is the zero ring.  Hence
\[
(D(f))_{\mathfrak p}
\cong
\begin{cases}
\Spec\kappa(\mathfrak p), & \mathfrak p\in D(f),\\[3pt]
\varnothing, & \mathfrak p\notin D(f).
\end{cases}
\]
The scheme-theoretic fiber therefore reproduces exactly the ordinary membership condition for the principal open.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Open-subscheme membership is detected by whether the fiber is a residue-field point or empty.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Covering a general open immersion by principal opens yields the same statement for arbitrary open subschemes.

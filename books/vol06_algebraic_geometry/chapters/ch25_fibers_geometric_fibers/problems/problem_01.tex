\subsection*{VI.25.P1: Legacy DG4-P12: intersecting fibers degenerating to parallel lines}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi25-p01}
Let \(k\) be a field and let \(\pi:\mathbb A^3_k\to\mathbb A^1_k\) be projection to \(z\).  Preserve the legacy construction by taking
\[
X=V\bigl(y(y-zx-1)\bigr)\subset\mathbb A^3_k.
\]
Show that for \(c\ne0\) the fiber \(X_c\) is the union of two intersecting lines, while \(X_0\) is the union of two parallel lines.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the chapter's main degeneration example: every fiber stays reduced and has two line components, yet the incidence relation between those components changes at the special parameter.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Compute the fiber by tensoring with \(k[z]/(z-c)\), factor the specialized equation, and then study the scheme-theoretic intersection of the two line components.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Write the fiber ring as \(k[x,y]/(y(y-cx-1))\).
\item For \(c\ne0\), solve \(y=0\) and \(y=cx+1\) simultaneously.
\item For \(c=0\), use the comaximality of \((y)\) and \((y-1)\) to see that the two lines are disjoint.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
The morphism to \(\mathbb A^1_k=\Spec k[z]\) is induced by the inclusion of the parameter \(z\).  The fiber over the \(k\)-rational point \(z=c\) is obtained by tensoring with \(k[z]/(z-c)\):
\[
k[x,y,z]/(y(y-zx-1))\otimes_{k[z]} k
\cong
k[x,y]/(y(y-cx-1)).
\]
Hence
\[
X_c=V(y)\cup V(y-cx-1).
\]
Both factors are distinct irreducible linear polynomials, so the defining polynomial is squarefree; consequently the fiber ring is reduced for every \(c\).

If \(c\ne0\), the two components
\[
L_1: y=0,\qquad L_2:y=cx+1
\]
meet where \(0=cx+1\), namely at
\[
(x,y)=(-c^{-1},0).
\]
Scheme-theoretically their intersection is
\[
\Spec k[x,y]/(y,y-cx-1)
\cong
\Spec k,
\]
so they meet in one reduced \(k\)-point.

At \(c=0\), the two components are
\[
L_1:y=0,\qquad L_2:y=1.
\]
The ideals \((y)\) and \((y-1)\) are comaximal, so
\[
k[x,y]/(y(y-1))
\cong
k[x]\times k[x].
\]
Thus \(X_0\) is the disjoint union of two affine lines, geometrically two parallel lines in the plane \(z=0\).  The degeneration changes incidence, not reducedness or the number of irreducible components.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Fibers can vary through incidence geometry even when their component count and reducedness remain constant.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
The same calculation is a model for later questions about flat families, Hilbert polynomials, and specialization of intersections, but those stronger theories are not needed here.

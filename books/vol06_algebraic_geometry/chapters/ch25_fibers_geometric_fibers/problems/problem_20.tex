\subsection*{VI.25.P20: Ordinary fiber, geometric fiber, and set-theoretic inverse image}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi25-p20}
Explain the three levels of information carried by \(f^{-1}(s)\), \(X_s\), and \(X_{\bar s}\).
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This final dossier prevents a common conceptual collapse: the same symbol 'fiber' can refer to a set, a scheme over a residue field, or its geometry after algebraic closure.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Compare what each construction remembers: points only; points plus local algebra over \(\kappa(s)\); then behavior after extending that field.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Start with the underlying points of \(X\) mapping to \(s\).
\item Explain what the tensor product with \(\kappa(s)\) adds.
\item Use separable splitting and purely inseparable nilpotence to explain what \(X_{\bar s}\) can reveal.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
The set-theoretic inverse image
\[
f^{-1}(s)=\{x\in X:f(x)=s\}
\]
records only which points of \(X\) lie over \(s\).  It forgets their local rings, residue fields, nilpotents, multiplicities, and the equations inherited from the family.

The scheme-theoretic fiber
\[
X_s=X\times_S\Spec\kappa(s)
\]
has the same basic point-theoretic target \(s\), but equips the fiber with a full scheme structure over the field \(\kappa(s)\).  Affine-locally its coordinate ring is
\[
B\otimes_A\kappa(s),
\]
so it detects repeated factors, nilpotents, residue-field extensions, component structure, and empty fibers.  For example, \(k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\) and \(k\) have the same one-point underlying space but define different scheme fibers.

Finally, for a geometric point \(\bar s:\Spec\Omega\to S\),
\[
X_{\bar s}=X_s\times_{\kappa(s)}\Omega.
\]
This further tests what happens after algebraically closing the coefficient field.  A degree-two separable point can split into two geometric points, while purely inseparable extension can expose nilpotent thickening.  Thus the three levels are:
\[
\text{set of points}
\quad\subset\quad
\text{scheme over }\kappa(s)
\quad\subset\quad
\text{geometry after algebraic closure}.
\]
Each refinement answers questions invisible at the preceding level.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Each successive notion of fiber retains more algebraic and geometric information.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Later notions such as geometrically reduced, geometrically connected, and geometrically irreducible are formulated using this final level.

\subsection*{VI.25.P9: Ordinary irreducibility versus geometric irreducibility}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi25-p09}
View \(X=\Spec\mathbb C\) as an \(\mathbb R\)-scheme. Compare the ordinary fiber with its base change to \(\mathbb C\).
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the minimal example showing why irreducibility over the residue field is weaker than geometric irreducibility.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Compute \(\mathbb C\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C\) and interpret the product decomposition on spectra.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Write one copy of \(\mathbb C\) as \(\mathbb R[T]/(T^2+1)\).
\item Factor \(T^2+1\) over \(\mathbb C\).
\item Use the Chinese remainder theorem.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
The ordinary \(\mathbb R\)-scheme \(X=\Spec\mathbb C\) has one point and is irreducible.  To form its geometric scalar extension, base change to \(\mathbb C\):
\[
X_{\mathbb C}
=
\Spec(\mathbb C\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C).
\]
Using
\[
\mathbb C\cong\mathbb R[T]/(T^2+1)
\]
gives
\[
\mathbb C\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C
\cong
\mathbb C[T]/(T^2+1)
=
\mathbb C[T]/((T-i)(T+i)).
\]
The factors are comaximal, so
\[
\mathbb C\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C
\cong
\mathbb C\times\mathbb C.
\]
Therefore
\[
X_{\mathbb C}
\cong
\Spec\mathbb C\amalg\Spec\mathbb C.
\]
The ordinary fiber is irreducible, whereas the geometric fiber has two irreducible components.  Hence \(X\) is not geometrically irreducible over \(\mathbb R\).
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Geometric irreducibility asks whether irreducibility survives algebraic closure of the ground field.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Finite separable points behave similarly: after a splitting field they decompose according to field embeddings.

\subsection*{VI.25.P15: Reduction modulo \(2\) creates a doubled fiber}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi25-p15}
Compute the fiber at \(2\) of
\[
X=\Spec\mathbb Z[x]/(x^2-2)\to\Spec\mathbb Z.
\]
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This example shows how arithmetic specialization turns a separable degree-two generic point into a nonreduced special point.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Reduce \(x^2-2\) modulo \(2\), then compare with the generic fiber over \(\mathbb Q\).

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Tensor the coordinate ring with \(\mathbb F_2\).
\item In characteristic \(2\), the relation becomes \(x^2=0\).
\item Compare the nilpotent special fiber with the field \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\) generically.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
At the point \((2)\), the residue field is \(\mathbb F_2\), so
\[
X_2
=
\Spec\!\left(
\mathbb Z[x]/(x^2-2)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb F_2
\right)
\cong
\Spec\mathbb F_2[x]/(x^2).
\]
The quotient has a unique prime ideal \((x)\), but the class of \(x\) is a nonzero nilpotent.  Thus the special fiber is one doubled nonreduced point.

For comparison, the generic fiber is
\[
X_{\mathbb Q}
=
\Spec\mathbb Q[x]/(x^2-2)
\cong
\Spec\mathbb Q(\sqrt2),
\]
a reduced degree-two point over \(\mathbb Q\).  The repeated factor after reduction modulo \(2\) is therefore the scheme-theoretic manifestation of ramification at \(2\).
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Arithmetic ramification appears in fibers as repeated factors and nilpotent thickening.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
P19 packages this observation into the general factorization criterion for monic polynomials.

\subsection*{VI.25.P8: Reduced versus nonreduced fibers}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi25-p08}
Give one family whose special fiber is nonreduced although nearby fibers are reduced.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
The example separates reducedness of the total space from reducedness of individual fibers and prepares the distinction between ordinary and geometric reducedness.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use \(u^2-t\) over a field of characteristic different from \(2\), then compare the squarefree fibers \(u^2-a\) with the repeated-root fiber \(u^2\).

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Take \(X=\Spec k[t,u]/(u^2-t)\) with \(\operatorname{char}k\ne2\).
\item At \(t=0\), the class of \(u\) is nilpotent.
\item For \(a\ne0\), verify that \(u^2-a\) has no repeated root.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Assume \(\operatorname{char}k\ne2\) and take
\[
X=\Spec k[t,u]/(u^2-t)\longrightarrow\Spec k[t].
\]
At \(t=0\),
\[
X_0=\Spec k[u]/(u^2),
\]
which is nonreduced because the nonzero class of \(u\) is square-zero.

For \(a\ne0\), the fiber ring is
\[
k[u]/(u^2-a).
\]
The derivative of \(u^2-a\) is \(2u\).  A repeated root would have to satisfy both \(u^2=a\) and \(2u=0\).  Since \(2\ne0\) and \(a\ne0\), this is impossible.  Thus \(u^2-a\) is squarefree.  Equivalently, after passing to a splitting field it has distinct roots, and the quotient ring is reduced.  Hence every nonzero fiber is reduced while the special fiber at \(0\) is not.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Fiber reducedness can jump when a specialized polynomial acquires a repeated factor.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
In VI/26 and later chapters, finiteness hypotheses make it possible to study how such fiber properties vary in families.

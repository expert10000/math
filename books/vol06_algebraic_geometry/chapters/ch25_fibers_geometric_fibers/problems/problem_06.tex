\subsection*{VI.25.P6: Gaussian integers: ramified, split, inert}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi25-p06}
Analyze the fibers of
\[
\Spec\mathbb Z[T]/(T^2+1)\to\Spec\mathbb Z.
\]
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
The three possible factorization patterns of one polynomial produce ramified, split, and inert fibers in a single arithmetic family.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Reduce \(T^2+1\) modulo \(p\).  Treat \(p=2\) separately and use the quadratic-residue criterion for \(-1\) when \(p\) is odd.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item At \(p=2\), \(T^2+1=(T+1)^2\).
\item If \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), \(-1\) is a square modulo \(p\).
\item If \(p\equiv3\pmod4\), the polynomial is irreducible of degree \(2\).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
At the closed point \((p)\), the fiber is
\[
X_p=\Spec\mathbb F_p[T]/(T^2+1).
\]
For \(p=2\),
\[
T^2+1=(T+1)^2,
\]
so
\[
X_2\cong\Spec\mathbb F_2[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2),
\]
a one-point nonreduced fiber.

Now assume \(p\) is odd.  If \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), then \(-1\) is a quadratic residue modulo \(p\).  Choose \(a\in\mathbb F_p\) with \(a^2=-1\).  Then
\[
T^2+1=(T-a)(T+a),
\]
and the roots are distinct.  Hence
\[
\mathbb F_p[T]/(T^2+1)\cong\mathbb F_p\times\mathbb F_p,
\]
so the fiber consists of two reduced rational points.

If \(p\equiv3\pmod4\), \(-1\) is not a square, so \(T^2+1\) is irreducible.  Therefore
\[
\mathbb F_p[T]/(T^2+1)\cong\mathbb F_{p^2},
\]
and the fiber is one reduced point of residue degree \(2\).  Thus repeated, split, and irreducible factorization patterns become ramified, split, and inert fiber behavior.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Factorization modulo \(p\) simultaneously controls components, residue degrees, and nilpotent structure.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
P19 generalizes this analysis from \(T^2+1\) to an arbitrary monic polynomial.

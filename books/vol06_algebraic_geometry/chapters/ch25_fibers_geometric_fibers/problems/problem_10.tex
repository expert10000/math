\subsection*{VI.25.P10: Constructing a geometric fiber}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi25-p10}
Let \(f:X\to S\), \(s\in S\), and \(\Omega/\kappa(s)\) be algebraically closed. Show
\[
X\times_S\Spec\Omega\cong X_s\times_{\Spec\kappa(s)}\Spec\Omega.
\]
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This identity makes precise that a geometric fiber is an ordinary fiber followed by scalar extension.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Factor the geometric point through \(\Spec\kappa(s)\) and use associativity of fiber products.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Write \(X_s=X\times_S\Spec\kappa(s)\).
\item Use the factorization \(\Spec\Omega\to\Spec\kappa(s)\to S\).
\item Reassociate the two pullbacks.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
The inclusion \(\kappa(s)\hookrightarrow\Omega\) gives
\[
\Spec\Omega\longrightarrow\Spec\kappa(s),
\]
and the composite with the canonical point morphism \(\Spec\kappa(s)\to S\) is the chosen geometric point over \(s\).  Therefore
\[
X\times_S\Spec\Omega
\cong
\bigl(X\times_S\Spec\kappa(s)\bigr)
\times_{\Spec\kappa(s)}
\Spec\Omega
\]
by associativity of fiber products.  The first factor in parentheses is \(X_s\), so
\[
\boxed{
X\times_S\Spec\Omega
\cong
X_s\times_{\Spec\kappa(s)}\Spec\Omega
}.
\]
The isomorphism is canonical because both sides represent exactly the same compatible maps from a test scheme.  Thus geometric fibers are not a separate construction: they are a special iterated base change.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Geometric fibers are ordinary fibers with an algebraically closed coefficient field.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Changing the algebraically closed overfield can be compared after embedding both choices into a common algebraically closed extension.

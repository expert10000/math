\paragraph{Solution to E1.} A domain is normal when every element of its fraction field integral over it already belongs to it.

\paragraph{Solution to E2.} If $A$ is integrally closed, every localization $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is integrally closed, so all local rings of $\Spec A$ are normal.

\paragraph{Solution to E3.} A field equals its own fraction field, so there are no missing integral elements.

\paragraph{Solution to E4.} If $z$ in the localized fraction field is integral over $S^{-1}A$, clear finitely many denominators from its monic equation to reduce to integral closedness of $A$; equivalently invoke localization compatibility of integral closure.

\paragraph{Solution to E5.} Since $A\subseteq\overline A\subseteq\Frac(A)$, one has $\Frac(\overline A)\subseteq\Frac(A)$, while $A\subseteq\overline A$ gives the reverse inclusion after taking fraction fields.

\paragraph{Solution to E6.} The ring extension $A\subseteq\overline A$ is integral by definition, and affine integral extensions correspond to integral morphisms.

\paragraph{Solution to E7.} Integral extensions satisfy lying over, so every prime of $A$ has a prime of $\overline A$ above it.

\paragraph{Solution to E8.} If $A$ is normal, its integral closure in $\Frac(A)$ is $A$ itself.

\paragraph{Solution to E9.} The element $t$ satisfies the monic polynomial $T^2-t^2\in A[T]$.

\paragraph{Solution to E10.} Adjoining $t$ gives $k[t]$; it is integrally closed and contains every element integral over $A$ by minimality of integral closure, so it equals $\overline A$.

\paragraph{Solution to E11.} The normalization is $\Spec(\overline A)_f=\Spec\overline{A_f}$.

\paragraph{Solution to E12.} Normalization agrees on distinguished affine opens by localization and these opens form a basis, so the restrictions glue.

\paragraph{Solution to E13.} On $\Spec A$ and $\Spec\overline A$, the generic points are $(0)$ because both rings are domains; the normalization sends one to the other.

\paragraph{Solution to E14.} On affine charts, $K(X^\nu)=\Frac(\overline A)=\Frac(A)=K(X)$.

\paragraph{Solution to E15.} $X^\nu$ is normal, so its integral closure on every affine chart is unchanged.

\paragraph{Solution to E16.} A reduced fraction integral over a UFD has denominator dividing a power of the numerator; coprimality forces the denominator to be a unit.

\paragraph{Solution to E17.} $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ is a UFD, hence integrally closed.

\paragraph{Solution to E18.} It is a root of the monic polynomial $T^2-T-1$ with coefficients in $\mathbb Z[\sqrt5]$.

\paragraph{Solution to E19.} The node has $x=0$, so $t^2-1=0$ and therefore $t=\pm1$ when the characteristic is not $2$.

\paragraph{Solution to E20.} The cusp $\Spec k[t^2,t^3]$ is reduced because its ring is a domain, but it is not normal because $t$ is missing and integral.

\paragraph{Solution to E21.} For a finitely generated domain over a field, the integral closure in its fraction field is a finite module; hence the normalization morphism is finite.

\paragraph{Solution to E22.} If $Z$ is normal integral and $f:Z\to X$ is dominant, there is a unique $\widetilde f:Z\to X^\nu$ with $f=\nu\circ\widetilde f$.

\paragraph{Solution to E23.} If $y$ corresponds to $\mathfrak q\subset\overline A$, then $\OO_{X^\nu,y}=(\overline A)_{\mathfrak q}$, an integrally closed subring of the common function field.

\paragraph{Solution to E24.} For a purely inseparable field extension $L/k$, $L\otimes_kL$ can be nonreduced, so base change of a normal point can fail even reducedness.

\paragraph{Solution to E25.} Normalization induces an isomorphism on function fields and, in the standard finite-type setting, is an isomorphism over a dense open; hence it is birational.

\paragraph{Solution to E26.} Examples include Krull dimension/codimension, height-one valuation criteria, Weil divisors, and divisor class groups.

% VI28 detailed exercise solutions -- VI/24+ proof-depth standard.

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-01
\paragraph{Solution to E1.}
A domain \(A\) with fraction field \(K=\Frac(A)\) is \emph{normal} when it is integrally closed in
\(K\): whenever \(z\in K\) satisfies a monic equation
\[
z^n+a_{n-1}z^{n-1}+\cdots+a_0=0,\qquad a_i\in A,
\]
one already has \(z\in A\).  The ambient field is specifically the fraction field of \(A\), not an
arbitrary algebraic extension.

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-02
\paragraph{Solution to E2.}
Assume \(A\) is a domain integrally closed in \(K=\Frac(A)\).  A point
\(x\leftrightarrow\mathfrak p\in\Spec A\) has local ring
\[
\mathcal O_{\Spec A,x}=A_{\mathfrak p}.
\]
Localization of an integrally closed domain is integrally closed: equivalently, integral closure
commutes with localization.  Hence each \(A_{\mathfrak p}\) is an integrally closed domain.
Therefore \(\Spec A\) is normal.

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-03
\paragraph{Solution to E3.}
If \(F\) is a field, then \(\Frac(F)=F\).  Thus any element of the fraction field integral over
\(F\) already lies in \(F\) before any integrality condition is used.  Hence every field is a
normal domain, and \(\Spec F\) is a normal integral scheme.

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-04
\paragraph{Solution to E4.}
Let \(A\) be a normal domain and \(S\subseteq A\) multiplicative.  Its integral closure in
\(K=\Frac(A)\) is \(A\).  Localization compatibility for integral closure gives
\[
\overline{S^{-1}A}^{\,K}=S^{-1}\overline A=S^{-1}A.
\]
Thus \(S^{-1}A\) is integrally closed in its fraction field and is normal.  For
\(S=A\setminus\mathfrak p\), this shows every local ring of a normal affine scheme is normal.

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-05
\paragraph{Solution to E5.}
For a domain \(A\) and its integral closure \(\overline A\subseteq K=\Frac(A)\),
\[
A\subseteq\overline A\subseteq K.
\]
Taking fraction fields gives
\[
\Frac(A)\subseteq\Frac(\overline A)\subseteq K.
\]
But \(\Frac(A)=K\), so all inclusions are equalities:
\[
\boxed{\Frac(\overline A)=\Frac(A).}
\]
Normalization therefore does not alter the rational function field.

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-06
\paragraph{Solution to E6.}
By definition every element of \(\overline A\) is integral over \(A\).  Hence
\(A\subseteq\overline A\) is an integral ring extension.  For affine schemes, a ring map
\(A\to B\) is integral exactly when
\[
\Spec B\longrightarrow\Spec A
\]
is an integral morphism.  Therefore the affine normalization morphism is integral.

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-07
\paragraph{Solution to E7.}
The extension \(A\subseteq\overline A\) is integral.  By the lying-over theorem, for every
\(\mathfrak p\in\Spec A\) there exists
\(\mathfrak q\in\Spec\overline A\) with
\[
\mathfrak q\cap A=\mathfrak p.
\]
Thus every point of \(\Spec A\) has a point above it, so
\[
\Spec\overline A\to\Spec A
\]
is surjective.

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-08
\paragraph{Solution to E8.}
If \(A\) is normal, it is already integrally closed in \(K=\Frac(A)\).  Hence its integral closure
is
\[
\overline A=A.
\]
The normalization morphism is therefore induced by the identity \(A\to A\), so
\[
(\Spec A)^\nu\cong\Spec A.
\]

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-09
\paragraph{Solution to E9.}
Let \(A=k[t^2,t^3]\subseteq k(t)\).  The element \(t\) satisfies
\[
T^2-t^2=0,
\]
a monic polynomial in \(A[T]\), because \(t^2\in A\).  Hence \(t\) is integral over \(A\).
It is not in \(A\), since exponents occurring in polynomials in \(t^2,t^3\) are
\(0,2,3,4,\ldots\), never \(1\).

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-10
\paragraph{Solution to E10.}
Put \(A=k[t^2,t^3]\).  From
\[
t=\frac{t^3}{t^2}
\]
we know \(\Frac(A)=k(t)\).  By E9, \(t\) is integral over \(A\), so
\[
k[t]=A[t]
\]
is integral over \(A\).  Therefore every element of \(k[t]\) belongs to the integral closure
\(\overline A\).

Conversely, \(k[t]\) is a PID, hence a UFD and therefore integrally closed in \(k(t)\).  Since it is
an integrally closed intermediate domain
\[
A\subseteq k[t]\subseteq k(t),
\]
minimality of integral closure gives \(\overline A\subseteq k[t]\).  Thus
\[
\boxed{\overline A=k[t].}
\]

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-11
\paragraph{Solution to E11.}
Let \(X=\Spec A\), with normalization \(\Spec\overline A\to\Spec A\).  The inverse image of
\(D(f)\) is
\[
D(f)\subseteq\Spec\overline A
=
\Spec(\overline A)_f.
\]
Integral closure commutes with localization:
\[
\overline{A_f}=(\overline A)_f.
\]
Hence the normalization of \(D(f)=\Spec A_f\) is exactly
\[
\Spec(\overline A)_f.
\]

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-12
\paragraph{Solution to E12.}
Every nonempty open \(U\subseteq X\) can be covered by distinguished opens inside affine charts.
On each such \(D(f)\), E11 identifies the inverse image under \(\nu\) with the normalization of
\(D(f)\).  These identifications are induced by localization, so on smaller distinguished opens
they agree automatically.  They therefore glue, proving
\[
\nu^{-1}(U)\longrightarrow U
\]
is the normalization of \(U\).

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-13
\paragraph{Solution to E13.}
On an affine chart \(U=\Spec A\), both \(A\) and \(\overline A\) are domains.  Hence their generic
points are the zero ideals.  Under contraction,
\[
(0)\subseteq\overline A
\longmapsto
(0)\subseteq A.
\]
Thus the normalization sends \(\eta_{X^\nu}\) to \(\eta_X\).  These affine descriptions agree
globally, so the generic points correspond canonically.

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-14
\paragraph{Solution to E14.}
On a nonempty affine chart,
\[
K(X^\nu)=\Frac(\overline A).
\]
By E5,
\[
\Frac(\overline A)=\Frac(A)=K(X).
\]
Both identifications arise from the same generic stalk, so they agree on overlaps and are canonical:
\[
\boxed{K(X^\nu)\cong K(X).}
\]

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-15
\paragraph{Solution to E15.}
The normalization \(X^\nu\) is normal.  A normal integral scheme equals its own normalization by
E8.  Therefore
\[
(X^\nu)^\nu\cong X^\nu.
\]
The isomorphism is canonical because on every affine normal chart it is induced by the equality of
the ring with its own integral closure.

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-16
\paragraph{Solution to E16.}
Let \(A\) be a UFD and let \(a/b\in\Frac(A)\) be written in lowest terms.  Suppose \(a/b\) is
integral over \(A\):
\[
(a/b)^n+c_{n-1}(a/b)^{n-1}+\cdots+c_0=0.
\]
Multiplying by \(b^n\) gives
\[
a^n=-b(c_{n-1}a^{n-1}+c_{n-2}a^{n-2}b+\cdots+c_0b^{n-1}).
\]
Hence \(b\mid a^n\).  Since \(a\) and \(b\) are coprime in the UFD, \(b\) must be a unit.
Thus \(a/b\in A\), proving \(A\) integrally closed.

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-17
\paragraph{Solution to E17.}
The polynomial ring
\[
k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]
\]
is a UFD.  By E16 it is integrally closed, hence a normal domain.  Therefore
\[
\mathbb A^n_k=\Spec k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]
\]
is normal.

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-18
\paragraph{Solution to E18.}
Set
\[
\alpha=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}.
\]
A direct calculation gives
\[
\alpha^2-\alpha-1=0.
\]
Thus \(\alpha\) satisfies the monic polynomial
\[
T^2-T-1\in\mathbb Z[\sqrt5][T],
\]
so \(\alpha\) is integral over \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt5]\).

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-19
\paragraph{Solution to E19.}
For
\[
x=t^2-1,\qquad y=t(t^2-1),
\]
a point maps to the node \((0,0)\) only if
\[
t^2-1=0.
\]
When \(\operatorname{char}k\ne2\), this gives
\[
t=1\quad\text{or}\quad t=-1.
\]
Both values indeed give \(x=y=0\).  Thus two distinct points upstairs lie over the node.

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-20
\paragraph{Solution to E20.}
The cusp
\[
X=\Spec k[t^2,t^3]
\]
is reduced because its coordinate ring is a domain.  It is not normal: \(t\in k(t)\) is integral
over \(k[t^2,t^3]\) but does not belong to that ring.  Thus reducedness is strictly weaker than
normality.

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-21
\paragraph{Solution to E21.}
If \(A\) is a finitely generated domain over a field \(k\), the finiteness theorem for integral
closure states that its integral closure \(\overline A\) in \(\Frac(A)\) is a finite \(A\)-module.
Consequently
\[
\Spec\overline A\longrightarrow\Spec A
\]
is finite.  This is the standard finite normalization theorem for affine varieties.

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-22
\paragraph{Solution to E22.}
Let \(\nu:X^\nu\to X\) be the normalization.  If \(Z\) is normal and integral and
\(f:Z\to X\) is dominant, then there exists a unique morphism
\[
\widetilde f:Z\to X^\nu
\]
such that
\[
f=\nu\circ\widetilde f.
\]
Affinely, dominance embeds \(K(X)\) in \(K(Z)\); integral elements over a coordinate ring of \(X\)
map to elements integral over a normal coordinate ring of \(Z\), hence become regular there.

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-23
\paragraph{Solution to E23.}
If \(X=\Spec A\), \(X^\nu=\Spec\overline A\), and
\(y\leftrightarrow\mathfrak q\subseteq\overline A\), then
\[
\mathcal O_{X^\nu,y}=(\overline A)_{\mathfrak q}.
\]
This localization is an integrally closed domain inside
\[
\Frac(\overline A)=K(X).
\]
If \(x\leftrightarrow\mathfrak p=\mathfrak q\cap A\), then localization of the integral extension
shows
\[
A_{\mathfrak p}\longrightarrow(\overline A)_{\mathfrak q}
\]
is integral.

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-24
\paragraph{Solution to E24.}
Let
\[
k=\mathbb F_p(u),\qquad L=k(t),\qquad t^p=u.
\]
Then \(L\) is a field, so \(\Spec L\) is normal over \(k\).  After scalar extension to \(L\),
\[
L\otimes_kL
\cong
L[T]/(T^p-u)
=
L[T]/((T-t)^p)
\cong
L[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^p).
\]
This ring is nonreduced.  Hence normality can be destroyed by a purely inseparable field extension;
geometric normality is a stronger condition.

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-25
\paragraph{Solution to E25.}
Normalization always preserves the function field:
\[
K(X^\nu)=K(X).
\]
For integral finite-type schemes over a field, the normalization is finite.  On an affine chart,
choose finite \(A\)-module generators of \(\overline A\) and clear their denominators in
\(\Frac(A)\).  After inverting one nonzero \(f\in A\),
\[
(\overline A)_f=A_f.
\]
Thus \(\nu\) is an isomorphism over a nonempty dense open.  Hence it is birational.

% VI28-DETAILED-EXERCISE-26
\paragraph{Solution to E26.}
VI/28 deliberately stops before several later topics: Krull dimension and codimension, height-one
valuation criteria for normal domains, Weil divisors, and divisor class groups.  Its output for
those chapters is the normal integral model \(X^\nu\), the common function field, and the
normalization morphism.

\begin{problem}[VI.28.P17: Normalization versus reduction]\label{prob:vi28-17}
Compare reduction and normalization and give an example showing they are different operations.

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical boundary dossier.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Reduction and normalization solve different defects.

For an affine scheme \(\Spec A\), reduction replaces \(A\) by
\[
A_{\mathrm{red}}=A/\sqrt{(0)}.
\]
It removes nilpotents and leaves the underlying topological space unchanged.

Normalization, in the integral case, embeds
\[
A\subseteq K=\Frac(A)
\]
and replaces \(A\) by its integral closure \(\overline A\subseteq K\). It repairs failure of integral closedness and may alter the fibers over singular points.

For the cusp
\[
A=k[t^2,t^3],
\]
the ring is already a domain, hence reduced. Thus reduction does nothing:
\[
A_{\mathrm{red}}=A.
\]
But \(t\in k(t)\) is integral over \(A\) and not in \(A\), so
\[
\overline A=k[t]\ne A.
\]
Thus normalization changes the cusp even though reduction is trivial.
\end{solution}
\fi

\begin{problem}[VI.28.P15: Normalization need not be a homeomorphism]\label{prob:vi28-15}
Explain using a nodal curve why a normalization morphism can have more than one point over a singular point.

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical geometric warning.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Consider the integral nodal curve
\[
C:\quad y^2=x^2(x+1)
\]
over a field of characteristic not \(2\), with parametrization
\[
x=t^2-1,\qquad y=t(t^2-1).
\]
The normalization is the parameter line \(\Spec k[t]\to C\). Over the node \((0,0)\), the equation \(x=0\) gives
\[
t^2-1=0,
\]
so
\[
t=1\quad\text{or}\quad t=-1.
\]
Both values also give \(y=0\). Thus two distinct points of the normalization map to the same singular point.

Hence normalization can separate branches. It remains finite and birational, and it preserves the function field, but it need not be injective on points and therefore need not be a homeomorphism.
\end{solution}
\fi

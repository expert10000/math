\begin{problem}[VI.28.P15: Normalization need not be a homeomorphism]\label{prob:vi28-15}
Explain using a nodal curve why a normalization morphism can have more than one point over a singular point.

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical geometric warning.
\end{problem}
\begin{solution}
For the parametrization $x=t^2-1$, $y=t(t^2-1)$ of $y^2=x^2(x+1)$, both $t=1$ and $t=-1$ map to $(0,0)$.  Thus two points of the normal parameter line lie above the node.  The normalization is still finite and birational, but it need not be injective on the underlying point set.
\end{solution}

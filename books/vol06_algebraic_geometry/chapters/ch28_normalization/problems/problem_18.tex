\begin{problem}[VI.28.P18: Normality after field extension: warning]\label{prob:vi28-18}
Explain why normality over $k$ need not survive arbitrary field extension in positive characteristic.

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical base-change warning; systematic base change was VI/24.
\end{problem}
\begin{solution}
Let $k$ be imperfect of characteristic $p$ and let $L/k$ be a nontrivial finite purely inseparable field extension.  $\Spec L$ is normal.  After base change from $k$ to $L$, the ring $L\otimes_k L$ can contain nilpotents (for a degree-$p$ extension it has the form of a $p$-fold thickening), so the base-changed scheme is not even reduced and therefore not normal.  Thus geometric normality is strictly stronger.
\end{solution}

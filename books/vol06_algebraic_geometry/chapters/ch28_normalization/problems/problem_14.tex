\begin{problem}[VI.28.P14: Finite normalization over a field]\label{prob:vi28-14}
Let \(X\) be integral and finite type over a field \(k\). Explain why \(X^\nu\to X\) is finite.

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical finite-type consequence.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Take an affine open \(U=\Spec A\subseteq X\). Because \(X\) is finite type over \(k\), \(A\) is a finitely generated \(k\)-domain. The commutative-algebra finiteness theorem for integral closure states that the integral closure
\[
\overline A\subseteq\Frac(A)
\]
is a finite \(A\)-module.

Therefore the affine normalization map
\[
\Spec\overline A\to\Spec A
\]
is finite. Finiteness of a morphism is affine-local on the target, and the affine normalized charts glue compatibly. Hence the global normalization morphism
\[
\nu:X^\nu\to X
\]
is finite.

The finite-type-over-a-field hypothesis is important: an arbitrary integral extension need not be module-finite.
\end{solution}
\fi

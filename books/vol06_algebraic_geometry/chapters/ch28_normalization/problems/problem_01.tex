\begin{problem}[VI.28.P1: AG7 T05 reconstruction: affine normalization]\label{prob:vi28-01}
Let \(A\) be a domain, \(K=\Frac(A)\), and \(\overline A\) the integral closure of \(A\) in \(K\). Prove that \(\Spec\overline A\to\Spec A\) is integral, dominant, surjective, normal on the source, and induces an isomorphism of function fields.

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical reconstruction of the affine normalization construction in AG7 T05; no numbered legacy Problem 8 block is claimed.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
The inclusion \(A\subseteq\overline A\) is integral by definition, so the induced affine morphism
\[
\nu:\Spec\overline A\to\Spec A
\]
is integral. Since the ring map is injective, the affine dominance criterion gives that \(\nu\) is dominant.

Surjectivity is stronger than dominance here. By lying over for integral extensions, every prime
\(\mathfrak p\subseteq A\) is the contraction of some prime
\(\mathfrak q\subseteq\overline A\). Hence \(\nu\) is surjective.

By construction \(\overline A\) is integrally closed in \(K\), so it is a normal domain and
\(\Spec\overline A\) is normal. Finally,
\[
A\subseteq\overline A\subseteq K=\Frac(A)
\]
implies
\[
\Frac(\overline A)=K=\Frac(A).
\]
Thus the generic points correspond and \(\nu\) induces the identity on the common function field after the canonical identifications.
\end{solution}
\fi

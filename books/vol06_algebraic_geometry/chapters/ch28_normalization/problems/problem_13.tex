\begin{problem}[VI.28.P13: A quadratic arithmetic normalization]\label{prob:vi28-13}
Compute the integral closure of $\mathbb Z[\sqrt5]$ in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$.

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical arithmetic example; algebraic integral-closure theory belongs to Volume V.
\end{problem}
\begin{solution}
The element $\alpha=(1+\sqrt5)/2$ satisfies $\alpha^2-\alpha-1=0$, so it is integral over $\mathbb Z[\sqrt5]$.  The full ring of integers of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ is $\mathbb Z[\alpha]$, which is integrally closed.  Thus it is the normalization of $\mathbb Z[\sqrt5]$ in its fraction field.
\end{solution}

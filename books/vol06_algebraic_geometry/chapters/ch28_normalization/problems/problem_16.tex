\begin{problem}[VI.28.P16: Cusp normalization can be bijective but not an isomorphism]\label{prob:vi28-16}
Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, explain why the cusp normalization can be bijective on points while failing to be an isomorphism.

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical geometric warning.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
For the cusp
\[
C=\Spec k[t^2,t^3],
\]
the normalization is
\[
\nu:\Spec k[t]\to C,\qquad t\mapsto(t^2,t^3).
\]
Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, every point of the cusp has a unique parameter \(t\): away from the origin one recovers \(t=y/x\), while the cusp point corresponds uniquely to \(t=0\). Thus \(\nu\) is bijective on closed points, and in fact on the underlying points in this example.

Nevertheless it is not an isomorphism. At the cusp point,
\[
\mathcal O_{C,(t^2,t^3)}
=
k[t^2,t^3]_{(t^2,t^3)},
\]
whereas upstairs
\[
\mathcal O_{\mathbb A^1,(t)}
=
k[t]_{(t)}.
\]
The latter contains the integral element \(t\), which is missing downstairs. Hence the local-ring map is not an isomorphism, so neither is \(\nu\). This shows that bijectivity on points does not determine scheme structure.
\end{solution}
\fi

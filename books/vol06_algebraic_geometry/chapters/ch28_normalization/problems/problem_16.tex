\begin{problem}[VI.28.P16: Cusp normalization can be bijective but not an isomorphism]\label{prob:vi28-16}
Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, explain why the cusp normalization can be bijective on points while failing to be an isomorphism.

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical geometric warning.
\end{problem}
\begin{solution}
The parametrization $t\mapsto(t^2,t^3)$ has a unique preimage for every point of the cusp, including $t=0$ over the cusp point.  Nevertheless the local ring at the cusp is $k[t^2,t^3]_{(t^2,t^3)}$, whereas the local ring upstairs is $k[t]_{(t)}$; the latter contains the integral element $t$ missing downstairs.  Hence the morphism is not an isomorphism.
\end{solution}

% VI28-DETAILED-PROBLEM-03
\begin{problem}[VI.28.P3: Universal property of normalization]\label{prob:vi28-03}
Prove that a dominant morphism \(f:Z\to X\) from a normal integral scheme factors uniquely through \(X^\nu\).

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical consequence of the AG7 normalization stream.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Dominance gives an embedding
\[
K(X)\hookrightarrow K(Z).
\]
Take an affine \(U=\Spec A\subseteq X\), and cover \(f^{-1}(U)\) by affine opens
\(W=\Spec B\). Since \(Z\) is normal, every such \(B\) is an integrally closed domain in \(K(Z)\).

For \(z\in\overline A\subseteq K(X)\), its image in \(K(Z)\) satisfies a monic equation with coefficients in the image of \(A\). Since \(A\to B\), the same equation has coefficients in \(B\); hence the image of \(z\) is integral over \(B\). Normality of \(B\) forces that image to lie in \(B\). Thus the ring map \(A\to B\) extends uniquely to
\[
\overline A\to B.
\]
These extensions commute with localization, so they agree on overlaps of the affine \(W\)'s and glue to a map
\[
f^{-1}(U)\to\Spec\overline A.
\]
Doing this for all affine \(U\) and using localization compatibility on target overlaps gives a global morphism
\[
\widetilde f:Z\to X^\nu
\]
with \(f=\nu\circ\widetilde f\).

For uniqueness, two factorizations induce the same embedding of \(K(X)\) into \(K(Z)\). On every affine chart of \(Z\), the images of elements of \(\overline A\subseteq K(X)\) are therefore forced. Hence the two ring maps, and thus the two morphisms, coincide.
\end{solution}
\fi

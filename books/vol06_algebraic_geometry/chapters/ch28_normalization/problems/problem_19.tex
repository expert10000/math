% VI28-DETAILED-PROBLEM-19
\begin{problem}[VI.28.P19: Normalization and arbitrary base change]\label{prob:vi28-19}
Why can one not assert \((X^\nu)_{S'}\cong (X_{S'})^\nu\) for arbitrary base change without hypotheses?

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical warning dossier.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Normalization produces a normal integral scheme, but arbitrary base change need not preserve either normality or even reducedness. Problem~18 gives an explicit example: \(X=\Spec L\) is already normal over
\(k=\mathbb F_p(u)\), so \(X^\nu=X\), yet after base change to \(L\),
\[
(X^\nu)_L=X_L=\Spec L[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^p),
\]
which is nonreduced.

Therefore the left-hand side of a naive compatibility formula need not be normal. The right-hand side, when normalization is defined after passing to an integral reduced component, is normal by construction. They cannot agree in general.

In the example, the reduced base change is \(\Spec L\), whose normalization is \(\Spec L\), whereas the base change of the normalization is the nonreduced thickening
\[
\Spec L[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^p).
\]
Hence normalization and arbitrary base change fail to commute without additional hypotheses.
\end{solution}

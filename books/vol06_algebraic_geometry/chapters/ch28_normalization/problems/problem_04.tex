\begin{problem}[VI.28.P4: Normalize the cusp]\label{prob:vi28-04}
Compute the normalization of \(A=k[x,y]/(y^2-x^3)\) by identifying it with \(k[t^2,t^3]\subset k[t]\).

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical cusp computation derived from the AG7 normalization theory.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Define
\[
k[x,y]/(y^2-x^3)\longrightarrow k[t],\qquad x\mapsto t^2,\quad y\mapsto t^3.
\]
Its image is \(k[t^2,t^3]\), and the kernel is exactly \((y^2-x^3)\), so
\[
A\cong k[t^2,t^3].
\]
The fraction field is \(k(t)\), because
\[
t=\frac{t^3}{t^2}.
\]
The element \(t\) is integral over \(A\): it satisfies
\[
T^2-x=0
\]
after identifying \(x=t^2\). It is not in \(A\), so \(A\) is not normal.

Adjoining \(t\) gives \(A[t]=k[t]\). Since \(k[t]\) is a PID, hence a UFD, it is integrally closed in \(k(t)\). Also \(k[t]\) is integral over \(A\). Therefore it is exactly the integral closure:
\[
\overline A=k[t].
\]
The normalization is
\[
\mathbb A^1_k=\Spec k[t]\to\Spec A,
\qquad t\mapsto(t^2,t^3),
\]
which parametrizes the cusp.
\end{solution}
\fi

\begin{problem}[VI.28.P10: Birationality of normalization]\label{prob:vi28-10}
Assume \(X\) is an integral finite-type scheme over a field. Using the definition that two integral schemes are birational when they contain isomorphic nonempty dense opens, show that the normalization \(X^\nu\to X\) is birational.

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical dossier; the later birational-geometry arc develops this language further.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Work on a nonempty affine chart \(U=\Spec A\). Since \(A\) is a finitely generated domain over a field, its integral closure \(\overline A\) is a finite \(A\)-module. Choose module generators
\[
b_1,\ldots,b_r\in\overline A\subseteq\Frac(A).
\]
Write \(b_i=a_i/s_i\) with \(s_i\ne0\), and put
\[
f=s_1\cdots s_r\ne0.
\]
After localizing at \(f\), every \(b_i\) lies in \(A_f\). Hence
\[
(\overline A)_f\subseteq A_f.
\]
The reverse inclusion is automatic, so
\[
(\overline A)_f=A_f.
\]
Thus normalization is an isomorphism over the nonempty principal open \(D(f)\subseteq U\). Because \(X\) is integral, \(D(f)\) is dense. Its inverse image is the same dense open in \(X^\nu\). Therefore \(X\) and \(X^\nu\) contain isomorphic nonempty dense opens and are birational.

The equality \(K(X^\nu)=K(X)\) is the field-theoretic shadow of the same result.
\end{solution}
\fi

\begin{problem}[VI.28.P20: Boundary to dimension and divisors]\label{prob:vi28-20}
State precisely what VI/28 establishes that later dimension and divisor chapters may use.

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical boundary dossier.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
For every integral scheme \(X\), VI/28 supplies a canonical normal integral scheme \(X^\nu\) and an integral, dominant, surjective morphism
\[
\nu:X^\nu\to X.
\]
The construction preserves the function field:
\[
K(X^\nu)=K(X),
\]
commutes with restriction to nonempty opens, is idempotent, and satisfies the universal factorization property for dominant maps from normal integral schemes.

For integral schemes of finite type over a field, VI/28 also proves that \(\nu\) is finite and birational, hence an isomorphism over a nonempty dense open.

What is not yet established is equally important: no height-one valuation criterion, no Krull-dimension or codimension formula, no Weil-divisor theory, and no divisor class group computation is proved here. Later chapters may use the normal model and common function field supplied by VI/28, but must develop those codimension-one and divisor-theoretic tools separately.
\end{solution}

\subsection*{Challenge VI.02.C2 --- Noetherianity of affine Zariski space}
\label{chal:vi02-c2}

\paragraph{Challenge.}
Using Hilbert's basis theorem, prove that every descending chain of Zariski closed subsets
\[
X_1\supseteq X_2\supseteq X_3\supseteq\cdots
\]
of \(\mathbb A_k^n\) eventually stabilizes.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this challenge matters.}
This is the topological shadow of polynomial-ring Noetherianity and explains why algebraic
geometry admits finite decompositions despite its coarse topology.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Apply the vanishing-ideal operation.  The inclusions reverse, giving an ascending chain
\[
I(X_1)\subseteq I(X_2)\subseteq\cdots.
\]
Use Hilbert's basis theorem and the fact that a closed set satisfies \(X=V(I(X))\).

\paragraph{Solution.}
The descending chain of closed sets gives an ascending chain of ideals
\[
I(X_1)\subseteq I(X_2)\subseteq I(X_3)\subseteq\cdots.
\]
Because \(k[x_1,\dots,x_n]\) is Noetherian, this ideal chain stabilizes: for some \(N\),
\[
I(X_m)=I(X_N)\qquad(m\ge N).
\]
Each \(X_m\) is closed, hence
\[
X_m=V(I(X_m)).
\]
Therefore \(X_m=X_N\) for all \(m\ge N\).  Thus affine Zariski space is a Noetherian
topological space.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Deduce that every open subset is quasi-compact and compare with Problem VI.02.P6.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New challenge on the algebra-topology bridge behind quasi-compactness.
\fi

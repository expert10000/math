\subsection*{Challenge VI.02.C1 --- The product-topology failure in every dimension}
\label{chal:vi02-c1}

\paragraph{Challenge.}
Assume \(k\) is infinite and \(n\ge2\).  Prove that the Zariski topology on
\(\mathbb A_k^n\) is strictly finer than the \(n\)-fold product of the cofinite Zariski
topologies on \(\mathbb A_k^1\).

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Every product-basic open is Zariski open.  For strictness, use the closed hyperplane
\(x_2-x_1=0\) and reduce to the two-dimensional diagonal argument.

\paragraph{Solution.}
A basic product open \(U_1\times\cdots\times U_n\) has each \(U_i\) cofinite, so choose a
one-variable polynomial \(f_i(x_i)\) whose zero set is the complement of \(U_i\).  Then
\[
U_1\times\cdots\times U_n=D(f_1(x_1)\cdots f_n(x_n)),
\]
which is Zariski open.  Thus the product topology is coarser.  The hyperplane
\[
H=V(x_2-x_1)
\]
is Zariski closed.  Its complement cannot be product-open: any basic product neighborhood
of a point outside \(H\) has cofinite first two coordinate sets, whose intersection is
nonempty, producing a point of \(H\) in the neighborhood.  Hence the inclusion is strict.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Characterize other Zariski closed sets that cannot be product-closed.

\fi

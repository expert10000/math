\subsection*{Problem VI.02.P5 --- Finite fields and polynomial point separators}
\label{prob:vi02-p5}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\).  Prove the rational-point Zariski topology on \(k^n\) is discrete.
Then construct, for each \(a=(a_1,\dots,a_n)\in k^n\), a polynomial \(\delta_a\) satisfying
\[
\delta_a(a)=1,
\qquad
\delta_a(b)=0\quad(b\neq a).
\]

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Topology becomes completely different over a finite field, and the topology can be made
explicit by polynomial indicator functions.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Use Fermat's identity \(u^{q-1}=1\) for \(u\neq0\), and consider a product of factors
\(1-(x_i-a_i)^{q-1}\).

\paragraph{Solution.}
The topology is discrete because every singleton is closed and \(k^n\) is finite.  Define
\[
\delta_a(x_1,\dots,x_n)
=\prod_{i=1}^n\left(1-(x_i-a_i)^{q-1}\right).
\]
At \(x=a\), every factor equals \(1\), so \(\delta_a(a)=1\).  If \(b\neq a\), then for
some \(i\), \(b_i-a_i\neq0\), so \((b_i-a_i)^{q-1}=1\) and that factor is zero.  Hence
\(\delta_a(b)=0\).

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Over a finite field, polynomial functions are rich enough to separate individual rational
points exactly, in sharp contrast with the cofinite topology over infinite fields.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Use the \(\delta_a\) to represent every function \(k^n\to k\) by a polynomial.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical problem extending the finite-field discussion and Chapter VI/01.
\fi

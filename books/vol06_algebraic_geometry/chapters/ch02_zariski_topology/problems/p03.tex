% Problem dossier: VI.02.P3
\subsection*{Problem VI.02.P3 --- Density of invertible and full-rank matrices}
\label{prob:vi02-p3}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Identify \(\mathbb A_{\mathbb C}^{mn}\) with the space of complex \(m\times n\) matrices.
Prove that \(GL_n(\mathbb C)\) is Zariski dense in \(\mathbb A_{\mathbb C}^{n^2}\).  More
generally, prove that the set of full-rank \(m\times n\) matrices is Zariski open and dense.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
It turns an elementary linear-algebra condition into a geometric statement: rank failure is
cut out by polynomial minors, while maximal rank is the generic topological behavior.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use determinant and minors as polynomials in matrix entries.  For density, it is enough to
find one nonempty principal open contained in the full-rank locus.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\textbf{Hint 1.} For square matrices, \(GL_n(\mathbb C)=D(\det)\).

\textbf{Hint 2.} The determinant polynomial is not zero: evaluate it at the identity matrix.

\textbf{Hint 3.} For rectangular matrices let \(r=\min(m,n)\).  The full-rank locus is the
union of the principal opens defined by the \(r\times r\) minors.  At least one such minor
is a nonzero polynomial.

\paragraph{Solution.}
For square matrices,
\[
GL_n(\mathbb C)=\set{M:\det M\neq0}=D(\det).
\]
The determinant is a nonzero polynomial because \(\det I_n=1\).  Since \(\mathbb C\) is
infinite, every nonzero principal open is dense, so \(GL_n(\mathbb C)\) is dense.

Now let \(r=\min(m,n)\).  A matrix has full rank exactly when at least one \(r\times r\)
minor is nonzero.  Hence the full-rank locus is
\[
\bigcup_{\Delta}D(\Delta),
\]
where \(\Delta\) runs over the maximal minors, and is therefore open.  Choose a maximal
minor, for example the top-left \(r\times r\) determinant.  It is a nonzero polynomial, as
one sees by evaluating it on a matrix containing an \(r\times r\) identity block.  Thus its
principal open is dense and lies inside the full-rank locus.  Any set containing a dense set
is dense, so the full-rank locus is dense.

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Rank drops are equation conditions; maximal rank is a nonvanishing condition.  Zariski
topology makes this algebraic dichotomy visible as closed versus open.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Show the rank-at-most-\(r\) locus is defined by all \((r+1)\)-minors.
\item For \(2\times2\) matrices, describe the determinant hypersurface geometrically.
\item Replace \(\mathbb C\) by an arbitrary infinite field.
\end{enumerate}

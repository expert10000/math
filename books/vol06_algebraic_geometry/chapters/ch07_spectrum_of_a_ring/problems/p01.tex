\begin{problem}[Legacy spectrum survey: four foundational spectra]
Describe the topological spaces
\[
\Spec\RR[x],\qquad
\Spec\CC[x,y],\qquad
\Spec\ZZ[x],\qquad
\Spec\FF_p[x].
\]
Identify their prime ideals and explain the principal specialization/closure relations.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the main legacy VI/07 problem.  It forces the abstract definition of $\Spec$ to work
across real, complex, arithmetic, and finite-field settings, and it exposes the difference
between ordinary rational points and all prime ideals.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use that $k[x]$ is a PID, $\CC[x,y]$ and $\ZZ[x]$ are UFDs, the weak Nullstellensatz over
$\CC$, Gauss's lemma, quotient prime correspondence, and the formula
$\overline{\{\mathfrak p\}}=V(\mathfrak p)$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Treat the four rings separately.  For $\ZZ[x]$, first split primes according to their
contraction to $\ZZ$: either $(0)$ or $(p)$.

\begin{solution}
For $\RR[x]$, the prime ideals are
\[
(0)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(f)
\]
with $f$ irreducible.  The irreducible real polynomials have degree one or two.  Thus the
nonzero points are $(x-a)$ and ideals generated by irreducible quadratics.  Since every
nonzero prime in the PID $\RR[x]$ is maximal, those points are closed, while
\[
\overline{\{(0)\}}=\Spec\RR[x].
\]
Every proper closed subset is finite.

For $\FF_p[x]$, exactly the same PID argument gives
\[
\Spec\FF_p[x]=\{(0)\}\cup\{(f):f\text{ monic irreducible}\}.
\]
Again every nonzero prime is maximal and closed, and every proper closed subset is finite.
Only degree-one primes are $\FF_p$-rational points; higher-degree primes record finite
extensions of $\FF_p$.

Now consider $\CC[x,y]$.  Its prime ideals occur in three levels:
\[
(0),
\]
the height-one primes
\[
(f)\qquad(f\text{ irreducible}),
\]
and the maximal ideals
\[
(x-a,y-b)\qquad(a,b\in\CC).
\]
Indeed $\CC[x,y]$ is a two-dimensional UFD, so every height-one prime is principal, and the
weak Nullstellensatz classifies the maximal ideals.  The closure relations are
\[
\overline{\{(0)\}}=\Spec\CC[x,y],
\]
\[
\overline{\{(f)\}}=V(f),
\]
and every maximal ideal is closed.  Thus the topology has a generic plane point, generic
points of irreducible curves, and ordinary closed points on those curves.

Finally let $A=\ZZ[x]$.  Let $P\in\Spec A$ and contract it to $\ZZ$.

If $P\cap\ZZ=(p)$ for a rational prime $p$, then $P$ corresponds to a prime of
\[
A/(p)\cong\FF_p[x].
\]
Hence either
\[
P=(p)
\]
or
\[
P=(p,g(x)),
\]
where the reduction $\bar g\in\FF_p[x]$ is irreducible.  The latter ideals are maximal.

If $P\cap\ZZ=(0)$, localize at $\ZZ\setminus\{0\}$ to obtain a prime of $\QQ[x]$.
Since $\QQ[x]$ is a PID, the extension is either $(0)$ or generated by an irreducible
polynomial.  In the first case $P=(0)$.  In the second case Gauss's lemma gives
\[
P=(f),
\]
where $f\in\ZZ[x]$ is primitive and irreducible over $\QQ$.

Therefore the points of $\Spec\ZZ[x]$ are
\[
\begin{aligned}
&(0),\qquad (p),\\
&(f)\quad &&\text{with $f$ primitive irreducible over $\QQ$},\\
&(p,g)\quad &&\text{with $\bar g$ irreducible in $\FF_p[x]$}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The maximal points $(p,g)$ are closed.  The closure of $(p)$ is the characteristic-$p$
fiber $V(p)$, and the closure of $(f)$ is $V(f)$.  The zero prime specializes to every
point.

These four spectra display the same general principle:
\[
\text{prime inclusion}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{specialization}.
\]
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
One-dimensional PIDs produce a generic point plus closed irreducible-polynomial points.
Two-dimensional rings introduce an intermediate layer of curve-like primes.  Arithmetic
coefficients create both horizontal primes $(f)$ and vertical primes $(p)$.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Draw the specialization diagram of $\Spec\ZZ[x]$ and compare its vertical fibers $V(p)$
with the generic characteristic-zero behavior.

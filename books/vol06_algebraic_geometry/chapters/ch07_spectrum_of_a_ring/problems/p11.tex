\begin{problem}[When is the whole spectrum irreducible?]
Prove that
\[
\Spec A\text{ is irreducible}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sqrt{(0)}\text{ is a prime ideal}.
\]
Deduce that every domain has irreducible spectrum, but the converse need not imply that $A$
itself is a domain.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This separates an intrinsic topological property of the spectrum from the stronger algebraic
condition of having no nilpotents.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
The whole spectrum is $V(0)=V(\sqrt0)$, and a closed set $V(I)$ is irreducible iff
$\sqrt I$ is prime.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Apply the irreducibility criterion to $I=(0)$.

\begin{solution}
By the irreducible-closed-set criterion,
\[
V(0)\text{ irreducible}
\iff
\sqrt{(0)}\text{ prime}.
\]
Since $V(0)=\Spec A$, this is the desired equivalence.

If $A$ is a domain, then $\sqrt{(0)}=(0)$ is prime, so $\Spec A$ is irreducible.
The converse fails for
\[
A=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\]
Its nilradical $(\bar\varepsilon)$ is prime because the quotient is $k$, so its spectrum is
irreducible, but $A$ is not a domain because $\bar\varepsilon^2=0$ with
$\bar\varepsilon\neq0$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The topology sees the reduced quotient.  Nilpotent information can disappear without changing
irreducibility of the underlying spectrum.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
VI/10 will turn this observation into the homeomorphism
$\Spec(A_{\mathrm{red}})\cong\Spec A$.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New synthesis of legacy irreducibility and nonreduced warnings, with systematic scheme
structure deferred to VI/10.

\begin{problem}[Closure of the image of a spectrum map]
For a ring homomorphism $\varphi:A\to B$, prove
\[
\overline{\varphi^\ast(\Spec B)}
=
V_A(\ker\varphi).
\]
Deduce that an injective ring map induces a dense map on spectra.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Surjectivity of a spectrum map is often too strong; density is the correct first topological
shadow of injectivity on rings.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the arbitrary-subset closure formula and the nilradical identity
\[
\sqrt{(0)_B}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak q\in\Spec B}\mathfrak q.
\]

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Compute the intersection of all contracted primes.

\begin{solution}
The ideal attached to the image is
\[
\bigcap_{\mathfrak q\in\Spec B}\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)
=
\varphi^{-1}\!\left(\bigcap_{\mathfrak q\in\Spec B}\mathfrak q\right)
=
\varphi^{-1}(\sqrt{(0)_B}).
\]
Now
\[
a\in\varphi^{-1}(\sqrt{(0)_B})
\]
iff $\varphi(a)$ is nilpotent, iff $\varphi(a^n)=0$ for some $n$, iff
$a\in\sqrt{\ker\varphi}$.  Therefore the closure is
\[
V_A(\sqrt{\ker\varphi})=V_A(\ker\varphi).
\]
If $\varphi$ is injective, this is $V_A(0)=\Spec A$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The image may omit points, but its closure is controlled exactly by the kernel.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Find examples of injective maps for which the induced spectrum map is not surjective.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical synthesis; the required ingredients are distributed through the legacy Spec
and nilradical discussions.

\begin{problem}[Product rings and disjoint spectra]
Prove that
\[
\Spec(A\times B)\cong\Spec A\sqcup\Spec B
\]
as topological spaces, not merely as sets.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
VI/05 connected idempotents with product decompositions.  Here the same algebra becomes an
explicit topological decomposition of the spectrum.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the orthogonal idempotents $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Every prime contains one of those idempotents.  Then identify closed sets on the two prime
families.

\begin{solution}
If $P$ is prime in $A\times B$, then
\[
(1,0)(0,1)=0\in P,
\]
so one of $(1,0),(0,1)$ belongs to $P$.  If $(0,1)\in P$, then
\[
P=\mathfrak p\times B
\]
for a unique $\mathfrak p\in\Spec A$; the other case gives
\[
P=A\times\mathfrak q.
\]
Thus the underlying set is the disjoint union of the two spectra.

The two families are closed:
\[
V(0\times B)=\{\mathfrak p\times B\},
\qquad
V(A\times0)=\{A\times\mathfrak q\}.
\]
They are complementary, hence also open.  On each family, ideal containment and the closed
sets are exactly those of the corresponding factor spectrum.  Therefore the bijection is a
homeomorphism with $\Spec A\sqcup\Spec B$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A product decomposition of rings becomes a clopen decomposition of spectra.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Generalize to a finite product $\prod_{i=1}^r A_i$.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
Topological continuation of the VI/05 legacy idempotent/product problem; no legacy problem is
duplicated here.

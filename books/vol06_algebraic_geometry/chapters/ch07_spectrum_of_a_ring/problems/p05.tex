\begin{problem}[The spectrum of two crossing axes]
Let
\[
R=k[x,y]/(xy).
\]
Describe $\Spec R$ as completely as possible in terms of its two prime families and their
specialization relations.  Identify its irreducible components and the behavior of the
origin.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
The crossing axes are the standard model where two irreducible components coexist inside
one connected affine spectrum.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Every prime contains $\bar x$ or $\bar y$ because $\bar x\bar y=0$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Use
\[
R/(\bar x)\cong k[y],
\qquad
R/(\bar y)\cong k[x].
\]

\begin{solution}
If $P\in\Spec R$, primality and $\bar x\bar y=0$ imply
\[
\bar x\in P\quad\text{or}\quad\bar y\in P.
\]
Primes containing $\bar x$ correspond to primes of $k[y]$; primes containing $\bar y$
correspond to primes of $k[x]$.

If $k$ is algebraically closed, these families are
\[
(\bar x),\quad(\bar x,\bar y-b),
\]
and
\[
(\bar y),\quad(\bar y,\bar x-a).
\]
The minimal primes $(\bar x)$ and $(\bar y)$ are the generic points of the two irreducible
components
\[
V(\bar x),\qquad V(\bar y).
\]
The origin
\[
(\bar x,\bar y)
\]
belongs to both components and is a specialization of both minimal primes.  Other closed
points lie on exactly one component.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A reducible ring does not produce a disjoint union unless idempotents split the ring.
Here the two components meet, and the shared specialization records that intersection.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Compare with $\Spec(k[x]\times k[y])$, where the two components are actually disjoint.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New topological synthesis of legacy crossing-axis examples; the legacy connectedness problem
itself remains canonically in VI/05.

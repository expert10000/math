\begin{problem}[Arithmetic spectra: $\Spec\ZZ$ and $\Spec(\ZZ/n\ZZ)$]
Describe every closed subset of $\Spec\ZZ$.  Then for $n\ge2$ determine
$\Spec(\ZZ/n\ZZ)$, its topology, and the map to $\Spec\ZZ$ induced by the quotient.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the simplest example in which algebraic geometry directly encodes arithmetic
divisibility.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
All ideals of $\ZZ$ are principal, and quotient prime correspondence identifies primes of
$\ZZ/n\ZZ$ with primes of $\ZZ$ containing $(n)$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Compute $V(n)$ in $\Spec\ZZ$ first.

\begin{solution}
The points of $\Spec\ZZ$ are $(0)$ and $(p)$ for rational primes $p$.  For $n\neq0$,
\[
V(n)=\{(p):p\mid n\}.
\]
Thus the proper closed sets are exactly finite sets of prime-number points, together with
$\varnothing$; the whole space is $V(0)$.

Primes of $\ZZ/n\ZZ$ correspond to prime ideals $(p)$ containing $(n)$, hence to prime
divisors $p$ of $n$.  All are maximal, so every point is closed.  Because the spectrum is
finite, it is discrete.

The quotient map
\[
\ZZ\twoheadrightarrow\ZZ/n\ZZ
\]
induces a homeomorphism
\[
\Spec(\ZZ/n\ZZ)\cong V(n)\subseteq\Spec\ZZ.
\]
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The prime divisors of an integer are literally the closed points of its vanishing locus in
the arithmetic spectrum.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Compare $\Spec(\ZZ/p^r\ZZ)$ for different $r\ge1$ topologically, then defer the
scheme-structure difference to VI/10.

\begin{problem}[Closed-set calculus and radical information]
Starting only from
\[
V(I)=\{\mathfrak p\in\Spec A:I\subseteq\mathfrak p\},
\]
prove the complete Zariski closed-set calculus and show that the topology determines
$\sqrt I$ from $V(I)$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
The Zariski topology is not an external topology placed on the prime ideals; it is forced by
ideal containment.  This problem isolates that mechanism.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use primality for finite unions and intersections of containing primes for radicals.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
First prove $V(IJ)=V(I)\cup V(J)$ and
$V(\sum I_\lambda)=\bigcap V(I_\lambda)$.  Then intersect all primes in $V(I)$.

\begin{solution}
The extreme cases are
\[
V(0)=\Spec A,\qquad V(A)=\varnothing.
\]
For a prime $\mathfrak p$,
\[
IJ\subseteq\mathfrak p
\iff
I\subseteq\mathfrak p\ \text{or}\ J\subseteq\mathfrak p,
\]
so
\[
V(IJ)=V(I)\cup V(J).
\]
Since $IJ\subseteq I\cap J$ and every prime containing $I\cap J$ contains $IJ$,
\[
V(I\cap J)=V(IJ).
\]
Also
\[
\sum_\lambda I_\lambda\subseteq\mathfrak p
\iff
I_\lambda\subseteq\mathfrak p\quad\forall\lambda,
\]
giving
\[
V\!\left(\sum_\lambda I_\lambda\right)=\bigcap_\lambda V(I_\lambda).
\]
Thus the $V(I)$ define the closed sets of a topology.

Finally,
\[
\bigcap_{\mathfrak p\in V(I)}\mathfrak p=\sqrt I.
\]
Therefore $V(I)$ determines $\sqrt I$, and
\[
V(I)=V(J)\iff\sqrt I=\sqrt J.
\]
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Finite unions are controlled by multiplication of ideals, arbitrary intersections by sums,
and the topology forgets exactly the difference between an ideal and its radical.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Reformulate every step as an order-reversing relation between the lattice of radical ideals
and the lattice of closed subsets.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical synthesis from the legacy Spec/Zariski sections of AG1 and CA13.

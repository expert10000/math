\begin{problem}[Recover the radical ideal lattice from the topology]
Show that the assignments
\[
I\longmapsto V(I),
\qquad
Z\longmapsto I(Z):=\bigcap_{\mathfrak p\in Z}\mathfrak p
\]
restrict to mutually inverse order-reversing bijections between radical ideals of $A$ and
closed subsets of $\Spec A$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This identifies exactly how much of the ideal theory of $A$ is encoded by the topology alone.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
For a closed set $Z=V(J)$,
\[
I(Z)=\sqrt J.
\]

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Check the two composites separately, remembering that $I$ is assumed radical in one direction.

\begin{solution}
If $I$ is radical, then
\[
I(V(I))
=
\bigcap_{\mathfrak p\supseteq I}\mathfrak p
=
\sqrt I
=
I.
\]
If $Z$ is closed, write $Z=V(J)$.  Then
\[
V(I(Z))
=
V(\sqrt J)
=
V(J)
=
Z.
\]
Both assignments reverse inclusion: $I\subseteq J$ implies $V(J)\subseteq V(I)$, while
$Z_1\subseteq Z_2$ implies $I(Z_2)\subseteq I(Z_1)$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The closed-set lattice of the spectrum is precisely the opposite of the radical-ideal lattice.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Ask which additional ring-theoretic information is lost when one remembers only this lattice;
nilpotent structure is the first answer.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical synthesis from the legacy radical/closed-set dictionary.

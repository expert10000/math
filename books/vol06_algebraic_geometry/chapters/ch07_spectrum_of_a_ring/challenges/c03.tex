\begin{problem}[Challenge VI.07 - Complex points over the real spectrum]
Analyze the continuous map
\[
\Spec\CC[t]\longrightarrow\Spec\RR[t]
\]
induced by $\RR[t]\hookrightarrow\CC[t]$.  Determine every contraction and prove the map is
surjective.  Explain why two conjugate complex closed points can map to one real closed point.
\end{problem}
\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Contract $(t-z)$ by finding the minimal polynomial of $z$ over $\RR$.
\begin{solution}
The generic point $(0)$ contracts to $(0)$.  If $z=a\in\RR$, then
\[
(t-a)\cap\RR[t]=(t-a).
\]
If $z=a+bi$ with $b\neq0$, a real polynomial vanishes at $z$ iff it is divisible by the
irreducible quadratic
\[
m_z(t)=(t-z)(t-\bar z)=t^2-2at+(a^2+b^2).
\]
Hence
\[
(t-z)\cap\RR[t]=(m_z).
\]
The conjugate point $(t-\bar z)$ has the same contraction.

Every prime of $\RR[t]$ is $(0)$, a real linear prime, or an irreducible quadratic prime.
Every irreducible quadratic splits over $\CC$ into a conjugate pair.  Therefore every point
of $\Spec\RR[t]$ has a preimage, so the map is surjective.
\end{solution}
\paragraph{Extension.}
Interpret this later as the topological shadow of base change from $\RR$ to $\CC$.
\paragraph{Provenance.}
New synthesis of legacy field-dependence and prime-splitting examples.

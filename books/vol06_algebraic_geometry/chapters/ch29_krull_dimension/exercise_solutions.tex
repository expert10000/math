% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-01
\paragraph{Solution to E1.}
The chain
\[
\mathfrak p_0\subsetneq\mathfrak p_1\subsetneq\mathfrak p_2
\]
contains three prime ideals but only two strict inclusions.  By definition, the length of a prime
chain is the number of strict inclusions.  Hence its length is
\[
\boxed{2}.
\]

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-02
\paragraph{Solution to E2.}
A field \(k\) has exactly one prime ideal, namely \((0)\).  Therefore there cannot be a strict
inclusion between two distinct prime ideals.  The only prime chains have length \(0\), so
\[
\boxed{\dim k=0}.
\]

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-03
\paragraph{Solution to E3.}
The prime ideals of \(\mathbb Z\) are
\[
(0)\quad\text{and}\quad(p)
\]
for rational primes \(p\).  Every nonzero prime \((p)\) is maximal.  Thus
\[
(0)\subsetneq(p)
\]
is a prime chain of length \(1\), while no chain can continue past \((p)\).  Hence
\[
\boxed{\dim\mathbb Z=1}.
\]

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-04
\paragraph{Solution to E4.}
The ring \(k[x]\) is a PID.  Its prime ideals are \((0)\) and the ideals \((f)\) generated by
irreducible polynomials \(f\).  Each nonzero prime \((f)\) is maximal because
\(k[x]/(f)\) is a field.  Therefore every nontrivial prime chain has the form
\[
(0)\subsetneq(f)
\]
and has length \(1\).  Hence
\[
\boxed{\dim k[x]=1}.
\]

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-05
\paragraph{Solution to E5.}
In \(k[x,y]\),
\[
(0)\subsetneq(x)\subsetneq(x,y)
\]
is a chain of length \(2\).  Each ideal is prime:
\[
k[x,y]/(x)\cong k[y]
\]
is a domain, while
\[
k[x,y]/(x,y)\cong k
\]
is a field.  Thus \(\dim k[x,y]\ge2\); the polynomial-ring dimension theorem gives equality.

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-06
\paragraph{Solution to E6.}
For \(\mathfrak p\in\Spec A\), its height is
\[
\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)
=
\sup\left\{
n:
\mathfrak p_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak p_n=\mathfrak p
\right\}.
\]
Thus height counts how many strict specialization steps can occur below \(\mathfrak p\).  Equivalently,
\[
\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)=\dim A_{\mathfrak p}.
\]

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-07
\paragraph{Solution to E7.}
Prime ideals of \(S^{-1}A\) correspond bijectively to prime ideals
\(\mathfrak p\subseteq A\) satisfying
\[
\mathfrak p\cap S=\varnothing,
\]
via
\[
\mathfrak p\longmapsto S^{-1}\mathfrak p.
\]
This correspondence preserves strict inclusions.  Therefore every prime chain in \(S^{-1}A\) comes
from a prime chain of the same length in \(A\).  Hence
\[
\boxed{\dim S^{-1}A\le\dim A}.
\]

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-08
\paragraph{Solution to E8.}
Prime ideals of \(A/I\) correspond to prime ideals \(\mathfrak p\subseteq A\) containing \(I\):
\[
\mathfrak p/I\longleftrightarrow\mathfrak p.
\]
The correspondence preserves strict inclusions.  Hence every chain in \(A/I\) is a chain in \(A\)
restricted to primes containing \(I\).  Therefore
\[
\boxed{\dim(A/I)\le\dim A}.
\]

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-09
\paragraph{Solution to E9.}
Every prime ideal of \(A\) contains the nilradical
\[
\sqrt{(0)}.
\]
Hence
\[
\Spec A\longrightarrow\Spec(A/\sqrt{(0)})
\]
induces an order-preserving bijection on prime ideals:
\[
\mathfrak p\longleftrightarrow\mathfrak p/\sqrt{(0)}.
\]
Prime-chain lengths are therefore identical, so
\[
\boxed{\dim A=\dim A_{\mathrm{red}}}.
\]

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-10
\paragraph{Solution to E10.}
Every prime ideal of \(A\times B\) is exactly one of
\[
\mathfrak p\times B
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
A\times\mathfrak q.
\]
A chain cannot move from one family to the other, since primes from different components are
incomparable.  Thus every chain lies entirely in \(\Spec A\) or entirely in \(\Spec B\), giving
\[
\boxed{\dim(A\times B)=\max\{\dim A,\dim B\}}.
\]

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-11
\paragraph{Solution to E11.}
Let
\[
A=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\]
Its nilradical is \((\varepsilon)\), and
\[
A_{\mathrm{red}}=A/(\varepsilon)\cong k.
\]
By invariance of dimension under reduction,
\[
\dim A=\dim k=0.
\]
Equivalently, \(A\) has only the prime ideal \((\varepsilon)\).

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-12
\paragraph{Solution to E12.}
Put
\[
A=k[x,y]/(xy).
\]
Its minimal primes are
\[
(x)/(xy)\qquad\text{and}\qquad(y)/(xy).
\]
The corresponding irreducible components have coordinate rings
\[
A/(x)\cong k[y],
\qquad
A/(y)\cong k[x],
\]
both of dimension \(1\).  Every prime chain lies above one of the minimal primes, so
\[
\boxed{\dim A=1}.
\]

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-13
\paragraph{Solution to E13.}
In \(A=k[x,y]\), the ideal \((x)\) is prime.  The chain
\[
(0)\subsetneq(x)
\]
shows
\[
\operatorname{ht}(x)\ge1.
\]
By the principal ideal theorem, a prime minimal over a principal ideal in a Noetherian ring has
height at most \(1\).  Since \((x)\) is itself minimal over \((x)\),
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{ht}(x)=1}.
\]

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-14
\paragraph{Solution to E14.}
The chain
\[
(0)\subsetneq(x)\subsetneq(x,y)
\]
shows
\[
\operatorname{ht}(x,y)\ge2.
\]
Since
\[
\dim k[x,y]=2,
\]
no prime can have height greater than \(2\).  Hence
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{ht}(x,y)=2}.
\]

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-15
\paragraph{Solution to E15.}
Let
\[
A=k[x_1,x_2,\ldots].
\]
For every \(n\ge1\),
\[
(0)\subsetneq(x_1)\subsetneq(x_1,x_2)\subsetneq\cdots
\subsetneq(x_1,\ldots,x_n)
\]
is a chain of length \(n\).  Each ideal is prime because the quotient is again a polynomial ring
over \(k\), hence a domain.  Since chain lengths are unbounded,
\[
\boxed{\dim A=\infty}.
\]

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-16
\paragraph{Solution to E16.}
For a domain \(A\), its normalization \(\overline A\subseteq\Frac(A)\) is integral over \(A\).
Integral extensions preserve Krull dimension: going up lifts chains from \(A\), while
incomparability ensures contraction of a strict chain upstairs remains strict.  Therefore
\[
\boxed{\dim\overline A=\dim A}.
\]

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-17
\paragraph{Solution to E17.}
The principal open
\[
D(f)=\Spec A_f
\]
contains exactly those primes \(\mathfrak p\) of \(A\) with \(f\notin\mathfrak p\).  Hence
\[
\dim A_f
\]
is the supremum of lengths of prime chains
\[
\mathfrak p_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak p_n
\]
all lying in \(D(f)\).  Thus localization computes the Krull dimension of that affine open.

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-18
\paragraph{Solution to E18.}
The closed subscheme
\[
V(I)=\Spec(A/I)
\]
has points corresponding to primes of \(A\) containing \(I\).  Therefore
\[
\dim(A/I)
\]
is the supremum of lengths of chains of irreducible closed subsets lying inside \(V(I)\), equivalently
of chains of primes
\[
I\subseteq\mathfrak p_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak p_n.
\]

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-19
\paragraph{Solution to E19.}
For points \(\mathfrak p,\mathfrak q\in\Spec A\),
\[
\mathfrak q\in\overline{\{\mathfrak p\}}
\]
if and only if
\[
\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak q.
\]
Thus \(\mathfrak q\) is a specialization of \(\mathfrak p\) exactly when
\[
\boxed{\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak q}.
\]
Prime chains therefore record successive specialization steps.

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-20
\paragraph{Solution to E20.}
The prime chain
\[
(0)\subsetneq(x)\subsetneq(x,y)
\]
reverses under \(V(-)\):
\[
V(x,y)\subsetneq V(x)\subsetneq V(0).
\]
Geometrically, in \(\mathbb A^2_k\), this is
\[
\{\text{origin}\}
\subsetneq
\{x=0\}
\subsetneq
\mathbb A^2_k.
\]
The length remains \(2\).

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-21
\paragraph{Solution to E21.}
The classical polynomial-ring dimension theorem gives
\[
\boxed{\dim k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]=n}.
\]
The lower bound comes from the coordinate-prime chain
\[
(0)\subsetneq(x_1)\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq(x_1,\ldots,x_n),
\]
which has length \(n\); the theorem supplies the matching upper bound.

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-22
\paragraph{Solution to E22.}
In a nonzero Artinian ring every prime ideal is maximal.  Therefore two distinct prime ideals cannot
occur in a strict inclusion
\[
\mathfrak p\subsetneq\mathfrak q,
\]
because \(\mathfrak p\) would already be maximal.  Hence all prime chains have length \(0\), so
\[
\boxed{\dim A=0}.
\]

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-23
\paragraph{Solution to E23.}
No.  Noetherianity means the ascending chain condition on ideals, but it does not force a uniform
finite bound on the lengths of \emph{prime} chains occurring in different parts of the spectrum.
There exist Noetherian rings of infinite Krull dimension (classically, examples of Nagata).
Thus
\[
\text{Noetherian}\not\Longrightarrow\text{finite Krull dimension}.
\]
Finite type over a field is a much stronger hypothesis and does imply finite dimension.

% VI29-DETAILED-EXERCISE-24
\paragraph{Solution to E24.}
A central theorem deliberately deferred to VI/30 is the finite-type integral formula
\[
\boxed{\dim X=\operatorname{trdeg}_kK(X)}
\]
for an integral scheme \(X\) of finite type over a field \(k\).  VI/29 uses the polynomial-ring
dimension theorem but does not yet globalize dimension or prove the full variety formula.

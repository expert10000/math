\begin{problem}[VI.29.P1: Prime-chain definition]\label{prob:vi29-01}
State the Krull-dimension definition carefully and explain why a chain with \(n+1\) primes has length \(n\).

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical VI/29 development.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
For a nonzero ring \(A\),
\[
\dim A
=
\sup\left\{
n:
\mathfrak p_0\subsetneq\mathfrak p_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak p_n
\text{ are prime}
\right\}.
\]
The displayed chain contains \(n+1\) prime ideals but exactly \(n\) strict inclusions:
\[
\mathfrak p_0\subsetneq\mathfrak p_1,\quad
\mathfrak p_1\subsetneq\mathfrak p_2,\quad\ldots,\quad
\mathfrak p_{n-1}\subsetneq\mathfrak p_n.
\]
Hence its length is \(n\).  This convention is geometrically natural: a chain
of three nested irreducible closed subsets has two strict specialization steps, hence length \(2\).
\end{solution}
\fi

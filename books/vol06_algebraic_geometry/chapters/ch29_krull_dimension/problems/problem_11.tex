\begin{problem}[VI.29.P11: Dimension of a product]\label{prob:vi29-11}
Prove \(\dim(A\times B)=\max\{\dim A,\dim B\}\).

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical VI/29 development.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Every prime ideal of \(A\times B\) has one of the forms
\[
\mathfrak p\times B
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
A\times\mathfrak q.
\]
These are the two connected components of \(\Spec(A\times B)=\Spec A\sqcup\Spec B\).  A strict chain cannot jump
between the two families because a prime from one family is incomparable with a prime from the other.  Thus every
prime chain has length at most \(\dim A\) or at most \(\dim B\), so
\[
\dim(A\times B)\le\max\{\dim A,\dim B\}.
\]
Conversely, any prime chain in \(A\) gives one in \(A\times B\) via \(\mathfrak p\mapsto\mathfrak p\times B\),
and similarly for \(B\).  Hence the reverse inequality holds, proving
\[
\boxed{\dim(A\times B)=\max\{\dim A,\dim B\}}.
\]
\end{solution}
\fi

\begin{problem}[VI.29.P2: Affine chain dictionary]\label{prob:vi29-02}
Prove that prime chains in \(A\) and irreducible-closed chains in \(\Spec A\) have the same possible lengths.

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical VI/29 development.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Every irreducible closed subset of \(\Spec A\) is uniquely of the form
\[
V(\mathfrak p)
\]
for a prime ideal \(\mathfrak p\).  Moreover,
\[
\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak q
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
V(\mathfrak q)\subseteq V(\mathfrak p).
\]
Strict inclusion is also reversed strictly.

Thus a prime chain
\[
\mathfrak p_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak p_n
\]
corresponds to
\[
V(\mathfrak p_n)\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq V(\mathfrak p_0),
\]
with the same \(n\) strict inclusions.  Conversely every irreducible-closed chain arises uniquely this way.
Therefore the supremum of prime-chain lengths equals the supremum of irreducible-closed-chain lengths.
\end{solution}
\fi

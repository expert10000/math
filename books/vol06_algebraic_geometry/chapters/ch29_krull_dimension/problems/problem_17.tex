\begin{problem}[VI.29.P17: Component dimensions]\label{prob:vi29-17}
Let \(\mathfrak p_1,\ldots,\mathfrak p_r\) be the minimal primes of a ring with finitely many minimal primes. Explain why \(\dim A=\max_i\dim(A/\mathfrak p_i)\).

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical VI/29 development.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Every prime ideal \(\mathfrak q\subseteq A\) contains at least one minimal prime \(\mathfrak p_i\).  Take any
prime chain
\[
\mathfrak q_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak q_n.
\]
Choose a minimal prime \(\mathfrak p_i\subseteq\mathfrak q_0\).  Then \(\mathfrak p_i\subseteq\mathfrak q_j\) for
all \(j\), so quotienting by \(\mathfrak p_i\) gives a strict chain of the same length in
\[
A/\mathfrak p_i.
\]
Therefore
\[
\dim A\le\max_i\dim(A/\mathfrak p_i).
\]

Conversely, any prime chain in \(A/\mathfrak p_i\) corresponds to a prime chain in \(A\) consisting of primes
containing \(\mathfrak p_i\).  Hence
\[
\dim(A/\mathfrak p_i)\le\dim A
\]
for every \(i\).  Taking the maximum gives equality:
\[
\boxed{\dim A=\max_i\dim(A/\mathfrak p_i)}.
\]
\end{solution}
\fi

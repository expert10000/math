\subsection*{Challenge VI.22.C1: Classify closed subschemes of a one-point affine scheme}
% VI22-CHALLENGE-FULL-01
\begin{solution}
Let $(A,\mathfrak m)$ be local Artinian and suppose $\Spec A$ has one point.  Since $A$ is
Artinian, every prime ideal is maximal, so the unique prime is $\mathfrak m$.  Every ideal
$I\subseteq A$ determines an affine closed subscheme
\[
 Z_I=\Spec(A/I)\hookrightarrow\Spec A.
\]
Conversely, every affine closed subscheme of $\Spec A$ is obtained from a unique ideal in this
way, because a closed immersion into an affine scheme is induced by a surjection
$A\twoheadrightarrow B$, and then $B\cong A/\ker(A\to B)$.

If $I=A$, then $A/I=0$ and the closed subscheme is empty.  Suppose $I\neq A$.  Then
$A/I$ is again an Artinian local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m/I$; therefore
$\Spec(A/I)$ again has exactly one point.  Thus every nonempty $Z_I$ has the same underlying
one-point topological space, although its scheme structure can vary greatly.

For example, if
\[
 A=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^n),
\]
the ideals $(\varepsilon^r)$ produce quotients
$k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^r)$ of different nilpotency lengths.  All nonzero quotients have
one point, but their local rings are nonisomorphic in general.  Hence even on a one-point
space the lattice of closed subschemes can retain a rich infinitesimal structure.
\end{solution}

\subsection*{Challenge VI.22.C2: Reduction is global}
% VI22-CHALLENGE-FULL-02
\begin{solution}
Take an affine open cover $X=\bigcup_i U_i$ with $U_i=\Spec A_i$.  On each chart define
\[
 (U_i)_{\mathrm{red}}
 =\Spec(A_i/N_i),
 \qquad
 N_i=\sqrt{(0)_{A_i}}.
\]
To glue these reductions, we must show that they agree on overlaps.  By VI/20, refine each
overlap $U_i\cap U_j$ by opens that are distinguished in both charts.  It therefore suffices
to compare reduction after localization.

Let $A$ be a ring and $f\in A$.  The nilradical of $A_f$ is
\[
 \sqrt{(0)_{A_f}}=N A_f,
 \qquad N=\sqrt{(0)_A}.
\]
Indeed, if $a/f^r$ is nilpotent, then $(a/f^r)^m=0$ for some $m$, so after clearing a power of
$f$ one has $f^s a^m=0$ in $A$.  Hence $(f^s a)^m=0$, so $f^s a\in N$, and
$a/f^r\in NA_f$.  The reverse inclusion is immediate because localization preserves
nilpotence.  Consequently
\[
 (A_f)_{\mathrm{red}}
 =A_f/NA_f
 \cong (A/N)_f.
\]
Thus the reductions constructed from two affine charts coincide canonically on every common
distinguished refinement.  The cocycle condition is inherited from the original transition
maps, so VI/20 glues the affine reductions to a global scheme $X_{\mathrm{red}}\to X$.

Now choose a different affine cover.  Passing to a common affine refinement, the two
constructions have identical restrictions there, hence are uniquely isomorphic by the gluing
uniqueness theorem.  Therefore the global reduction is independent of the chosen affine cover.
\end{solution}

\subsection*{Challenge VI.22.C3: Closed structures with fixed support}
% VI22-CHALLENGE-FULL-03
\begin{solution}
Every ideal $I\subseteq A$ defines the affine closed subscheme
\[
 Z_I=\Spec(A/I)\hookrightarrow X=\Spec A
\]
with support $|Z_I|=V(I)$.  We ask when this support equals the prescribed closed subset
$V(J)$.  The Zariski identity
\[
 V(I)=V(J)
 \iff \sqrt I=\sqrt J
\]
gives the classification: the closed subscheme $Z_I=\Spec(A/I)$ has the prescribed support
$V(J)$ exactly when
\[
 \sqrt I=\sqrt J.
\]
Thus embedded closed subscheme structures on this fixed support are specified by ideals with
that radical.  Different ideals can occasionally give isomorphic abstract schemes, but as
subschemes of the fixed ambient $\Spec A$ the defining quotient map records the ideal.

The reduced induced structure corresponds to the largest ideal among those with this radical,
namely $\sqrt J$:
\[
 V(J)_{\mathrm{red}}=\Spec(A/\sqrt J).
\]
Smaller ideals with the same radical give thicker structures.  For instance on
$\mathbb A^1_k$ with support $V(x)$, the ideals
\[
 (x),\ (x^2),\ (x^3),\ldots
\]
all have radical $(x)$ and hence the same point support, but they encode successively thicker
nilpotent neighborhoods.
\end{solution}

\subsection*{Challenge VI.22.C4: Immersions are locally quotients of localizations}
% VI22-CHALLENGE-FULL-04
\begin{solution}
Let $i:Z\to X=\Spec A$ be an immersion.  By definition, $i$ factors as
\[
 Z\xrightarrow{k}U\xrightarrow{j}X,
\]
where $j$ is an open immersion and $k$ is a closed immersion.  Identify $U$ with an open
subscheme of $\Spec A$.

Fix $z\in Z$ and let $x=i(z)\in U$.  Distinguished opens form a basis of $\Spec A$, so choose
$f\in A$ such that
\[
 x\in D(f)\subseteq U.
\]
Then
\[
 D(f)\cong\Spec A_f.
\]
Restrict the closed immersion $k:Z\to U$ to the open neighbourhood $D(f)$.  The inverse image
\[
 Z_f:=i^{-1}(D(f))
\]
is a closed subscheme of $D(f)$.  By the affine description of closed subschemes, after perhaps
shrinking further around $z$ if one only wants a local presentation, there is an ideal
$J\subseteq A_f$ such that
\[
 Z_f\cong\Spec(A_f/J),
\]
and the restricted immersion is induced by the surjection
\[
 \boxed{A_f\twoheadrightarrow A_f/J.}
\]
More generally, writing $B=A_f/J$, this is a surjection $A_f\twoheadrightarrow B$.

Thus every immersion into an affine scheme is locally obtained in two algebraic stages:
first invert a function to pass to an open chart, then quotient by an ideal to impose the
closed equations.  This is the affine algebra behind the factorization
``closed immersion followed by open immersion.''
\end{solution}

\subsection*{Challenge VI.22.C5: Pointwise versus scheme-theoretic vanishing}
% VI22-CHALLENGE-FULL-05
\begin{solution}
Let $\alpha:A\to B$ correspond to
\[
 f:\Spec B\longrightarrow\Spec A.
\]
First suppose every point of $\Spec B$ maps into $V(I)$.  For every prime
$\mathfrak q\subseteq B$, its image is $\alpha^{-1}(\mathfrak q)$, so
\[
 I\subseteq\alpha^{-1}(\mathfrak q).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 \alpha(I)\subseteq\mathfrak q
 \qquad\text{for every }\mathfrak q\in\Spec B.
\]
Intersecting all primes gives
\[
 \alpha(I)\subseteq\sqrt{(0)_B}.
\]
Thus pointwise containment means precisely that all elements of $I$ pull back to nilpotents.

By contrast, $f$ factors through the closed subscheme $\Spec(A/I)$ exactly when the ring map
$\alpha$ factors through $A/I$, and the quotient universal property says this is equivalent to
\[
 \alpha(I)=0.
\]
Therefore scheme-theoretic vanishing is strictly stronger unless $B$ is reduced.

For a concrete example, take
\[
 A=k[t],
 \qquad
 B=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2),
 \qquad
 \alpha(t)=\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 I=(t).
\]
The spectrum of $B$ has one prime, $(\varepsilon)$.  Since $\varepsilon$ belongs to that prime,
the unique point maps to the origin $V(t)\subseteq\mathbb A^1_k$.  However
$\alpha(t)=\varepsilon\neq0$, so $\alpha$ does not factor through
$k[t]/(t)\cong k$.  Hence the point map lands in the closed support while the scheme morphism
does not factor through the reduced closed subscheme supported there.
\end{solution}

\subsection*{VI.22.P10: Universal property of reduction}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi22-p10}
Let $T$ be reduced.  Prove every morphism $T\to X$ factors uniquely through $X_{\rm red}$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
% VI22-FULL-P10
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This characterizes reduction intrinsically and proves it is independent of the affine cover used to construct it.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
First prove the affine statement: maps into a reduced ring kill nilpotents.  Then use affine covers and uniqueness to glue.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item On $U=\Spec A\subseteq X$, restrict the morphism to inverse images of affine opens in $T$.
\item A ring map $A\to B$ with $B$ reduced kills $\sqrt{(0)_A}$.
\item Use uniqueness of quotient factorization on overlaps.
\item Glue the local factorizations.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
First suppose $X=\Spec A$ and $T=\Spec B$ is affine and reduced.  A morphism
$T\to X$ corresponds to a ring map $\alpha:A\to B$.  If $a\in\sqrt{(0)_A}$, then
$a^n=0$ for some $n$, so
\[
 \alpha(a)^n=\alpha(a^n)=0.
\]
Since $B$ is reduced, $\alpha(a)=0$.  Hence $\sqrt{(0)_A}\subseteq\ker\alpha$, and the quotient
universal property gives a unique factorization
\[
 A\longrightarrow A/\sqrt{(0)_A}\longrightarrow B.
\]
Contravariantly, the morphism factors uniquely through $X_{\rm red}$.

For general $X$ and reduced $T$, take affine opens $U_\alpha=\Spec A_\alpha$ in $X$ and cover
$f^{-1}(U_\alpha)$ by affine opens of $T$.  Every coordinate ring on such an affine open is
reduced, so the affine argument gives a factorization through
$(U_\alpha)_{\rm red}$.  On overlaps the factorizations agree because quotient factorization
is unique.  They therefore glue to a morphism $T\to X_{\rm red}$.  Uniqueness can be checked
on the same open cover, so the global factorization is unique.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Reduction is universal for maps from reduced schemes.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Deduce immediately that reduction is idempotent.

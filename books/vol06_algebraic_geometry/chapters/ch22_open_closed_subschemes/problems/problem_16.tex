\subsection*{VI.22.P16: Closed immersions compose}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi22-p16}
Prove the composite of two closed immersions is a closed immersion.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
% VI22-FULL-P16
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Closed subschemes must remain closed when one imposes equations inside an already closed subscheme.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Work affine-locally on the final target.  Closed immersions there correspond to surjective ring maps, and composites of surjections are surjective.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Choose an affine target chart $\Spec A$.
\item Write the first closed immersion as $\Spec(A/I)\to\Spec A$.
\item Write the second locally as a further quotient.
\item Compose the corresponding surjections.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Let $Z\xrightarrow{i}Y\xrightarrow{j}X$ be closed immersions.  Closedness is
affine-local on the target, so choose an affine open $U=\Spec A\subseteq X$.  Since $j$ is a
closed immersion,
\[
 j^{-1}(U)=\Spec B
\]
with a surjective ring map $A\twoheadrightarrow B$.  The inverse image
$i^{-1}(j^{-1}(U))$ is affine, say $\Spec C$, and because $i$ is a closed immersion the map
$B\twoheadrightarrow C$ is surjective.  The composite
\[
 A\twoheadrightarrow B\twoheadrightarrow C
\]
is surjective.  Hence, over every affine open $U$ of $X$, the composite $j\circ i$ is induced
by a surjective ring map.  The affine-local criterion therefore shows that
\[
 Z\longrightarrow X
\]
is a closed immersion.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Imposing equations in stages is the same as passing to a composite quotient.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Identify the defining ideal of the composite in terms of the two quotient ideals.

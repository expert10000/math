\subsection*{VI.22.P17: A locally closed immersion}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi22-p17}
Let $U\subseteq X$ be open and $Z\hookrightarrow U$ closed.  Show $Z\to X$ is an immersion and its image is locally closed.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
% VI22-FULL-P17
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the basic model of an immersion and explains the topological phrase ``locally closed.''

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the given factorization and then inspect its image.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Use $Z\hookrightarrow U\hookrightarrow X$.
\item The first arrow is closed and the second is open.
\item A subset closed in an open subset is locally closed.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
By hypothesis the map $Z\hookrightarrow U$ is a closed immersion, while the
inclusion $U\hookrightarrow X$ is the canonical open immersion.  Therefore the composite
\[
 Z\hookrightarrow U\hookrightarrow X
\]
is, by definition, an immersion.

Let $|Z|$ denote the image of the underlying point map.  Because $Z\hookrightarrow U$ is a
closed immersion, $|Z|$ is closed in the open subset $U\subseteq X$.  Hence $|Z|$ is locally
closed in $X$.  Equivalently, one may write
\[
 |Z|=U\cap F
\]
for some closed subset $F$ of $X$ locally around the points of $Z$.  The scheme structure,
however, is not determined merely by this subset: it is the closed subscheme structure inside
the chosen open neighbourhood that matters.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Immersions are precisely the morphisms that look like closed embeddings after restricting the target to a suitable open neighbourhood.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Show locally in an affine target that an immersion is represented by a quotient of a localization.

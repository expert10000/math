\subsection*{VI.22.P9: The reduced subscheme of an affine scheme}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi22-p09}
For $X=\Spec A$, show $X_{\rm red}=\Spec(A/\sqrt{(0)})$ has the same underlying space as $X$ and is reduced.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
% VI22-FULL-P09
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Reduction isolates the topological scheme while discarding nilpotent structure and provides a canonical example of a closed immersion with unchanged support.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Every prime contains the nilradical.  Then identify the nilradical of the quotient.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Use the prime correspondence for $A\twoheadrightarrow A/\sqrt{(0)}$.
\item Show the resulting map is a homeomorphism.
\item Compute the nilradical of $A/\sqrt{(0)}$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Let $N=\sqrt{(0)_A}$ be the nilradical.  Every prime ideal of $A$ contains
$N$, because $N$ is the intersection of all prime ideals.  Thus the quotient map
$A\twoheadrightarrow A/N$ induces a bijection
\[
 \Spec(A/N)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Spec A
\]
on points.  As for every quotient, this is a homeomorphism onto $V(N)$; but
$V(N)=\Spec A$, so it is a homeomorphism onto the whole underlying space.

It remains to check reducedness.  If $\bar a\in A/N$ is nilpotent, then
$\bar a^{\,m}=0$ for some $m$, so $a^m\in N$.  Hence some further power of $a$ is zero in
$A$, which means $a\in N$, and therefore $\bar a=0$.  Thus $A/N$ has zero nilradical and is
reduced.

Consequently $X_{\rm red}\hookrightarrow X$ is a closed immersion that is a homeomorphism on
underlying topological spaces.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Reduction preserves the underlying prime space while removing nilpotents.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Check explicitly that the reduction of $\Spec k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ is $\Spec k$.

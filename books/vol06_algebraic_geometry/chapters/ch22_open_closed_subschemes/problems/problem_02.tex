\subsection*{VI.22.P2: Uniqueness of the open subscheme structure}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi22-p02}
Suppose a scheme structure on the open set $U\subseteq X$ makes the inclusion an open immersion.  Prove it is uniquely isomorphic to $(U,\OO_X|_U)$ over $X$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
% VI22-FULL-P02
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This guarantees that the phrase ``the open subscheme $U$'' is unambiguous: the topology and the ambient structure sheaf already determine it.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the definition of open immersion.  Any candidate sheaf on $U$ must be identified with the same restriction $\OO_X|_U$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Let $(U,\mathcal F)$ be a candidate scheme structure.
\item An open immersion identifies $\mathcal F$ with $\OO_X|_U$.
\item Show that any isomorphism over $X$ has to be the identity on points and on the restricted sheaf.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Let $(U,\mathcal F)$ be a candidate scheme structure.  Assume that its inclusion
\[
 i:(U,\mathcal F)\longrightarrow(X,\OO_X)
\]
is an open immersion.  By definition, $i$ is a homeomorphism from $U$ onto the given open
subset of $X$, and the structural morphism induces an isomorphism
\[
 \mathcal F\xrightarrow{\sim}\OO_X|_U.
\]
The canonical open subscheme is $(U,\OO_X|_U)$.  Hence the identity map on the set $U$,
together with the displayed sheaf isomorphism, gives an isomorphism
\[
 (U,\mathcal F)\xrightarrow{\sim}(U,\OO_X|_U)
\]
over $X$.

For uniqueness, any morphism over $X$ between these two structures must have underlying point
map equal to the identity, because both inclusions into $X$ are literal inclusions.  Its sheaf
map is forced by commutativity with the inclusion morphisms to be the same identification with
$\OO_X|_U$.  Thus the isomorphism is unique.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Open subscheme structure is not additional data once the open subset of the ambient scheme has been fixed.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Use the same argument to show that a composite of open immersions is again an open immersion.

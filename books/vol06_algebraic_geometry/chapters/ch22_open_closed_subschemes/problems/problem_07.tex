\subsection*{VI.22.P7: The affine-local criterion for closed immersions}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi22-p07}
Prove that a morphism that is induced by surjective ring maps on an affine cover of the target is a closed immersion.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
% VI22-FULL-P07
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the practical recognition criterion for closed immersions and the reason quotient rings are the correct local model.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Each surjection identifies the inverse image of an affine target chart with an affine closed subscheme.  Then glue these local closed-subscheme identifications.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Write $U_\alpha=\Spec A_\alpha$ and $i^{-1}(U_\alpha)=\Spec B_\alpha$.
\item Set $I_\alpha=\ker(A_\alpha\to B_\alpha)$.
\item Use $B_\alpha\cong A_\alpha/I_\alpha$.
\item Check compatibility on overlaps and glue.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Let $\{U_\alpha=\Spec A_\alpha\}$ be an affine open cover of $X$ such
that
\[
 i^{-1}(U_\alpha)=\Spec B_\alpha
\]
and the induced map $\varphi_\alpha:A_\alpha\to B_\alpha$ is surjective.  Put
$I_\alpha=\ker\varphi_\alpha$.  Then
\[
 B_\alpha\cong A_\alpha/I_\alpha,
\]
so the restricted morphism
\[
 i^{-1}(U_\alpha)\longrightarrow U_\alpha
\]
is the standard affine closed immersion defined by $I_\alpha$.

On $U_\alpha\cap U_\beta$, both quotient descriptions are restrictions of the same global
morphism $i$, so their closed-subscheme structures agree.  By the local gluing result, these
affine closed subschemes glue to a closed subscheme $Z\hookrightarrow X$.  The source of $i$
and $Z$ are covered by the same affine opens and agree on every member of the cover; the local
identifications glue to an isomorphism over $X$.  Therefore $i$ itself is a closed immersion.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Surjectivity of coordinate-ring pullback is the affine algebraic signature of a closed immersion.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Use the criterion to prove that composites of closed immersions are closed immersions.

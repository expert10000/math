\subsection*{VI.22.P20: Open immersion and local rings}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi22-p20}
For $j:U\hookrightarrow X$ open and $u\in U$, prove $\OO_{X,u}\to\OO_{U,u}$ is an isomorphism.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
% VI22-FULL-P20
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This expresses algebraically that passing to an open neighbourhood does not change the germ of a function at a retained point.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use either the restricted structure sheaf directly or compare the two directed systems of neighbourhoods.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Recall $\OO_U=\OO_X|_U$.
\item Every neighbourhood of $u$ in $X$ yields a neighbourhood after intersecting with $U$.
\item These intersections are cofinal among neighbourhoods of $u$ in $U$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Because $j$ is an open immersion, after identifying $U$ with its image we have
\[
 \OO_U=\OO_X|_U.
\]
The stalk $\OO_{U,u}$ is the direct limit of $\OO_U(V)$ over open neighbourhoods
$V\subseteq U$ of $u$.  Such a neighbourhood is also open in $X$, and conversely for every
open neighbourhood $W\subseteq X$ of $u$, the intersection $W\cap U$ is a neighbourhood of
$u$ in $U$.  The family of intersections $W\cap U$ is cofinal in the neighbourhood system
used to compute the stalk of $\OO_U$.

Since
\[
 \OO_U(W\cap U)=\OO_X(W\cap U),
\]
the two directed systems have canonically isomorphic direct limits.  Hence
\[
 \OO_{X,u}\xrightarrow{\sim}\OO_{U,u}.
\]
The isomorphism is exactly the stalk map induced by the open immersion.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Open restriction preserves all local algebra at the points that remain in the open subset.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Deduce immediately that an open immersion induces an isomorphism on residue fields.

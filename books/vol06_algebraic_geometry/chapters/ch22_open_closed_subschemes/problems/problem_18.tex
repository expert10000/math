\subsection*{VI.22.P18: Closedness is not determined by point image alone}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi22-p18}
Explain why knowing only the subset $|Z|\subseteq|X|$ cannot determine a closed subscheme structure.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
% VI22-FULL-P18
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This clarifies the conceptual distinction between topological support and infinitesimal scheme structure.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the fact that $V(I)$ depends only on $\sqrt I$, while the quotient sheaf depends on $I$ itself.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Work in $X=\Spec k[x]$.
\item Compare $(x)$ and $(x^2)$.
\item Inspect the local/global rings of the resulting one-point subschemes.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
In an affine scheme $X=\Spec A$, the underlying point set of the closed
subscheme defined by $I$ is $V(I)$.  But
\[
 V(I)=V(\sqrt I),
\]
so the point set only detects the radical of $I$.  The structure sheaf of the closed subscheme,
on the other hand, is locally the quotient by the full ideal $I$.

For example, in $\Spec k[x]$ the ideals $(x)$ and $(x^2)$ have the same radical and hence the
same support, the origin.  The corresponding coordinate rings are
\[
 k[x]/(x)\cong k
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 k[x]/(x^2).
\]
The second has a nonzero nilpotent while the first does not.  Thus the two closed subschemes
have identical point spaces but different local rings and structure sheaves.  Therefore the
subset $|Z|\subseteq|X|$ cannot determine the closed subscheme structure.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Topology sees radicals; schemes retain full ideals.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Classify all closed subscheme structures on a fixed affine support $V(J)$ in terms of ideals with radical $\sqrt J$.

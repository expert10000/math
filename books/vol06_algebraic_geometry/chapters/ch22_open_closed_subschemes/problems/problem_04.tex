\subsection*{VI.22.P4: Same support, different closed subschemes}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi22-p04}
Show that $(x)$ and $(x^2)$ in $k[x]$ define the same closed subset but nonisomorphic closed subschemes of $\mathbb A^1_k$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
% VI22-FULL-P04
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the minimal example showing why the word ``subscheme'' contains more information than ``subset.''

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Compare radicals for the support, then compare nilpotents in the quotient rings.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Compute $\sqrt{(x^2)}$.
\item Compare $k[x]/(x)$ and $k[x]/(x^2)$.
\item Use preservation of nilpotents under ring isomorphisms.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Since
\[
 \sqrt{(x^2)}=(x),
\]
we have
\[
 V(x^2)=V(x)
\]
as subsets of $\Spec k[x]$.  Both supports consist of the origin.

The corresponding closed subschemes have coordinate rings
\[
 k[x]/(x)\cong k
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 k[x]/(x^2).
\]
The first ring is reduced.  In the second ring the class $\bar x$ is nonzero and satisfies
$\bar x^2=0$.  Therefore the second ring is nonreduced.  A ring isomorphism sends nilpotent
elements to nilpotent elements and preserves reducedness, so these rings cannot be isomorphic.
By affine anti-equivalence the associated closed subschemes are not isomorphic.

Thus the topological support detects only the radical of the defining ideal, while the scheme
structure retains the full ideal and hence the infinitesimal thickening.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Nilpotent information is invisible to the underlying topological space but visible in the structure sheaf.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Replace $(x^2)$ by $(x^n)$ and compare the nilpotency order of the normal coordinate.

\subsection*{VI.22.P12: Closed subschemes inside closed subschemes}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi22-p12}
If $I\subseteq J\subseteq A$, identify $\Spec(A/J)$ as a closed subscheme of $\Spec(A/I)$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
% VI22-FULL-P12
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This records how nested systems of equations translate into nested quotient schemes.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Factor the quotient $A\twoheadrightarrow A/J$ through $A/I$ and compute its kernel.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Use the map $A/I\twoheadrightarrow A/J$.
\item Compute its kernel as $J/I$.
\item Apply the affine closed-subscheme theorem.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Because $I\subseteq J$, the quotient map $A\twoheadrightarrow A/J$ kills $I$
and therefore factors uniquely as
\[
 A\twoheadrightarrow A/I\twoheadrightarrow A/J.
\]
The second map is surjective and has kernel
\[
 J/I\subseteq A/I.
\]
Consequently the induced morphism
\[
 \Spec(A/J)\longrightarrow\Spec(A/I)
\]
is the affine closed immersion defined by the ideal $J/I$.  Its support consists of the primes
$\mathfrak p/I$ of $A/I$ for which $J/I\subseteq\mathfrak p/I$, equivalently the primes
$\mathfrak p$ of $A$ containing $J$.

The composite closed immersion into $\Spec A$ is the one defined by $J$, exactly as expected
from composition of the two quotient maps.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Passing to a closed subscheme replaces ideals of $A$ containing $I$ by ideals of $A/I$.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Use this description to prove composition of affine closed immersions directly.

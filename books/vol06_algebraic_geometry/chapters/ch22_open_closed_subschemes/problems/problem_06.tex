\subsection*{VI.22.P6: Local gluing of closed subschemes}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi22-p06}
Let $X=U\cup V$ with $U,V$ affine.  Suppose closed subschemes $Z_U\hookrightarrow U$ and $Z_V\hookrightarrow V$ agree on $U\cap V$.  Show they glue uniquely to a closed subscheme of $X$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
% VI22-FULL-P06
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the local-to-global mechanism behind closed subschemes on nonaffine schemes.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Glue the two source schemes along the identified restrictions, glue their maps to $X$, then verify closedness locally on the target.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Use VI/20 to glue $Z_U$ and $Z_V$.
\item The maps to $X$ agree on the overlap by hypothesis.
\item Check the resulting morphism on the affine cover $U,V$.
\item Use uniqueness of gluing for uniqueness of the closed subscheme.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Let
\[
 Z_{UV}=Z_U|_{U\cap V}\cong Z_V|_{U\cap V}
\]
be the given identification.  By the gluing theorem of VI/20, the schemes $Z_U$ and $Z_V$
glue along $Z_{UV}$ to a scheme $Z$ with open cover by the two original pieces.

The closed immersions $Z_U\to U\to X$ and $Z_V\to V\to X$ agree on the identified overlap.
The mapping property of the gluing therefore produces a unique morphism
\[
 i:Z\longrightarrow X.
\]
Now test $i$ on the affine open cover $X=U\cup V$.  Its inverse image of $U$ is $Z_U$, and
the induced morphism $Z_U\to U$ is a closed immersion; similarly over $V$.  By the affine-local
criterion for closed immersions, $i$ is a closed immersion.  Hence $Z$ is a closed subscheme
of $X$.

If $Z'$ is another closed subscheme inducing the same restrictions on $U$ and $V$, its two
open pieces are identified with $Z_U$ and $Z_V$ compatibly on the overlap.  Uniqueness in the
gluing theorem gives a unique isomorphism $Z'\cong Z$ over $X$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Closedness is local on the target, so compatible affine quotient data globalize by the ordinary scheme-gluing theorem.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Reformulate the proof using compatible ideal sheaves.

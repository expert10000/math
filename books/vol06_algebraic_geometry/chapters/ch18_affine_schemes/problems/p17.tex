\begin{problem}[Classical affine variety versus spectrum]\label{prob:vi18-p17}
For an algebraically closed field $k$ and radical ideal $I\subseteq k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$, compare $V(I)$ with $\Spec(k[x_1,\dots,x_n]/I)$ and identify exactly which points correspond to classical points.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem consolidates the classical/scheme bridge aspect of affine geometry and turns a structural statement into a reusable calculation.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the Nullstellensatz for maximal ideals; nonmaximal primes are extra scheme points.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Use the Nullstellensatz for maximal ideals; nonmaximal primes are extra scheme points.
\item Reduce the statement to a ring-theoretic property whenever possible.
\item Translate the result back into topology, sheaves, or local rings so that all affine-scheme layers are visible.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Put
\[
A=k[x_1,\dots,x_n]/I.
\]
The classical algebraic set $V(I)\subseteq k^n$ consists of points $a=(a_1,\dots,a_n)$ satisfying every polynomial in $I$. To such a point associate the maximal ideal
\[
\mathfrak m_a=(x_1-a_1,\dots,x_n-a_n)/I\subseteq A.
\]
Because $k$ is algebraically closed, the weak Nullstellensatz says that every maximal ideal of $A$ is of this form. Thus the classical points of $V(I)$ correspond exactly to the closed points of $\Spec A$.

The scheme $\Spec A$, however, contains all prime ideals, not only maximal ones. Nonmaximal primes give generic points of irreducible subvarieties and their specializations. For example, if $A$ is a domain, $(0)$ is the generic point of the whole variety and has no counterpart as an ordinary $k$-point.

The assumption that $I$ is radical ensures that $A$ is reduced, so no nilpotent thickening is present. Even in this reduced case, $\Spec A$ is richer than $V(I)$ because it remembers all prime ideals and their specialization relations, not merely the maximal ideals.
\end{solution}


\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The calculation illustrates that global affine geometry is controlled by commutative algebra once the structure sheaf is kept in view.

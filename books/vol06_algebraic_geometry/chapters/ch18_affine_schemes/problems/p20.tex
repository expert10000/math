\begin{problem}[Affine cover does not imply affine]\label{prob:vi18-p20}
Explain precisely why the standard two-affine cover of $\mathbb P^1$ does not contradict its non-affineness, and identify which global invariant fails to match a single spectrum.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem consolidates the local versus global aspect of affine geometry and turns a structural statement into a reusable calculation.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the distinction between locally affine and globally affine, and the global-section calculation.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Use the distinction between locally affine and globally affine, and the global-section calculation.
\item Reduce the statement to a ring-theoretic property whenever possible.
\item Translate the result back into topology, sheaves, or local rings so that all affine-scheme layers are visible.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
The two standard opens $U_0,U_1$ of $\mathbb P^1_k$ are individually affine, but affineness is a global statement: one ring must recover the whole locally ringed space.  The cover only proves that $\mathbb P^1_k$ is locally affine.

The global section calculation gives
\[
\Gamma(\mathbb P^1_k,\OO)=k.
\]
If the whole projective line were affine, it would be isomorphic to
\[
\Spec\Gamma(\mathbb P^1_k,\OO)=\Spec k,
\]
which has one point.  The failure is therefore visible in the global recovery invariant, not in the existence of affine local charts.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The calculation illustrates that global affine geometry is controlled by commutative algebra once the structure sheaf is kept in view.

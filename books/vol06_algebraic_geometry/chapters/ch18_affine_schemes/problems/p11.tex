\begin{problem}[Nilpotent thickenings]\label{prob:vi18-p11}
Compare $\Spec A$ and $\Spec A_{\mathrm{red}}$ topologically and explain, using global sections, why they need not be isomorphic as affine schemes.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem consolidates the nilpotents aspect of affine geometry and turns a structural statement into a reusable calculation.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
All primes contain the nilradical.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item All primes contain the nilradical.
\item Reduce the statement to a ring-theoretic property whenever possible.
\item Translate the result back into topology, sheaves, or local rings so that all affine-scheme layers are visible.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Every prime ideal of $A$ contains $\sqrt{(0)}$, so contraction and extension along
\[
A\longrightarrow A_{\mathrm{red}}=A/\sqrt{(0)}
\]
give a bijection of prime ideals preserving inclusions and Zariski closed sets.  Hence
\[
\Spec A_{\mathrm{red}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Spec A
\]
is a homeomorphism.

The affine schemes need not be isomorphic because their global section rings are $A_{\mathrm{red}}$ and $A$.  For $A=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ the reduction is $k$; the spaces are both one point, but one global ring has a nonzero nilpotent and the other does not.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The calculation illustrates that global affine geometry is controlled by commutative algebra once the structure sheaf is kept in view.

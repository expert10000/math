\begin{problem}[Nilpotent thickenings]\label{prob:vi18-p11}
Compare $\Spec A$ and $\Spec A_{\mathrm{red}}$ topologically and explain, using global sections, why they need not be isomorphic as affine schemes.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem consolidates the nilpotents aspect of affine geometry and turns a structural statement into a reusable calculation.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
All primes contain the nilradical.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item All primes contain the nilradical.
\item Reduce the statement to a ring-theoretic property whenever possible.
\item Translate the result back into topology, sheaves, or local rings so that all affine-scheme layers are visible.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Let $N=\sqrt{(0)}$ and $A_{\mathrm{red}}=A/N$. Every prime ideal of $A$ contains $N$. Hence extension and contraction through the quotient map give a bijection
\[
\Spec(A/N)\longleftrightarrow\Spec A,
\qquad
\mathfrak p/N\longleftrightarrow\mathfrak p.
\]
For an ideal $J\supseteq N$, the closed set $V_{A/N}(J/N)$ corresponds to $V_A(J)$, so this bijection is a homeomorphism.

The structure sheaves need not agree. At corresponding points one has
\[
(A/N)_{\mathfrak p/N}\cong A_{\mathfrak p}/N_{\mathfrak p},
\]
so reduction kills the localized nilpotent ideal in every stalk. Globally,
\[
\Gamma(\Spec A,\mathcal O)=A,
\qquad
\Gamma(\Spec A_{\mathrm{red}},\mathcal O)=A/N.
\]
If $A$ is nonreduced, the first ring contains a nonzero nilpotent whereas the second is reduced; therefore they cannot be isomorphic. Thus the same topological space may support genuinely different affine-scheme structures.
\end{solution}


\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The calculation illustrates that global affine geometry is controlled by commutative algebra once the structure sheaf is kept in view.

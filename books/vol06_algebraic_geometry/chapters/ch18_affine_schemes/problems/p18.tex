\begin{problem}[Affine schemes with identical topology]\label{prob:vi18-p18}
Construct two affine schemes with homeomorphic underlying spaces but nonisomorphic structure sheaves and prove they are not isomorphic.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem consolidates the topology versus sheaf aspect of affine geometry and turns a structural statement into a reusable calculation.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use $k$ and the dual numbers, or higher nilpotent thickenings.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Use $k$ and the dual numbers, or higher nilpotent thickenings.
\item Reduce the statement to a ring-theoretic property whenever possible.
\item Translate the result back into topology, sheaves, or local rings so that all affine-scheme layers are visible.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Take
\[
X=\Spec k,\qquad Y=\Spec k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\]
Both underlying spaces consist of one point, hence are homeomorphic.  But
\[
\Gamma(X,\OO_X)=k,
\qquad
\Gamma(Y,\OO_Y)=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\]
The second ring has a nonzero nilpotent and the first does not, so the rings are not isomorphic.  An isomorphism of affine schemes would induce an isomorphism on global sections.  Therefore $X$ and $Y$ are not isomorphic as affine schemes despite identical topology.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The calculation illustrates that global affine geometry is controlled by commutative algebra once the structure sheaf is kept in view.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Vary the ring by products, quotients, localizations, arithmetic examples, or nilpotent thickenings and repeat the same dictionary.

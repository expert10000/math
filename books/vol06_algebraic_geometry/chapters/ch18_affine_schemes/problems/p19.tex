\begin{problem}[Distinguished-open transitivity]\label{prob:vi18-p19}
Starting with $D(f)\cong\Spec A_f$, prove that a distinguished open $D(g/1)$ inside it corresponds to $D(fg)$ inside $\Spec A$, including the sheaf identification.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem consolidates the iterated localization aspect of affine geometry and turns a structural statement into a reusable calculation.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use $(A_f)_{g/1}\cong A_{fg}$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Use $(A_f)_{g/1}\cong A_{fg}$.
\item Reduce the statement to a ring-theoretic property whenever possible.
\item Translate the result back into topology, sheaves, or local rings so that all affine-scheme layers are visible.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Identify $D(f)$ with $\Spec A_f$ by VI.18.P1. A distinguished open in this affine scheme determined by $g/1\in A_f$ is
\[
D_{\Spec A_f}(g/1).
\]
A point of $\Spec A_f$ corresponds to a prime $\mathfrak p\subset A$ with $f\notin\mathfrak p$. The corresponding localized prime contains $g/1$ exactly when $g\in\mathfrak p$. Hence the condition $g/1\notin\mathfrak pA_f$ is equivalent to
\[
f\notin\mathfrak p\quad\text{and}\quad g\notin\mathfrak p,
\]
which is equivalent to $fg\notin\mathfrak p$. Thus topologically this open is precisely $D(fg)\subseteq\Spec A$.

For the sheaf, apply localization again:
\[
D_{\Spec A_f}(g/1)\cong\Spec (A_f)_{g/1}.
\]
Associativity of localization gives the canonical ring isomorphism
\[
(A_f)_{g/1}\cong A_{fg}.
\]
On the other hand, VI.18.P1 applied directly in $\Spec A$ gives
\[
D(fg)\cong\Spec A_{fg}.
\]
These identifications agree on basic opens and on stalks, so the distinguished-open construction is transitive as an identification of locally ringed spaces, not merely as a topological equality.
\end{solution}


\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The calculation illustrates that global affine geometry is controlled by commutative algebra once the structure sheaf is kept in view.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Vary the ring by products, quotients, localizations, arithmetic examples, or nilpotent thickenings and repeat the same dictionary.

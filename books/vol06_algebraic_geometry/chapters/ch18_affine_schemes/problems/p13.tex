\begin{problem}[Arithmetic affine geometry]\label{prob:vi18-p13}
For $X=\Spec\mathbb Z$, identify $D(n)$, its coordinate ring, its closed points, and the local rings at the remaining closed points.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem consolidates the Spec Z aspect of affine geometry and turns a structural statement into a reusable calculation.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Localization inverts the prime factors of $n$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Localization inverts the prime factors of $n$.
\item Reduce the statement to a ring-theoretic property whenever possible.
\item Translate the result back into topology, sheaves, or local rings so that all affine-scheme layers are visible.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
For $X=\Spec\mathbb Z$ and $n\neq0$, the distinguished-open localization theorem gives
\[
D(n)\cong\Spec\mathbb Z[1/n].
\]
The closed points of $X$ are the ideals $(p)$ for rational primes $p$. Such a point lies in $D(n)$ iff $n\notin(p)$, equivalently iff $p\nmid n$. Thus $D(n)$ is obtained from the arithmetic line by removing precisely the closed points $(p)$ for prime divisors $p$ of $n$. The generic point $(0)$ remains.

Let $(p)$ be a remaining closed point, so $p\nmid n$. In the localized ring $\mathbb Z[1/n]$, the corresponding prime is $(p)\mathbb Z[1/n]$. Its local ring is
\[
(\mathbb Z[1/n])_{(p)}\cong\mathbb Z_{(p)},
\]
because all prime factors of $n$ are already units in $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$ when $p\nmid n$. Its residue field is still $\mathbb F_p$. Thus localization removes selected closed points without changing the local ring at any point that remains.
\end{solution}


\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The calculation illustrates that global affine geometry is controlled by commutative algebra once the structure sheaf is kept in view.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Vary the ring by products, quotients, localizations, arithmetic examples, or nilpotent thickenings and repeat the same dictionary.

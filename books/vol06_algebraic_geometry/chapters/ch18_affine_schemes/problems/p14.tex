\begin{problem}[Laurent tori as distinguished opens]\label{prob:vi18-p14}
Show that the distinguished open
\[
D(x_1\cdots x_n)\subseteq\mathbb A^n_k
\]
is the affine scheme
\[
\Spec k[x_1^{\pm1},\dots,x_n^{\pm1}].
\]
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem consolidates the torus aspect of affine geometry and turns a structural statement into a reusable calculation.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Inverting the product inverts each factor.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Inverting the product inverts each factor.
\item Reduce the statement to a ring-theoretic property whenever possible.
\item Translate the result back into topology, sheaves, or local rings so that all affine-scheme layers are visible.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Let
\[
A=k[x_1,\dots,x_n],\qquad f=x_1\cdots x_n.
\]
By the basic-open theorem,
\[
D(f)\cong\Spec A_f.
\]
In $A_f$, the product $x_1\cdots x_n$ is a unit. Each individual $x_i$ is then a unit, because
\[
x_i^{-1}=\frac{\prod_{j\ne i}x_j}{x_1\cdots x_n}.
\]
Therefore $A_f$ contains all $x_i^{\pm1}$ and hence contains the Laurent polynomial ring
\[
k[x_1^{\pm1},\dots,x_n^{\pm1}].
\]
Conversely, in the Laurent polynomial ring the product $f$ is already invertible, so the universal property of localization gives a map $A_f$ into that ring. The two maps are inverse. Thus
\[
A_f\cong k[x_1^{\pm1},\dots,x_n^{\pm1}],
\]
and consequently
\[
\boxed{D(x_1\cdots x_n)\cong\Spec k[x_1^{\pm1},\dots,x_n^{\pm1}].}
\]
This is the algebraic $n$-torus $(\mathbb G_m)^n$.
\end{solution}


\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The calculation illustrates that global affine geometry is controlled by commutative algebra once the structure sheaf is kept in view.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Vary the ring by products, quotients, localizations, arithmetic examples, or nilpotent thickenings and repeat the same dictionary.

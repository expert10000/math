\begin{problem}[Laurent tori as distinguished opens]\label{prob:vi18-p14}
Show that the distinguished open
\[
D(x_1\cdots x_n)\subseteq\mathbb A^n_k
\]
is the affine scheme
\[
\Spec k[x_1^{\pm1},\dots,x_n^{\pm1}].
\]
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem consolidates the torus aspect of affine geometry and turns a structural statement into a reusable calculation.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Inverting the product inverts each factor.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Inverting the product inverts each factor.
\item Reduce the statement to a ring-theoretic property whenever possible.
\item Translate the result back into topology, sheaves, or local rings so that all affine-scheme layers are visible.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Let $F=x_1\cdots x_n$.  In $A_F=k[x_1,\dots,x_n]_F$, each $x_i$ is invertible because
\[
x_i^{-1}=\frac{\prod_{j\neq i}x_j}{F}.
\]
Hence there is a natural map
\[
k[x_1^{\pm1},\dots,x_n^{\pm1}]\longrightarrow A_F.
\]
Conversely $F$ is a unit in the Laurent polynomial ring, so the universal property of localization gives the reverse map.  They are inverse.  Therefore
\[
D(x_1\cdots x_n)\cong\Spec k[x_1^{\pm1},\dots,x_n^{\pm1}].
\]
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The calculation illustrates that global affine geometry is controlled by commutative algebra once the structure sheaf is kept in view.

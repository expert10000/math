\begin{problem}[Legacy AG6 Problem 6.4: a scheme that is not affine]\label{prob:vi18-p02}
Give an example of a scheme that is not affine and justify the claim.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Affine schemes are the local models of scheme theory, but the category of schemes is larger.
The projective line is the first standard warning that global geometry need not be one
spectrum.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Use the two standard affine charts of $\mathbb P^1_k$, compute global functions on their
overlap, and then apply affine global-section recovery.
\fi

\begin{solution}
Take
\[
X=\mathbb P^1_k.
\]
Let $U_0\cong\Spec k[t]$ and $U_1\cong\Spec k[u]$ be the standard charts, with
$u=t^{-1}$ on $U_0\cap U_1$.  A global regular function is represented by
\[
p(t)\in k[t],
\qquad
q(u)\in k[u]
\]
whose restrictions agree:
\[
p(t)=q(t^{-1})\quad\text{in }k[t,t^{-1}].
\]
The left side has only nonnegative powers of $t$ and the right side only nonpositive powers.
Therefore their common value is constant, and
\[
\Gamma(\mathbb P^1_k,\OO)=k.
\]
If $\mathbb P^1_k$ were affine, global-section recovery would imply
\[
\mathbb P^1_k\cong\Spec k.
\]
But $\Spec k$ has one point whereas $\mathbb P^1_k$ has more than one point.  Hence
\[
\boxed{\mathbb P^1_k\text{ is not affine}.}
\]
\end{solution}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The global function ring is too small to reconstruct the projective-line space.  That cannot
happen for an affine scheme.

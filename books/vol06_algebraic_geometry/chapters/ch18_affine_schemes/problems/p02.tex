\begin{problem}[Legacy AG6 Problem 6.4: A scheme that is not affine]\label{prob:vi18-p02}
Show that the projective line $\mathbb P^1_k$ is not affine. Give a self-contained proof using its two standard affine charts and the affine reconstruction principle $X\cong\Spec\Gamma(X,\OO_X)$ valid for affine $X$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
The example prevents the common mistake that a space covered by affine charts must itself be affine. It is the corpus-level negative test for affineness reserved by the volume ledger.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use $U_0\cong\Spec k[t]$, $U_1\cong\Spec k[u]$, and $u=t^{-1}$ on the overlap. Compute global functions by sheaf gluing, then compare with $\Spec k$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A global regular function is a compatible pair $f(t)\in k[t]$, $g(u)\in k[u]$.
\item On the overlap require $f(t)=g(t^{-1})$ in $k[t,t^{-1}]$.
\item Compute $k[t]\cap k[t^{-1}]$ inside $k[t,t^{-1}]$.
\item If $\mathbb P^1_k$ were affine, reconstruct it from its global section ring.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Let
\[
U_0\cong\Spec k[t],\qquad U_1\cong\Spec k[u]
\]
be the standard affine cover of $\mathbb P^1_k$, with $u=t^{-1}$ on
$U_0\cap U_1\cong\Spec k[t,t^{-1}]$.
A global section is a pair
\[
f(t)\in k[t],\qquad g(u)\in k[u]
\]
whose restrictions agree on the overlap. Substituting $u=t^{-1}$, compatibility is
\[
f(t)=g(t^{-1})\quad\text{in }k[t,t^{-1}].
\]
The left side has only nonnegative powers of $t$ and the right side only nonpositive powers, so their intersection is $k$. Thus
\[
\Gamma(\mathbb P^1_k,\OO)=k.
\]
If $\mathbb P^1_k$ were affine, affine reconstruction would give
\[
\mathbb P^1_k\cong\Spec\Gamma(\mathbb P^1_k,\OO)=\Spec k.
\]
But $\Spec k$ has one point while $\mathbb P^1_k$ has at least $[1:0]$ and $[0:1]$. Contradiction. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\mathbb P^1_k\text{ is not affine}.}
\]
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The obstruction is global: each chart is affine, but the global regular-function ring is too small to recover the whole projective line.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
The same argument shows $\mathbb P^n_k$ is not affine for $n\ge1$ once one knows its global regular functions are $k$.

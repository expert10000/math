\begin{problem}[Affine schemes are quasi-compact]\label{prob:vi18-p05}
Prove directly that every affine scheme is quasi-compact by converting a principal-open cover into an equation $1=\sum a_if_i$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem consolidates the compactness aspect of affine geometry and turns a structural statement into a reusable calculation.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
First refine an arbitrary cover by distinguished opens.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item First refine an arbitrary cover by distinguished opens.
\item Reduce the statement to a ring-theoretic property whenever possible.
\item Translate the result back into topology, sheaves, or local rings so that all affine-scheme layers are visible.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Let $X=\Spec A$ and let $\{U_\lambda\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}$ be an arbitrary open cover. For every point $\mathfrak p\in X$, choose an index $\lambda(\mathfrak p)$ with $\mathfrak p\in U_{\lambda(\mathfrak p)}$. Since distinguished opens form a basis, choose $f_{\mathfrak p}\notin\mathfrak p$ such that
\[
\mathfrak p\in D(f_{\mathfrak p})\subseteq U_{\lambda(\mathfrak p)}.
\]
The distinguished opens $D(f_{\mathfrak p})$ form a refinement of the original cover.

Let $I$ be the ideal generated by all $f_{\mathfrak p}$. If $I$ were proper, it would be contained in a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$. Then every generator $f_{\mathfrak p}$ would lie in $\mathfrak m$, so $\mathfrak m$ would lie in none of the opens $D(f_{\mathfrak p})$, contradicting that they cover $X$. Hence $I=A$.

Therefore $1$ is a finite linear combination
\[
1=a_1f_{\mathfrak p_1}+\cdots+a_rf_{\mathfrak p_r}.
\]
The finite cover criterion now gives
\[
X=D(f_{\mathfrak p_1})\cup\cdots\cup D(f_{\mathfrak p_r}).
\]
Since each $D(f_{\mathfrak p_i})$ lies inside the corresponding $U_{\lambda(\mathfrak p_i)}$, those finitely many original opens cover $X$. Thus $X$ is quasi-compact.
\end{solution}


\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The calculation illustrates that global affine geometry is controlled by commutative algebra once the structure sheaf is kept in view.

Vary the ring by products, quotients, localizations, arithmetic examples, or nilpotent thickenings and repeat the same dictionary.

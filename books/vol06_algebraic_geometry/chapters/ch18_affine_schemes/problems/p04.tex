\begin{problem}[Principal opens give an affine basis]\label{prob:vi18-p04}
Using the principal-open basis theorem from VI/08 together with VI.18.P1, prove that every affine scheme has a basis of affine open subschemes of the form
\[
D(f)\cong\Spec A_f.
\]
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem consolidates the basis aspect of affine geometry and turns a structural statement into a reusable calculation.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}

\item Reduce the statement to a ring-theoretic property whenever possible.

\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Let $X=\Spec A$ and let $\mathfrak p\in X$. The distinguished opens $D(f)$ with $f\notin\mathfrak p$ form a neighborhood basis at $\mathfrak p$: given any open neighborhood $U$ of $\mathfrak p$, the principal-open basis theorem produces $f\notin\mathfrak p$ such that
\[
\mathfrak p\in D(f)\subseteq U.
\]
By VI.18.P1, each such distinguished open, with the restricted structure sheaf, is canonically an affine scheme:
\[
(D(f),\mathcal O_X|_{D(f)})\cong\Spec A_f.
\]
Thus every point has a basis of affine neighborhoods, and the family of all $D(f)$ is an affine basis for $X$.

The intersection behavior is compatible with this basis: $D(f)\cap D(g)=D(fg)$ and
\[
(A_f)_{g/1}\cong A_{fg}.
\]
Hence intersections of these basic affine charts are again basic affine charts, a fact that will later make gluing and separatedness calculations concrete.
\end{solution}


\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The calculation illustrates that global affine geometry is controlled by commutative algebra once the structure sheaf is kept in view.

\begin{problem}[The empty affine scheme]\label{prob:vi18-p08}
Show that $\Spec A$ is empty iff $A$ is the zero ring.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem consolidates the empty spectrum aspect of affine geometry and turns a structural statement into a reusable calculation.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
If $A\neq0$, use existence of a maximal ideal.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $A\neq0$, use existence of a maximal ideal.
\item Reduce the statement to a ring-theoretic property whenever possible.
\item Translate the result back into topology, sheaves, or local rings so that all affine-scheme layers are visible.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
If $A=0$, then $1=0$, so $A$ has no proper ideals and therefore no prime ideals. Hence $\Spec A=\varnothing$.

Conversely suppose $A\neq0$. Then the zero ideal is proper, and by the maximal-ideal theorem it is contained in some maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$. Every maximal ideal in a commutative unital ring is prime, so $\mathfrak m\in\Spec A$. Thus the spectrum is nonempty.

Therefore
\[
\boxed{\Spec A=\varnothing\iff A=0.}
\]
Geometrically this is compatible with localization: if one localizes at a nilpotent element, the localized ring becomes zero and the corresponding distinguished open is empty.
\end{solution}


\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The calculation illustrates that global affine geometry is controlled by commutative algebra once the structure sheaf is kept in view.

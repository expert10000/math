\begin{problem}[Local rings and unique closed points]\label{prob:vi18-p09}
Prove that $A$ is local iff $\Spec A$ has exactly one closed point.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem consolidates the local geometry aspect of affine geometry and turns a structural statement into a reusable calculation.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Closed points are maximal ideals.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Closed points are maximal ideals.
\item Reduce the statement to a ring-theoretic property whenever possible.
\item Translate the result back into topology, sheaves, or local rings so that all affine-scheme layers are visible.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
For any ring $A$, the closed points of $\Spec A$ are precisely the maximal ideals. Indeed, the closure of the point $\mathfrak p$ is $V(\mathfrak p)$, so $\{\mathfrak p\}$ is closed exactly when there is no strictly larger prime ideal; this is equivalent to $\mathfrak p$ being maximal.

If $A$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, then $\mathfrak m$ is the unique maximal ideal and hence the unique closed point of $\Spec A$.

Conversely, if $\Spec A$ has a unique closed point $\mathfrak m$, then $A$ has a unique maximal ideal, because every maximal ideal determines a closed point. A commutative ring with a unique maximal ideal is local. Hence
\[
\boxed{A\text{ local}\iff\Spec A\text{ has exactly one closed point}.}
\]
Note that the spectrum can still contain nonclosed primes below $\mathfrak m$.
\end{solution}


\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The calculation illustrates that global affine geometry is controlled by commutative algebra once the structure sheaf is kept in view.

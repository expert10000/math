\begin{problem}[Recover the coordinate ring]\label{prob:vi18-p03}
Let $X$ be affine. Prove that the isomorphism class of $\Gamma(X,\OO_X)$ is determined by $X$, and that any presentation $X\cong\Spec A$ yields $A\cong\Gamma(X,\OO_X)$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem consolidates the global sections aspect of affine geometry and turns a structural statement into a reusable calculation.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use invariance of global sections under locally ringed-space isomorphism and $\Gamma(\Spec A,\OO)=A$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Use invariance of global sections under an isomorphism of locally ringed spaces.
\item Then use $\Gamma(\Spec A,\OO)=A$.
\item Reduce the statement to a ring-theoretic property whenever possible.
\item Translate the result back into topology, sheaves, or local rings so that all affine-scheme layers are visible.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Let $X$ be affine and choose any affine presentation $\phi:X\xrightarrow{\sim}\Spec A$.  The sheaf isomorphism carried by $\phi$ induces an isomorphism on sections over the whole space:
\[
\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gamma(\Spec A,\mathcal O_{\Spec A}).
\]
By the global-section theorem for an affine spectrum, the ring on the right is canonically $A$. Hence every presentation $X\cong\Spec A$ yields
\[
A\cong\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X).
\]
In particular, if $X\cong\Spec A\cong\Spec B$, then both $A$ and $B$ are isomorphic to the same intrinsic ring $\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)$, so $A\cong B$.

The point is that $\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)$ is defined from the locally ringed space $X$ itself, without choosing coordinates. Therefore its isomorphism class is an invariant of $X$. Taking spectra gives the reconstruction
\[
X\cong\Spec\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X).
\]
This reconstruction statement is used here only for affine $X$.  The equivalence involving morphisms is deferred to VI/19.
\end{solution}


\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The calculation illustrates that global affine geometry is controlled by commutative algebra once the structure sheaf is kept in view.

\begin{problem}[Product rings and disjoint unions]\label{prob:vi18-p07}
Prove $\Spec(A\times B)\cong\Spec A\sqcup\Spec B$ as affine schemes, including the structure sheaf.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem consolidates the products aspect of affine geometry and turns a structural statement into a reusable calculation.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Classify primes of a product and compare principal opens factorwise.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Classify primes of a product and compare principal opens factorwise.
\item Reduce the statement to a ring-theoretic property whenever possible.
\item Translate the result back into topology, sheaves, or local rings so that all affine-scheme layers are visible.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Let $e=(1,0)$ and $1-e=(0,1)$.  Every prime $P\subset A\times B$ contains exactly one of these orthogonal idempotents, so every prime has one of the forms
\[
\mfp\times B\quad(\mfp\in\Spec A),\qquad A\times\mfq\quad(\mfq\in\Spec B).
\]
These form disjoint clopen sets $V(1-e)=D(e)$ and $V(e)=D(1-e)$.  The first is canonically homeomorphic to $\Spec A$ and the second to $\Spec B$.

On the first component, localizing $A\times B$ at $e$ kills the $B$ factor and gives $A$; similarly the second component gives $B$.  More generally the basic-open structure-sheaf rings agree factorwise.  Therefore
\[
\Spec(A\times B)\cong\Spec A\sqcup\Spec B
\]
as locally ringed spaces, hence as affine schemes.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The calculation illustrates that global affine geometry is controlled by commutative algebra once the structure sheaf is kept in view.

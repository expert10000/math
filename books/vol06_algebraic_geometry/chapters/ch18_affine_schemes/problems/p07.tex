\begin{problem}[Product rings and disjoint unions]\label{prob:vi18-p07}
Prove $\Spec(A\times B)\cong\Spec A\sqcup\Spec B$ as affine schemes, including the structure sheaf.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem consolidates the products aspect of affine geometry and turns a structural statement into a reusable calculation.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Classify primes of a product and compare principal opens factorwise.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Classify primes of a product and compare principal opens factorwise.
\item Reduce the statement to a ring-theoretic property whenever possible.
\item Translate the result back into topology, sheaves, or local rings so that all affine-scheme layers are visible.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Let $R=A\times B$, with complementary idempotents
\[
e_A=(1,0),\qquad e_B=(0,1).
\]
For a prime ideal $\mathfrak r\subset R$, the relation $e_Ae_B=0$ implies that $e_A\in\mathfrak r$ or $e_B\in\mathfrak r$. They cannot both belong to $\mathfrak r$, since $e_A+e_B=1$. Thus every prime belongs to exactly one of the two clopen subsets $D(e_A)$ and $D(e_B)$.

If $e_B\in\mathfrak r$, contraction to the first factor shows
\[
\mathfrak r=\mathfrak p\times B
\]
for a unique $\mathfrak p\in\Spec A$. Similarly the primes containing $e_A$ are exactly $A\times\mathfrak q$ with $\mathfrak q\in\Spec B$. This gives the topological decomposition
\[
\Spec(A\times B)\cong\Spec A\sqcup\Spec B.
\]

To compare the structure sheaves, localize at the idempotents. Since $e_A$ becomes $1$ and $e_B$ becomes $0$ in the first localization,
\[
R_{e_A}\cong A,
\qquad
R_{e_B}\cong B.
\]
By the distinguished-open localization theorem,
\[
D(e_A)\cong\Spec A,
\qquad
D(e_B)\cong\Spec B
\]
as locally ringed spaces. Because these clopen pieces are disjoint and cover the spectrum, the two identifications combine to
\[
\boxed{\Spec(A\times B)\cong\Spec A\sqcup\Spec B}
\]
as affine schemes.
\end{solution}


\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The calculation illustrates that global affine geometry is controlled by commutative algebra once the structure sheaf is kept in view.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Vary the ring by products, quotients, localizations, arithmetic examples, or nilpotent thickenings and repeat the same dictionary.

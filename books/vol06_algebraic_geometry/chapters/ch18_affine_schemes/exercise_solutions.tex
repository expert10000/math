\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E1 (Definition check).}
The affine scheme is $(\Spec A,\OO_{\Spec A})$ and $\OO_{\Spec A,\mfp}=A_{\mfp}$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E2 (Global sections).}
Since $\Spec A=D(1)$, $\Gamma(\Spec A,\OO)=A_1\cong A$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E3 (Affine reconstruction).}
If $X\cong\Spec A$, then $\Gamma(X,\OO_X)\cong A$, hence $\Spec\Gamma(X,\OO_X)\cong\Spec A\cong X$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E4 (Field spectrum).}
$\Spec k=\{(0)\}$, with global ring and unique stalk $k$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E5 (Empty affine scheme).}
A prime ideal is proper. The zero ring has no proper ideal, so its spectrum is empty.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E6 (Affine line).}
The generic point is $(0)$. Closed points are $(t-a)$ for $a\in k$ when $k$ is algebraically closed.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E7 (Arithmetic line).}
Points are $(0)$ and $(p)$. Their residue fields are $\mathbb Q$ at $(0)$ and $\mathbb F_p$ at $(p)$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E8 (Principal open).}
$D(x)\cong\Spec k[x,y]_x=\Spec k[x,y,x^{-1}]$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E9 (Intersection).}
$D(f)\cap D(g)=D(fg)$, so the localization theorem gives $D(fg)\cong\Spec A_{fg}$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E10 (Unit principal open).}
No prime contains a unit, hence every prime lies in $D(u)$. Also $A_u\cong A$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E11 (Nilpotent principal open).}
If $f^n=0$, then every prime contains $f$, so $D(f)=\varnothing$. In $A_f$, $f$ is invertible and nilpotent, forcing $1=0$, so $A_f=0$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E12 (Finite cover criterion).}
The cover condition is $V(f_1,\dots,f_r)=\varnothing$, equivalent to $\sqrt{(f_1,\dots,f_r)}=A$, hence already $(f_1,\dots,f_r)=A$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E13 (Quasi-compactness).}
Refine by principal opens $D(f_i)$. Their cover means the $f_i$ generate $A$, so $1$ is a finite linear combination and finitely many of the $D(f_i)$ cover.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E14 (Product ring).}
Each prime of $A\times B$ is $\mfp\times B$ or $A\times\mfq$. The two families form disjoint clopen pieces canonically homeomorphic, with matching structure sheaves, to $\Spec A$ and $\Spec B$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E15 (Local ring geometry).}
Closed points of $\Spec A$ are maximal ideals. Thus exactly one closed point is equivalent to exactly one maximal ideal, which is the definition of a local ring.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E16 (Dual numbers).}
Both underlying spaces contain one point. Their global rings are $k$ and $k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$, so the affine schemes are not isomorphic.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E17 (Laurent line).}
$k[t]_t$ consists of Laurent polynomials, hence equals $k[t,t^{-1}]$, and $D(t)\cong\Spec k[t,t^{-1}]$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E18 (Arithmetic localization).}
$D(30)\cong\Spec\mathbb Z[1/30]$. The closed points $(2),(3),(5)$ are removed; all other $(p)$ remain.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E19 (Iterated localization).}
Localizing $A_f$ at $g/1$ inverts both $f$ and $g$, exactly as $A_{fg}$ does. The two universal properties give mutually inverse maps.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E20 (Residue field).}
$\mathbb R[t]/(t^2+1)\cong\mathbb C$, so the residue field is $\mathbb C$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E21 (Classical affine set).}
The map $k[x,y]/(y-x^2)\to k[x]$ sending $y$ to $x^2$ is an isomorphism. Thus the generic point is $(0)$ and the closed points are $(x-a,y-a^2)$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E22 (One-point criterion).}
$\Spec A$ has one point iff $A$ has exactly one prime ideal. That unique prime contains the nilradical and is maximal because every maximal ideal is prime, so it equals both the nilradical and the unique maximal ideal.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E23 (Principal neighborhoods).}
The $D(f)$ with $f\notin\mfp$ form a neighborhood basis at $\mfp$, and every such $D(f)$ is $\Spec A_f$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E24 (Recover an isomorphism invariant).}
If $X\cong Y$ as affine schemes, then applying global sections yields $\Gamma(X,\OO_X)\cong\Gamma(Y,\OO_Y)$. Therefore different global rings obstruct isomorphism.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E25 (Projective-line global functions).}
A global function gives $f(t)\in k[t]$ and $g(u)\in k[u]$ with $f(t)=g(t^{-1})$ in $k[t,t^{-1}]$. The intersection $k[t]\cap k[t^{-1}]$ is $k$, so both are constant.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.18.E26 (Boundary to VI/19).}
A scheme morphism is a morphism of locally ringed spaces: a continuous map together with a compatible morphism of structure sheaves whose induced maps on stalks are local ring homomorphisms. VI/19 develops the affine/ring-map correspondence.

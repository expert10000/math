\section*{Purpose and learning goals}

VI/17 attached the structure sheaf to a spectrum.  This chapter packages that construction
into the basic geometric object of scheme theory.  An affine scheme is not merely a set of
prime ideals or a Zariski topological space: it is the locally ringed space
\[
(\Spec A,\OO_{\Spec A}).
\]
The decisive compatibility between localization and restriction is now geometric: for every
$f\in A$, the principal open $D(f)$ is itself an affine scheme, canonically isomorphic to
$\Spec A_f$.

The legacy audit has two explicit obligations.  AG3 Problem 11 is recovered below in full:
the isomorphism
\[
\bigl(D(f),\OO_{\Spec A}|_{D(f)}\bigr)\cong \Spec A_f
\]
is proved as an isomorphism of locally ringed spaces, including the prime correspondence,
topology, sheaf maps, restriction compatibility, and stalks.  The affineness subpart of
AG6 Problem 6 is also retained: $\Pone_k$ is a standard non-affine scheme.  AG3 Problems
12--14 are not consumed here; they remain reserved for the later chapters on morphisms,
gluing, and general schemes.

After this chapter, the reader should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
\item define an affine scheme as a locally ringed space isomorphic to a spectrum;
\item recover the coordinate ring of an affine scheme from global sections;
\item prove that $D(f)$ is canonically $\Spec A_f$ as a locally ringed space;
\item translate nested distinguished opens into iterated localizations;
\item use distinguished opens as a basis of affine neighborhoods;
\item prove that every affine scheme is quasi-compact;
\item recognize standard affine schemes over fields and over $\mathbb Z$;
\item distinguish affineness from irreducibility, reducedness, and integrality;
\item use global functions to obstruct affineness in the projective-line example;
\item identify precisely what is deferred to VI/19--VI/22.
\end{itemize}

\section{From affine spectra to affine schemes}

\begin{definition}[Affine scheme]
An \emph{affine scheme} is a locally ringed space $(X,\OO_X)$ for which there exists a
commutative ring $A$ and an isomorphism of locally ringed spaces
\[
(X,\OO_X)\cong(\Spec A,\OO_{\Spec A}).
\]
When such an identification is fixed, we write simply $X=\Spec A$.
\end{definition}

Thus ``affine'' packages three compatible layers: the ring $A$, the Zariski space
$\Spec A$, and the structure sheaf $\OO_{\Spec A}$ with stalks $A_{\mfp}$.
The topology records specialization of primes; the structure sheaf records local rings and
therefore remembers nilpotents and other local algebra invisible to topology alone.

\begin{example}[A point]
If $k$ is a field, $\Spec k$ has one point and structure ring $k$.  It is the basic one-point
affine scheme.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Affine space]
For a field $k$,
\[
\An_k:=\Spec k[x_1,\dots,x_n].
\]
In particular $\Aone_k=\Spec k[t]$.  Its scheme-theoretic points are all prime ideals, not
only maximal ideals corresponding to ordinary $k$-points.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[The arithmetic spectrum]
The scheme $\Spec\mathbb Z$ is affine.  Its generic point is $(0)$ and its closed points are
$(p)$ for prime integers $p$; its local ring at $(p)$ is $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$.
\end{example}

\section{Global sections recover the affine coordinate ring}

For $X=\Spec A$, VI/17 established
\[
\Gamma(X,\OO_X)=\OO_X(D(1))\cong A.
\]
Hence an affine scheme remembers its defining ring.

\begin{proposition}[Affine rigidity from global sections]\label{prop:vi18-global-recovery}
If $X$ is affine, then
\[
X\cong\Spec\Gamma(X,\OO_X).
\]
Consequently, if $\Spec A$ and $\Spec B$ are isomorphic as locally ringed spaces, then
$A\cong B$ as rings.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose an affine presentation $X\cong\Spec A$.  Taking global sections gives
$\Gamma(X,\OO_X)\cong A$, and substituting this ring isomorphism into the presentation gives
the first assertion.  An isomorphism of locally ringed spaces induces an isomorphism on
global section rings, giving the second.
\end{proof}

This is deliberately weaker than the full anti-equivalence between rings and affine schemes.
The correspondence between \emph{morphisms} and ring maps is the subject of VI/19.

\section{Principal opens are affine}\label{sec:vi18-principal-opens}

Let $A$ be a ring, $f\in A$, and $X=\Spec A$.  Recall
\[
D(f)=\{\mfp\in\Spec A:f\notin\mfp\}.
\]
The localization map $A\to A_f$ suggests that $D(f)$ should be the spectrum of $A_f$.
The next theorem makes this exact at the level of locally ringed spaces.

\begin{theorem}[Basic-open affine theorem]\label{thm:vi18-basic-open-affine}
There is a canonical isomorphism of locally ringed spaces
\[
\boxed{
\bigl(D(f),\OO_X|_{D(f)}\bigr)
\cong
\bigl(\Spec A_f,\OO_{\Spec A_f}\bigr).}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Prime ideals of $A_f$ correspond exactly to primes $\mfp\subseteq A$ with $f\notin\mfp$,
by contraction and extension:
\[
\mfq\longmapsto\mfq\cap A,
\qquad
\mfp\longmapsto\mfp A_f.
\]
A basic open $D(g/1)\subseteq\Spec A_f$ corresponds to
\[
D(g)\cap D(f)=D(fg)\subseteq\Spec A,
\]
so the point bijection is a homeomorphism.

On these corresponding basic opens,
\[
\OO_X(D(fg))=A_{fg},
\qquad
\OO_{\Spec A_f}(D(g/1))=(A_f)_{g/1}.
\]
Associativity of localization gives
\[
(A_f)_{g/1}\xrightarrow{\sim}A_{fg},
\qquad
\frac{a/f^m}{(g/1)^n}\longmapsto\frac{a}{f^m g^n}.
\]
These maps commute with restriction maps, hence identify the sheaves on a basis and
therefore on all opens.  Finally, at corresponding points
$\mfp\leftrightarrow\mfp A_f$,
\[
(A_f)_{\mfp A_f}\cong A_{\mfp},
\]
and this is an isomorphism of local rings.  Therefore the homeomorphism is an isomorphism
of locally ringed spaces.
\end{proof}

\section{Nested principal opens and repeated localization}

Under Theorem~\ref{thm:vi18-basic-open-affine}, a principal open inside $D(f)$ has the form
\[
D_{\Spec A_f}(g/1).
\]
Its image in $\Spec A$ is
\[
D(f)\cap D(g)=D(fg),
\]
and its coordinate ring is
\[
(A_f)_{g/1}\cong A_{fg}.
\]
Thus restriction to a smaller principal affine neighborhood is literally further
localization:
\[
\boxed{
D(f)\supseteq D(fg)
\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
A_f\longrightarrow A_{fg}.}
\]

\begin{corollary}[Affine neighborhood basis]\label{cor:vi18-affine-basis}
Every affine scheme has a basis of open subsets that are affine schemes.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The distinguished opens $D(f)$ form a basis of $\Spec A$, and each is affine by
Theorem~\ref{thm:vi18-basic-open-affine}.
\end{proof}

\section{Quasi-compactness of affine schemes}

\begin{proposition}[Affine schemes are quasi-compact]\label{prop:vi18-qc}
Every affine scheme is quasi-compact.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
It suffices to consider $X=\Spec A$.  Let $X=\bigcup_{i\in I}U_i$ be an open cover.
Refine it by distinguished opens, obtaining a cover
\[
X=\bigcup_{j\in J}D(f_j).
\]
If the ideal $(f_j:j\in J)$ were proper, it would lie in a maximal ideal $\mfm$, and then
$\mfm$ would lie in none of the $D(f_j)$, a contradiction.  Thus
\[
1=a_1f_{j_1}+\cdots+a_rf_{j_r}
\]
for finitely many indices.  No prime can contain all $f_{j_1},\dots,f_{j_r}$, so
\[
X=D(f_{j_1})\cup\cdots\cup D(f_{j_r}).
\]
Choosing parent members of the original cover gives a finite subcover.
\end{proof}

\section{Extreme principal opens}

The spectrum-localization dictionary gives two useful tests:
\[
D(f)=\varnothing\iff f\text{ is nilpotent},
\qquad
D(f)=\Spec A\iff f\text{ is a unit}.
\]
In the first case $A_f=0$; in the second $A_f\cong A$.

\section{Standard affine constructions}

\subsection{Laurent affine line}
For $A=k[t]$ and $f=t$,
\[
D(t)\cong\Spec k[t,t^{-1}].
\]
Thus the affine line with the origin removed is still affine.

\subsection{Reducible affine schemes}
For
\[
A=k[x,y]/(xy),
\]
$\Spec A$ is affine although reducible.  Affineness is not the same as irreducibility,
reducedness, or integrality.

\subsection{Nonreduced affine schemes}
For
\[
A=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2),
\]
$\Spec A$ is a one-point affine scheme with a nonzero nilpotent global section.  It has the
same underlying topological space as $\Spec k$ but is not isomorphic to it as a locally
ringed space.

\subsection{Product rings}
For rings $A$ and $B$, $\Spec(A\times B)$ is the disjoint union of two clopen affine pieces.
The idempotents $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ cut out those distinguished opens.

\section{Affineness is a genuine global condition}

Not every scheme is affine.  A standard counterexample, already present in the legacy
corpus, is the projective line.

\begin{proposition}[The projective line is not affine]\label{prop:vi18-p1-nonaffine}
Let $k$ be a field.  Then $\Pone_k$ is not affine.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Using the two standard affine charts $U_0\cong\Spec k[t]$ and
$U_1\cong\Spec k[u]$, with $u=t^{-1}$ on the overlap, a global regular function is a pair
\[
p(t)\in k[t],\qquad q(u)\in k[u]
\]
whose restrictions agree in $k[t,t^{-1}]$.  Hence
\[
p(t)=q(t^{-1}).
\]
The left side contains only nonnegative powers of $t$ and the right side only nonpositive
powers.  Their common elements are exactly the constants.  Therefore
\[
\Gamma(\Pone_k,\OO_{\Pone_k})=k.
\]
If $\Pone_k$ were affine, Proposition~\ref{prop:vi18-global-recovery} would give
\[
\Pone_k\cong\Spec k,
\]
which is impossible because $\Pone_k$ is not a one-point space.  Thus $\Pone_k$ is not affine.
\end{proof}

The systematic construction of projective schemes appears later in Volume VI.  The example
is retained here because it is the affineness subproblem explicitly reserved by the legacy
ledger.

\section{Common pitfalls}
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\Spec A$ as a topological space is not yet the affine scheme; the structure sheaf is
part of the object.
\item Homeomorphic spectra need not define isomorphic affine schemes; nilpotent thickenings
are the basic warning.
\item $D(f)$ is not merely homeomorphic to $\Spec A_f$; the canonical statement is an
isomorphism of locally ringed spaces.
\item Matching section rings on isolated basis opens is not enough; the sheaf maps must be
compatible with restrictions.
\item The stalk comparison must preserve the maximal ideals, because morphisms of locally
ringed spaces are local on stalks.
\item Affineness is independent of reducedness, irreducibility, and integrality.
\item The equality $\Gamma(X,\OO_X)=A$ for $X=\Spec A$ does not by itself establish the
ring--affine-scheme anti-equivalence; morphisms are treated in VI/19.
\item AG3 Problems 12--14 remain reserved for VI/19, VI/21, and VI/20 respectively and
must not be absorbed into this chapter.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Summary}

An affine scheme is the locally ringed space attached to a ring:
\[
X=\Spec A,
\qquad
\Gamma(X,\OO_X)=A,
\qquad
\OO_{X,\mfp}=A_{\mfp}.
\]
Its distinguished opens are affine charts:
\[
\boxed{(D(f),\OO_X|_{D(f)})\cong\Spec A_f.}
\]
This is the full content of legacy AG3 Problem 11 and is now recovered at its reserved
canonical destination.  Affine schemes have bases of affine principal opens and are
quasi-compact, while $\Pone_k$ supplies the reserved non-affine counterexample.  VI/19
next adds the morphism theorem that turns the ring--spectrum construction into the
contravariant equivalence governing affine geometry.

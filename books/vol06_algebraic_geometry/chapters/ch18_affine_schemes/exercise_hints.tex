\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E1.}] Use the definition and VI/17 stalk formula.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E2.}] Use $X=D(1)$ and the principal-open formula.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E3.}] Write $X\cong\Spec A$ and replace $A$ by its global-section ring.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E4.}] A field has only the prime ideal $(0)$.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E5.}] The zero ring has no proper ideals, hence no prime ideals.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E6.}] Use the prime ideals of the PID $k[t]$.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E7.}] The primes are $(0)$ and $(p)$; compute localizations and quotients.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E8.}] Apply the distinguished-open theorem with $f=x$.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E9.}] Use $D(f)\cap D(g)=D(fg)$ and then localize.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E10.}] A unit lies in no prime ideal.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E11.}] Every prime contains every nilpotent; localization at a nilpotent collapses to zero.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E12.}] The complement is $V(f_1,\dots,f_r)$.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E13.}] Refine an open cover by distinguished opens and use the unit-ideal argument.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E14.}] Prime ideals of a product are $\mfp\times B$ or $A\times\mfq$.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E15.}] Closed points are maximal ideals.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E16.}] The spaces are both one point; compare global rings.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E17.}] Localizing $k[t]$ at powers of $t$ gives Laurent polynomials.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E18.}] $D(30)=\Spec\mathbb Z[1/30]$; primes dividing 30 disappear.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E19.}] Use the universal property of localization twice.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E20.}] The ideal is maximal and the quotient is $\mathbb C$.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E21.}] The ring is isomorphic to $k[x]$; identify the corresponding primes.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E22.}] Exactly one prime ideal must exist; it is automatically maximal and equals the nilradical.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E23.}] Distinguished opens containing the point form a basis.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E24.}] An affine-scheme isomorphism induces an isomorphism on global sections.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E25.}] A pair $f(t),g(u)$ agreeing for $u=t^{-1}$ lies in $k[t]\cap k[t^{-1}]$.
\item[\textbf{VI.18.E26.}] A morphism of locally ringed spaces needs a sheaf map whose stalk maps are local.
\end{enumerate}

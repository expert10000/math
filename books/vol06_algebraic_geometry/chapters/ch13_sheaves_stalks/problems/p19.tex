\begin{problem}[Sheaf data on an irreducible space]
Let $X$ be irreducible and $A$ an abelian group. Prove that the naive constant presheaf $A^{pre}$ with value $A$ on nonempty opens and $0$ on the empty open is a sheaf. Explain why this does not contradict the need for the constant sheaf on general spaces.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
The example connects sheaf gluing to irreducibility developed earlier in Volume VI.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Any two nonempty opens in an irreducible space intersect. Thus compatible constants on a cover must all coincide.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
First prove that every nonempty open subset of an irreducible space is itself irreducible, so the intersection graph of a nonempty open cover is complete.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Let $U\subseteq X$ be nonempty open. Since $X$ is irreducible, $U$ is irreducible, hence any two nonempty open subsets of $U$ intersect. For a cover $U=\bigcup U_i$, a compatible family $a_i\in A$ satisfies $a_i=a_j$ whenever both $U_i,U_j$ are nonempty because their overlap is nonempty and restrictions are identities. Thus all nonempty members carry one common value $a$, which is the unique gluing in $A^{pre}(U)$. Locality is equally immediate. The empty set convention handles the empty cover. On a general disconnected space, disjoint cover members need not meet, so different local values can be compatible and the naive presheaf fails gluing; the locally constant sheaf repairs precisely that failure.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Irreducibility can force enough overlap that a seemingly too-rigid presheaf becomes a sheaf.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Determine the weakest connectedness condition on the intersection graph of every open cover that makes the same argument work.

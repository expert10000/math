\begin{problem}[Stalks of the constant sheaf and the skyscraper sheaf]
Let $\underline A$ be the constant sheaf on $X$, and let $\mathcal S_x(B)$ be the skyscraper sheaf at $x$. Compute all stalks of both sheaves. For the skyscraper, give the correct answer on an arbitrary topological space and then simplify it under the $T_1$ hypothesis.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
These are the two most useful model computations for understanding what filtered colimits over neighborhoods actually do.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
For $\underline A$, every germ has a locally constant representative, hence a single local value. For the skyscraper, ask whether neighborhoods of $y$ can avoid $x$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
At $y$, the skyscraper stalk is $B$ precisely when every neighborhood of $y$ contains $x$.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
For $\underline A$, a locally constant section is constant on some neighborhood of $y$, so the map sending a germ to that local value gives $(\underline A)_y\cong A$. For $\mathcal S_x(B)$, if some neighborhood $V$ of $y$ omits $x$, then the system becomes zero on $V$ and smaller neighborhoods, so the stalk is $0$. If every neighborhood of $y$ contains $x$, every neighborhood value is $B$ and the transition maps are identities, so the stalk is $B$. Thus
\[
(\mathcal S_x(B))_y\cong
\begin{cases}B,&x\in\bigcap_{V\ni y}V,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]
In a $T_1$ space, for $y\neq x$ there is a neighborhood of $y$ avoiding $x$, hence only the stalk at $x$ is $B$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Stalks encode specialization behavior in non-$T_1$ spaces as well as point-local algebra.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Interpret the arbitrary-space answer using the specialization preorder.

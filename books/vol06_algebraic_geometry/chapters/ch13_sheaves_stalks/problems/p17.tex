\begin{problem}[A presheaf with all zero stalks but nonzero global sections]
Construct a presheaf $\mathcal P$ of abelian groups on a topological space such that $\mathcal P(X)\neq0$ but $\mathcal P_x=0$ for every $x\in X$. Prove that $\mathcal P$ cannot be a sheaf and identify exactly which sheaf axiom fails.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This shows why stalkwise detection is a theorem about sheaves, not arbitrary presheaves.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use a discrete space with a nonzero group only on the whole space and zero on all smaller opens.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
On $X=\{p,q\}$ discrete, set $\mathcal P(X)=A\neq0$ and $\mathcal P(V)=0$ for $V\neq X$.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Let $X=\{p,q\}$ discrete and define $\mathcal P(X)=A\neq0$, while $\mathcal P(\{p\})=\mathcal P(\{q\})=\mathcal P(\varnothing)=0$. All proper restrictions from $A$ are zero, so this is a presheaf. For the stalk at $p$, the neighborhood system contains the singleton $\{p\}$, and every global section restricts to zero there; hence the colimit is zero. Similarly $\mathcal P_q=0$. Yet $\mathcal P(X)=A$. Locality fails for the singleton cover: any two distinct elements of $A$ have equal (zero) restrictions to both singletons. Therefore $\mathcal P$ is not a sheaf.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Sheaf locality removes global data that cannot be detected on any sufficiently small open.

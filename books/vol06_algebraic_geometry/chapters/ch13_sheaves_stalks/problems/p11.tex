\begin{problem}[Sheaves on the Sierpinski space]
Let $X=\{\eta,x\}$ with opens $\varnothing,\{\eta\},X$. Classify sheaves of abelian groups on $X$. Compute their stalks, determine morphisms between them, and explain how the restriction map records specialization from $\eta$ toward $x$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Finite spaces convert sheaf theory into explicit algebraic diagrams and expose topology encoded in stalk relations.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
A presheaf is one map $A=\mathcal F(X)\to B=\mathcal F(\{\eta\})$; covers impose no extra condition.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Every open cover of $X$ must contain $X$ because $x$ belongs to no smaller nonempty open.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
A sheaf is precisely a homomorphism $r:A\to B$, with $\mathcal F(X)=A$, $\mathcal F(\{\eta\})=B$, and $\mathcal F(\varnothing)=0$. There is no nontrivial cover of $X$ avoiding $X$ itself, so the sheaf axiom adds nothing. The neighborhood system of $x$ has only $X$, hence $\mathcal F_x\cong A$. The open $\{\eta\}$ is a smallest neighborhood of $\eta$, so $\mathcal F_\eta\cong B$. A morphism between $A\to B$ and $A'\to B'$ is a commutative square of homomorphisms. The restriction map from $A$ to $B$ expresses that information valid near the more closed point $x$ restricts to information near the generic point $\eta$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The specialization order of a finite topological space becomes a diagram of stalk-like algebraic objects.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Generalize to finite Alexandrov spaces and compare sheaves with functors on their specialization posets.

\begin{problem}[A morphism of sheaves is determined by its stalk maps]
Let $\varphi,\psi:\mathcal F\to\mathcal G$ be morphisms of sheaves. Prove that $\varphi_x=\psi_x$ for every $x$ implies $\varphi=\psi$. Also prove that $\varphi=0$ iff every $\varphi_x=0$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the morphism-level form of local detection and is used constantly in later exactness arguments.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Apply equality of germs to the two target sections $\varphi_U(s)$ and $\psi_U(s)$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
For each $s\in\mathcal F(U)$, compute the germ of $\varphi_U(s)$ at $x$.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
For $s\in\mathcal F(U)$ and $x\in U$, naturality gives $(\varphi_U(s))_x=\varphi_x(s_x)$ and similarly for $\psi$. If all stalk maps agree, the two target sections have equal germs at every point. Since $\mathcal G$ is a sheaf, they are equal. This holds for every $U$ and $s$, hence $\varphi=\psi$. Taking $\psi=0$ yields the zero criterion.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
All information in a sheaf morphism can be recovered from the family of local maps.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
In VI/16, combine this with kernels and images to prove exactness can be checked stalkwise.

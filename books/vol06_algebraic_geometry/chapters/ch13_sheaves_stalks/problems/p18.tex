\begin{problem}[Local branches and failure of global lifting]
Consider the covering map $p:\CC^*\to\CC^*$, $p(w)=w^n$ for $n\ge2$. Let $\mathcal S(U)$ be the set of continuous sections of $p$ over $U$. Prove $\mathcal S$ is a sheaf, compute its stalk cardinalities, and show $\mathcal S(\CC^*)=\varnothing$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This packages roots as a sheaf of local choices and displays monodromy before formal covering-space language is used in this volume.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
A small simply connected neighborhood has exactly $n$ continuous root branches. A global section would give a continuous $n$th root of the identity map on the circle.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Restrict a hypothetical global section to $S^1$ and compare degrees: $\deg(z\mapsto s(z)^n)=n\deg(s)$.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Sections of any map glue as maps when compatible, so $\mathcal S$ is a sheaf. Around each $z\in\CC^*$ choose a small disk avoiding $0$; there are exactly $n$ continuous branches of the $n$th root, so the stalk has $n$ elements. If a global section $s$ existed, restrict to $S^1$. Then $s:S^1\to S^1$ after radial normalization and $s(z)^n=z$. Taking degrees gives $n\deg(s)=1$, impossible for integer degree. Hence no global section exists.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
All stalks can be nonempty (even uniformly finite) while no global section exists.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Study the associated locally constant sheaf of $n$th roots of unity acting transitively on each stalk.

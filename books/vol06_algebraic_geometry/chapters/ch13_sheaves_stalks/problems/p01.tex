\begin{problem}[The sheaf axiom as an equalizer]
Let $\mathcal F$ be a presheaf of abelian groups and $U=\bigcup_iU_i$ an open cover. Define
\[
\Delta:\mathcal F(U)\to\prod_i\mathcal F(U_i),
\]
and the two restriction maps $\alpha,\beta$ to $\prod_{i,j}\mathcal F(U_i\cap U_j)$. Prove that $\mathcal F$ is a sheaf exactly when, for every cover, $\Delta$ identifies $\mathcal F(U)$ with the equalizer of $\alpha$ and $\beta$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This turns the verbal locality-and-gluing axioms into an algebraic universal property used throughout sheaf theory.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Separate uniqueness from existence: injectivity of $\Delta$ is locality, while its image condition is gluing.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Write out $\alpha((s_i))$ and $\beta((s_i))$ coordinatewise on every pairwise overlap.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
If $\mathcal F$ is a sheaf, locality says $\Delta$ is injective. A family $(s_i)$ lies in $\ker(\alpha-\beta)$ exactly when $s_i|_{U_i\cap U_j}=s_j|_{U_i\cap U_j}$ for all $i,j$. Gluing supplies a section $s\in\mathcal F(U)$ with $\Delta(s)=(s_i)$, so the image is exactly the equalizer. Conversely, if $\Delta$ is injective, two sections with identical restrictions are equal, giving locality. If every compatible family lies in the equalizer and hence in the image of $\Delta$, a gluing exists; injectivity makes it unique.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The sheaf axiom became an exactness/equalizer statement, the form later generalized to Cech complexes.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Rewrite the condition for sheaves of sets using categorical equalizers rather than subtraction.

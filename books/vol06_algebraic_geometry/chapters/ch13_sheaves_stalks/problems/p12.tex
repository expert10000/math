\begin{problem}[A sheaf of local logarithms with no global section]
On $X=\CC^*$, let $\mathcal L(U)$ be the set of holomorphic functions $g$ on $U$ satisfying $e^{g(z)}=z$. Prove that $\mathcal L$ is a sheaf of sets, that every stalk is nonempty, and that $\mathcal L(X)$ is empty.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This sharply separates local existence from global existence and gives geometric meaning to stalks without yet invoking cohomology.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Local logarithms exist on small disks avoiding $0$. A global logarithm would have derivative $1/z$ and lead to a contradiction around the unit circle.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
If $g$ were global, differentiate $e^g=z$ to obtain $g' = 1/z$ and integrate around $|z|=1$.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Restriction is clear. Compatible local logarithms agree as functions on overlaps and therefore glue to a holomorphic function $g$; the equation $e^g=z$ holds locally and hence globally, so $\mathcal L$ is a sheaf. Around any $x\neq0$, choose a small simply connected disk avoiding $0$ and a holomorphic branch of logarithm, so every stalk is nonempty. If a global $g$ existed, differentiating gives $g'(z)=1/z$. But the integral of a derivative around a closed curve is zero, whereas
\[
\int_{|z|=1}\frac{dz}{z}=2\pi i\neq0.
\]
Contradiction. Thus $\mathcal L(\CC^*)=\varnothing$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Stalkwise existence is only local existence; topology can obstruct global gluing.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Repeat for local square roots and compare the two branches on simply connected subdomains.

\begin{problem}[Basis criterion for sheaves]
Let $\mathcal B$ be a basis for $X$. Suppose groups $F(B)$ and restriction maps are given for basis opens, with locality and gluing for basis covers of basis opens. Construct a sheaf $\widetilde F$ on all opens and prove uniqueness up to unique isomorphism.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the practical engine behind constructing the structure sheaf from principal opens $D(f)$.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
For an arbitrary open $U$, define sections as compatible families $(s_B)$ over basis opens $B\subseteq U$, or equivalently as the inverse limit of the basis data.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Set
\[
\widetilde F(U)=\varprojlim_{B\in\mathcal B,\,B\subseteq U}F(B).
\]
Then verify gluing componentwise.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Define $\widetilde F(U)$ to be families $(s_B)$ indexed by basis opens $B\subseteq U$ such that whenever $C\subseteq B$ are basis opens, $s_B|_C=s_C$. Restriction to $V\subseteq U$ forgets components not contained in $V$. This is a presheaf. If compatible families are given on an open cover $U_i$, then for each basis $B\subseteq U$ cover $B$ by basis opens $C\subseteq B\cap U_i$ and glue their specified sections using the assumed basis sheaf condition. The result is independent of choices by locality and produces a compatible family over all $B\subseteq U$. Uniqueness is again local. On a basis open $B$, the canonical map $F(B)\to\widetilde F(B)$ is an isomorphism by the basis locality/gluing hypotheses. Any other sheaf extending $F$ has sections over $U$ determined by their restrictions to basis opens, giving a unique natural isomorphism with $\widetilde F$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The full sheaf can be reconstructed from sufficiently fine local building blocks.

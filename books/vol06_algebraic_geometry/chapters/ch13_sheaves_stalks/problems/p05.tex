\begin{problem}[Germs as an explicit equivalence relation]
Fix $x\in X$ and a presheaf $\mathcal F$. On pairs $(U,s)$ with $x\in U$ and $s\in\mathcal F(U)$, define $(U,s)\sim(V,t)$ when $s$ and $t$ agree on some neighborhood of $x$ inside $U\cap V$. Prove that this is an equivalence relation and that its set of classes has the universal property of the filtered colimit $\varinjlim_{x\in U}\mathcal F(U)$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the concrete construction hidden behind the notation $\mathcal F_x$.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Transitivity uses intersection of witnessing neighborhoods. For the colimit property, compatible maps out of neighborhood sections must identify equivalent pairs.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Given compatible maps $q_U:\mathcal F(U)\to Q$, define $q([U,s])=q_U(s)$ and prove well-definedness by shrinking.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Reflexivity and symmetry are immediate. If $(U,s)\sim(V,t)$ on $W_1$ and $(V,t)\sim(Z,r)$ on $W_2$, then on the neighborhood $W_1\cap W_2$ of $x$ the restrictions of $s$ and $r$ agree, proving transitivity. Let $G$ be the set/group of equivalence classes. Each $\mathcal F(U)$ maps to $G$ by $s\mapsto[U,s]$, and these maps commute with restriction. If a compatible family $q_U:\mathcal F(U)\to Q$ is given, set $q([U,s])=q_U(s)$. If $(U,s)\sim(V,t)$, choose $W$ where they agree; compatibility gives $q_U(s)=q_W(s|_W)=q_W(t|_W)=q_V(t)$. Thus $q$ is well defined and uniquely factors every $q_U$. This is the colimit universal property.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The stalk is not merely notation for ``values near $x$''; it is a genuine quotient/colimit construction.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Carry out the same construction in rings and modules, checking the algebraic operations on classes.

\begin{problem}[Restriction of sheaves and invariance of stalks]
Let $j:U\hookrightarrow X$ be an open inclusion and $\mathcal F$ a sheaf on $X$. Prove that $\mathcal F|_U$ is a sheaf on $U$ and that for every $x\in U$ there is a canonical isomorphism
\[
(\mathcal F|_U)_x\cong\mathcal F_x.
\]
Prove naturality in $\mathcal F$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Local objects should not change when the ambient space is replaced by an open neighborhood.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Open covers inside $U$ are also open covers inside $X$. Neighborhoods contained in $U$ are cofinal among neighborhoods of $x$ after intersecting with $U$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Map a germ represented by $s\in\mathcal F(V)$ to the same section restricted to $V\cap U$.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
The sheaf axioms for $\mathcal F|_U$ are inherited because every cover by opens of $U$ is a cover by opens of $X$ of the same union. For stalks, the system of neighborhoods $W$ of $x$ with $W\subseteq U$ is cofinal in all neighborhoods of $x$: given $V\ni x$, $V\cap U$ is such a neighborhood and restriction maps $\mathcal F(V)\to\mathcal F(V\cap U)$. Cofinal filtered colimits are canonically isomorphic, yielding $(\mathcal F|_U)_x\cong\mathcal F_x$. A morphism of sheaves commutes with all restriction maps, so these isomorphisms commute with the induced stalk maps.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A stalk depends only on an arbitrarily small neighborhood of its point, not on distant ambient geometry.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Compare with the stalk of a direct image, which generally remembers an entire fiber of nearby points.

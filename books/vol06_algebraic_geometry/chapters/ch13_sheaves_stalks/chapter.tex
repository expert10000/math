\section*{Purpose and learning goals}

VI/12 introduced presheaves: algebraic data attached contravariantly to open sets.  The
present chapter adds the decisive local-to-global principle.  A \emph{sheaf} is a presheaf in
which compatible local sections glue uniquely, and a \emph{stalk} records everything a
sheaf knows arbitrarily close to one point.

These two ideas are complementary.  The sheaf axiom moves from local data to a global
section; the stalk moves from sections on neighborhoods to a single local object.  Together
they form the basic language of modern geometry.  Later chapters will sheafify presheaves,
construct kernels and quotients, define the structure sheaf, and use exact sequences and
cohomology.  All of those constructions depend on the material developed here.

This chapter retains the example-heavy policy introduced in VI/12.  The ordinary example
bank builds fluency with locality, gluing, germs, and stalks; a separate advanced bank
studies discrete and irreducible spaces, skyscraper sheaves, local branches, support,
basis criteria, and stalkwise detection.

After this chapter, the reader should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
\item state the locality and gluing axioms of a sheaf;
\item reformulate the sheaf axiom as an equalizer condition for sheaves of groups;
\item prove that continuous, smooth, holomorphic, and locally constant functions form sheaves;
\item diagnose standard presheaves that fail the sheaf axiom;
\item define germs explicitly as equivalence classes of local sections;
\item construct the stalk $\mathcal F_x$ as a filtered colimit;
\item test equality of germs by shrinking neighborhoods;
\item compute stalks in standard analytic, topological, and algebraic examples;
\item construct the stalk map induced by a morphism of sheaves;
\item prove that sheaf sections and sheaf morphisms are detected by all stalks;
\item prove that a morphism of sheaves is an isomorphism when every stalk map is an isomorphism;
\item define and compute skyscraper sheaves;
\item describe sheaves on discrete spaces directly from their stalks;
\item understand why a naive constant presheaf differs from the constant sheaf;
\item use a basis of the topology to verify the sheaf condition;
\item define the support of a section using its germs;
\item keep sheafification, quotient sheaves, and exactness in their proper later chapters.
\end{itemize}

\section{From presheaves to sheaves}

Let $X$ be a topological space and let $\mathcal F$ be a presheaf of sets, groups, rings,
or modules.  Presheaf axioms tell us only how to restrict data.  They do not say that a
section is determined by its restrictions, nor that compatible local sections arise from a
global one.

\begin{definition}[Sheaf]
A presheaf $\mathcal F$ is a \emph{sheaf} if for every open set $U\subseteq X$, every open
cover $U=\bigcup_{i\in I}U_i$, and every family $s_i\in\mathcal F(U_i)$, the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Locality:} if $s,t\in\mathcal F(U)$ and $s|_{U_i}=t|_{U_i}$ for all $i$, then
$s=t$.
\item \textbf{Gluing:} if
\[
s_i|_{U_i\cap U_j}=s_j|_{U_i\cap U_j}
\qquad\text{for all }i,j,
\]
then there exists $s\in\mathcal F(U)$ with $s|_{U_i}=s_i$ for every $i$.
\end{enumerate}
By locality, a glued section is automatically unique.
\end{definition}

\input{chapters/ch13_sheaves_stalks/figures/vi13_gluing.tex}

The slogan is
\[
\boxed{\text{compatible local data glue to one and only one global section}.}
\]

\section{The empty set and uniqueness}

The empty cover of $\varnothing$ forces a small but useful convention.  For a sheaf of sets,
$\mathcal F(\varnothing)$ is a singleton.  For a sheaf of groups, rings with zero object in
the chosen category, or modules, it is the terminal object appropriate to the category; in
particular for abelian groups and modules,
\[
\mathcal F(\varnothing)=0.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Uniqueness follows from locality]
Suppose a presheaf satisfies locality.  If a compatible family $s_i\in\mathcal F(U_i)$ has
two gluings $s,t\in\mathcal F(U)$, then $s=t$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Both sections restrict to $s_i$ on every $U_i$, so locality gives equality.
\end{proof}

\section{The equalizer form of the sheaf axiom}

For sheaves of abelian groups, the two axioms can be packaged into one exactness statement.
Let $U=\bigcup_iU_i$.  Define
\[
\Delta:\mathcal F(U)\longrightarrow\prod_i\mathcal F(U_i),
\qquad s\longmapsto(s|_{U_i})_i.
\]
The two maps to pairwise overlaps are restriction from the first and second index.

\input{chapters/ch13_sheaves_stalks/figures/vi13_equalizer.tex}

\begin{proposition}[Equalizer criterion]
A presheaf of abelian groups is a sheaf if and only if for every open cover the displayed
sequence is exact at the first two terms; equivalently, $\mathcal F(U)$ is the equalizer of
$\alpha$ and $\beta$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Injectivity of $\Delta$ is locality.  Its image consists precisely of families whose two
restrictions to every overlap agree, and gluing says exactly that every such family lies in
the image.
\end{proof}

% BEGIN VOL06-EXPANSION VI13-example-01
\begin{example}[Gluing regular functions]\label{ex:vi13-expand-01}
If functions on an open cover agree on every overlap, the sheaf axiom produces a unique function on the union.  For ordinary continuous functions, the glued function is defined pointwise from any local representative.
\end{example}
% END VOL06-EXPANSION VI13-example-01
\section{Standard sheaves of functions}

The most important elementary examples are defined by local conditions on functions.

\begin{proposition}[Continuous functions]
For a topological space $X$, the assignment
\[
U\longmapsto C^0(U,\RR)
\]
with ordinary restriction is a sheaf of rings.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Functions agreeing on a cover agree pointwise.  Compatible continuous functions define a
unique function $f:U\to\RR$ pointwise; continuity is local, so $f$ is continuous.
\end{proof}

Exactly the same proof works for smooth functions on a smooth manifold, holomorphic
functions on a complex manifold, and sections of a vector bundle: the defining condition is
local and compatible sections glue as ordinary functions or local bundle sections.

\begin{proposition}[Locally constant functions]
For an abelian group $A$, the assignment
\[
\underline A(U)=\{f:U\to A: f\text{ is locally constant}\}
\]
is a sheaf of abelian groups.  It is called the \emph{constant sheaf} with value $A$.
\end{proposition}

\input{chapters/ch13_sheaves_stalks/figures/vi13_constant_components.tex}

This is different from the naive constant presheaf of VI/12.  On a disconnected open set,
locally constant functions may take different values on different connected components.

\section{Presheaves that fail to be sheaves}

The sheaf axiom is strong enough to rule out natural-looking global conditions.

\begin{example}[Bounded continuous functions]
Let $\mathcal B(U)$ be the bounded continuous real-valued functions on $U$.  Restriction
makes $\mathcal B$ a presheaf.  On $\RR$ cover by $U_n=(-n,n)$.  The functions
$s_n(x)=x$ on $U_n$ are bounded on each $U_n$ and compatible, but their only possible
gluing is $x\mapsto x$, which is unbounded on $\RR$.  Thus $\mathcal B$ is not a sheaf.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[The naive constant presheaf]
On a space with a disconnected open $U=U_1\sqcup U_2$, choose distinct $a,b\in A$ on the
two components.  They are compatible because $U_1\cap U_2=\varnothing$, but there is no
single element of the naive constant presheaf on $U$ restricting to both.  Hence the naive
constant presheaf generally fails gluing.
\end{example}

\section{Germs of sections}

A stalk is built from germs, so it is useful to define germs concretely before invoking a
colimit.

Fix $x\in X$.  Consider pairs $(U,s)$ with $x\in U$ open and $s\in\mathcal F(U)$.  Declare
\[
(U,s)\sim(V,t)
\]
if there exists an open neighborhood $W$ of $x$ with $W\subseteq U\cap V$ and
\[
s|_W=t|_W.
\]
This is an equivalence relation.  The class of $(U,s)$ is the \emph{germ of $s$ at $x$},
denoted $s_x$.

\input{chapters/ch13_sheaves_stalks/figures/vi13_germ_equivalence.tex}

\begin{warning}[Same value does not mean same germ]
For function sheaves, equality $f(x)=g(x)$ is much weaker than $f_x=g_x$.  For example,
$f(t)=t$ and $g(t)=0$ have the same value at $0$ but different germs there.
\end{warning}

\section{The stalk as a filtered colimit}

\begin{definition}[Stalk]
The \emph{stalk} of a presheaf $\mathcal F$ at $x\in X$ is
\[
\mathcal F_x:=\varinjlim_{x\in U}\mathcal F(U),
\]
where neighborhoods are ordered by reverse inclusion through restriction maps.
\end{definition}

\input{chapters/ch13_sheaves_stalks/figures/vi13_stalk_colimit.tex}

The equivalence-class construction above and this filtered-colimit definition give the same
object.  If $\mathcal F$ is a presheaf of groups, rings, or modules, the stalk inherits the
same algebraic structure.

\begin{proposition}[Equality in a stalk]
For sections $s\in\mathcal F(U)$ and $t\in\mathcal F(V)$ with $x\in U\cap V$,
\[
s_x=t_x
\]
if and only if there exists a neighborhood $W\subseteq U\cap V$ of $x$ such that
$s|_W=t|_W$.
\end{proposition}

% BEGIN VOL06-EXPANSION VI13-example-02
\begin{example}[A stalk as germs]\label{ex:vi13-expand-02}
The stalk $F_x$ identifies sections defined on possibly different neighborhoods of $x$ when they agree on some smaller neighborhood.  Thus only behavior arbitrarily close to $x$ survives.
\end{example}
% END VOL06-EXPANSION VI13-example-02
\section{What a stalk remembers and forgets}

A germ remembers all behavior on some sufficiently small unspecified neighborhood; it
forgets what happens far from the point.  In particular:
\begin{itemize}
\item two global sections can define the same germ even when they differ elsewhere;
\item a stalk is generally much richer than point evaluation;
\item global topological obstructions can disappear in a stalk;
\item sheafification will later preserve stalks even when it changes global sections.
\end{itemize}

For the sheaf $C^0_X$ of continuous real-valued functions, $(C^0_X)_x$ is the ring of germs
of continuous functions at $x$.  For $C^\infty_M$, it is the local ring of smooth germs.
For the holomorphic structure sheaf on a complex manifold, it is the local ring of
holomorphic germs.

\section{Morphisms and maps on stalks}

Let $\varphi:\mathcal F\to\mathcal G$ be a morphism of presheaves.  Define
\[
\varphi_x:\mathcal F_x\longrightarrow\mathcal G_x,
\qquad
\varphi_x(s_x):=(\varphi_U(s))_x.
\]
Naturality with restriction makes this independent of the representative.

\input{chapters/ch13_sheaves_stalks/figures/vi13_stalk_map.tex}

\begin{proposition}[Functoriality on stalks]
For morphisms $\mathcal F\xrightarrow{\varphi}\mathcal G\xrightarrow{\psi}\mathcal H$,
\[
(\psi\circ\varphi)_x=\psi_x\circ\varphi_x,
\qquad
(\id_{\mathcal F})_x=\id_{\mathcal F_x}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\section{Stalks detect sections and morphisms}

The next statements are special to sheaves; they can fail for arbitrary presheaves.

\begin{theorem}[Sections are determined by their germs]
Let $\mathcal F$ be a sheaf and $s,t\in\mathcal F(U)$.  Then
\[
s=t\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s_x=t_x\text{ for every }x\in U.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Only the reverse implication needs proof.  For each $x$, equality of germs gives a
neighborhood $V_x\subseteq U$ on which $s$ and $t$ agree.  The $V_x$ cover $U$, and
locality gives $s=t$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Morphisms are determined stalkwise]
If $\varphi,\psi:\mathcal F\to\mathcal G$ are morphisms of sheaves and
$\varphi_x=\psi_x$ for every $x\in X$, then $\varphi=\psi$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Stalkwise isomorphism criterion]
A morphism of sheaves of sets, groups, rings, or modules
\[
\varphi:\mathcal F\longrightarrow\mathcal G
\]
is an isomorphism if and only if every stalk map
\[
\varphi_x:\mathcal F_x\longrightarrow\mathcal G_x
\]
is an isomorphism.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Necessity is immediate.  Conversely, let $t\in\mathcal G(U)$.  Stalkwise surjectivity gives,
for each $x\in U$, a germ $a_x\in\mathcal F_x$ mapping to $t_x$.  Represent $a_x$ by a
section $s_x$ on a neighborhood $V_x$ and shrink so that
$\varphi(s_x)=t|_{V_x}$.  On overlaps, stalkwise injectivity implies the germs of the two
local lifts agree; after shrinking around every point they agree as sections, and locality
shows they agree on the whole overlap.  The sheaf axiom glues the $s_x$ uniquely to a
section $s\in\mathcal F(U)$ with $\varphi(s)=t$.  The same stalkwise injectivity argument
shows uniqueness.  Thus every $\varphi_U$ is bijective and the inverse maps commute with
restrictions.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A boundary for VI/16]
The full theory of injective/surjective morphisms and exact sequences of sheaves is deferred
to VI/16.  In particular, sectionwise surjectivity is stronger than the natural local notion
of surjectivity for sheaves.
\end{remark}

\section{Skyscraper sheaves}

Fix $x\in X$ and an abelian group $A$.

\begin{definition}[Skyscraper sheaf]
Define
\[
\mathcal S_x(A)(U)=
\begin{cases}
A,&x\in U,\\
0,&x\notin U,
\end{cases}
\]
with the evident restriction maps.  This is the \emph{skyscraper sheaf at $x$ with value
$A$}.
\end{definition}

\input{chapters/ch13_sheaves_stalks/figures/vi13_skyscraper.tex}

Its stalk is $A$ at $x$ and $0$ at every $y\neq x$ provided points can be separated by a
neighborhood of $y$ avoiding $x$; in particular this holds in $T_1$ spaces.  On arbitrary
spaces, the stalk at $y$ is $A$ exactly when every neighborhood of $y$ contains $x$.

% BEGIN VOL06-EXPANSION VI13-example-03
\begin{example}[Structure-sheaf stalk preview]\label{ex:vi13-expand-03}
For $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$, the stalk of the structure sheaf at $\mathfrak p$ is $A_{\mathfrak p}$.  Fractions become equivalent precisely when they agree on a sufficiently small basic neighborhood.
\end{example}
% END VOL06-EXPANSION VI13-example-03
\section{Sheaves on discrete spaces}

If $X$ is discrete, every singleton is open and every open $U$ is the disjoint union of its
points.  The sheaf axiom therefore gives a complete classification.

\begin{theorem}[Discrete-space classification]
For a sheaf of sets or abelian groups on a discrete space $X$,
\[
\mathcal F(U)\cong\prod_{x\in U}\mathcal F_x.
\]
Thus a sheaf on $X$ is determined by the family of its stalks.
\end{theorem}

\input{chapters/ch13_sheaves_stalks/figures/vi13_discrete_product.tex}

\section{Sheaf condition on a basis}

In geometry one rarely wants to verify gluing on every open set separately.  A basis is
enough.

\begin{theorem}[Basis criterion]
Let $\mathcal B$ be a basis for the topology of $X$, closed under taking sufficiently small
basis neighborhoods inside intersections.  Suppose algebraic data $\mathcal F(B)$ and
restriction maps are given for $B\in\mathcal B$, and suppose locality and gluing hold for
covers of basis opens by basis opens.  Then the data extend uniquely, up to unique
isomorphism, to a sheaf on all open subsets of $X$.
\end{theorem}

\input{chapters/ch13_sheaves_stalks/figures/vi13_basis_test.tex}

This criterion is the bridge to the structure sheaf: on $\Spec A$, principal opens $D(f)$
form a basis and carry localization rings $A_f$.

\section{Support of a section}

Let $\mathcal F$ be a sheaf of abelian groups and $s\in\mathcal F(U)$.  Define
\[
\operatorname{NZ}(s)=\{x\in U:s_x\neq0\}.
\]
Because vanishing of a germ means vanishing on some neighborhood, the zero-germ locus
\[
\{x\in U:s_x=0\}
\]
is open.  Therefore $\operatorname{NZ}(s)$ is already closed in $U$.

\begin{definition}[Support]
The \emph{support} of $s$ is the closed set
\[
\operatorname{supp}(s)=\{x\in U:s_x\neq0\}.
\]
For ordinary function sheaves this equals the closure of the pointwise nonvanishing set
$\{x:s(x)\neq0\}$.
\end{definition}

\input{chapters/ch13_sheaves_stalks/figures/vi13_support.tex}

For ordinary continuous or smooth functions this agrees with the usual support.

\section{Common pitfalls}

\begin{enumerate}
\item A presheaf is not automatically a sheaf; restriction axioms do not imply gluing.
\item Equality at a point is not equality of germs.
\item A germ is not a section on a distinguished smallest neighborhood; most spaces have no
such neighborhood.
\item Stalks forget global topology.  A global obstruction can vanish locally at every point.
\item A family of arbitrary germs does not necessarily come from a section; the family must
vary locally in a representable way.
\item The naive constant presheaf and the constant sheaf are different on disconnected opens.
\item Stalkwise surjectivity of a morphism does not mean every section has one global lift.
\item The structure sheaf is not yet being defined; VI/17 will specialize the present theory
to regular functions on spectra.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Summary}

A sheaf is a presheaf with a precise local-to-global principle: local equality detects global
equality, and compatible local sections glue uniquely.  A stalk
\[
\mathcal F_x=\varinjlim_{x\in U}\mathcal F(U)
\]
packages all sections defined near a point into germs.  Sheaf sections and sheaf morphisms
are detected by stalks, and a morphism is an isomorphism exactly when every stalk map is.
The examples of locally constant functions, discrete spaces, skyscraper sheaves, and local
function germs show how much geometric information can be organized by these two ideas.

VI/14 now addresses the next unavoidable question: given a presheaf that fails locality or
gluing, how can one construct the closest sheaf without changing its local germs?

\textbf{1.} Reflexivity uses \(\lambda=1\).  Symmetry replaces \(\lambda\) by \(\lambda^{-1}\).  If \(b=\lambda a\) and \(c=\mu b\), then \(c=(\mu\lambda)a\), proving transitivity.

\textbf{2.} The nonzero coordinates are the zeroth and second, so the point lies exactly in \(U_0\) and \(U_2\).

\textbf{3.} The map is \([a_0:\cdots:a_n]\mapsto(a_1/a_0,\ldots,a_n/a_0)\); the inverse sends \((t_1,\ldots,t_n)\) to \([1:t_1:\cdots:t_n]\).

\textbf{4.} On \(u\ne0\), \((u,v)\mapsto(1/u,v/u)\).  The inverse is \((r,s)\mapsto(1/r,s/r)\).

\textbf{5.} In \(U_0\), the overlap is \(D(t)=\Spec k[t,t^{-1}]\).  In \(U_1\) it is \(D(u)\), and the coordinate rings are identified by \(u\mapsto t^{-1}\).

\textbf{6.} For \(S=k[x_0]\), the only homogeneous prime avoiding \((x_0)\) is \((0)\).  The standard chart \(D_+(x_0)\) has ring \(S_{(x_0)}=k\), hence \(\mathbb P^0_k\cong\Spec k\).

\textbf{7.} The complement is \(V_+(x_0)\).  A point there has the form \([0:a_1:\cdots:a_n]\) with not all \(a_i\) zero, hence corresponds to \([a_1:\cdots:a_n]\in\mathbb P^{n-1}\).

\textbf{8.} The chart \(U_0\cong\mathbb A^n\) gives dimension at least \(n\).  Every point has an affine neighborhood of dimension \(n\), so the scheme has dimension at most \(n\).

\textbf{9.} The remaining coordinates are \([x_3:\cdots:x_n]\), giving \(\mathbb P^{n-3}\).

\textbf{10.} The span is \(\{[\lambda:\mu:0]:[\lambda:\mu]\in\mathbb P^1\}\).

\textbf{11.} The point corresponds to the maximal ideal \((t^2+1)\subset\mathbb R[t]\), so its residue field is \(\mathbb R[t]/(t^2+1)\cong\mathbb C\).

\textbf{12.} After tensoring with \(K\), the charts become \(\Spec K[t]\) and \(\Spec K[u]\), and their common localization is still glued by \(u=t^{-1}\).  This is exactly \(\mathbb P^1_K\).

\textbf{13.} In \(S(1)_{(x_i)}\), the element \(x_i\) has shifted degree zero and is invertible in the homogeneous localization, so it freely generates the degree-zero module over \(S_{(x_i)}\).

\textbf{14.} On the overlap, \(x_i^d=(x_i/x_j)^d x_j^d\), so the transition factor is \((x_i/x_j)^d\).

\textbf{15.} The space is \(S_1\), with basis \(x_0,\ldots,x_n\), hence dimension \(n+1\).

\textbf{16.} The dimension is the number of degree-two monomials in \(n+1\) variables, namely \(\binom{n+2}{2}\).

\textbf{17.} On \(U_0\): \(t_2-t_1^2\).  On \(U_1\): \((x_0/x_1)(x_2/x_1)-1\).  On \(U_2\): \(x_0/x_2-(x_1/x_2)^2\).

\textbf{18.} With affine coordinates \(x=x_1/x_0\), \(y=x_2/x_0\), clearing denominators gives \(x_0x_2-x_1^2\).  At infinity \(x_0=0\), so \(x_1=0\); the unique projective point is \([0:0:1]\).

\textbf{19.} Replacing \(a\) by \(\lambda a\) multiplies every degree-\(d\) monomial by \(\lambda^d\), a common scalar, so the projective point is unchanged.

\textbf{20.} \(XZ-Y^2=s^2t^2-(st)^2=0\).

\textbf{21.} If \(x\mapsto\lambda x\) and \(y\mapsto\mu y\), then every product \(x_i y_j\) is multiplied by \(\lambda\mu\), one common scalar.

\textbf{22.} \((su)(tv)-(sv)(tu)=stuv-stuv=0\).

\textbf{23.} For \(d=0\), the theorem gives \(\Gamma(\mathbb P^n,\mathcal O)=S_0=k\).

\textbf{24.} The equality of global rings is not an affine reconstruction theorem for arbitrary schemes.  Indeed \(\mathbb P^n\) has positive dimension and many points for \(n>0\), whereas \(\Spec k\) has dimension zero and one point.

\chapter{Graded Rings}
\label{ch:vi34-graded-rings}

Projective geometry begins with an algebraic asymmetry: scalar multiples should describe the same projective point, so polynomial equations must respect degree. Graded rings are the algebraic device that records this degree information. The legacy AG4 source places graded rings, homogeneous primes, the irrelevant ideal, and degree-zero homogeneous localization at the entrance to the construction of \(\operatorname{Proj}\). This chapter extracts that algebra into a self-contained bridge and extends it with graded modules, shifts, exact sequences, Veronese subrings, weighted gradings, and affine-cone geometry. The actual space \(\operatorname{Proj}S\) is reserved for VI/35.

The preceding chapter developed the affine dictionary \(M\mapsto\widetilde M\). The projective replacement will use a graded module \(M\), localize at a homogeneous element \(f\), and then retain only the degree-zero part:
\[
\boxed{(A,M,M_f)\quad\leadsto\quad(S,M,(S_f)_0,(M_f)_0).}
\]

\section*{Learning goals}
By the end of the chapter, the reader should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
\item define \(\mathbb Z\)-graded and nonnegatively graded rings and identify homogeneous elements;
\item recognize homogeneous ideals, quotients, radicals, and homogeneous prime ideals;
\item use the irrelevant ideal \(S_+=\bigoplus_{n>0}S_n\);
\item define graded modules, homogeneous maps, and shifts \(M(d)\);
\item localize graded rings and modules at homogeneous elements;
\item compute \(S_{(f)}=(S_f)_0\) and \(M_{(f)}=(M_f)_0\);
\item calculate \(k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]_{(x_i)}\);
\item construct Veronese subrings and compare standard with weighted gradings;
\item interpret \(\operatorname{Spec}S\) as an affine cone in standard homogeneous situations;
\item state exactly which graded-algebra data VI/35 needs to construct \(\operatorname{Proj}S\).
\end{itemize}

\section{Graded rings and homogeneous elements}
\begin{definition}[Graded ring]
A \emph{\(\mathbb Z\)-graded ring} is a ring \(S\) with additive subgroups \(S_n\), \(n\in\mathbb Z\), such that
\[
S=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb Z}S_n,\qquad S_mS_n\subseteq S_{m+n}.
\]
If \(S_n=0\) for \(n<0\), the grading is \emph{nonnegative}. A nonzero element of \(S_n\) is homogeneous of degree \(n\).
\end{definition}

Every \(s\in S\) has a unique finite decomposition \(s=\sum_ns_n\), \(s_n\in S_n\). If \(a,b\) are homogeneous and \(ab\neq0\), then \(\deg(ab)=\deg a+\deg b\).

The standard example is
\[
S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n],\qquad \deg x_i=1,
\]
where \(S_d\) consists of homogeneous polynomials of total degree \(d\). Positive weights \(\deg x_i=w_i\) give weighted gradings.

\section{Graded maps and standard graded algebras}
A ring homomorphism \(\varphi:S\to T\) is graded of degree zero if \(\varphi(S_n)\subseteq T_n\) for all \(n\).

A nonnegatively graded ring \(S\) is \emph{standard graded over \(S_0\)} if it is generated as an \(S_0\)-algebra by \(S_1\). Thus \(k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]\) with all variables of degree one is standard graded, while \(k[x,y]\) with \(\deg x=1,\deg y=2\) is not.

\section{Homogeneous ideals and quotients}
\begin{definition}[Homogeneous ideal]
An ideal \(I\subseteq S\) is homogeneous if it is generated by homogeneous elements.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Component criterion]
For an ideal \(I\subseteq S\), the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(I\) is homogeneous;
\item if \(f\in I\), every homogeneous component of \(f\) lies in \(I\);
\item \(I=\bigoplus_n(I\cap S_n)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

If \(I\) is homogeneous, then \(S/I\) inherits a grading. Conversely, the natural quotient map is graded for the quotient grading only when the kernel is homogeneous.

\begin{proposition}[Homogeneous radicals]
If \(I\) is homogeneous, then \(\sqrt I\) is homogeneous.
\end{proposition}

\section{Homogeneous prime ideals}
A prime ideal need not be homogeneous. The primes compatible with projective grading are the homogeneous ones.

\begin{proposition}[Homogeneous primality test]
Let \(\mathfrak p\subsetneq S\) be homogeneous. Then \(\mathfrak p\) is prime if and only if
\[
ab\in\mathfrak p\Longrightarrow a\in\mathfrak p\text{ or }b\in\mathfrak p
\]
for all homogeneous \(a,b\in S\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Pass to \(S/\mathfrak p\). Suppose products of nonzero homogeneous elements are nonzero. If nonzero \(f,g\) satisfy \(fg=0\), choose the least-degree nonzero homogeneous components \(f_r,g_s\). Then \(f_rg_s\) is the least-degree component of \(fg\) and cannot cancel with any other term, contradiction.
\end{proof}

\section{The irrelevant ideal}
Assume \(S=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}S_n\).

\begin{definition}[Irrelevant ideal]
The irrelevant ideal is
\[
S_+:=\bigoplus_{n>0}S_n.
\]
\end{definition}

In \(S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]\) with the standard grading,
\[
S_+=(x_0,\ldots,x_n).
\]
The terminology anticipates VI/35: homogeneous primes containing all of \(S_+\) correspond to the cone-vertex direction and are excluded from the projective spectrum.

\section{Graded modules}
\begin{definition}[Graded module]
A graded \(S\)-module is an \(S\)-module \(M\) with
\[
M=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb Z}M_n,\qquad S_mM_n\subseteq M_{m+n}.
\]
\end{definition}

A map \(\varphi:M\to N\) is homogeneous of degree \(r\) if \(\varphi(M_n)\subseteq N_{n+r}\). Degree-zero maps are the usual morphisms of graded modules. Kernels, images, and cokernels of degree-zero maps are graded, so exactness is degreewise.

\section{Degree shifts}
\begin{definition}[Shift]
For \(d\in\mathbb Z\), define
\[
M(d)_n:=M_{n+d}.
\]
\end{definition}
An element originally of degree \(r\) has degree \(r-d\) in \(M(d)\).

If \(f\in S_d\) is homogeneous, multiplication by \(f\) is a degree-zero map
\[
S(-d)\xrightarrow{\cdot f}S,
\]
because \(S(-d)_n=S_{n-d}\) maps to \(S_n\).

The ideal $(x_0^2,x_0x_1,x_1^2)$ in $k[x_0,x_1]$ is homogeneous because it is generated by homogeneous elements.  Its quotient inherits a grading.

\section{Homogeneous localization}
Let \(f\in S_d\) be homogeneous of positive degree. The ordinary localization \(S_f\) becomes \(\mathbb Z\)-graded by
\[
\deg\!\left(\frac{a}{f^r}\right)=\deg a-rd
\]
for homogeneous \(a\). Negative degrees appear because \(f\) becomes invertible.

For a graded module \(M\), define the grading on \(M_f\) similarly:
\[
\deg\!\left(\frac{m}{f^r}\right)=\deg m-rd.
\]
Localization is exact and preserves homogeneous degree-zero maps.

\section{Degree-zero localization}
\begin{definition}[Degree-zero homogeneous localization]
For homogeneous \(f\) of positive degree, write
\[
S_{(f)}:=(S_f)_0,\qquad M_{(f)}:=(M_f)_0.
\]
\end{definition}

Thus \(S_{(f)}\) consists of fractions \(a/f^r\) satisfying \(\deg a=r\deg f\). It is an ordinary ring, and \(M_{(f)}\) is an ordinary \(S_{(f)}\)-module.

For the standard polynomial ring and \(f=x_i\),
\[
S_{(x_i)}
=k\!\left[\frac{x_0}{x_i},\ldots,\widehat{\frac{x_i}{x_i}},\ldots,\frac{x_n}{x_i}\right]
\cong k[t_1,\ldots,t_n].
\]
This is the algebraic reason projective \(n\)-space will have affine \(n\)-space charts.

\input{chapters/ch34_graded_rings/figures/figure_06}

\begin{proposition}[Exactness of degree-zero localization]
For an exact sequence of graded modules with degree-zero maps,
\[
0\to M'\to M\to M''\to0,
\]
and homogeneous \(f\), the sequence
\[
0\to M'_{(f)}\to M_{(f)}\to M''_{(f)}\to0
\]
is exact.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Ordinary localization is exact and yields an exact sequence of graded modules. Exactness of a sequence of graded modules with degree-zero maps holds in each degree, hence in degree zero.
\end{proof}

\section{Localization in stages and overlap algebra}
When homogeneous \(f,g\) have the same positive degree, \(g/f\) is degree zero in \(S_f\), and
\[
(S_{(f)})_{g/f}\cong S_{(fg)}.
\]
More generally one uses suitable equal-degree powers of \(f\) and \(g\). This compatibility is exactly what the affine chart rings will need on overlaps in VI/35.

\section{Localized shifted modules}
For a graded module \(M\),
\[
M(d)_{(f)}=(M(d)_f)_0
\]
is an \(S_{(f)}\)-module. In particular, the modules \(S(d)_{(f)}\) are the local algebra from which twisting sheaves will later be built. This chapter constructs the modules but does not yet glue them into sheaves on \(\operatorname{Proj}S\).

\section{Veronese subrings}
\begin{definition}[Veronese subring]
For \(d\ge1\), the \(d\)-th Veronese subring is
\[
S^{(d)}:=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}S_{nd},
\]
regraded by \((S^{(d)})_n=S_{nd}\).
\end{definition}

For \(S=k[x,y]\),
\[
S^{(2)}=k[x^2,xy,y^2]\cong k[u,v,w]/(uw-v^2).
\]
Thus a regrading can turn a polynomial ring into the coordinate ring of a quadratic cone. VI/35 will explain the projective invariance behind Veronese regrading.

For a graded module \(M\), one likewise defines
\[
M^{(d)}:=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb Z}M_{nd}.
\]

\section{Weighted gradings}
Let
\[
S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n],\qquad \deg x_i=w_i>0.
\]
A monomial has weighted degree \(\sum_i a_iw_i\). Weighted gradings obey the same formal definitions, but \(S_1\) can vanish and the chart rings \(S_{(x_i)}\) need not look like ordinary polynomial rings. This is why hypotheses such as ``standard graded'' must be stated rather than assumed.

\section{Affine cones and scaling}
Let
\[
S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]/I
\]
with \(I\) homogeneous. Then \(C=\operatorname{Spec}S\) is naturally an affine cone: for every homogeneous \(F\) of degree \(d\),
\[
F(\lambda a)=\lambda^dF(a).
\]
Hence homogeneous zero loci are stable under scalar multiplication.

In the standard case over a field, \(S_+\) cuts out the cone vertex. Projectivization will discard that vertex and encode the remaining scaling directions without choosing representatives.

\input{chapters/ch34_graded_rings/figures/figure_08}

\section{The bridge to \texorpdfstring{\(\operatorname{Proj}\)}{Proj}}
VI/35 will define \(\operatorname{Proj}S\) from homogeneous prime ideals not containing \(S_+\). For homogeneous \(f\in S_+\), the basic projective open will have coordinate ring
\[
\boxed{S_{(f)}=(S_f)_0.}
\]
For a graded module \(M\), the corresponding local module will be
\[
\boxed{M_{(f)}=(M_f)_0.}
\]
The purpose of VI/34 is to make these formulas routine before topology and sheaf gluing are introduced.

\section{Common pitfalls}
\begin{itemize}
\item A general element of a graded ring need not have a degree; only nonzero homogeneous elements do.
\item Not every ideal is homogeneous.
\item A prime ideal need not be homogeneous.
\item In a standard polynomial ring, \(S_+\) is \((x_0,\ldots,x_n)\), not the zero ideal.
\item \(S_f\) and \(S_{(f)}\) are different: the latter keeps only degree zero.
\item Homogeneous localization naturally creates negative degrees.
\item Shift conventions vary. This chapter uses \(M(d)_n=M_{n+d}\).
\item Standard and weighted gradings are not interchangeable.
\item VI/34 does not define \(\operatorname{Proj}\); it prepares the algebra that VI/35 will assemble geometrically.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Source and scope audit}
The inspected project audit describes legacy `theory-of-algebraic-geometry-4.tex` as containing graded rings, homogeneous primes, the irrelevant ideal, degree-zero localization, \(\operatorname{Proj}S\), its structure sheaf, standard opens, projective space, and closed projective subschemes. The canonical architecture isolates the `graded ring|homogeneous` thematic block for VI/34. This chapter therefore consumes that graded-algebra bridge and does not claim a separately numbered legacy problem. Graded modules, shifts, Veronese subrings, weighted gradings, and the affine-cone synthesis are canonical extensions needed to make the next chapter self-contained.

\section*{Chapter synthesis}
A graded ring remembers how algebra transforms under scaling. Homogeneous ideals preserve that structure, homogeneous primes are the primes compatible with it, and the irrelevant ideal isolates the positive-degree direction removed by projective geometry. After localizing at homogeneous \(f\), taking degree zero gives
\[
S_{(f)}=(S_f)_0,\qquad M_{(f)}=(M_f)_0.
\]
These ordinary rings and modules are the affine algebra from which VI/35 will build \(\operatorname{Proj}S\) and its sheaves.

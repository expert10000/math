% VI34-DETAILED-CHALLENGE-01
\subsection*{Challenge VI.34.C1 solution}
\begin{solution}
Let
\[
I^{\mathrm{sat}}
=
I:S_+^\infty
=
\bigcup_{m\ge0}(I:S_+^m).
\]
For fixed \(m\), the ideal \(I:S_+^m\) is homogeneous. Indeed, write \(s=\sum_ns_n\) and suppose
\[
sS_+^m\subseteq I.
\]
For every homogeneous \(h\in S_+^m\),
\[
sh=\sum_n s_nh\in I.
\]
The summands have distinct degrees, so the component criterion for the homogeneous ideal \(I\) gives
\[
s_nh\in I
\]
for every \(n\). Hence each \(s_n\in I:S_+^m\). Since the colon ideals form an ascending chain, their union is
homogeneous. Thus
\[
\boxed{I^{\mathrm{sat}}\text{ is homogeneous}}.
\]

Now let \(f\in S_+\) be homogeneous. We claim
\[
(I^{\mathrm{sat}})_f=I_f.
\]
One inclusion is obvious. If \(h\in I^{\mathrm{sat}}\), choose \(m\) with
\[
hS_+^m\subseteq I.
\]
Then \(f^m\in S_+^m\), so
\[
hf^m\in I.
\]
After inverting \(f\), this implies \(h/1\in I_f\). Therefore the localizations agree.

This is exactly why saturation is projectively natural. The projective basic opens will be built from homogeneous
\(f\in S_+\), and on every such chart \(I\) and \(I^{\mathrm{sat}}\) define the same localized ideal. Saturation
removes homogeneous information supported only in the irrelevant direction, which is the cone-vertex direction
discarded by projectivization.
\end{solution}

% VI34-DETAILED-CHALLENGE-02
\subsection*{Challenge VI.34.C2 solution}
\begin{solution}
Let
\[
S^{(d)}=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}S_{nd}
\]
with the regrading \((S^{(d)})_n=S_{nd}\). Suppose
\[
f\in S_{md}.
\]
Then \(f\) is homogeneous of degree \(m\) in \(S^{(d)}\). A degree-zero fraction in the Veronese chart has the form
\[
\frac a{f^r}
\]
with
\[
a\in(S^{(d)})_{rm}=S_{rmd}.
\]
But that is exactly the condition for the same fraction to lie in \((S_f)_0\). Hence
\[
\boxed{(S^{(d)})_{(f)}\cong S_{(f)}}.
\]

This comparison requires that the chosen chart element actually belong to the Veronese. If \(g\in S\) has degree
not divisible by \(d\), replace it by \(g^d\). Then \(g^d\in S^{(d)}\), and ordinary localization at \(g\) or
\(g^d\) is the same. Moreover the degree-zero pieces agree:
\[
S_{(g)}=S_{(g^d)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
S_{(g)}
\cong
(S^{(d)})_{(g^d)}.
}
\]

The same argument works for graded modules using the Veronese module \(M^{(d)}\), with the usual care about which
degrees survive. This chart equality is the algebraic core of the later theorem that
\[
\Proj S\cong\Proj S^{(d)}.
\]
VI/34 proves the chart algebra only; VI/35 will supply the topology and sheaf gluing.
\end{solution}

% VI34-DETAILED-CHALLENGE-03
\subsection*{Challenge VI.34.C3 solution}
\begin{solution}
Take the weighted polynomial ring
\[
S=k[x,y,z],
\qquad
\deg x=\deg y=1,\quad \deg z=2.
\]
The \(x\)-chart is straightforward:
\[
S_{(x)}
=
k\!\left[\frac yx,\frac z{x^2}\right]
\cong k[u,v],
\]
because \(y/x\) and \(z/x^2\) have degree zero. Similarly
\[
S_{(y)}
\cong
k\!\left[\frac xy,\frac z{y^2}\right]
\cong k[u',v'].
\]

The \(z\)-chart is different. Since \(z\) has degree \(2\), a degree-zero numerator over \(z^r\) must have weighted
degree \(2r\). The basic degree-\(2\) monomials are
\[
x^2,\quad xy,\quad y^2,\quad z.
\]
Set
\[
u=\frac{x^2}{z},
\qquad
v=\frac{xy}{z},
\qquad
w=\frac{y^2}{z}.
\]
Then
\[
uw=v^2.
\]
Every degree-zero monomial is generated by \(u,v,w\), and the relation is the only one. Hence
\[
\boxed{
S_{(z)}
\cong
k[u,v,w]/(uw-v^2).
}
\]

Thus the weighted chart corresponding to the weight-two variable is an affine quadric cone, not affine plane.
Two phenomena absent from ordinary projective space are already visible:

\begin{itemize}
\item chart rings need not be polynomial rings;
\item singular or quotient-type affine charts can appear even though the original graded ring is a polynomial ring.
\end{itemize}

The simpler two-variable weighting
\[
\deg x=2,\qquad\deg y=3
\]
also gives
\[
S_{(x)}=k[y^2/x^3],
\qquad
S_{(y)}=k[x^3/y^2],
\]
showing that equal-degree powers rather than first powers control the overlap algebra.
\end{solution}

% VI34-DETAILED-CHALLENGE-04
\subsection*{Challenge VI.34.C4 solution}
\begin{solution}
Let \(M\) be a graded \(S\)-module generated by homogeneous elements
\[
m_1,\ldots,m_r
\]
of degrees
\[
d_1,\ldots,d_r.
\]
Under the convention
\[
S(-d_i)_n=S_{n-d_i},
\]
the element \(1\in S(-d_i)\) has shifted degree \(d_i\). Therefore
\[
1_i\longmapsto m_i
\]
defines a degree-zero graded surjection
\[
F_0:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^rS(-d_i)\twoheadrightarrow M.
\]

Let
\[
K=\ker(F_0\to M).
\]
Since the map has degree zero, \(K\) is graded. Suppose \(K\) is generated by homogeneous relations
\[
r_1,\ldots,r_s
\]
of degrees \(e_1,\ldots,e_s\). Then each \(r_j\) defines a degree-zero map
\[
S(-e_j)\longrightarrow F_0,
\qquad
1\longmapsto r_j.
\]
Combining them gives
\[
F_1:=\bigoplus_{j=1}^sS(-e_j)\longrightarrow F_0
\]
with image \(K\). Hence
\[
\boxed{
F_1\longrightarrow F_0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow0
}
\]
is a finite graded presentation by shifts.

This makes clear what ``finitely presented in the graded sense'' means: finitely many homogeneous generators and
finitely many homogeneous relations, with the shifts recording their degrees.

If \(S\) is Noetherian and \(M\) is finitely generated graded, \(K\) is finitely generated as an ordinary module.
Because \(K\) is graded, the homogeneous components of a finite set of generators form a finite homogeneous
generating set. Thus finitely generated graded modules over Noetherian graded rings admit finite presentations by
finite sums of shifts.
\end{solution}

% VI34-DETAILED-CHALLENGE-05
\subsection*{Challenge VI.34.C5 solution}
\begin{solution}
Using only the algebra developed in VI/34 and the sheaf gluing of VI/33, the future \(\Proj\) construction should
be organized into definitions and theorems.

\textbf{Definitions to make in VI/35.}

For a nonnegatively graded ring \(S\), define
\[
\Proj S
=
\left\{
\mathfrak p\subseteq S:
\mathfrak p\text{ homogeneous prime and }S_+\not\subseteq\mathfrak p
\right\}.
\]
For homogeneous \(f\in S_+\), define the basic subset
\[
D_+(f)
=
\{\mathfrak p\in\Proj S:f\notin\mathfrak p\}.
\]
The candidate affine coordinate ring is
\[
\boxed{S_{(f)}=(S_f)_0}.
\]
For a graded module \(M\), the candidate local module is
\[
\boxed{M_{(f)}=(M_f)_0}.
\]

\textbf{Statements requiring proof.}

First, the \(D_+(f)\) must form a basis for a topology on \(\Proj S\). In particular,
\[
D_+(f)\cap D_+(g)
\]
must again be describable by homogeneous localization.

Second, there must be canonical homeomorphisms
\[
D_+(f)\cong\Spec S_{(f)}.
\]
This is the decisive affine-chart theorem.

Third, on overlaps the ring identifications must agree. VI/34 has already supplied the algebra:
after replacing \(f,g\) by equal-degree powers,
\[
(S_{(f)})_{g^b/f^a}
\cong
S_{(f^ag^b)}.
\]
These maps must satisfy the cocycle condition.

Fourth, the affine structure sheaves on the rings \(S_{(f)}\) must glue to a sheaf
\[
\mathcal O_{\Proj S}.
\]
VI/33 gives the general gluing formalism once the overlap ring maps are known.

Fifth, for a graded module \(M\), the affine sheaves associated with \(M_{(f)}\) must glue compatibly to a
quasi-coherent sheaf \(\widetilde M\) on \(\Proj S\).

Finally, shifts \(M(d)\) should give the future twisting sheaves. VI/34 already predicts their local models:
\[
M(d)_{(f)}=(M_f)_d.
\]

Thus VI/35 should not invent new algebra. Its task is to prove that the homogeneous-prime topology and the
degree-zero localization data glue into a scheme and into quasi-coherent sheaves.
\end{solution}

% VI34 detailed exercise solutions -- VI/24+ proof-depth standard.

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-01
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E1.}
Suppose
\[
s=\sum_n s_n=\sum_n t_n,\qquad s_n,t_n\in S_n.
\]
Then
\[
0=\sum_n(s_n-t_n).
\]
Because \(S=\bigoplus_nS_n\) is a direct sum, every homogeneous component must vanish. Hence
\(s_n=t_n\) for every \(n\). Therefore homogeneous decomposition is unique.

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-02
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E2.}
If \(a\in S_m\) and \(b\in S_n\), the grading rule gives
\[
ab\in S_{m+n}.
\]
Since \(ab\ne0\), it belongs to one and only one graded piece. Thus
\[
\boxed{\deg(ab)=m+n=\deg a+\deg b}.
\]
The nonzero hypothesis matters because zero has no unique degree.

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-03
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E3.}
The degree-\(d\) monomials
\[
x^ay^bz^c,\qquad a+b+c=d,
\]
form a basis of \(S_d\). The number of nonnegative solutions is
\[
\binom{d+2}{2}
\]
by stars and bars. Hence
\[
\boxed{\dim_kS_d=\binom{d+2}{2}}.
\]

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-04
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E4.}
Suppose \(I\) is generated by homogeneous elements \(h_\alpha\). If
\[
f=\sum_\alpha a_\alpha h_\alpha\in I,
\]
decompose each coefficient as \(a_\alpha=\sum_j a_{\alpha,j}\) into homogeneous parts. Then
\[
f=\sum_{\alpha,j}a_{\alpha,j}h_\alpha,
\]
and each product is homogeneous and still belongs to \(I\). Grouping the products by total degree gives exactly
the homogeneous components of \(f\). Hence every homogeneous component of \(f\) lies in \(I\).

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-05
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E5.}
If \((x+1)\) were homogeneous, the component criterion applied to
\[
x+1\in(x+1)
\]
would imply that both \(x\) and \(1\) lie in the ideal. Then \(1\in(x+1)\), so the ideal would be the whole ring.
But \(x+1\) is not a unit. Contradiction. Thus
\[
\boxed{(x+1)\text{ is not homogeneous}}.
\]

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-06
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E6.}
If \(I\) is homogeneous, define
\[
(S/I)_n=(S_n+I)/I\cong S_n/(I\cap S_n).
\]
Every coset \(s+I\) decomposes as the sum of the cosets of the homogeneous components of \(s\). If a sum of
homogeneous cosets is zero, the corresponding sum lies in \(I\), so the component criterion forces every component
to lie in \(I\). Hence the sum is direct. Products satisfy
\[
(S/I)_m(S/I)_n\subseteq(S/I)_{m+n}.
\]
Therefore \(S/I\) inherits a grading.

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-07
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E7.}
Pass to \(S/I\). It suffices to prove that the nilradical of a graded ring is homogeneous.

Let
\[
x=x_r+x_{r+1}+\cdots+x_s
\]
be nilpotent, with \(x_r\ne0\) the lowest-degree component, and choose \(N\) with \(x^N=0\). The degree-\(Nr\)
component of \(x^N\) is uniquely
\[
x_r^N,
\]
so \(x_r^N=0\). Thus \(x_r\) is nilpotent. Since nilpotent elements form an ideal in a commutative ring,
\(x-x_r\) is nilpotent. Induction on the number of nonzero components shows every \(x_i\) is nilpotent. Hence the
nilradical, and therefore \(\sqrt I\), is homogeneous.

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-08
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E8.}
The forward implication is immediate. Conversely, let \(\mathfrak p\) be homogeneous and assume the prime test
holds for homogeneous elements. In
\[
R=S/\mathfrak p,
\]
the product of two nonzero homogeneous elements is nonzero. Suppose nonzero
\[
f=\sum_if_i,\qquad g=\sum_jg_j
\]
satisfy \(fg=0\). Let \(f_r,g_s\) be their lowest-degree nonzero components. Then the degree-\(r+s\) component of
\(fg\) is exactly \(f_rg_s\), which is nonzero. Contradiction. Thus \(R\) is a domain and \(\mathfrak p\) is prime.

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-09
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E9.}
In the standard grading, every positive-degree monomial is divisible by one of \(x_0,\ldots,x_n\), so every
positive-degree polynomial lies in
\[
(x_0,\ldots,x_n).
\]
Conversely every \(x_i\) has degree \(1\), hence lies in \(S_+\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{S_+=(x_0,\ldots,x_n)}.
\]

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-10
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E10.}
For
\[
S=k[x,y],\qquad \deg x=2,\quad \deg y=3,
\]
every nonconstant monomial has positive degree, so
\[
\boxed{S_+=(x,y)}.
\]
There is no monomial of weighted degree \(1\), since \(2a+3b=1\) has no solution in nonnegative integers. Hence
\[
\boxed{S_1=0}.
\]

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-11
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E11.}
Let \(\varphi:M\to N\) have degree zero and let
\[
m=\sum_nm_n\in\ker\varphi.
\]
Then
\[
0=\varphi(m)=\sum_n\varphi(m_n),
\qquad
\varphi(m_n)\in N_n.
\]
Directness of the grading on \(N\) forces \(\varphi(m_n)=0\) for every \(n\). Hence every homogeneous component of
an element of \(\ker\varphi\) is again in the kernel, so the kernel is graded.

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-12
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E12.}
By convention,
\[
M(d)_n=M_{n+d}.
\]
If \(m\in M_r\), then \(m\) belongs to \(M(d)_n\) precisely when \(n+d=r\). Therefore its shifted degree is
\[
\boxed{r-d}.
\]

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-13
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E13.}
If \(f\in S_d\), then
\[
S(-d)_n=S_{n-d}.
\]
For \(a\in S_{n-d}\),
\[
fa\in S_{d+n-d}=S_n.
\]
Thus multiplication by \(f\) preserves the shifted degree:
\[
\boxed{S(-d)\xrightarrow{\cdot f}S\text{ has degree zero}}.
\]

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-14
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E14.}
Let \(f\) be homogeneous of degree \(d>0\). Suppose two nonzero homogeneous fractions represent the same element:
\[
\frac{a}{f^r}=\frac{b}{f^s}.
\]
Equality in the localization means that for some \(N\ge0\),
\[
f^N(f^sa-f^rb)=0.
\]
The two terms are homogeneous. For a nonzero localized fraction they must have the same degree, hence
\[
\deg a+sd=\deg b+rd.
\]
Therefore
\[
\deg a-rd=\deg b-sd.
\]
So the degree assigned to a homogeneous fraction is independent of its representation.

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-15
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E15.}
A degree-zero fraction in \(k[x,y]_x\) has the form
\[
\frac{F(x,y)}{x^r},
\qquad
\deg F=r.
\]
Homogeneity gives
\[
F(x,y)=x^rF(1,y/x),
\]
so
\[
\frac{F(x,y)}{x^r}=F(1,y/x)\in k[y/x].
\]
Conversely \(y/x\) has degree zero. Hence
\[
\boxed{k[x,y]_{(x)}=k[y/x]}.
\]

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-16
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E16.}
After inverting \(x_2\), set
\[
u=\frac{x_0}{x_2},
\qquad
v=\frac{x_1}{x_2}.
\]
Both have degree zero. Every homogeneous numerator of degree \(r\), divided by \(x_2^r\), becomes a polynomial in
\(u,v\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{
k[x_0,x_1,x_2]_{(x_2)}
=
k[u,v]
=
k\!\left[\frac{x_0}{x_2},\frac{x_1}{x_2}\right].
}
\]

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-17
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E17.}
Let
\[
S=k[x,y,z]/(xy-z^2)
\]
with the standard grading. In \(S_x\), set
\[
u=\frac zx.
\]
Dividing the relation by \(x^2\) gives
\[
\frac yx=\left(\frac zx\right)^2=u^2.
\]
Thus every degree-zero fraction is generated by \(u\), and
\[
\boxed{S_{(x)}\cong k[u]}.
\]

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-18
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E18.}
The localized module \(M_f\) is graded. If
\[
a\in(S_f)_0,\qquad m\in(M_f)_0,
\]
then
\[
\deg(am)=0+0=0.
\]
Hence \(am\in(M_f)_0\). The module axioms are inherited from \(M_f\), so
\[
\boxed{M_{(f)}=(M_f)_0\text{ is an }S_{(f)}\text{-module}}.
\]

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-19
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E19.}
Given an exact sequence of graded modules with degree-zero maps,
\[
0\to M'\to M\to M''\to0,
\]
ordinary localization at the multiplicative set \(\{1,f,f^2,\ldots\}\) is exact:
\[
0\to M'_f\to M_f\to M''_f\to0.
\]
Because \(f\) is homogeneous, the localizations inherit gradings and the maps remain degree zero. Therefore the
localized sequence is exact in the graded category.

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-20
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E20.}
Exactness of a sequence of graded modules with degree-zero maps can be read degree by degree. Thus
\[
0\to M'\to M\to M''\to0
\]
gives, for every \(n\),
\[
0\to M'_n\to M_n\to M''_n\to0.
\]
At \(n=0\),
\[
\boxed{
0\to M'_0\to M_0\to M''_0\to0
}
\]
is exact. Hence taking degree zero is exact in this category.

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-21
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E21.}
The second Veronese of \(k[x,y]\) is generated by
\[
x^2,\quad xy,\quad y^2.
\]
Define
\[
\phi:k[u,v,w]\to k[x,y]^{(2)}
\]
by
\[
u\mapsto x^2,\qquad v\mapsto xy,\qquad w\mapsto y^2.
\]
It is surjective and satisfies
\[
uw-v^2\in\ker\phi.
\]
Using \(uw=v^2\), every monomial reduces to one containing either no \(u\) or no \(w\); distinct such reduced
monomials map to distinct monomials in \(x,y\). Hence there are no further relations, and
\[
\boxed{k[x,y]^{(2)}\cong k[u,v,w]/(uw-v^2)}.
\]

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-22
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E22.}
A monomial \(x^ay^b\) has weighted degree
\[
2a+3b.
\]
Solving
\[
2a+3b=12
\]
gives
\[
(a,b)=(6,0),(3,2),(0,4).
\]
Thus the degree-\(12\) monomials are
\[
\boxed{x^6,\quad x^3y^2,\quad y^4}.
\]

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-23
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E23.}
If \(F\) is homogeneous of degree \(d\), then
\[
F(\lambda a)=\lambda^dF(a).
\]
Hence \(F(a)=0\) implies \(F(\lambda a)=0\) for every scalar \(\lambda\). If \(I\) is generated by homogeneous
polynomials, the same holds simultaneously for every generator, so
\[
\boxed{V(I)\text{ is stable under scalar multiplication}}.
\]

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-24
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E24.}
A homogeneous fraction of net degree \(r\) transforms under scaling by the factor \(\lambda^r\). Thus fractions of
nonzero degree depend on the chosen scalar representative of a projective point. The full localization \(S_f\)
contains all such degrees, including negative ones created by \(f^{-1}\).

Degree-zero fractions have equal numerator and denominator degree and are invariant under simultaneous scaling.
Therefore the natural chart ring is
\[
\boxed{S_{(f)}=(S_f)_0},
\]
not the full localization \(S_f\).

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-25
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E25.}
Assume
\[
\deg f=\deg g=d>0.
\]
Then
\[
g/f\in S_{(f)}.
\]
Localizing \(S_{(f)}\) at \(g/f\) allows both \(f\) and \(g\) as homogeneous denominators while retaining net
degree zero. This is precisely the degree-zero part of the localization where \(fg\) is invertible. Hence
\[
\boxed{(S_{(f)})_{g/f}\cong S_{(fg)}}.
\]

If \(\deg f=e\) and \(\deg g=d\), choose positive integers \(a,b\) with
\[
ae=bd.
\]
Then \(g^b/f^a\) has degree zero and
\[
\boxed{
(S_{(f)})_{g^b/f^a}\cong S_{(f^ag^b)}.
}
\]
This equal-power device is the correct overlap algebra for unequal weights.

% VI34-DETAILED-EXERCISE-26
\paragraph{Solution VI.34.E26.}
Localization commutes with shifting:
\[
S(d)_f\cong S_f(d).
\]
Under the convention \(S(d)_n=S_{n+d}\), the degree-zero piece of \(S_f(d)\) is the original degree-\(d\) piece:
\[
\boxed{S(d)_{(f)}=(S_f)_d}.
\]

If \(\deg f=e\) and \(d=qe\), multiplication by the invertible homogeneous element \(f^{-q}\) sends degree \(d\)
to degree zero:
\[
(S_f)_d\xrightarrow{\cdot f^{-q}}(S_f)_0.
\]
Its inverse is multiplication by \(f^q\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{S(d)_{(f)}\cong S_{(f)}}
\]
whenever \(e\mid d\). This is the algebraic source of local triviality for later twisting sheaves.

\paragraph{Exercise 1.} If \(\sum s_n=\sum t_n\), then \(\sum(s_n-t_n)=0\). Directness forces every \(s_n=t_n\).

\paragraph{Exercise 2.} Write \(a\in S_m,b\in S_n\). Then \(ab\in S_{m+n}\), and a nonzero homogeneous element belongs to only one graded piece.

\paragraph{Exercise 3.} Stars-and-bars gives \(\binom{d+2}{2}\).

\paragraph{Exercise 4.} Expand each coefficient into homogeneous components and group products by total degree; every grouped component remains in the ideal.

\paragraph{Exercise 5.} If it were homogeneous, \(1\) would lie in \((x+1)\), impossible.

\paragraph{Exercise 6.} Homogeneity of the ideal makes decomposition of cosets well-defined, and products preserve degree addition.

\paragraph{Exercise 7.} For a nilpotent sum of homogeneous components, the extreme nonzero component is nilpotent by comparing an extreme component of a power; subtract and induct.

\paragraph{Exercise 8.} If \(fg=0\) with both nonzero, their least-degree nonzero components have nonzero product, producing a nonzero least-degree component of \(fg\), contradiction.

\paragraph{Exercise 9.} Thus all positive-degree polynomials lie in \((x_0,\ldots,x_n)\), while each variable lies in \(S_+\).

\paragraph{Exercise 10.} \(S_+=(x,y)\) and \(S_1=0\), so the grading is not standard.

\paragraph{Exercise 11.} Images of distinct-degree components lie in distinct graded pieces, so each component maps to zero separately.

\paragraph{Exercise 12.} The new degree is \(r-d\).

\paragraph{Exercise 13.} \(S(-d)_n=S_{n-d}\), and multiplication by \(f\) lands in \(S_n\).

\paragraph{Exercise 14.} Equality after clearing denominators equates homogeneous terms, forcing the same value of \(\deg a-r\deg f\) for a nonzero fraction.

\paragraph{Exercise 15.} Write a degree-\(r\) numerator as \(x^rP(y/x)\); division by \(x^r\) gives the claim.

\paragraph{Exercise 16.} It is \(k[x_0/x_2,x_1/x_2]\cong k[u,v]\).

\paragraph{Exercise 17.} The relation gives \(y/x=u^2\), hence \(S_{(x)}\cong k[u]\).

\paragraph{Exercise 18.} Degree zero plus degree zero stays degree zero.

\paragraph{Exercise 19.} Localization is exact and maps remain degree zero; hence the localized sequence is exact degreewise.

\paragraph{Exercise 20.} Restrict the exact sequence to degree zero.

\paragraph{Exercise 21.} The map is surjective; \(uw-v^2\) is the sole relation by monomial reduction or dimension comparison.

\paragraph{Exercise 22.} The solutions are \((6,0),(3,2),(0,4)\), giving \(x^6,x^3y^2,y^4\).

\paragraph{Exercise 23.} For homogeneous \(F\), \(F(\lambda a)=\lambda^{\deg F}F(a)\).

\paragraph{Exercise 24.} A fraction of degree \(r\) scales by \(\lambda^r\); only degree-zero fractions are invariant under changing the representative of a projective point.

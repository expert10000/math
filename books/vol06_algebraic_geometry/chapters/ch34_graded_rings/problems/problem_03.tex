% VI34-DETAILED-PROBLEM-03
\begin{problem}[VI.34.P3: Homogeneous primality]\label{prob:vi34-03}
Let \(\mathfrak p\) be homogeneous. Prove that it is prime if and only if it passes the prime test on homogeneous elements.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
If \(\mathfrak p\) is prime, the test is automatic.

Conversely assume
\[
ab\in\mathfrak p\Longrightarrow a\in\mathfrak p\text{ or }b\in\mathfrak p
\]
for homogeneous \(a,b\). Pass to \(R=S/\mathfrak p\). Then products of nonzero homogeneous elements of \(R\)
are nonzero.

Suppose nonzero \(f,g\in R\) satisfy \(fg=0\). Let \(f_r\) and \(g_s\) be their least-degree nonzero homogeneous
components. The degree-\(r+s\) component of \(fg\) is exactly \(f_rg_s\), because no other pair of components can
have smaller total degree. By hypothesis \(f_rg_s\neq0\), contradiction. Thus \(R\) is a domain and
\[
\boxed{\mathfrak p\text{ is prime}}.
\]
\end{solution}
\fi

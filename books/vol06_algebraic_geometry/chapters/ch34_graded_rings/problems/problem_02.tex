% VI34-DETAILED-PROBLEM-02
\begin{problem}[VI.34.P2: Homogeneous radicals]\label{prob:vi34-02}
Prove that \(\sqrt I\) is homogeneous whenever \(I\) is homogeneous.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Pass to the graded quotient \(R=S/I\). Since
\[
\sqrt I/I=\sqrt{(0)}_R,
\]
it is enough to prove that the nilradical of a graded ring is homogeneous.

Let \(x=\sum_{j=r}^s x_j\) be nilpotent, with \(x_r\neq0\) of lowest degree. Choose \(N\) with \(x^N=0\).
The degree-\(Nr\) component of \(x^N\) is uniquely \(x_r^N\), hence \(x_r^N=0\). Thus \(x_r\) is nilpotent.
Because nilpotent elements form an ideal in a commutative ring, \(x-x_r\) is nilpotent. Induct on the number of
nonzero homogeneous components. Every \(x_j\) is nilpotent.

Therefore the nilradical is a direct sum of its homogeneous pieces, so it is homogeneous. Pulling back through
\(S\to S/I\) gives
\[
\boxed{\sqrt I\text{ homogeneous}}.
\]
\end{solution}
\fi

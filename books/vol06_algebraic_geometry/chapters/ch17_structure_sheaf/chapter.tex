\section*{Purpose and learning goals}

A spectrum is not only a topological space of prime ideals.  Its geometry is carried by a
sheaf of rings that records which algebraic functions are available on each open region.
For an affine spectrum $X=\Spec A$, that sheaf is the \emph{structure sheaf} $\OO_X$.
On a principal open $D(f)$ it has the fundamental form
\[
\OO_X(D(f))\cong A_f,
\]
and at a point $\mfp$ its stalk is
\[
\OO_{X,\mfp}\cong A_{\mfp}.
\]
These two formulas are the bridge from the sheaf theory of VI/12--VI/16 to affine schemes
in VI/18.

The corpus audit for this chapter is deliberately conservative.  The explicit VI/17 source
stream in \texttt{theory-of-algebraic-geometry-3.tex} contains structure-sheaf theory and
regular-function examples, but no numbered Problem whose unique mathematical center is the
structure sheaf alone.  Four numbered Problems in the same legacy file are tracked rather
than absorbed by the fallback rule: Problem 11 is reserved for VI/18, Problem 12 for VI/19,
Problem 13 for VI/21, and Problem 14 for VI/20.  The structure-sheaf examples in the
commutative-algebra corpus are merged into this chapter as examples, not promoted to false
legacy Problems.

After this chapter, the reader should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
\item define $\OO_{\Spec A}$ by locally representable fractions;
\item prove that the definition gives a sheaf of rings;
\item compute sections on $D(f)$ as $A_f$;
\item identify the stalk at $\mfp$ with $A_{\mfp}$ and its residue field with
$\Frac(A/\mfp)$;
\item interpret restriction maps as further localization;
\item evaluate regular functions in the varying residue fields $\kappa(\mfp)$;
\item distinguish topology from scheme-theoretic function data, especially in nonreduced
examples;
\item compute structure sheaves on $\Spec\mathbb Z$, polynomial spectra, product rings, and
reducible rings;
\item use the sheaf equalizer condition to compute sections on unions of principal opens;
\item recognize exactly what remains for the definition of an affine scheme in VI/18.
\end{itemize}

\section{From a spectrum to functions}

Let $A$ be a commutative ring and let
\[
X=\Spec A.
\]
A point of $X$ is a prime ideal $\mfp$.  The ring $A$ supplies global algebraic
expressions, but an open subset may admit expressions with denominators that are invertible
there.  This is why localization is the correct local algebra.

For $f\in A$ the principal open is
\[
D(f)=\{\mfp\in\Spec A:f\notin\mfp\}.
\]
On $D(f)$ the element $f$ is nowhere contained in a prime, so it is natural to allow
powers of $f$ in denominators.  The expected ring of functions is therefore $A_f$.

\input{chapters/ch17_structure_sheaf/figures/vi17_structure_sheaf_pipeline.tex}

\section{Definition by locally representable fractions}

For each $\mfp\in X$, let $A_{\mfp}$ be the localization of $A$ at
$A\setminus\mfp$.  For an open subset $U\subseteq X$, define $\OO_X(U)$ to be the set of
families
\[
s=(s_{\mfp})_{\mfp\in U},\qquad s_{\mfp}\in A_{\mfp},
\]
that are \emph{locally represented by one fraction from $A$}: for every
$\mfp\in U$ there exist an open neighborhood $V\subseteq U$ of $\mfp$ and elements
$a,b\in A$ such that
\[
V\subseteq D(b)
\quad\text{and}\quad
s_{\mfq}=\frac{a}{b}\in A_{\mfq}
\qquad(\mfq\in V).
\]
Addition and multiplication are defined pointwise in the localizations $A_{\mfp}$.

\begin{definition}[Structure sheaf of an affine spectrum]
The assignment $U\mapsto\OO_X(U)$ with restriction given by deleting components of the
family outside the smaller open set is the \emph{structure sheaf} of $X=\Spec A$.
\end{definition}

\input{chapters/ch17_structure_sheaf/figures/vi17_local_fraction.tex}

\begin{theorem}[Sheaf property]
The assignment $U\mapsto\OO_X(U)$ is a sheaf of commutative rings.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Locality is immediate: two families equal on an open cover have the same component at every
point.  For gluing, let $U=\bigcup_i U_i$ and let $s_i\in\OO_X(U_i)$ agree on overlaps.
Define $s_{\mfp}$ using any $i$ with $\mfp\in U_i$.  Compatibility makes this well defined.
Each $s_i$ is locally represented by a fraction $a/b$, so the glued family is also locally
represented by such fractions.  Hence it is a section of $\OO_X(U)$, and uniqueness follows
pointwise.
\end{proof}

\section{Sections on principal opens}

The central computation is the following.

\begin{theorem}[Principal-open formula]\label{thm:vi17-basic-open}
For every $f\in A$ there is a natural isomorphism
\[
\OO_X(D(f))\cong A_f.
\]
Under this isomorphism, $a/f^n$ is the section whose germ at
$\mfp\in D(f)$ is the same fraction in $A_{\mfp}$.
\end{theorem}

The theorem says that a principal open carries exactly the algebra obtained by making its
defining function invertible.  In particular,
\[
\OO_X(X)=\OO_X(D(1))\cong A.
\]
Thus the global ring can be recovered from its affine structure sheaf.

\input{chapters/ch17_structure_sheaf/figures/vi17_basic_open_sections.tex}

\begin{remark}[What is not being consumed here]
The stronger legacy statement that the restricted locally ringed space
$(D(f),\OO_X|_{D(f)})$ is canonically isomorphic to $\Spec A_f$ is AG3 Problem 11.  It is
reserved for VI/18, where principal opens are treated as affine schemes.  This chapter uses
only the structure-sheaf computations needed to prepare that result.
\end{remark}

\section{Restriction maps are further localization}

If $g\in A$, then
\[
D(fg)=D(f)\cap D(g)\subseteq D(f).
\]
Under the identifications from Theorem~\ref{thm:vi17-basic-open}, the restriction map is
\[
A_f\longrightarrow A_{fg},
\]
the localization map that also makes $g$ invertible.  Composition of restrictions is
therefore associativity of localization.

\input{chapters/ch17_structure_sheaf/figures/vi17_restriction_localization.tex}

\section{Stalks are local rings}

Fix $\mfp\in\Spec A$.  Principal opens $D(f)$ with $f\notin\mfp$ form a neighborhood
basis at $\mfp$, hence
\[
\OO_{X,\mfp}
=\varinjlim_{\mfp\in D(f)}\OO_X(D(f))
\cong
\varinjlim_{f\notin\mfp} A_f.
\]
The colimit is exactly $A_{\mfp}$.

\begin{theorem}[Stalk formula]\label{thm:vi17-stalk}
For every prime $\mfp$,
\[
\boxed{\OO_{X,\mfp}\cong A_{\mfp}.}
\]
The unique maximal ideal is $\mfp A_{\mfp}$.
\end{theorem}

\input{chapters/ch17_structure_sheaf/figures/vi17_stalk_colimit.tex}

At $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}A$, the stalk $\mathcal O_{X,\mathfrak p}$ is $A_{\mathfrak p}$.  A function germ may use any denominator outside $\mathfrak p$.
\end{example}

\section{Residue fields and values of regular functions}

The residue field at $\mfp$ is
\[
\kappa(\mfp)
:=\OO_{X,\mfp}/\mfp\OO_{X,\mfp}
\cong A_{\mfp}/\mfp A_{\mfp}.
\]
Since $A/\mfp$ is a domain,
\[
\boxed{\kappa(\mfp)\cong\Frac(A/\mfp).}
\]
A section $s\in\OO_X(U)$ therefore has a value at each $\mfp\in U$, namely the residue
class of its germ
\[
s(\mfp)\in\kappa(\mfp).
\]
The target field varies with the point.  This is one of the basic differences between a
scheme and an ordinary set carrying scalar-valued functions.

\section{Generic and closed points revisited}

If $A$ is a domain, the generic point $(0)$ has stalk
\[
\OO_{X,(0)}\cong\Frac(A),
\]
so the function field is already present as the local ring at the generic point.  If
$\mfm$ is maximal, then the closed point has residue field $A/\mfm$.

For $A=k[t]$ with $k$ algebraically closed,
\[
\OO_{X,(0)}=k(t),
\qquad
\OO_{X,(t-a)}=k[t]_{(t-a)},
\qquad
\kappa(t-a)=k.
\]

\input{chapters/ch17_structure_sheaf/figures/vi17_generic_closed_stalks.tex}

\section{The arithmetic line \texorpdfstring{$\Spec\mathbb Z$}{Spec Z}}

For $X=\Spec\mathbb Z$,
\[
\OO_X(D(n))=\mathbb Z[1/n].
\]
At a closed point $(p)$,
\[
\OO_{X,(p)}=\mathbb Z_{(p)},
\qquad
\kappa(p)=\mathbb F_p,
\]
while the generic point has
\[
\OO_{X,(0)}=\mathbb Q.
\]
Thus one sheaf simultaneously remembers rational generic information and modular special
fibers.

\input{chapters/ch17_structure_sheaf/figures/vi17_spec_z.tex}

\section{Nilpotents are invisible topologically but visible sheaf-theoretically}

Let
\[
A=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\]
Its spectrum has a single point, just like $\Spec k$, but
\[
\OO_{\Spec A}(\Spec A)=A
\]
contains the nonzero nilpotent section $\varepsilon$.  Hence the same underlying topological
space can support different structure sheaves.

More generally, $A$ and $A_{\mathrm{red}}=A/\Nil(A)$ have homeomorphic spectra, but their
structure sheaves differ by nilpotent functions.

\input{chapters/ch17_structure_sheaf/figures/vi17_nilpotent_visible.tex}

\end{example}

\section{Gluing regular functions}

If $U=D(f)\cup D(g)$, a section over $U$ is determined by sections
\[
s_f\in A_f,
\qquad
s_g\in A_g
\]
that agree after restriction to
\[
D(fg)=D(f)\cap D(g).
\]
Thus
\[
\OO_X(U)
\cong
\left\{(s_f,s_g)\in A_f\times A_g:
 s_f|_{A_{fg}}=s_g|_{A_{fg}}\right\}.
\]
This is the equalizer form of the sheaf axiom.

\input{chapters/ch17_structure_sheaf/figures/vi17_gluing_equalizer.tex}

\section{Units and local vanishing}

For $a\in A$, the germ $a/1\in A_{\mfp}$ is a unit if and only if
$a\notin\mfp$.  Hence the unit locus of the global section $a$ is precisely
\[
D(a).
\]
By contrast,
\[
\frac a1=0\text{ in }A_{\mfp}
\]
if and only if there exists $s\notin\mfp$ with $sa=0$ in $A$.  In a ring with zero
divisors, local vanishing is therefore subtler than membership $a\in\mfp$.

\section{Common pitfalls}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A regular section over $D(f)$ need not come from an element of $A$; it may use powers
of $f$ in the denominator.
\item The value of a regular function at $\mfp$ lies in $\kappa(\mfp)$, not in one fixed
base field in general.
\item The topology of $\Spec A$ does not determine $A$: nilpotents can change the structure
sheaf without changing the underlying topological space.
\item The stalk at $\mfp$ is $A_{\mfp}$, not the residue field $\kappa(\mfp)$; the latter is
a quotient of the stalk.
\item A fraction $a/b$ represents a section only where $b$ is invertible, i.e. on an open
contained in $D(b)$.
\item The full statement $D(f)\cong\Spec A_f$ as locally ringed spaces is reserved for
VI/18; the universal property of affine schemes is reserved for VI/19.
\item The fallback migration rule for the legacy AG3 file must not send Problems 11--14 to
VI/17; the ledger records their later destinations explicitly.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Summary}

For $X=\Spec A$, the structure sheaf is the local algebraic function theory determined by
localization:
\[
\boxed{\OO_X(D(f))=A_f,\qquad \OO_{X,\mfp}=A_{\mfp},\qquad
\kappa(\mfp)=\Frac(A/\mfp).}
\]
The sheaf glues locally representable fractions, recovers $A$ from global sections, records
nilpotents that topology cannot see, and makes every point carry a local ring.  This completes
Part III: the sheaf machinery is now attached to spectra.  VI/18 packages the pair
$(\Spec A,\OO_{\Spec A})$ as an affine scheme.

The Volume VI ledger records that no numbered legacy Problem is consumed by VI/17 itself.
AG3 Problems 11--14 are reserved exactly once for VI/18, VI/19, VI/21, and VI/20,
respectively, while the structure-sheaf theory/examples from AG3 and CA13 are merged here.

\subsection*{Proofs}

\begin{exercise}[Basic-open formula]\label{exr:vi17-expand-06}
Prove: Prove $\mathcal O_X(D(f))\cong A_f$ for the affine structure sheaf.
\end{exercise}
\begin{hint}
Work directly from the structure sheaf, local fractions, and stalks.
\end{hint}
\begin{solution}
Use the construction by locally represented fractions and the distinguished-open basis.
\end{solution}

\begin{exercise}[Stalk formula]\label{exr:vi17-expand-07}
Prove: Prove $\mathcal O_{X,\mathfrak p}\cong A_{\mathfrak p}$.
\end{exercise}
\begin{hint}
Work directly from the structure sheaf, local fractions, and stalks.
\end{hint}
\begin{solution}
Pass to the direct limit over distinguished neighborhoods $D(f)$ with $f\notin\mathfrak p$.
\end{solution}

\begin{exercise}[Local ring]\label{exr:vi17-expand-08}
Prove: Prove each structure-sheaf stalk is local.
\end{exercise}
\begin{hint}
Work directly from the structure sheaf, local fractions, and stalks.
\end{hint}
\begin{solution}
Under the localization identification the unique maximal ideal is $\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{exercise}[Restriction localization]\label{exr:vi17-expand-09}
Prove: Prove the restriction $A_f\to A_{fg}$ is the usual localization map.
\end{exercise}
\begin{hint}
Work directly from the structure sheaf, local fractions, and stalks.
\end{hint}
\begin{solution}
Both sheaf restriction and algebraic localization send a fraction to the same further-localized fraction.
\end{solution}

\subsection*{Counterexamples and hypothesis tests}

\begin{exercise}[Topology enough]\label{exr:vi17-expand-10}
Test the claim: the structure sheaf is determined by the underlying point set alone.
\end{exercise}
\begin{hint}
Test the claim on a concrete affine or local model before generalizing.
\end{hint}
\begin{solution}
False; reduced and nonreduced schemes can share the same topology.
\end{solution}

\begin{exercise}[Polynomial only]\label{exr:vi17-expand-11}
Test the claim: regular functions on $D(f)$ must come from elements of $A$ without denominators.
\end{exercise}
\begin{hint}
Test the claim on a concrete affine or local model before generalizing.
\end{hint}
\begin{solution}
False; they are elements of $A_f$.
\end{solution}

\begin{exercise}[All stalks field]\label{exr:vi17-expand-12}
Test the claim: every structure-sheaf stalk is a field.
\end{exercise}
\begin{hint}
Test the claim on a concrete affine or local model before generalizing.
\end{hint}
\begin{solution}
False; it is a local ring, a field only at special generic situations.
\end{solution}

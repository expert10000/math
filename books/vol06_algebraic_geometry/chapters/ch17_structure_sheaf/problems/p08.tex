\begin{problem}[A nonreduced point is detected by its structure sheaf]\label{prob:vi17-p08}
Compare $(\Spec k,\OO)$ with $(\Spec k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2),\OO)$.  Prove that the underlying spaces are homeomorphic but the ringed spaces are not isomorphic.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This isolates the exact information topology loses and the structure sheaf restores.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Both spectra have one prime, so compare global rings.  Ringed-space isomorphisms induce isomorphisms on global sections.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Find the unique prime of the dual-number ring.
\item One global ring contains a nonzero nilpotent and the other does not.
\end{enumerate}

% VI17_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINED
% VI17_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINED
\begin{solution}
The ring $B=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ has nilradical $(\varepsilon)$, and every prime ideal contains the nilradical.  Since
\[
B/(\varepsilon)\cong k
\]
is a field, $(\varepsilon)$ is the unique prime ideal of $B$.  Therefore both $\Spec B$ and $\Spec k$ are one-point topological spaces and are homeomorphic.

An isomorphism of ringed spaces would induce an isomorphism on sections over the whole space.  But
\[
\Gamma(\Spec k,\OO)=k,
\qquad
\Gamma(\Spec B,\OO)=B.
\]
The ring $B$ contains the nonzero nilpotent element $\varepsilon$, whereas the field $k$ has no nonzero nilpotents.  Reducedness is preserved by ring isomorphism, so $k\not\cong B$.  Consequently the two ringed spaces are not isomorphic.

Thus the structure sheaf detects infinitesimal nilpotent information that the underlying Zariski topology cannot see.
\end{solution}



\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Nilpotent functions are geometric data even though they produce no new topological points.

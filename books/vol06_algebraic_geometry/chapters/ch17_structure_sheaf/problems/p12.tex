\begin{problem}[Global functions on the punctured affine plane]\label{prob:vi17-p12}
Let $A=k[x,y]$ and $U=D(x)\cup D(y)$.  Prove $\OO_X(U)\cong A$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is a first striking example where deleting a codimension-two closed point creates no new global regular functions.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Use the equalizer $A_x\times_{A_{xy}}A_y$ and view both localizations inside $k(x,y)$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A compatible pair determines one rational function lying in both $A_x$ and $A_y$.
\item Show $A_x\cap A_y=A$ inside $k(x,y)$ using unique factorization.
\end{enumerate}

% VI17_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINED
% VI17_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINED
\begin{solution}
Let $K=k(x,y)$ be the fraction field of $A=k[x,y]$.  The two localizations $A_x$ and $A_y$ embed in $K$.  By the two-open equalizer,
\[
\Gamma(U,\OO_X)
\cong
A_x\times_{A_{xy}}A_y,
\]
and a compatible pair corresponds to a single rational function
\[
r\in A_x\cap A_y\subseteq K.
\]
It remains to compute the intersection.

Take $r\in A_x\cap A_y$.  We may write
\[
r=\frac{p}{x^m}=\frac{q}{y^n}
\]
with $p,q\in A$ and $m,n\ge0$.  Equality in the fraction field gives
\[
y^n p=x^m q.
\]
The ring $k[x,y]$ is a UFD, and the irreducibles $x$ and $y$ are relatively prime.  Since $x^m$ divides $y^np$ and shares no irreducible factor with $y^n$, Euclid's lemma implies $x^m\mid p$.  Write $p=x^m p'$.  Then
\[
r=p'\in A.
\]
Thus $A_x\cap A_y\subseteq A$, while the reverse inclusion is immediate.  Hence
\[
A_x\cap A_y=A
\]
and therefore
\[
\Gamma(U,\OO_X)\cong A.
\]
Although $U=\mathbb A^2_k\setminus\{(x,y)\}$ is a proper open subset, removing this codimension-two point creates no new global regular functions.
\end{solution}



\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The sheaf can have the same global section ring on a proper open subset; global sections alone do not determine the open set.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Investigate $\mathbb A^n\setminus\{0\}$ for $n\ge2$ and normal domains.

\begin{problem}[Restriction maps and transitivity]\label{prob:vi17-p13}
For $f,g,h\in A$, identify the restriction maps along $D(fgh)\subseteq D(fg)\subseteq D(f)$ and prove their composition equals the direct restriction $A_f\to A_{fgh}$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
A sheaf is a contravariant functor, and here that functoriality is exactly associativity of localization.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Write the maps as successive localizations at $g$ and $h$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Use $(A_f)_g\cong A_{fg}$.
\item Then localize once more at $h$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
The first restriction is $A_f\to(A_f)_g\cong A_{fg}$ and the second is $A_{fg}\to(A_{fg})_h\cong A_{fgh}$.  Their composite sends a fraction in $A_f$ to the same fraction viewed in $A_{fgh}$.  This is exactly the canonical localization map $A_f\to A_{fgh}$, which is the direct restriction.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Restriction compatibility is not extra structure pasted onto the localizations; it is built into their universal property.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Formulate restrictions for arbitrary inclusions of principal opens using universal properties rather than chosen divisibility relations.

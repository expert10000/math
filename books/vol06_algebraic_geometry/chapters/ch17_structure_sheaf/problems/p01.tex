\begin{problem}[Construct the structure sheaf from local fractions]\label{prob:vi17-p01}
Let $X=\Spec A$.  Define $\OO_X(U)$ to be families $s_{\mfp}\in A_{\mfp}$ locally represented by a fraction $a/b$ on neighborhoods contained in $D(b)$.  Prove that this assignment is a sheaf of rings.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem turns the slogan "regular functions are locally fractions" into an actual sheaf and makes the local-to-global mechanism explicit.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Check ring operations locally, then prove locality and gluing pointwise.  The only nontrivial closure issue is that sums and products of locally represented fractions are again locally represented.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item For two local representatives $a/b$ and $c/d$, shrink to $D(bd)$.
\item Define restrictions by forgetting components outside the smaller open.
\item For gluing, define the germ-family pointwise from the compatible local sections.
\item Local representability is inherited from whichever member of the cover contains the point.
\end{enumerate}

% VI17_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINED
\begin{solution}
We verify the ring operations, functoriality, locality, and gluing separately.

Fix an open $U\subseteq X$.  If $s,t\in\OO_X(U)$ and near $\mfp\in U$ they are represented by $a/b$ and $c/d$, shrink to the intersection of the two representing neighborhoods.  It is contained in $D(bd)$, and there
\[
s+t=\frac{ad+bc}{bd},\qquad st=\frac{ac}{bd}.
\]
Thus sums and products are again locally represented by fractions.  The constant families $0$ and $1$ provide the additive and multiplicative identities, and the ring laws hold pointwise in the stalks $A_{\mfp}$.  Hence $\OO_X(U)$ is a ring.

For $V\subseteq U$, define restriction by deleting the components outside $V$.  Local representability is preserved after restriction, because a fraction representing a section near $\mfp\in V$ still represents the restricted section after intersecting its neighborhood with $V$.  Identity and composition of restrictions are immediate, so $U\mapsto\OO_X(U)$ is a presheaf of rings.

For locality, suppose $U=\bigcup_iU_i$ and $s,t\in\OO_X(U)$ have the same restriction to every $U_i$.  Every point lies in some $U_i$, so the two germ-families have the same component at every point; hence $s=t$.

For gluing, take compatible sections $s_i\in\OO_X(U_i)$.  For $\mfp\in U$, choose $i$ with $\mfp\in U_i$ and set $s_{\mfp}=(s_i)_{\mfp}$.  If two choices are possible, compatibility on $U_i\cap U_j$ gives the same germ, so the definition is independent of the choice.  Near $\mfp$, the chosen section $s_i$ is represented by some fraction $a/b$ on a neighborhood $V\subseteq U_i$ contained in $D(b)$; the glued family is represented by the same fraction on $V$.  Hence the glued family lies in $\OO_X(U)$.  It restricts to every $s_i$, and locality proves uniqueness.  Therefore the assignment is a sheaf of rings.
\end{solution}



\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The sheaf axiom is almost tautological because a section is defined as a pointwise family subject to a local representability condition.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Repeat the construction for modules: replace $A_{\mfp}$ by $M_{\mfp}$ to obtain the sheaf associated with an $A$-module.

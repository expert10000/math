\begin{problem}[Detecting zero germs algebraically]\label{prob:vi17-p17}
For $a\in A$ and $\mfp\in\Spec A$, prove
\[a/1=0\text{ in }\OO_{X,\mfp}\iff\exists s\notin\mfp\text{ with }sa=0.\]
Give a nontrivial example in $A=k[x,y]/(xy)$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the correct local vanishing test in rings with zero divisors.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Use the zero criterion in a localization; for the example exploit the relation $xy=0$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Apply the localization zero criterion directly.
\item At the prime $(x)$, the element $y$ is outside the prime.
\end{enumerate}

% VI17_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINED
% VI17_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINED
\begin{solution}
By the stalk theorem, the germ of $a$ at $\mfp$ is the fraction $a/1\in A_{\mfp}$.  The multiplicative set defining this localization is $S=A\setminus\mfp$.  The localization zero criterion says
\[
\frac a1=0\text{ in }S^{-1}A
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists s\in S\text{ such that }sa=0.
\]
Thus
\[
a/1=0\text{ in }\OO_{X,\mfp}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists s\notin\mfp\text{ with }sa=0.
\]

For a nontrivial example, let
\[
A=k[x,y]/(xy)
\]
and take the prime $\mfp=(x)$.  The class of $x$ is nonzero in $A$, while $y\notin\mfp$.  The relation $yx=0$ supplies an annihilator outside the prime.  Therefore
\[
x/1=0\quad\text{in }A_{(x)}.
\]
So a nonzero global section can have zero germ at a point when zero divisors are present.  This is why local vanishing cannot be tested merely by asking whether the numerator lies in the prime.
\end{solution}



\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Local vanishing is sensitive to annihilators that become units after localization.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Describe the set of primes where a fixed global section has zero germ in terms of its annihilator.

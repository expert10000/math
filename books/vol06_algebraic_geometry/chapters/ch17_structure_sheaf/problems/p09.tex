\begin{problem}[Product rings and clopen decomposition]\label{prob:vi17-p09}
For $A=A_1\times A_2$, describe $\Spec A$ and its structure sheaf in terms of the two factors.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem shows how algebraic product decompositions become disconnected geometric pieces with independent function rings.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Use the idempotents $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ and classify prime ideals of a product ring.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every prime contains exactly one of the two complementary idempotents.
\item The two corresponding principal opens are disjoint and cover the spectrum.
\item Compute their section rings by localization.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
The spectrum is the disjoint union $\Spec A_1\sqcup\Spec A_2$.  Let $e_1=(1,0)$ and $e_2=(0,1)$.  Then $D(e_1)$ and $D(e_2)$ are complementary clopen subsets.  Localization gives
\[A_{e_1}\cong A_1,\qquad A_{e_2}\cong A_2.\]
Hence the restriction of the structure sheaf to each component is the structure sheaf of that factor, and sections on a union are the product of sections on the two disjoint components.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Idempotents split both the ring and its local function theory.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Prove the converse: a nontrivial clopen decomposition of $\Spec A$ comes from a nontrivial idempotent.

\begin{problem}[Product rings and clopen decomposition]\label{prob:vi17-p09}
For $A=A_1\times A_2$, describe $\Spec A$ and its structure sheaf in terms of the two factors.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem shows how algebraic product decompositions become disconnected geometric pieces with independent function rings.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Use the idempotents $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ and classify prime ideals of a product ring.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every prime contains exactly one of the two complementary idempotents.
\item The two corresponding principal opens are disjoint and cover the spectrum.
\item Compute their section rings by localization.
\end{enumerate}

% VI17_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINED
% VI17_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINED
\begin{solution}
Let $A=A_1\times A_2$ and write
\[
e_1=(1,0),\qquad e_2=(0,1).
\]
These are complementary idempotents: $e_1+e_2=1$ and $e_1e_2=0$.  If $\mfp$ is prime, then $0=e_1e_2\in\mfp$, so $\mfp$ contains $e_1$ or $e_2$; it cannot contain both because then it would contain $1$.  Thus every prime lies in exactly one of the disjoint opens $D(e_1)$ and $D(e_2)$.

A prime containing $e_2$ is of the form $\mfp_1\times A_2$ with $\mfp_1\in\Spec A_1$, and similarly for the other factor.  Hence
\[
\Spec(A_1\times A_2)\cong\Spec A_1\sqcup\Spec A_2.
\]
The two components are clopen.

On the first component,
\[
\OO(D(e_1))=A_{e_1}\cong A_1,
\]
because inverting $e_1$ forces $e_2=1-e_1$ to vanish; similarly $A_{e_2}\cong A_2$.  More generally, the restriction of the structure sheaf to each clopen component is the ordinary affine structure sheaf of the corresponding factor.  Since the components are disjoint, sections over a union split as a product.  This identifies the entire structure sheaf with the disjoint union of the two affine structure sheaves.
\end{solution}



\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Idempotents split both the ring and its local function theory.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Prove the converse: a nontrivial clopen decomposition of $\Spec A$ comes from a nontrivial idempotent.

\begin{problem}[A principal cover of the whole spectrum]\label{prob:vi17-p20}
Assume $D(f)\cup D(g)=\Spec A$.  Prove that compatible elements $u\in A_f$ and $v\in A_g$ arise from a unique element $a\in A$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the most concrete algebraic form of the sheaf axiom for the structure sheaf.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Use the equalizer description and $\OO_X(X)=A$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Compatibility means equality in $A_{fg}$.
\item The pair is therefore a compatible pair of sections on a cover of $X$.
\end{enumerate}

% VI17_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINED
% VI17_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINED
\begin{solution}
The equality $D(f)\cup D(g)=X$ says that $D(f)$ and $D(g)$ form an open cover of the spectrum.  Let
\[
u\in A_f,
\qquad
v\in A_g
\]
have the same image in $A_{fg}$.  By the principal-open theorem, $u$ and $v$ are regular sections on $D(f)$ and $D(g)$, and their equality in $A_{fg}$ says exactly that the two sections agree on the overlap $D(fg)$.  The sheaf gluing axiom produces a unique
\[
s\in\OO_X(X)
\]
with these two restrictions.  Since $\OO_X(X)\cong A$, there is a unique $a\in A$ corresponding to $s$.  Its images in $A_f$ and $A_g$ are $u$ and $v$.

There is also a purely algebraic way to read the uniqueness.  If $a,a'\in A$ have the same images in both $A_f$ and $A_g$, then their difference has zero restriction on both members of a cover.  The corresponding global section is therefore locally zero everywhere and hence zero by the sheaf locality axiom; equivalently $a=a'$ under $A\cong\Gamma(X,\OO_X)$.  Thus the sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow A_f\oplus A_g\longrightarrow A_{fg}
\]
is exact at the first two terms, with the last map $(u,v)\mapsto u-v$.
\end{solution}



\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A ring element can be reconstructed from compatible localizations whenever the corresponding opens cover the spectrum.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Prove the analogous statement for finite principal covers and compare it with the first stage of a Čech complex.

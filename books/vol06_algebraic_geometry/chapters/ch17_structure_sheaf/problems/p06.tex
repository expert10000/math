\begin{problem}[Regular functions on the punctured affine line]\label{prob:vi17-p06}
Let $X=\Spec k[x]$.  Compute $\OO_X(D(x))$ and prove that $1/x$ is regular on $D(x)$ but does not extend to a global regular section of $X$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the cleanest example showing why a sheaf contains more local functions than the global ring.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Use localization and the global-section theorem.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Compute $k[x]_x$.
\item If $1/x$ extended globally, it would be a polynomial whose product with $x$ is $1$.
\end{enumerate}

% VI17_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINED
% VI17_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINED
\begin{solution}
The open set is principal, so
\[
\OO_X(D(x))\cong k[x]_x=k[x,x^{-1}].
\]
Thus $x^{-1}$ is a perfectly regular section on $D(x)$: the possible pole at $x=0$ has been removed from the domain.

Suppose $x^{-1}$ extended to a global section of $X$.  Since
\[
\Gamma(X,\OO_X)\cong k[x],
\]
there would be a polynomial $p(x)$ whose restriction to $D(x)$ equals $x^{-1}$.  The localization map $k[x]\to k[x,x^{-1}]$ is injective because $k[x]$ is a domain.  Hence equality $p=x^{-1}$ in the localization implies
\[
xp(x)=1
\]
in $k[x]$.  This is impossible, since $x$ is not a unit in the polynomial ring.  Therefore $1/x$ is regular on $D(x)$ but has no global regular extension to $X$.
\end{solution}



\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Regularity is relative to an open domain: denominators are permitted exactly where they are invertible.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Replace $x$ by any polynomial $f$ and characterize functions regular on $D(f)$.

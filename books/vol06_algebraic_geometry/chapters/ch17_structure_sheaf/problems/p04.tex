\begin{problem}[Recover the ring from global sections]\label{prob:vi17-p04}
Show that the canonical map $A\to\Gamma(\Spec A,\OO_{\Spec A})$ is an isomorphism.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the first indication that an affine ring is not lost when it is converted into geometry.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Use $\Spec A=D(1)$ and the principal-open formula.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Compute $A_1$.
\item Check that $a\in A$ maps to the family $a/1$ of germs.
\end{enumerate}

% VI17_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINED
% VI17_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINED
\begin{solution}
Let $X=\Spec A$.  Because $1$ belongs to no prime, $X=D(1)$.  Applying the principal-open theorem gives a canonical isomorphism
\[
\Gamma(X,\OO_X)=\OO_X(D(1))\cong A_1.
\]
The localization map $A\to A_1$, $a\mapsto a/1$, is an isomorphism because the only elements being inverted are powers of $1$.  Hence
\[
A\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gamma(X,\OO_X).
\]

To check that this is the canonical map rather than merely an abstract ring isomorphism, observe that $a\in A$ is sent by the structure-sheaf construction to the global section whose germ at $\mfp$ is $a/1\in A_{\mfp}$.  Under $\OO_X(D(1))\cong A_1$, that section corresponds exactly to $a/1$.  The inverse therefore recovers the original element $a$.  Thus the affine ring is recovered functorially as the ring of global regular functions.
\end{solution}



\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Affine geometry keeps the original ring as the ring of global regular functions.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
VI/19 upgrades this observation to the universal property of affine schemes.

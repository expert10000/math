\begin{problem}[Local fractions and the basis-sheaf construction agree]\label{prob:vi17-p19}
Compare two constructions of $\OO_{\Spec A}$: (i) locally representable families in the stalks $A_{\mfp}$, and (ii) the sheaf determined on the basis $D(f)$ by $A_f$.  Prove they are canonically isomorphic.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
The two standard definitions emphasize different ideas---germs versus localization on a basis---and should produce the same object.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Use the principal-open theorem for construction (i), then apply basis reconstruction.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Construction (i) has $\OO(D(f))\cong A_f$.
\item The restriction maps coincide with localization maps.
\item Invoke uniqueness of a sheaf reconstructed from basis data.
\end{enumerate}

% VI17_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINED
% VI17_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINED
\begin{solution}
Let $\OO^{\mathrm{germ}}$ denote the sheaf defined by locally representable families of germs, and let $\OO^{\mathrm{bas}}$ denote the sheaf obtained from the localization data $D(f)\mapsto A_f$ on the distinguished basis.

For the germ construction, the principal-open theorem gives canonical isomorphisms
\[
\OO^{\mathrm{germ}}(D(f))\xrightarrow{\sim}A_f.
\]
Moreover, if $D(fg)\subseteq D(f)$, then under these identifications the restriction map is exactly the localization map
\[
A_f\longrightarrow A_{fg}.
\]
Thus the restriction of $\OO^{\mathrm{germ}}$ to the principal-open basis is precisely the same basis presheaf used to define $\OO^{\mathrm{bas}}$.

A sheaf on a space is determined, up to unique isomorphism, by its compatible section data on a basis stable under finite intersections.  Explicitly, on an arbitrary open $U$, cover by principal opens, identify local sections through the maps above, and glue.  Compatibility on overlaps ensures that this construction is independent of the chosen cover and commutes with restrictions.

Therefore there is a unique sheaf isomorphism
\[
\OO^{\mathrm{germ}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\OO^{\mathrm{bas}}
\]
whose restriction to every $D(f)$ is the identity on $A_f$.  Hence the two standard constructions of the affine structure sheaf are canonically equivalent.
\end{solution}



\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The structure sheaf can be viewed either as a sheaf of locally varying germs or as the sheaf generated by the localization presheaf on principal opens.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Carry out the same comparison for the sheaf $\widetilde M$ associated to an $A$-module.

\begin{problem}[Units in a stalk]\label{prob:vi17-p14}
Prove that $a/s\in A_{\mfp}$ is a unit iff $a\notin\mfp$, and deduce that the maximal ideal is $\mfp A_{\mfp}$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
The locally ringed nature of affine geometry starts with this elementary criterion.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Denominators are already outside $\mfp$; a numerator outside $\mfp$ is also invertible.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $a\notin\mfp$, write the inverse $s/a$.
\item If a fraction with numerator in $\mfp$ were a unit, clear denominators and use primality.
\end{enumerate}

% VI17_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINED
% VI17_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINED
\begin{solution}
Every element of $A_{\mfp}$ is represented by $a/s$ with $s\notin\mfp$.
If $a\notin\mfp$, then both $a$ and $s$ belong to the multiplicative set being inverted, so
\[
\frac{s}{a}\in A_{\mfp}
\]
and
\[
\frac as\frac sa=1.
\]
Hence $a/s$ is a unit.

Conversely suppose $a\in\mfp$ and $a/s$ were a unit.  Then for some $b/t\in A_{\mfp}$,
\[
\frac{ab}{st}=1.
\]
The localization equality criterion gives $u\notin\mfp$ such that
\[
u(ab-st)=0,
\quad\text{so}\quad
uab=ust.
\]
The left side lies in $\mfp$ because $a\in\mfp$.  Hence $ust\in\mfp$.  But $u,s,t\notin\mfp$, contradicting primality of $\mfp$.  Thus $a/s$ is not a unit.

Therefore the nonunits of $A_{\mfp}$ are precisely the fractions with numerator in $\mfp$, which form the ideal $\mfp A_{\mfp}$.  Since every element outside this ideal is a unit, $\mfp A_{\mfp}$ is the unique maximal ideal.
\end{solution}



\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The prime $\mfp$ becomes the unique closed direction in its local ring.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Use the criterion to show the unit locus of a global section $a$ is $D(a)$.

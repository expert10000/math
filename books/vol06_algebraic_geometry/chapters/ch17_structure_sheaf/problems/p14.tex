\begin{problem}[Units in a stalk]\label{prob:vi17-p14}
Prove that $a/s\in A_{\mfp}$ is a unit iff $a\notin\mfp$, and deduce that the maximal ideal is $\mfp A_{\mfp}$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
The locally ringed nature of affine geometry starts with this elementary criterion.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Denominators are already outside $\mfp$; a numerator outside $\mfp$ is also invertible.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $a\notin\mfp$, write the inverse $s/a$.
\item If a fraction with numerator in $\mfp$ were a unit, clear denominators and use primality.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
If $a\notin\mfp$, then $s/a$ is a valid element of $A_{\mfp}$ and is inverse to $a/s$.  Conversely, suppose $a\in\mfp$ and $(a/s)(b/t)=1$.  Then for some $u\notin\mfp$, $u(ab-st)=0$.  Hence $uab=ust$.  The left side lies in $\mfp$, so $ust\in\mfp$.  Since $u,s,t\notin\mfp$ and $\mfp$ is prime, this is impossible.  Thus the nonunits are exactly fractions with numerator in $\mfp$, namely $\mfp A_{\mfp}$, the unique maximal ideal.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The prime $\mfp$ becomes the unique closed direction in its local ring.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Use the criterion to show the unit locus of a global section $a$ is $D(a)$.

% VI/05 exercise solutions. Problems and Challenges carry their own full solutions.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.1}
The parabola has coordinate ring \(k[x,y]/(y-x^2)\cong k[x]\), a domain, so it is
irreducible.  The ring \(k[x,y]/(xy)\) has the nonzero zero divisors \(\bar x\) and
\(\bar y\), so \(V(xy)\) is reducible.  Finally
\[
k[x,y]/(xy-1)\cong k[x,x^{-1}]
\]
is a domain, so the hyperbola is irreducible.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.2}
The set \(D_X(f)\) is nonempty and open.  Every nonempty open subset of an irreducible
space is dense, hence \(\overline{D_X(f)}=X\).

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.3}
Set \(h=f-g\).  The closed set \(V_X(h)\) contains the dense subset \(U\), hence it is all
of \(X\).  Therefore \(h=0\) as a polynomial function on \(X\), so \(f=g\) in \(k[X]\).

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.4}
The affine line is irreducible because \(k[t]\) is a domain.  The image of an irreducible
space under a continuous map is irreducible.  Every point \((x,y)\in C\) with \(x\neq0\)
has \(t=y/x\), giving \(t^2=x\) and \(t^3=y\); the point with \(x=0\) is the origin and
comes from \(t=0\).  Thus the parametrization is surjective and \(C\) is irreducible.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.5}
We have
\[
X=V(x)\cup V(y)\cup V(x-y)\cup V(x+y).
\]
Under \(\operatorname{char}k\neq2\) these are four distinct irreducible lines, none
containing another.  All contain the origin, so their component graph is connected.
Therefore \(X\) is connected but reducible.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.6}
The equation \(xy=0\) gives \(X=V(x)\cup V(y)\), two irreducible planes in
\(\mathbb A^3\).  They meet along the \(z\)-axis \(V(x,y)\).  Thus the component graph is
one edge; \(X\) is connected and reducible.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.7}
The classes \(\bar x,\bar y\) are nonzero and satisfy \(\bar x\bar y=0\).  Geometrically
\(V(xy)=V(x)\cup V(y)\), and the two components meet at the origin, so the space is
connected.  Over an algebraically closed field the connectedness--idempotent criterion
therefore rules out nontrivial idempotents.  Zero divisors detect reducibility; idempotents
detect disconnectedness.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.8}
Since \((x)+(x-1)=(1)\), CRT gives
\[
k[x,y]/(x(x-1))\cong k[y]\times k[y].
\]
The class \(\bar x\) satisfies \(\bar x^2=\bar x\) and is neither \(0\) nor \(1\).  Its
zero and one loci are the two vertical lines.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.9}
Every \(X_i\) is connected.  Since \(X_1\) meets \(X_2\), their union is connected; that
union meets \(X_3\), and the enlarged union meets \(X_4\).  Inductively all of \(X\) is
connected.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.10}
If \(f(X)=A\cup B\) with \(A,B\) closed in the image, then
\[
X=f^{-1}(A)\cup f^{-1}(B).
\]
Irreducibility forces one inverse image to be all of \(X\), so one of \(A,B\) is all of
the image.  Thus the image is irreducible.  The closure of an irreducible subset is
irreducible.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.11}
Because \(Z\subsetneq X\), the complement is nonempty open.  Every nonempty open subset of
an irreducible space is irreducible and dense.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.12}
The affine line is irreducible and connected; \(k[t]\) is a domain with only trivial
idempotents.  The crossing axes are reducible but connected; \(k[x,y]/(xy)\) has zero
divisors but no nontrivial idempotents.  The two parallel lines are reducible and
disconnected; their ring is \(k[y]\times k[y]\).  The three distinct points are reducible
and disconnected, with coordinate ring \(k^3\).

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.13}
The inclusion \((xy,xz)\subseteq(x)\cap(y,z)\) is clear.  Conversely, if
\(h\in(x)\cap(y,z)\), write \(h=xq=ay+bz\).  Reducing modulo \(x\) gives
\(\bar a y+\bar b z=0\) in \(k[y,z]\), so the coefficients may be adjusted to show
\(q\in(y,z)\); equivalently, using that \((y,z)\) is prime and \(x\notin(y,z)\),
\(xq\in(y,z)\) implies \(q\in(y,z)\).  Hence \(h\in x(y,z)=(xy,xz)\).
Thus
\[
V(xy,xz)=V(x)\cup V(y,z).
\]
The plane and line meet at the origin, so the set is connected and reducible.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.14}
The factorization gives three irreducible vertical lines \(V(x)\), \(V(x-1)\), and
\(V(x-a)\).  They are pairwise disjoint.  Their defining ideals are pairwise comaximal,
so CRT yields
\[
k[X]\cong k[x,y]/(x)\times k[x,y]/(x-1)\times k[x,y]/(x-a)
\cong k[y]^3.
\]
Hence the irreducible components are also the connected components.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.15}
Let \(f:X\to Y\) be continuous with \(X\) connected.  If \(f(X)=U\sqcup V\) were a
separation in the image topology, then \(f^{-1}(U)\) and \(f^{-1}(V)\) would be disjoint,
nonempty, open subsets whose union is \(X\), a contradiction.  Thus \(f(X)\) is connected.
Every morphism is continuous, so the algebraic-geometric statement follows.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.16}
Applying \(V\) gives
\[
V(I)=V(\mathfrak p_1)\cup\cdots\cup V(\mathfrak p_r).
\]
Each \(V(\mathfrak p_i)\) is irreducible because \(\mathfrak p_i\) is prime.  If
\(V(\mathfrak p_i)\subseteq V(\mathfrak p_j)\), the Nullstellensatz and radicality give
\(\mathfrak p_j\subseteq\mathfrak p_i\), contradicting the noncontainment hypothesis unless
\(i=j\).  Thus the displayed pieces are exactly the maximal irreducible closed subsets.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.17}
Write
\[
L_1=V(x,y),\quad L_2=V(x,z),\quad C=V(z-1,y-x^2).
\]
Then
\[
L_1\cap L_2=\{(0,0,0)\},\qquad
L_1\cap C=\{(0,0,1)\},\qquad
L_2\cap C=\varnothing.
\]
The component graph is therefore the path \(L_2---L_1---C\).  It is connected, so \(Y\)
is connected.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.18}
For \(r=2\), two nonempty opens in an irreducible space intersect.  Their intersection is
again nonempty open and hence dense and irreducible.  Induction gives the result for every
finite \(r\).  For principal opens,
\[
D_X(f_1)\cap\cdots\cap D_X(f_r)=D_X(f_1\cdots f_r),
\]
which is therefore nonempty and dense whenever all the original principal opens are
nonempty.

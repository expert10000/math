\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.1}
The parabola has coordinate ring
\[
k[x,y]/(y-x^2)\cong k[x],
\]
which is a domain, so it is irreducible.

For \(V(xy)\),
\[
k[x,y]/(xy)
\]
has the nonzero zero divisors \(\bar x\) and \(\bar y\), so the set is reducible.

For \(V(xy-1)\),
\[
k[x,y]/(xy-1)\cong k[x,x^{-1}],
\]
a domain, so the hyperbola is irreducible.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.2}
The set \(D_X(f)\) is open.  By assumption it is nonempty.  Every nonempty open subset of
an irreducible space is dense, hence
\[
\overline{D_X(f)}=X.
\]

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.3}
The function
\[
h=f-g
\]
vanishes on \(U\).  Its zero set
\[
V_X(h)
\]
is closed and contains the dense subset \(U\).  Therefore
\[
V_X(h)=X,
\]
so \(h=0\) in \(k[X]\), i.e. \(f=g\).

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.4}
The affine line is irreducible because its coordinate ring \(k[t]\) is a domain.  The image
of an irreducible space under a continuous map is irreducible, so
\[
\phi(\mathbb A_k^1)
\]
is irreducible.  Its closure is therefore irreducible.

For any \((x,y)\in C\) with \(x\neq0\), put \(t=y/x\).  Then
\[
t^2=x,\qquad t^3=y.
\]
Thus the image contains \(C\setminus\{(0,0)\}\), and it also contains the origin at \(t=0\).
Hence the image is all of \(C\).  Therefore \(C\) is irreducible.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.5}
We have
\[
X=V(x)\cup V(y)\cup V(x-y)\cup V(x+y).
\]
Under \(\operatorname{char}k\neq2\), these are four distinct lines.  Each line is
irreducible, and none contains another, so they are exactly the irreducible components.
All four contain the origin, hence the component-intersection graph is connected.  Therefore
\(X\) is connected but reducible.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.6}
The equation \(xy=0\) gives
\[
X=V(x)\cup V(y).
\]
These are two irreducible planes in \(\mathbb A_k^3\).  Their intersection is
\[
V(x,y),
\]
the \(z\)-axis, so the component-intersection graph consists of two vertices joined by an
edge.  Hence \(X\) is connected.  Since it is a union of two proper closed subsets, it is
reducible.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.7}
The nonzero classes \(\bar x,\bar y\) satisfy
\[
\bar x\bar y=0,
\]
so \(A\) has zero divisors.

Geometrically,
\[
V(xy)=V(x)\cup V(y)
\]
is the union of two irreducible lines meeting at the origin, hence it is connected.  Over an
algebraically closed field, connectedness is equivalent to the absence of nontrivial
idempotents in the coordinate ring.  Thus \(A\) has no nontrivial idempotents.

Zero divisors detect reducibility; idempotents detect disconnectedness.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.8}
Since
\[
(x)+(x-1)=(1),
\]
the Chinese remainder theorem gives
\[
k[x,y]/(x(x-1))
\cong
k[x,y]/(x)\times k[x,y]/(x-1)
\cong
k[y]\times k[y].
\]
The class \(\bar x\) satisfies
\[
\bar x^2=\bar x
\]
and is neither \(0\) nor \(1\).  It is therefore a nontrivial idempotent.  The two clopen
pieces are the lines
\[
V(x)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
V(x-1).
\]

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.9}
Each \(X_i\) is connected because it is irreducible.  The union
\[
X_1\cup X_2
\]
is connected because the two pieces meet.  This union meets \(X_3\), so
\[
X_1\cup X_2\cup X_3
\]
is connected.  That union meets \(X_4\), hence all of \(X\) is connected.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.10}
Let \(f:X\to Y\) be continuous and \(X\) irreducible.  Suppose
\[
f(X)=A\cup B
\]
with \(A,B\) closed in \(f(X)\).  Then
\[
X=f^{-1}(A)\cup f^{-1}(B)
\]
is a union of two closed subsets.  Irreducibility forces one preimage to equal \(X\), hence
one of \(A,B\) equals \(f(X)\).  Thus \(f(X)\) is irreducible.  The closure of an
irreducible subset is irreducible, so \(\overline{\phi(X)}\) is irreducible.

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.11}
Because \(Z\subsetneq X\), the complement
\[
U=X\setminus Z
\]
is nonempty open.  A nonempty open subset of an irreducible space is irreducible and dense,
so
\[
\overline U=X.
\]

\subsection*{Solution to Exercise VI.05.12}
The affine line is irreducible and connected; its coordinate ring \(k[t]\) is a domain and
has only trivial idempotents.

The crossing axes \(V(xy)\) are reducible but connected; the coordinate ring has zero
divisors but no nontrivial idempotents.

The two parallel lines \(V(x(x-1))\) are reducible and disconnected; the coordinate ring
splits as
\[
k[y]\times k[y]
\]
and has nontrivial idempotents.

The three-point set \(\{0,1,2\}\) is reducible and disconnected.  Its coordinate ring is
isomorphic to
\[
k\times k\times k
\]
when the three points are distinct in \(k\), and therefore contains many nontrivial
idempotents.

\subsection*{Problem VI.05.P3 --- Three irreducible components}
\label{prob:vi-05-p3}

\paragraph{Problem.}
\label{prob:vi05-p3}
Let
\[
Y
=
V(x^2-yz,\;xz-x)
\subseteq
\mathbb A_k^3.
\]
Show that \(Y\) has exactly three irreducible components.  Describe the components and their
prime ideals.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is a model decomposition problem.  The equations do not initially display the
components; one must split cases, recognize each resulting piece, and certify irreducibility
algebraically.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
\begin{itemize}
\item factor \(xz-x=x(z-1)\);
\item coordinate rings of lines and parabolas;
\item prime ideals characterize irreducible affine algebraic sets.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\textbf{Hint 1.} Split the second equation into the cases \(x=0\) and \(z=1\).

\textbf{Hint 2.} In the case \(x=0\), the first equation becomes \(yz=0\).

\textbf{Hint 3.} In the case \(z=1\), the first equation becomes \(y=x^2\).
Check that the resulting three ideals are prime.

\paragraph{Solution.}
The equations are
\[
x^2-yz=0,
\qquad
x(z-1)=0.
\]
Hence every point satisfies either
\[
x=0
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
z=1.
\]

If \(z=1\), the first equation becomes
\[
y=x^2,
\]
giving
\[
C=V(z-1,\;y-x^2).
\]
Its coordinate ring is isomorphic to \(k[x]\), so its ideal is prime and \(C\) is
irreducible.

If \(x=0\), the first equation becomes
\[
yz=0.
\]
Thus we obtain
\[
L_1=V(x,y)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
L_2=V(x,z).
\]
Their coordinate rings are \(k[z]\) and \(k[y]\), so both ideals are prime.

Therefore
\[
Y=L_1\cup L_2\cup C.
\]
No one of these three components contains another, so they are exactly the irreducible
components, with prime ideals
\[
(x,y),
\qquad
(x,z),
\qquad
(z-1,\;y-x^2).
\]

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A seemingly nonlinear system decomposes because one equation factors.  The three
branches are two coordinate lines and a parabola in the plane \(z=1\).  The prime ideals
certify that no further decomposition is possible.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Compute all pairwise intersections of the three components.
\item Determine whether \(Y\) is connected.
\item Compute the coordinate ring of each component.
\item Revisit the same example in VI/32 and compute tangent spaces at the intersection
points.
\end{enumerate}

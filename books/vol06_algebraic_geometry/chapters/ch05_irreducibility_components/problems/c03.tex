\subsection*{Challenge VI.05.C3 --- The rank-one determinantal variety is irreducible}
\label{chal:vi05-c3}
\paragraph{Problem.}
Assume \(k\) is algebraically closed and let \(m,n\ge2\).  Identify
\(\mathbb A_k^{mn}\) with the space of \(m\times n\) matrices.  Let \(R_{m,n}\) be the
subset of matrices of rank at most \(1\).

\begin{enumerate}
\item Show that \(R_{m,n}\) is algebraic, cut out by the \(2\times2\) minors.
\item Consider the morphism
\[
\Phi:\mathbb A_k^m\times\mathbb A_k^n\longrightarrow\mathbb A_k^{mn},
\qquad (u,v)\longmapsto uv^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Show that \(\Phi\) is surjective onto \(R_{m,n}\).
\item Deduce that \(R_{m,n}\) is irreducible.
\end{enumerate}
\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Determinantal varieties are fundamental examples throughout algebraic geometry.  This
challenge shows how irreducibility can be proved without factoring equations: parametrize
the entire algebraic set by an irreducible source.
\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
A matrix has rank at most one exactly when every two columns are proportional, equivalently
all \(2\times2\) minors vanish.  Images of irreducible spaces under continuous maps are
irreducible.
\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\textbf{Hint 1.} Rank \(\le1\) is equivalent to vanishing of all \(2\times2\) minors.
\textbf{Hint 2.} For a nonzero rank-one matrix, choose one nonzero column \(u\); every
column is a scalar multiple of \(u\).
\textbf{Hint 3.} The domain \(\mathbb A^{m+n}\) is irreducible.
\paragraph{Solution.}
Every \(2\times2\) minor is a polynomial in the matrix entries, so their common zero set is
algebraic.  Linear algebra gives that all these minors vanish exactly when the rank is at
most one.
The map \(\Phi\) is polynomial in the coordinates.  If \(A\) has rank one, choose a
nonzero column \(u\).  Every column of \(A\) is \(v_j u\) for a unique scalar \(v_j\), so
\(A=uv^{\mathsf T}\).  The zero matrix is obtained by taking \(u=0\).  Hence
\(\Phi(\mathbb A^{m+n})=R_{m,n}\).
Because \(k\) is algebraically closed, it is infinite, and affine space is irreducible.
The continuous image of an irreducible space is irreducible.  Therefore \(R_{m,n}\) is
irreducible.
\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The minors certify closedness; the outer-product parametrization certifies irreducibility.
This pair of arguments is a prototype for many orbit closures and determinantal varieties.
\paragraph{Extensions.}
For rank at most \(r\), use matrix multiplication
\(\mathbb A^{m\times r}\times\mathbb A^{r\times n}\to\mathbb A^{m\times n}\).  Determine
what additional argument is needed to prove irreducibility of the rank-\(\le r\) locus.

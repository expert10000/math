\subsection*{Challenge VI.05.C2 --- Primitive idempotents and connected components}
\label{chal:vi05-c2}
\paragraph{Problem.}
Assume \(k\) is algebraically closed and let \(X\) be an affine algebraic set with connected
components \(C_1,\dots,C_s\).  Prove that there exist pairwise orthogonal idempotents
\(e_1,\dots,e_s\in k[X]\) satisfying
\[
e_1+\cdots+e_s=1,
\]
with \(e_i=1\) on \(C_i\) and \(e_i=0\) on every other component.  Deduce
\[
k[X]\cong\prod_{i=1}^s k[C_i].
\]
Show that the \(e_i\) are primitive idempotents and are unique up to reordering.
\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the finite canonical form of connectedness for affine algebraic sets.  The connected
components are not merely visible topologically; they are encoded by a unique system of
primitive idempotents in the coordinate ring.
\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Affine algebraic sets have finitely many irreducible components, hence finitely many
connected components.  Each connected component is a finite union of irreducible components
and therefore clopen.  Apply the two-piece idempotent criterion repeatedly.
\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\textbf{Hint 1.} The ideals \(I(C_i)\) are pairwise comaximal.
\textbf{Hint 2.} Apply the finite Chinese remainder theorem.
\textbf{Hint 3.} An idempotent is primitive exactly when the corresponding clopen set cannot
be split into two nonempty clopen subsets.
\paragraph{Solution.}
Each \(C_i\) is a finite union of irreducible components, hence closed; its complement is
the finite union of the remaining connected components, so it is open as well.  Thus the
\(C_i\) are pairwise disjoint clopen algebraic subsets.  By the Nullstellensatz their
vanishing ideals are pairwise comaximal.  Finite CRT gives
\[
k[X]\cong\prod_i k[C_i].
\]
Let \(e_i\) be the inverse image of the tuple with \(1\) in the \(i\)-th factor and \(0\)
elsewhere.  Then
\[
e_i^2=e_i,\qquad e_ie_j=0\ (i\neq j),\qquad \sum_i e_i=1,
\]
and the required value conditions are immediate.
If \(e_i=f+g\) were a decomposition into two nonzero orthogonal idempotents, the associated
clopen subset \(C_i\) would split into two nonempty clopen pieces, contradicting connectedness.
Hence each \(e_i\) is primitive.  Conversely, every primitive idempotent defines one
connected component, so the set of primitive idempotents is unique up to permutation.
\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The Boolean algebra of clopen subsets is visible inside the ring as its idempotents.  The
primitive atoms of that Boolean algebra are precisely the connected components.
\paragraph{Extensions.}
Revisit this statement for \(\Spec A\), where it holds for arbitrary commutative rings and
requires no algebraically closed base field.

% VI46-DETAILED-CHALLENGE-01
\subsection*{Challenge VI.46.1}
\begin{solution}
Flabbiness is an open-restriction extension property; softness concerns extension from closed subsets, and fineness uses enough local endomorphisms, typically built from partitions of unity.  On paracompact spaces, fine and soft sheaves satisfy useful acyclicity theorems under additional hypotheses.  These are distinct mechanisms and should not be identified without the topology needed for the implications.
\end{solution}

% VI46-DETAILED-CHALLENGE-02
\subsection*{Challenge VI.46.2}
\begin{solution}
Define \(G^0(\mathcal F)=\prod_x\mathcal F_x\), embed \(\mathcal F\), take the cokernel, and iterate.  Stalk maps make the process functorial.  Every term is flabby because restriction is a coordinate projection.  Exactness follows inductively from the definition of each successive cokernel.
\end{solution}

% VI46-DETAILED-CHALLENGE-03
\subsection*{Challenge VI.46.3}
\begin{solution}
Well-order the cover and construct a contracting operator by extending cocycle components successively from intersections to larger opens.  Flabbiness supplies every extension, while the cocycle equations give compatibility.  The resulting homotopy satisfies \(\delta h+h\delta=\mathrm{id}\) in positive degree, so all positive Cech classes vanish.
\end{solution}

% VI46-DETAILED-CHALLENGE-04
\subsection*{Challenge VI.46.4}
\begin{solution}
For every open \(V\subseteq Y\), \(\mathcal F|_{f^{-1}V}\) is flabby and hence has \(H^p=0\) for \(p>0\).  The local data used to form \(R^pf_*\mathcal F\) therefore vanish identically, so the higher direct-image sheaf is zero.
\end{solution}

% VI46-DETAILED-CHALLENGE-05
\subsection*{Challenge VI.46.5}
\begin{solution}
The exact sequence
\[
0\to\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal K_X\to\mathcal K_X/\mathcal O_X\to0
\]
places regular functions inside the flabby rational-function sheaf.  The quotient records local principal parts/poles modulo regular functions.  On curves it reconnects the divisor language with cohomology and leads naturally to principal-part exact sequences.
\end{solution}

% VI44-DETAILED-PROBLEM-SOLUTIONS
% VI44-DETAILED-PROBLEM-01
\subsection*{Problem VI.44.1}
\begin{solution}
A general conic has six coefficients.  Vanishing at the three coordinate points kills the \(x^2,y^2,z^2\) coefficients, leaving exactly
\[
\langle yz,xz,xy\rangle.
\]
Projectivizing gives the complete net of conics through \(p_1,p_2,p_3\).  Choosing the ordered basis \(yz,xz,xy\) as target coordinates gives
\[
[x:y:z]\dashrightarrow[yz:xz:xy],
\]
so the standard transformation is exactly the rational map associated to this net.
\end{solution}

% VI44-DETAILED-PROBLEM-02
\subsection*{Problem VI.44.2}
\begin{solution}
Substitute once more:
\[
\sigma^2[x:y:z]
=[(xz)(xy):(yz)(xy):(yz)(xz)]
=[x^2yz:xy^2z:xyz^2].
\]
The common factor is \(xyz\), of degree three.  Primitive reduction cancels it and leaves \([x:y:z]\).  Geometrically the three factors correspond to the three coordinate lines contracted by the first transformation into base points of the second.
\end{solution}

% VI44-DETAILED-PROBLEM-03
\subsection*{Problem VI.44.3}
\begin{solution}
Because \(\sigma^{-1}=\sigma\), express source coordinates in target variables by
\[
x=YZ,\quad y=XZ,\quad z=XY
\]
up to a common factor.  Substituting into \(ax+by+cz=0\) gives
\[
aYZ+bXZ+cXY=0.
\]
This conic passes through all three target coordinate points.  For general coefficients it is irreducible and is the closure of the birational image of the original line.
\end{solution}

% VI44-DETAILED-PROBLEM-04
\subsection*{Problem VI.44.4}
\begin{solution}
The equation \(ayz+bxz+cxy=0\) is exactly the pullback under \(\sigma\) of the target line \(aX+bY+cZ=0\).  Since the conic is assumed irreducible and is not exceptional, \(\sigma\) is birational on its generic point.  Therefore its strict/birational transform is precisely that target line.
\end{solution}

% VI44-DETAILED-PROBLEM-05
\subsection*{Problem VI.44.5}
\begin{solution}
Let \(M\) be a general target line.  Its pullback is a general conic \(Q\) through the three base points.  Bézout gives \(C\cdot Q=2d\).  At \(p_i\), genericity of \(Q\) gives local intersection contribution \(m_i\).  All remaining intersections lie in the isomorphism locus of \(\sigma\) and correspond one-for-one to intersections of \(M\) with \(C'=\sigma_*(C)\).  Hence
\[
\deg C'=2d-m_1-m_2-m_3.
\]
\end{solution}

% VI44-DETAILED-PROBLEM-06
\subsection*{Problem VI.44.6}
\begin{solution}
For a smooth cubic meeting each base point transversely, \(d=3\) and \(m_i=1\), so the image degree is \(6-3=3\).  The restriction induces an isomorphism of function fields, hence the normalization of the image is the original genus-one curve.  Degree alone does not guarantee the plane image is smooth; that depends on the configuration and must be checked separately.
\end{solution}

% VI44-DETAILED-PROBLEM-07
\subsection*{Problem VI.44.7}
\begin{solution}
If the cubic avoids the base points, the transform has degree six.  Birationality preserves the normalization, so its geometric genus is one.  A smooth plane sextic has arithmetic/geometric genus \(10\).  Therefore the sextic image must be singular, with total delta contribution \(9\) when the image is reduced and irreducible.  The singularities arise from the interaction with curves contracted by the Cremona map.
\end{solution}

% VI44-DETAILED-PROBLEM-08
\subsection*{Problem VI.44.8}
\begin{solution}
Choose \(A\in\operatorname{PGL}_3(k)\) taking \(q_i\) to the coordinate points \(p_i\).  Then
\[
\phi=A^{-1}\sigma A
\]
has source base locus \(\{q_1,q_2,q_3\}\), is quadratic, and is birational.  More generally one may post-compose by an arbitrary target automorphism \(B\), obtaining \(B\sigma A\); this changes target coordinates but not the essential quadratic base configuration.
\end{solution}

% VI44-DETAILED-PROBLEM-09
\subsection*{Problem VI.44.9}
\begin{solution}
On the affine torus, the standard map is \((u,v)\mapsto(u^{-1},v^{-1})\).  Therefore the induced automorphism of \(k(u,v)\) is
\[
u\mapsto u^{-1},\qquad v\mapsto v^{-1}.
\]
Its square fixes the generators, hence it has order two.  This function-field calculation proves birationality independently of the homogeneous involution computation.
\end{solution}

% VI44-DETAILED-PROBLEM-10
\subsection*{Problem VI.44.10}
\begin{solution}
The first transformation contracts each coordinate line to a base point of the inverse transformation.  Under coordinate substitution in the second quadratic triple, these exceptional components occur as the common factor \(xyz\).  Cancelling the gcd removes fixed exceptional contributions from the moving linear system and lowers the algebraic degree from the naive \(4\) to \(1\).  Thus degree collapse records exceptional-to-base incidence.
\end{solution}

% VI44-DETAILED-PROBLEM-11
\subsection*{Problem VI.44.11}
\begin{solution}
A target line pulls back to a conic, giving coefficient \(2H\).  Every conic in the defining net passes simply through each base point, so one subtracts one assigned base-point condition at each point.  On the blow-up those conditions become divisor classes \(E_i\), leading to the predicted class
\[
\sigma^*H=2H-E_1-E_2-E_3.
\]
VI/45 turns this bookkeeping identity into an actual equality in the Picard group of the resolved surface.
\end{solution}

% VI44-DETAILED-PROBLEM-12
\subsection*{Problem VI.44.12}
\begin{solution}
Use \(u=y/x\), \(v=z/x\) near \(p_1\).  Then
\[
\sigma[1:u:v]=[uv:v:u].
\]
Along \(v=tu\),
\[
[uv:v:u]=[tu^2:tu:u]=[tu:t:1]
\]
for \(u\neq0\), which tends to \([0:t:1]\).  Different slopes \(t\) have different limits along the target line \(X=0\).  Thus a resolution must remember the complete projective line of directions; this is the exceptional \(\mathbb P^1\) introduced by blowing up \(p_1\).
\end{solution}

\subsection*{VI.21.P17: Topological points do not determine a scheme}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi21-p17}
Compare $X=\Spec k$ and $Y=\Spec k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$.  Prove their underlying spaces are homeomorphic but the schemes are not isomorphic.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
It gives the simplest demonstration that a scheme point is more than a topological point.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate point statements through local rings and residue fields; when the target is affine, use the VI/19 universal property and then verify the pointwise interpretation.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Identify the relevant canonical ring or stalk homomorphism.
\item Track maximal ideals or prime contractions to identify the image point.
\item Use locality to pass to residue fields when needed.
\item Check independence, uniqueness, or functoriality explicitly rather than only on points.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
The field $k$ has the single prime $(0)$.  In the dual-number ring
$A=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ every prime contains the nilpotent $\varepsilon$, and
$A/(\varepsilon)\cong k$, so $(\varepsilon)$ is the unique prime.  Hence both spectra are one-point
spaces and are automatically homeomorphic.

However the affine schemes recover their global rings:
\[
\Gamma(X,\OO_X)=k,
\qquad
\Gamma(Y,\OO_Y)=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\]
The second ring contains the nonzero nilpotent $\varepsilon$, whereas a field has no nonzero
nilpotents, so the rings are not isomorphic.  An affine-scheme isomorphism would induce an
isomorphism on global sections, a contradiction.  Thus topology alone forgets infinitesimal
scheme structure.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
It gives the simplest demonstration that a scheme point is more than a topological point.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Compare different nilpotent thickenings and one-point spectra of nonisomorphic fields.

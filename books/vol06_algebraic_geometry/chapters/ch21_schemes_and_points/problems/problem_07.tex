\subsection*{VI.21.P7: Specialization in an affine spectrum}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi21-p07}
For primes $\mathfrak p,\mathfrak q\subset A$, prove
\[
\mathfrak q\in\overline{\{\mathfrak p\}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak q.
\]
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
It converts the topological specialization relation into a concrete order relation on prime ideals.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate point statements through local rings and residue fields; when the target is affine, use the VI/19 universal property and then verify the pointwise interpretation.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Identify the relevant canonical ring or stalk homomorphism.
\item Track maximal ideals or prime contractions to identify the image point.
\item Use locality to pass to residue fields when needed.
\item Check independence, uniqueness, or functoriality explicitly rather than only on points.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
The closure of a point $\mathfrak p\in\Spec A$ is the intersection of all closed subsets
containing it.  A closed set $V(I)$ contains $\mathfrak p$ exactly when $I\subseteq\mathfrak p$.
The smallest such closed set is therefore
\[
V(\mathfrak p)=\{\mathfrak q\in\Spec A:\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak q\}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\mathfrak q\in\overline{\{\mathfrak p\}}
\iff \mathfrak q\in V(\mathfrak p)
\iff \mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak q.
\]
Thus inclusion of primes runs from general to special: smaller primes are more generic and larger
primes are their specializations.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Apply it to domains, local rings, and reducible affine schemes.

\subsection*{VI.21.P15: Factorization through characteristic $p$}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi21-p15}
Prove that the absolute structure morphism $\pi_X$ factors through
\[
\Spec\mathbb F_p\longrightarrow\Spec\mathbb Z
\]
if and only if $p\cdot1=0$ in $\Gamma(X,\OO_X)$, and prove uniqueness of the factorization.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Pointwise residue characteristic and scheme-theoretic factorization are related but not identical; the latter detects nilpotent $p$-torsion in the structure sheaf.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate point statements through local rings and residue fields; when the target is affine, use the VI/19 universal property and then verify the pointwise interpretation.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Identify the relevant canonical ring or stalk homomorphism.
\item Track maximal ideals or prime contractions to identify the image point.
\item Use locality to pass to residue fields when needed.
\item Check independence, uniqueness, or functoriality explicitly rather than only on points.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
By VI/19's universal property for the affine target $\Spec\mathbb F_p$, morphisms
$X\to\Spec\mathbb F_p$ correspond to unital ring maps
\[
\mathbb F_p\longrightarrow\Gamma(X,\OO_X).
\]
Such a map exists exactly when the canonical map $\mathbb Z\to\Gamma(X,\OO_X)$ kills $p$, i.e.
when
\[
p\cdot1_{\Gamma(X,\OO_X)}=0.
\]
Indeed, this is precisely the universal property of the quotient $\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/(p)$.
When the map exists it is unique, since $\mathbb F_p$ is generated as a ring by $1$.  Its
associated morphism $X\to\Spec\mathbb F_p$ composed with
$\Spec\mathbb F_p\to\Spec\mathbb Z$ corresponds to the canonical map
$\mathbb Z\to\Gamma(X,\OO_X)$, so the composite is $\pi_X$.  Conversely any geometric
factorization gives the required ring factorization and hence forces $p1=0$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}

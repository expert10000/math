\subsection*{VI.21.P8: Morphisms preserve specialization}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi21-p08}
If $x\in\overline{\{y\}}$ and $f:X\to Y$ is a scheme morphism, prove
$f(x)\in\overline{\{f(y)\}}$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Specialization order is functorial under scheme morphisms.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate point statements through local rings and residue fields; when the target is affine, use the VI/19 universal property and then verify the pointwise interpretation.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Identify the relevant canonical ring or stalk homomorphism.
\item Track maximal ideals or prime contractions to identify the image point.
\item Use locality to pass to residue fields when needed.
\item Check independence, uniqueness, or functoriality explicitly rather than only on points.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
A scheme morphism is continuous on underlying topological spaces.  Since
$x\in\overline{\{y\}}$, applying $f$ gives
\[
f(x)\in f(\overline{\{y\}}).
\]
For every continuous map and every subset $S$, one has
$f(\overline S)\subseteq\overline{f(S)}$.  Therefore
\[
f(x)\in f(\overline{\{y\}})
\subseteq\overline{f(\{y\})}
=\overline{\{f(y)\}}.
\]
Hence $f(x)$ is a specialization of $f(y)$.  Equality of the two image points is allowed, so a
strict specialization can collapse under a morphism.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Specialization order is functorial under scheme morphisms.

\subsection*{VI.21.P11: Closed does not mean rational}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi21-p11}
Give a closed point of $\mathbb A^1_{\mathbb R}$ that is not $\mathbb R$-rational, and prove both assertions.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Closedness concerns the point topology; rationality also constrains the residue field over the base.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate point statements through local rings and residue fields; when the target is affine, use the VI/19 universal property and then verify the pointwise interpretation.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Identify the relevant canonical ring or stalk homomorphism.
\item Track maximal ideals or prime contractions to identify the image point.
\item Use locality to pass to residue fields when needed.
\item Check independence, uniqueness, or functoriality explicitly rather than only on points.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Take the ideal $\mathfrak m=(t^2+1)\subset\mathbb R[t]$.  Since $t^2+1$ is irreducible over
$\mathbb R$,
\[
\mathbb R[t]/(t^2+1)\cong\mathbb C
\]
is a field.  Thus $\mathfrak m$ is maximal and hence a closed point of
$\mathbb A^1_{\mathbb R}$.  Its residue field is $\kappa(\mathfrak m)\cong\mathbb C$.

If this point were $\mathbb R$-rational, the field-valued point description over $\mathbb R$ would
supply an $\mathbb R$-algebra homomorphism $\mathbb C\to\mathbb R$.  No such homomorphism exists:
the image of $i$ would have to be a real square root of $-1$.  Therefore the point is closed but not
$\mathbb R$-rational.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Generalize to irreducible polynomials of degree $>1$ over a non-algebraically-closed field.

\subsection*{VI.21.P20: Recovering the image point from a field map}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi21-p20}
Let $U=\Spec A\subseteq X$ and let $u:\Spec K\to X$ have image in $U$.  Prove that its image point in $U$ corresponds to the kernel of the induced ring map $A\to K$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
For field-valued points, the image prime, local map, and residue-field embedding are all recoverable directly from the affine ring homomorphism.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate point statements through local rings and residue fields; when the target is affine, use the VI/19 universal property and then verify the pointwise interpretation.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Identify the relevant canonical ring or stalk homomorphism.
\item Track maximal ideals or prime contractions to identify the image point.
\item Use locality to pass to residue fields when needed.
\item Check independence, uniqueness, or functoriality explicitly rather than only on points.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Because the unique source point maps into the open set $U$, the morphism $u$ factors uniquely
through $U$.  Thus we have an affine morphism
\[
\Spec K\longrightarrow\Spec A.
\]
By affine anti-equivalence it corresponds to a ring homomorphism
\[
\alpha:A\longrightarrow K.
\]
The unique prime ideal of the field $K$ is $(0)$.  The underlying spectrum map sends it to its
contraction under $\alpha$, namely
\[
\alpha^{-1}((0))=\ker\alpha.
\]
Hence the image point is exactly the prime $\ker(A\to K)$.  The local map at that point is the
localization $A_{\ker\alpha}\to K$, and the associated residue-field embedding is the factor of
$A\to K$ through $\kappa(\ker\alpha)$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Apply this to evaluation maps and non-rational closed points.

\subsection*{VI.21.P4: Classify morphisms from a field spectrum}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi21-p04}
Let $K$ be a field.  Prove that morphisms $\Spec K\to X$ are naturally in bijection with pairs
\[
(x,\iota),\qquad x\in X,\quad \iota:\kappa(x)\to K
\]
where $\iota$ is a field homomorphism.  State the relative version over a base field.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This separates the underlying image point from the extra residue-field embedding carried by a field-valued point.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate point statements through local rings and residue fields; when the target is affine, use the VI/19 universal property and then verify the pointwise interpretation.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Identify the relevant canonical ring or stalk homomorphism.
\item Track maximal ideals or prime contractions to identify the image point.
\item Use locality to pass to residue fields when needed.
\item Check independence, uniqueness, or functoriality explicitly rather than only on points.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
A morphism $u:\Spec K\to X$ has a unique image point $x$.  Its stalk map at the unique source
point is a local homomorphism
\[
\OO_{X,x}\longrightarrow K.
\]
The maximal ideal of the field $K$ is $(0)$.  Locality therefore says that the inverse image of
$(0)$ is $\mathfrak m_x$.  Hence the stalk map kills exactly $\mathfrak m_x$ and factors uniquely
through the quotient
\[
\kappa(x)=\OO_{X,x}/\mathfrak m_x\longrightarrow K.
\]
Conversely, given $(x,\iota)$, the field map $\iota$ induces
$\Spec K\to\Spec\kappa(x)$, and composing with the canonical point morphism
$i_x:\Spec\kappa(x)\to X$ gives a morphism with image $x$.  The two constructions are inverse
because they recover the same image point and the same local map.

If $X$ is a $k$-scheme and $K/k$ is a field extension, then $K$-valued points over $k$ correspond
to those pairs for which $\kappa(x)\to K$ is compatible with the structural maps from $k$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Use it to explain why two distinct $K$-valued points can have the same topological image.

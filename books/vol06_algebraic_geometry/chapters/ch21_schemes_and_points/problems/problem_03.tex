\subsection*{VI.21.P3: The canonical morphism of a point}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi21-p03}
For $x\in X$, construct canonically a morphism
\[
i_x:\Spec\kappa(x)\longrightarrow X
\]
with image $x$, and prove independence of the affine neighbourhood used in the construction.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
A scheme point is represented canonically by a one-point field spectrum carrying exactly the residue field needed at that point.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate point statements through local rings and residue fields; when the target is affine, use the VI/19 universal property and then verify the pointwise interpretation.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Identify the relevant canonical ring or stalk homomorphism.
\item Track maximal ideals or prime contractions to identify the image point.
\item Use locality to pass to residue fields when needed.
\item Check independence, uniqueness, or functoriality explicitly rather than only on points.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Choose an affine open neighbourhood $U=\Spec A$ of $x$, with $x$ corresponding to
$\mathfrak p\subset A$.  The canonical local evaluation is
\[
A\longrightarrow A_{\mathfrak p}\longrightarrow
A_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}=\kappa(\mathfrak p).
\]
By affine anti-equivalence it induces
\[
\Spec\kappa(\mathfrak p)\longrightarrow\Spec A=U\longrightarrow X.
\]
The unique source point $(0)$ contracts to the kernel $\mathfrak p$, so its image is exactly $x$.

To prove independence, shrink first to a principal neighbourhood $D(f)$ of $x$.  Since
$f\notin\mathfrak p$, its image in $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is nonzero and hence invertible, so the
map $A\to\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ factors uniquely through $A_f$.  Thus the point morphism constructed
inside $D(f)$ has the same composite to $U$.  Any two affine neighbourhoods have a smaller affine
neighbourhood of $x$ inside their intersection, and repeating the argument there shows the two
constructions coincide.  Intrinsically, all of them are induced by the same local quotient
$\OO_{X,x}\twoheadrightarrow\kappa(x)$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A scheme point is represented canonically by a one-point field spectrum carrying exactly the residue field needed at that point.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Show that $i_x$ factors through every open neighbourhood of $x$ and study its behavior under a scheme morphism.

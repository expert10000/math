\subsection*{VI.21.P13: The local scheme at a point}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi21-p13}
Construct the canonical morphism
\[
\lambda_x:\Spec\OO_{X,x}\longrightarrow X
\]
and prove that the closed point of the source maps to $x$, independently of the affine chart.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
The local ring itself defines a scheme that captures all sufficiently local generalizations of the chosen point.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate point statements through local rings and residue fields; when the target is affine, use the VI/19 universal property and then verify the pointwise interpretation.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Identify the relevant canonical ring or stalk homomorphism.
\item Track maximal ideals or prime contractions to identify the image point.
\item Use locality to pass to residue fields when needed.
\item Check independence, uniqueness, or functoriality explicitly rather than only on points.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Choose an affine neighbourhood $U=\Spec A$ of $x$, with $x$ corresponding to
$\mathfrak p$.  The canonical identification $\OO_{X,x}\cong A_{\mathfrak p}$ and the localization
map $A\to A_{\mathfrak p}$ induce
\[
\Spec A_{\mathfrak p}\longrightarrow\Spec A=U\longrightarrow X.
\]
The source is a local affine scheme whose unique closed point is
$\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}$.  Under contraction this maximal ideal maps to
$\mathfrak p$, hence to $x$.

If $V$ is another affine neighbourhood of $x$, choose a smaller affine neighbourhood
$W\subseteq U\cap V$ of $x$.  The localization maps obtained from $U$, $V$, and $W$ all induce the
same maps on the local ring $\OO_{X,x}$, because stalks are direct limits over neighbourhoods.
Their associated spectrum morphisms therefore agree.  Hence $\lambda_x$ is intrinsic to the point
$x$ and not to the chosen chart.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Identify the image of $\lambda_x$ and compare its closed point with the residue-field point $i_x$.

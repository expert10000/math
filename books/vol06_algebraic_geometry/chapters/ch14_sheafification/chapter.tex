\section*{Purpose and learning goals}

VI/12 introduced presheaves and VI/13 imposed the local-to-global sheaf axiom.  The next
question is unavoidable: what should one do with a useful presheaf that fails locality or
gluing?  \emph{Sheafification} is the canonical repair.  It replaces a presheaf by a sheaf
without changing its germs at any point, and it is characterized by a universal mapping
property rather than by an arbitrary choice of construction.

This chapter treats sheafification from three complementary viewpoints:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the universal property, which says what the associated sheaf \emph{is};
\item the espace \'{e}tal\'{e} construction, which proves existence concretely from stalks;
\item the plus construction, which makes the ``separate, then glue'' mechanism explicit.
\end{enumerate}
The ordinary and advanced example banks are intentionally large.  They show both ways in
which sheafification changes a presheaf: it may \emph{add} locally compatible sections, and
it may \emph{identify or kill} sections that have the same germs.

A corpus-wide legacy-problem ledger is now maintained for Volume VI.  The central legacy
item of this chapter is AG1 Exercise 4, a long associated-sheaf problem requiring existence,
the canonical morphism, the universal property, preservation of stalks, functoriality, and
the adjunction.  It is preserved as VI.14.P1 at essentially its full mathematical depth and
merged with the preferred reorganized sheaf notes rather than duplicated.

After this chapter, the reader should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
\item state the universal property of the associated sheaf;
\item distinguish a construction of sheafification from its universal characterization;
\item build the espace \'{e}tal\'{e} $E_{\mathcal F}=\coprod_x\mathcal F_x$;
\item prove that the germ images $\widetilde s(U)$ form a basis for a topology on $E_{\mathcal F}$;
\item construct the associated sheaf as locally representable continuous sections of
$E_{\mathcal F}\to X$;
\item construct the canonical morphism $\eta:\mathcal F\to\mathcal F^\#$;
\item prove $(\mathcal F^\#)_x\cong\mathcal F_x$ for every $x$;
\item prove and use the universal property;
\item prove uniqueness of sheafification up to a unique isomorphism;
\item sheafify morphisms and establish functoriality;
\item express sheafification as a left adjoint $a\dashv i$;
\item prove that sheafification of an already existing sheaf is canonically isomorphic to it;
\item understand why the unit need be neither injective nor surjective on sections;
\item compute sheafifications in constant, bounded-function, finite-space, and basis examples;
\item use the plus construction and its separatedness step;
\item keep quotient sheaves, image sheaves, and exactness for VI/15--VI/16, where the relevant
legacy problems are reserved.
\end{itemize}

\section{The universal problem}

Let $X$ be a topological space and let $\mathcal F$ be a presheaf of sets, abelian groups,
rings, or modules.  A sheafification should be a sheaf receiving a map from $\mathcal F$
through which every map from $\mathcal F$ to a sheaf factors uniquely.

\begin{definition}[Sheafification]
A \emph{sheafification} or \emph{associated sheaf} of $\mathcal F$ is a pair
$(\mathcal F^\#,\eta_{\mathcal F})$ consisting of a sheaf $\mathcal F^\#$ and a morphism of
presheaves
\[
\eta_{\mathcal F}:\mathcal F\longrightarrow\mathcal F^\#
\]
such that for every sheaf $\mathcal G$ and every presheaf morphism
$u:\mathcal F\to\mathcal G$, there exists a unique sheaf morphism
$\bar u:\mathcal F^\#\to\mathcal G$ satisfying
\[
 u=\bar u\circ\eta_{\mathcal F}.
\]
\end{definition}

\input{chapters/ch14_sheafification/figures/vi14_universal_triangle.tex}

The phrase ``closest sheaf'' means precisely this universal property.  It does \emph{not}
mean that $\mathcal F^\#(U)$ is numerically or set-theoretically close to $\mathcal F(U)$.
Indeed, on some opens sheafification can add sections, while on others it can identify
sections.

\section{Uniqueness before existence}

Universal properties give uniqueness automatically.

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness of sheafification]
If $(\mathcal A,\eta)$ and $(\mathcal B,\theta)$ are two sheafifications of the same
presheaf $\mathcal F$, there is a unique isomorphism
\[
\Phi:\mathcal A\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal B
\]
satisfying $\Phi\circ\eta=\theta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the universal property of $\mathcal A$ to $\theta:\mathcal F\to\mathcal B$ to obtain
$\Phi:\mathcal A\to\mathcal B$.  Similarly obtain $\Psi:\mathcal B\to\mathcal A$ with
$\Psi\theta=\eta$.  Then $(\Psi\Phi)\eta=\eta$.  By uniqueness in the universal property
of $\mathcal A$, $\Psi\Phi=\id_{\mathcal A}$.  Likewise $\Phi\Psi=\id_{\mathcal B}$.
Uniqueness of $\Phi$ is built into the same factorization property.
\end{proof}

Thus one may use whichever construction is most convenient and still speak of
\emph{the} sheafification up to canonical isomorphism.

\section{The espace \texorpdfstring{\'{e}tal\'{e}}{etale}: all germs over all points}

We now prove existence.  For each $x\in X$, let $\mathcal F_x$ be the stalk of the presheaf
from VI/13.  Form the disjoint union
\[
E_{\mathcal F}:=\coprod_{x\in X}\mathcal F_x
\]
and the projection
\[
\pi:E_{\mathcal F}\longrightarrow X,
\qquad a\in\mathcal F_x\longmapsto x.
\]

For an open $U\subseteq X$ and $s\in\mathcal F(U)$ define
\[
\widetilde s:U\longrightarrow E_{\mathcal F},
\qquad x\longmapsto s_x.
\]
Its image is
\[
\widetilde s(U)=\{s_x:x\in U\}.
\]

\input{chapters/ch14_sheafification/figures/vi14_etale_space.tex}

\begin{proposition}[The germ images form a basis]
The sets $\widetilde s(U)$, as $U$ and $s\in\mathcal F(U)$ vary, form a basis for a topology
on $E_{\mathcal F}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every point $a\in\mathcal F_x$ is represented by some $s\in\mathcal F(U)$ with $x\in U$,
so $a=s_x\in\widetilde s(U)$.  Suppose
$a\in\widetilde s(U)\cap\widetilde t(V)$ and $a\in\mathcal F_x$.  Then $s_x=t_x$.
Equality of germs gives a neighborhood $W\subseteq U\cap V$ of $x$ on which
$s|_W=t|_W$.  Hence
\[
a\in\widetilde{s|_W}(W)\subseteq\widetilde s(U)\cap\widetilde t(V).
\]
This is the basis-intersection criterion.
\end{proof}

With this topology, $\\pi$ is a local homeomorphism: on each basic open
$\widetilde s(U)$, the inverse of $\pi$ is $x\mapsto s_x$.

\section{Locally representable sections}

\begin{definition}[Associated sheaf from the espace \texorpdfstring{\'{e}tal\'{e}}{etale}]
For an open set $U\subseteq X$, define
\[
\mathcal F^\#(U)
=
\left\{\sigma:U\to E_{\mathcal F}:
\pi\circ\sigma=\id_U,
\ \sigma\text{ is continuous}\right\}.
\]
Restrictions are ordinary restrictions of maps.
\end{definition}

Continuity has a useful algebraic meaning.

\begin{proposition}[Local representability]
A section $\sigma:U\to E_{\mathcal F}$ belongs to $\mathcal F^\#(U)$ if and only if every
$x\in U$ has a neighborhood $V\subseteq U$ and some $s\in\mathcal F(V)$ such that
\[
\sigma(y)=s_y\qquad (y\in V).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $\sigma$ is continuous and $x\in U$, choose a basic neighborhood
$\widetilde s(V)$ of $\sigma(x)$.  Shrinking $V$ if necessary inside $U$, the local
homeomorphism property forces $\sigma|_V=\widetilde s$.  Conversely, a map which is locally
of the form $\widetilde s$ is continuous because each $\widetilde s$ is a homeomorphism
onto a basic open.
\end{proof}

\input{chapters/ch14_sheafification/figures/vi14_local_representability.tex}

\begin{theorem}[The associated presheaf is a sheaf]
$U\mapsto\mathcal F^\#(U)$ is a sheaf.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Compatible continuous sections of $\pi$ glue uniquely as ordinary maps.  Continuity is local
on the domain, so the glued map is continuous.  Because every local section satisfies
$\pi\sigma=\id$, the gluing does also.  Locality is equality of maps on an open cover.
\end{proof}

\section{The canonical morphism}

Define
\[
\eta_U:\mathcal F(U)\longrightarrow\mathcal F^\#(U),
\qquad
s\longmapsto\widetilde s.
\]
The compatibility
\[
\widetilde{s|_V}=\widetilde s|_V
\]
shows that these maps form a morphism of presheaves
\[
\eta_{\mathcal F}:\mathcal F\longrightarrow\mathcal F^\#.
\]

\input{chapters/ch14_sheafification/figures/vi14_unit_restriction.tex}

The unit may fail to be injective on sections: two sections of a presheaf can have the same
germ at every point without being equal if locality fails.  It may also fail to be
surjective: a locally representable family of germs can fail to arise from one original
global section if gluing fails.

\section{Sheafification preserves every stalk}

This theorem is the central local invariant of sheafification.

\begin{theorem}[Stalk preservation]
For every $x\in X$, the map induced by the unit is an isomorphism
\[
(\eta_{\mathcal F})_x:\mathcal F_x\xrightarrow{\sim}(\mathcal F^\#)_x.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Define
\[
\Phi_x:(\mathcal F^\#)_x\longrightarrow\mathcal F_x,
\qquad
[U,\sigma]_x\longmapsto\sigma(x).
\]
This is well defined: if two sheafified sections have the same germ, they agree on some
neighborhood of $x$ and hence have the same value in the fiber $\mathcal F_x$.

For surjectivity, let $a\in\mathcal F_x$.  Choose $s\in\mathcal F(U)$ representing $a$.
Then $\widetilde s\in\mathcal F^\#(U)$ and $\Phi_x((\widetilde s)_x)=a$.

For injectivity, suppose $\Phi_x(\sigma_x)=\Phi_x(\tau_x)$.  Then
$\sigma(x)=\tau(x)$ in the fiber.  By local representability, after shrinking around $x$,
write $\sigma=\widetilde s$ and $\tau=\widetilde t$.  Equality at $x$ means $s_x=t_x$ in
$\mathcal F_x$, so after shrinking once more $s$ and $t$ agree.  Hence $\sigma_x=\tau_x$.
The inverse is induced by $a=s_x\mapsto(\widetilde s)_x$, exactly the stalk map of
$\eta_{\mathcal F}$.
\end{proof}

\input{chapters/ch14_sheafification/figures/vi14_stalk_preservation.tex}

Thus sheafification changes global bookkeeping while leaving all local germs unchanged.

\section{The universal property: existence of the factorization}

Let $\mathcal G$ be a sheaf and $u:\mathcal F\to\mathcal G$ a presheaf morphism.  Every
$x$ has an induced stalk map $u_x:\mathcal F_x\to\mathcal G_x$.  If
$\sigma\in\mathcal F^\#(U)$, define a family of germs
\[
x\longmapsto u_x(\sigma(x))\in\mathcal G_x.
\]
Because $\sigma$ is locally represented by sections of $\mathcal F$, this family is locally
represented by sections of $\mathcal G$.  Since $\mathcal G$ is a sheaf, these local
representatives glue uniquely to a section of $\mathcal G(U)$.

\begin{theorem}[Universal property]
There exists a unique morphism of sheaves
\[
\bar u:\mathcal F^\#\longrightarrow\mathcal G
\]
with $u=\bar u\eta_{\mathcal F}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $\sigma\in\mathcal F^\#(U)$ choose an open cover $U=\bigcup_iU_i$ and
$s_i\in\mathcal F(U_i)$ with $\sigma|_{U_i}=\widetilde{s_i}$.  Set
$g_i=u(s_i)\in\mathcal G(U_i)$.  On $U_i\cap U_j$, equality of the sheafified sections
means the germs of $s_i$ and $s_j$ agree at every point.  Applying $u$ gives equal germs of
$g_i$ and $g_j$; because $\mathcal G$ is a sheaf, equality of all germs implies equality of
sections.  Hence the $g_i$ glue uniquely to $g\in\mathcal G(U)$.  Define
$\bar u_U(\sigma)=g$.  This is independent of the chosen local presentation and commutes
with restrictions.  For $s\in\mathcal F(U)$, the construction applied to $\widetilde s$
gives $u(s)$, so $\bar u\eta=u$.

If $v:\mathcal F^\#\to\mathcal G$ is another factorization, every sheafified section is
locally of the form $\widetilde s$, and on such a neighborhood
$v(\widetilde s)=u(s)=\bar u(\widetilde s)$.  Locality in $\mathcal G$ gives $v=\bar u$.
\end{proof}

\section{Functoriality}

Given $f:\mathcal F\to\mathcal G$ in $\PreSh(X)$, apply the universal property to
\[
\mathcal F\xrightarrow{f}\mathcal G\xrightarrow{\eta_{\mathcal G}}\mathcal G^\#.
\]
There is a unique sheaf morphism
\[
f^\#:\mathcal F^\#\longrightarrow\mathcal G^\#
\]
with
\[
f^\#\eta_{\mathcal F}=\eta_{\mathcal G}f.
\]

\input{chapters/ch14_sheafification/figures/vi14_functorial_square.tex}

\begin{proposition}[Sheafification is a functor]
The assignments $\mathcal F\mapsto\mathcal F^\#$ and $f\mapsto f^\#$ satisfy
\[
(\id_{\mathcal F})^\#=\id_{\mathcal F^\#},
\qquad
(gf)^\#=g^\#f^\#.
\]
Moreover, under the canonical stalk identifications,
\[
(f^\#)_x=f_x.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The identity and composition formulas follow from uniqueness in the universal property:
both candidate maps have the same composite with the unit.  The stalk formula follows from
naturality of the unit and the fact that both vertical unit maps are stalk isomorphisms.
\end{proof}

\section{Adjunction}

Let
\[
i:\Sh(X)\hookrightarrow\PreSh(X)
\]
be the inclusion functor.  Sheafification is left adjoint to $i$.

\begin{theorem}[Adjunction]
For every presheaf $\mathcal F$ and sheaf $\mathcal G$ there is a natural bijection
\[
\Hom_{\Sh(X)}(\mathcal F^\#,\mathcal G)
\cong
\Hom_{\PreSh(X)}(\mathcal F,i\mathcal G),
\]
given by precomposition with $\eta_{\mathcal F}$.  Symbolically,
\[
a:\PreSh(X)\rightleftarrows\Sh(X):i,
\qquad a\dashv i.
\]
\end{theorem}

\input{chapters/ch14_sheafification/figures/vi14_adjunction.tex}

This compact statement contains existence, uniqueness, and functoriality at once.

\section{Sheafifying a sheaf does nothing}

\begin{corollary}[Idempotence]
If $\mathcal F$ is already a sheaf, the unit
\[
\eta_{\mathcal F}:\mathcal F\longrightarrow\mathcal F^\#
\]
is an isomorphism.  Consequently
\[
(\mathcal F^\#)^\#\cong\mathcal F^\#.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply the universal property to the identity map $\mathcal F\to\mathcal F$.  It produces a
retraction $r:\mathcal F^\#\to\mathcal F$ with $r\eta=\id$.  The composites
$\eta r$ and $\id_{\mathcal F^\#}$ agree after precomposition with $\eta$, so uniqueness in
the universal property gives $\eta r=\id$.
\end{proof}

\section{Separated presheaves and the plus construction}

There is another construction that makes the repair mechanism transparent.  A presheaf is
\emph{separated} if locality holds, even if gluing may fail.

For an open $U$, consider pairs consisting of an open cover $U=\bigcup_iU_i$ and a compatible
family $s_i\in\mathcal F(U_i)$.  Two such families are equivalent if they agree after passing
to a common refinement.  Denote the resulting presheaf by $\mathcal F^+$.  There is a
natural map $\mathcal F\to\mathcal F^+$ sending a section to its restrictions.

\begin{theorem}[Plus construction]
For presheaves of sets or abelian groups:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathcal F^+$ is separated;
\item if $\mathcal F$ is separated, then $\mathcal F^+$ is a sheaf;
\item therefore $\mathcal F^{++}$ is always a sheaf and is canonically isomorphic to
$\mathcal F^\#$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\input{chapters/ch14_sheafification/figures/vi14_plus_construction.tex}

The first ``plus'' removes failure of uniqueness; the second removes failure of existence.
This notation is different from the legacy AG1 Exercise 4, where $\mathcal F^+$ denotes the
final associated sheaf built from stalks.  VI.14.P1 preserves the legacy notation inside the
problem while the chapter uses $\mathcal F^\#$ for the final sheafification.

\section{Restriction to an open subset}

If $j:U\hookrightarrow X$ is open, then sheafification commutes with restriction:
\[
(\mathcal F|_U)^\#\cong\mathcal F^\#|_U.
\]
This can be proved either by comparing stalks and universal properties or by observing that
the espace \'{e}tal\'{e} over $U$ is simply the restriction of $E_{\mathcal F}$ to $U$.

\input{chapters/ch14_sheafification/figures/vi14_restriction_open.tex}

\section{Sheafification on a basis}

Let $\mathcal B$ be a basis for $X$.  Suppose data are given on basis opens with compatible
restrictions, but locality or gluing may fail.  One can form germs using only basis
neighborhoods because they are cofinal among all neighborhoods.  The same espace
\'{e}tal\'{e} construction then produces the sheafification on all opens.  This is especially
important in algebraic geometry: principal opens $D(f)$ form a basis of $\Spec A$, and the
localization data $D(f)\mapsto A_f$ will feed directly into VI/17.

\section{What the unit can do to sections}

The map
\[
\eta_U:\mathcal F(U)\to\mathcal F^\#(U)
\]
can behave in three qualitatively different ways.
\begin{enumerate}
\item It can be an isomorphism, as when $\mathcal F$ is already a sheaf.
\item It can fail to be surjective: the constant presheaf on a disconnected open has too few
sections, while its sheafification contains independently chosen locally constant values.
\item It can fail to be injective: a presheaf can contain distinct sections whose restrictions
coincide near every point; sheafification identifies them because their germs are equal.
\end{enumerate}

\input{chapters/ch14_sheafification/figures/vi14_add_identify.tex}

This explains the right mental model:
\[
\boxed{\text{sheafification preserves local germs, not the original global section sets}.}
\]

\section{Common pitfalls}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Sheafification is not defined by simply imposing an equivalence relation on each
$\mathcal F(U)$ independently; new sections may also have to be added.
\item Equality of all stalks does not mean two presheaves are isomorphic.  It does mean their
sheafifications may be locally equivalent, and the universal property decides the actual
comparison.
\item The canonical map need be neither injective nor surjective on a fixed open set.
\item The phrase ``associated sheaf'' refers to a universal property; the espace
\'{e}tal\'{e} and plus constructions are models of that property.
\item In the standard plus construction, one application produces a separated presheaf and
two applications produce a sheaf.  Do not confuse that notation with sources that call the
final sheafification $\mathcal F^+$ directly.
\item The image presheaf and quotient presheaf of sheaves need not themselves be sheaves.
Those are VI/15 problems, although their repair uses the present chapter.
\item Stalkwise exactness and sheaf surjectivity belong to VI/16; sheafification is the tool,
not the final exactness theory.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Summary}

Sheafification is the universal local-to-global repair of a presheaf.  The associated sheaf
$\mathcal F^\#$ is characterized by
\[
\Hom_{\Sh(X)}(\mathcal F^\#,\mathcal G)
\cong
\Hom_{\PreSh(X)}(\mathcal F,i\mathcal G)
\]
for every sheaf $\mathcal G$, and it preserves all stalks:
\[
(\mathcal F^\#)_x\cong\mathcal F_x.
\]
The espace \'{e}tal\'{e} construction proves existence by turning germs into a local
homeomorphism over $X$; the plus construction explains the repair as separation followed by
gluing.  The unit may add sections or identify sections, but it never changes the local germ
seen at a point.

VI/15 uses this machinery to construct kernels, images, and quotient sheaves.  The Volume VI
legacy-problem ledger records AG1 Exercise 5 and AG2 Problem 8 there, so the sheafification
chapter does not consume or duplicate them.

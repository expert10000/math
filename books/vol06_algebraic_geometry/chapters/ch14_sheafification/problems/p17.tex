\begin{problem}[Sheafification as an idempotent reflector]
Prove that $a\circ i\cong\id_{\Sh_X}$ and $a\circ a\cong a$. Show that the second isomorphism is compatible with the unit.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Sheafification repairs a presheaf once; repeating the repair changes nothing.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the universal property, stalk preservation, and the fact that a morphism of sheaves is an isomorphism when it is so on every stalk.  Keep the distinction between a presheaf statement on sections and a local statement on germs explicit.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Apply the universal property to a presheaf that is already a sheaf.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
If $\mathcal G$ is a sheaf, the identity map $\mathcal G\to\mathcal G$ factors uniquely through the unit $\eta_{\mathcal G}:\mathcal G\to a\mathcal G$. Thus there is $r:a\mathcal G\to\mathcal G$ with $r\eta=\id$. By uniqueness, $\eta r=\id_{a\mathcal G}$ because both $\eta r$ and the identity have the same composite with $\eta$. Hence $a(i\mathcal G)\cong\mathcal G$. Taking $\mathcal G=a\mathcal F$ gives $a(a\mathcal F)\cong a\mathcal F$. The construction is defined through the unit and therefore is compatible with it; naturality follows from uniqueness of factorization.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The inclusion of sheaves is reflective: sheaves are precisely the fixed objects of the reflector.

\paragraph{Alternative approaches.}
Whenever possible one may replace the espace-etale model by the two-plus construction, or conversely replace a cover/refinement argument by the universal property.  The two approaches agree because both construct the same associated sheaf.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Relate fixed objects to the sheaf axiom and local equivalences.

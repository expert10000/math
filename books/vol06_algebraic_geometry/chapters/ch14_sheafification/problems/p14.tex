\begin{problem}[When is the naive constant presheaf already a sheaf?]
Let $A$ have at least two elements. On a topological space $X$, define the naive constant presheaf by $A(U)=A$ for nonempty $U$ and $A(\varnothing)=0$. Prove it is a sheaf iff every nonempty open subset of $X$ is connected.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem pinpoints exactly which topology makes ``one value on every nonempty open compatible with sheaf gluing.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the universal property, stalk preservation, and the fact that a morphism of sheaves is an isomorphism when it is so on every stalk.  Keep the distinction between a presheaf statement on sections and a local statement on germs explicit.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
A disconnected open gives an immediate counterexample. Conversely, show that the overlap graph of any open cover of a connected open is connected.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
If some nonempty open $U$ is disconnected, write $U=V\sqcup W$ with $V,W$ nonempty open. Choose distinct $a,b\in A$. The sections $a$ on $V$ and $b$ on $W$ are vacuously compatible on the empty overlap but cannot come from one element of $A(U)=A$, so the presheaf is not a sheaf.

Conversely assume every nonempty open is connected. Let $U=\bigcup U_i$ and let $a_i\in A$ be compatible. If the graph whose vertices are nonempty $U_i$ and whose edges indicate nonempty intersection were disconnected, the unions of the two vertex classes would give a separation of $U$. Hence the graph is connected. On every edge compatibility says the two constants agree; therefore all $a_i$ are equal, and that common value gives the unique global section. Thus the sheaf axiom holds.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The failure of the constant presheaf is exactly a gluing failure across disconnected pieces.

\paragraph{Alternative approaches.}
Whenever possible one may replace the espace-etale model by the two-plus construction, or conversely replace a cover/refinement argument by the universal property.  The two approaches agree because both construct the same associated sheaf.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Compare with irreducible spaces, where every two nonempty opens meet.

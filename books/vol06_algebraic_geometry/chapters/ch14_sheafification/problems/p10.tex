\begin{problem}[Equivalence of plus and espace-etale sheafification]
Let $a_{\mathrm{et}}(\mathcal F)$ be the sheaf built from the espace etale of germs and let $a_+(\mathcal F)=\mathcal F^{++}$ be the two-plus construction. Assuming each satisfies the sheafification universal property, construct the canonical isomorphism between them and prove it is natural in $\mathcal F$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Two very different-looking constructions should produce one canonical object.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the universal property, stalk preservation, and the fact that a morphism of sheaves is an isomorphism when it is so on every stalk.  Keep the distinction between a presheaf statement on sections and a local statement on germs explicit.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Use P6 objectwise. For naturality, compare two maps after precomposing with the unit.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
By uniqueness of sheafification there is a unique isomorphism
\[
\alpha_{\mathcal F}:a_{\mathrm{et}}(\mathcal F)\xrightarrow{\sim}a_+(\mathcal F)
\]
carrying the espace-etale unit to the plus unit. For $f:\mathcal F\to\mathcal G$, compare
\[
\alpha_{\mathcal G}\,a_{\mathrm{et}}(f)
\quad\text{and}\quad
a_+(f)\,\alpha_{\mathcal F}.
\]
Both are maps $a_{\mathrm{et}}(\mathcal F)\to a_+(\mathcal G)$. After precomposition with the unit of $\mathcal F$, both equal the unit of $\mathcal G$ composed with $f$. The universal property's uniqueness therefore makes the square commute. Hence $\alpha$ is a natural isomorphism.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Canonical equivalence comes from the mapping property, not from a laborious element-by-element comparison.

\paragraph{Alternative approaches.}
Whenever possible one may replace the espace-etale model by the two-plus construction, or conversely replace a cover/refinement argument by the universal property.  The two approaches agree because both construct the same associated sheaf.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Use this strategy whenever a later construction has two models with the same universal property.

\begin{problem}[Bounded continuous functions sheafify to all continuous functions]
On $X=\mathbb R$, let $\mathcal B(U)$ be the abelian group of bounded continuous functions $U\to\mathbb R$. Prove that $\mathcal B$ is a presheaf, generally not a sheaf, and that
\[
\mathcal B^\#\cong\mathcal C^0_{\mathbb R}.
\]
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is a clean model of sheafification replacing a global constraint by its local content.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the universal property, stalk preservation, and the fact that a morphism of sheaves is an isomorphism when it is so on every stalk.  Keep the distinction between a presheaf statement on sections and a local statement on germs explicit.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Restrictions preserve boundedness.  Use an unbounded continuous function for failure of gluing.  Every continuous function is bounded on a sufficiently small neighborhood of each point.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Restriction of a bounded continuous function is bounded and continuous, so $\mathcal B$ is a presheaf. The function $x\mapsto x$ is not in $\mathcal B(\mathbb R)$, but on the cover $U_n=(-n,n)$ its restrictions are bounded and compatible. Thus $\mathcal B$ fails gluing. For every $x$, a continuous function is bounded on some small interval around $x$, so the germs of $\mathcal B$ and $\mathcal C^0$ coincide. The inclusion $\mathcal B\to\mathcal C^0$ therefore induces an isomorphism on all stalks. By the universal property it factors through $\mathcal B^\#$, and the resulting map $\mathcal B^\#\to\mathcal C^0$ is a stalkwise isomorphism of sheaves, hence an isomorphism.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The sheafification forgets the nonlocal condition ``bounded on all of $U$ while retaining continuity.

\paragraph{Alternative approaches.}
Whenever possible one may replace the espace-etale model by the two-plus construction, or conversely replace a cover/refinement argument by the universal property.  The two approaches agree because both construct the same associated sheaf.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Repeat with locally bounded measurable, smooth, or holomorphic functions where appropriate.

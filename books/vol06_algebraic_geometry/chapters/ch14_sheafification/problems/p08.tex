\begin{problem}[Sheafification does not change the map on germs]
For a morphism of presheaves $f:\mathcal F\to\mathcal G$, prove that under the canonical identifications
$(\mathcal F^\#)_x\cong\mathcal F_x$ and $(\mathcal G^\#)_x\cong\mathcal G_x$ one has
\[
(f^\#)_x=f_x.
\]
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the precise sense in which sheafification changes global organization without changing local germ data.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the universal property, stalk preservation, and the fact that a morphism of sheaves is an isomorphism when it is so on every stalk.  Keep the distinction between a presheaf statement on sections and a local statement on germs explicit.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Take stalks of the naturality square for the units.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
The defining relation for $f^\#$ is
\[
f^\#\eta_{\mathcal F}=\eta_{\mathcal G}f.
\]
Taking the stalk at $x$ gives
\[
(f^\#)_x(\eta_{\mathcal F})_x=(\eta_{\mathcal G})_x f_x.
\]
Both unit maps on stalks are isomorphisms, and the canonical identifications of the sheafified stalks with the original stalks are precisely these maps. After transporting along them, the equation reads $(f^\#)_x=f_x$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Stalk preservation is natural, not merely a pointwise coincidence.

\paragraph{Alternative approaches.}
Whenever possible one may replace the espace-etale model by the two-plus construction, or conversely replace a cover/refinement argument by the universal property.  The two approaches agree because both construct the same associated sheaf.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Deduce that a stalkwise isomorphism of presheaves becomes an isomorphism after sheafification.

\begin{problem}[Explicit sheafification on a finite discrete space]
Let $X$ be a finite discrete space and $\mathcal F$ any presheaf of abelian groups. Prove
\[
\mathcal F^\#(U)\cong\prod_{x\in U}\mathcal F_x
\]
for every $U\subseteq X$, and describe the unit.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Finite discrete spaces turn the abstract germ construction into an exact finite formula.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the universal property, stalk preservation, and the fact that a morphism of sheaves is an isomorphism when it is so on every stalk.  Keep the distinction between a presheaf statement on sections and a local statement on germs explicit.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
The singleton neighborhoods are final in the stalk systems, and a sheaf on a discrete space is a product over singletons.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
For each $x$, the singleton $\{x\}$ is the smallest neighborhood, so $\mathcal F_x\cong\mathcal F(\{x\})$. Any sheaf $\mathcal S$ on a discrete space satisfies
\[
\mathcal S(U)\cong\prod_{x\in U}\mathcal S(\{x\})
\]
by gluing over the disjoint singleton cover. Applying this to $\mathcal F^\#$ and using stalk preservation gives
\[
\mathcal F^\#(U)\cong\prod_{x\in U}(\mathcal F^\#)_x\cong\prod_{x\in U}\mathcal F_x.
\]
The unit sends $s\in\mathcal F(U)$ to its tuple of germs $(s_x)_{x\in U}$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The formula exposes both ways the unit can fail: different sections may have the same germs, and not every tuple of germs need come from a global presheaf section.

\paragraph{Alternative approaches.}
Whenever possible one may replace the espace-etale model by the two-plus construction, or conversely replace a cover/refinement argument by the universal property.  The two approaches agree because both construct the same associated sheaf.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Use the formula to recover P2 and P3-type behavior on finite spaces.

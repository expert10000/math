\begin{problem}[A noninjective sheafification unit]
Let $A\neq 0$ be an abelian group and $X=\mathbb R$. Define a presheaf $\mathcal F$ by
\[
\mathcal F(X)=A,\qquad \mathcal F(U)=0\quad\text{for every proper open }U\subsetneq X,
\]
with the only possible restriction maps. Prove that every stalk $\mathcal F_x$ is zero, identify $\mathcal F^\#$, and show that the unit
$\eta_X:\mathcal F(X)\to\mathcal F^\#(X)$ is not injective.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This example isolates the part of sheafification that discards globally recorded data which is invisible in every sufficiently small neighborhood.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the universal property, stalk preservation, and the fact that a morphism of sheaves is an isomorphism when it is so on every stalk.  Keep the distinction between a presheaf statement on sections and a local statement on germs explicit.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Every point of $\\mathbb R$ has a proper open neighborhood.  Compute the filtered colimit defining the stalk before invoking stalk preservation.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Fix $x\in\mathbb R$. Choose a proper open interval $U$ containing $x$. Every section over $X$ restricts to zero in $\mathcal F(U)$, and every section over a sufficiently small proper neighborhood is already zero. Hence the directed system of neighborhoods is cofinally zero and $\mathcal F_x=0$. Sheafification preserves stalks, so $(\mathcal F^\#)_x=0$ for all $x$. A sheaf with all zero stalks is the zero sheaf, hence $\mathcal F^\#=0$. Therefore
\[
\eta_X:A=\mathcal F(X)\longrightarrow 0
\]
is the zero map and is not injective because $A\ne0$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The unit need not embed the original presheaf.  Its kernel consists precisely of sections that become zero locally everywhere.

\paragraph{Alternative approaches.}
Whenever possible one may replace the espace-etale model by the two-plus construction, or conversely replace a cover/refinement argument by the universal property.  The two approaches agree because both construct the same associated sheaf.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Generalize to any space in which every point has a proper open neighborhood.

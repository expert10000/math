\begin{problem}[Principal-open localization and its stalks]
Let $A$ be a ring. On the basis of principal opens of $\Spec A$, assign
\[
D(f)\longmapsto A_f,
\]
with the usual localization maps on refinements. Show that the germ at $\mathfrak p$ is canonically $A_{\mathfrak p}$. Explain why any sheafification/extension of this basis presheaf must preserve these local rings.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem is the bridge from abstract sheafification to the structure sheaf constructed in VI/17.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the universal property, stalk preservation, and the fact that a morphism of sheaves is an isomorphism when it is so on every stalk.  Keep the distinction between a presheaf statement on sections and a local statement on germs explicit.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Principal opens containing $\mathfrak p$ are indexed by $f\notin\mathfrak p$. Take the filtered colimit of $A_f$.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
The stalk of the basis presheaf at $\mathfrak p$ is
\[
\varinjlim_{f\notin\mathfrak p}A_f.
\]
Every fraction $a/s\in A_{\mathfrak p}$ has $s\notin\mathfrak p$ and therefore comes from $A_s$. Conversely the maps $A_f\to A_{\mathfrak p}$ are compatible, so they induce a map from the colimit. The two constructions are inverse, yielding
\[
\varinjlim_{f\notin\mathfrak p}A_f\cong A_{\mathfrak p}.
\]
Sheafification preserves stalks, so extending/sheafifying the basis data cannot alter this local ring. Consequently the eventual structure sheaf has stalk $A_{\mathfrak p}$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The local rings encountered in VI/11 reappear naturally as stalks of the function sheaf.

\paragraph{Alternative approaches.}
Whenever possible one may replace the espace-etale model by the two-plus construction, or conversely replace a cover/refinement argument by the universal property.  The two approaches agree because both construct the same associated sheaf.

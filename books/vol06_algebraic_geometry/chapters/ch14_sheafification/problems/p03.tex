\begin{problem}[A nonsurjective sheafification unit]
Let $X=\{p,q\}$ with the discrete topology and let $A\neq0$. Let $\mathcal F$ be the naive constant presheaf: $\mathcal F(U)=A$ for nonempty $U$ and $\mathcal F(\varnothing)=0$, with identity restrictions between nonempty opens. Compute $\mathcal F^\#$ and the unit on $X$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Sheafification may add sections even while preserving all stalks.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the universal property, stalk preservation, and the fact that a morphism of sheaves is an isomorphism when it is so on every stalk.  Keep the distinction between a presheaf statement on sections and a local statement on germs explicit.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
A sheaf on a discrete space is the product of its point stalks.  Track what a single constant section does at $p$ and $q$.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Both stalks of $\mathcal F$ are canonically $A$. A sheaf on the two-point discrete space is determined by its stalks, so
\[
\mathcal F^\#(X)\cong A\times A,
\qquad \mathcal F^\#(\{p\})\cong A,
\qquad \mathcal F^\#(\{q\})\cong A.
\]
Equivalently $\mathcal F^\#$ is the sheaf of locally constant $A$-valued functions. The unit sends $a\in A$ to the pair of its two germs,
\[
\eta_X(a)=(a,a).
\]
Thus $\eta_X$ is the diagonal $A\to A^2$, which is not surjective when $A\ne0$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The added sections are independent local choices on distinct connected components.

\paragraph{Alternative approaches.}
Whenever possible one may replace the espace-etale model by the two-plus construction, or conversely replace a cover/refinement argument by the universal property.  The two approaches agree because both construct the same associated sheaf.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Replace two points by an arbitrary discrete set $I$ and obtain $A\to A^I$.

\begin{problem}[Legacy AG1 Exercise 4: construct the associated sheaf]
Let $\mathcal F$ be a presheaf of abelian groups on a topological space $X$.  In the legacy
notation write $\mathcal F^+$ for the associated sheaf obtained from the disjoint union of
stalks.  Complete the construction and prove all of the following.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Form
\[
E_{\mathcal F}=\coprod_{p\in X}\mathcal F_p,
\qquad
\pi:E_{\mathcal F}\to X,
\]
endow $E_{\mathcal F}$ with the topology generated by the sets
$\widetilde s(U)=\{s_p:p\in U\}$, and show that the continuous sections of $\pi$ form a sheaf
$\mathcal F^+$.
\item Construct the natural morphism
\[
\theta:\mathcal F\longrightarrow\mathcal F^+,
\qquad
s\longmapsto\widetilde s,
\]
and prove that $(\mathcal F^+,\theta)$ has the universal property of the associated sheaf.
\item Prove that for every $p\in X$ the induced map on stalks is an isomorphism
\[
(\mathcal F^+)_p\cong\mathcal F_p.
\]
\item Given a morphism of presheaves $f:\mathcal F\to\mathcal G$, construct the induced
morphism
\[
f^+:\mathcal F^+\longrightarrow\mathcal G^+
\]
and prove that, under the canonical stalk identifications,
\[
(f^+)_p=f_p.
\]
\item Let $i:\Sh_X\to\PreSh_X$ be the inclusion.  Prove the natural adjunction
\[
\Hom_{\Sh_X}(\mathcal F^+,\mathcal G)
\cong
\Hom_{\PreSh_X}(\mathcal F,i\mathcal G)
\]
for every sheaf $\mathcal G$; equivalently, sheafification is left adjoint to inclusion.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the main legacy sheafification problem in the Volume VI corpus.  It does not ask for
one isolated fact: it asks for the complete mechanism that turns a presheaf into a sheaf and
then proves that the construction is canonical.  The existence theorem, stalk preservation,
functoriality, and adjunction that appear throughout modern sheaf theory are all contained in
this one problem.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
There are three recurring moves.  First, equality in a stalk means equality after shrinking.
Second, a section of the associated sheaf is locally represented by an original presheaf
section.  Third, once the target is a sheaf, equal germs force equality of local sections and
compatible local sections glue uniquely.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item To prove that $\widetilde s(U)$ form a basis, take a germ lying in the intersection of
two such sets and shrink to a neighborhood on which the two representatives agree.
\item To prove that $\mathcal F^+$ is a sheaf, glue the continuous maps
$U_i\to E_{\mathcal F}$ pointwise; continuity is local on the domain.
\item For stalk preservation, define
\[
\Phi_p:(\mathcal F^+)_p\to\mathcal F_p,
\qquad
\sigma_p\mapsto\sigma(p).
\]
Use local representability for injectivity and a representative $s$ of a given germ for
surjectivity.
\item For the universal property, cover a sheafified section by opens on which it equals
$\widetilde{s_i}$, apply the given morphism to $s_i$, and glue in the target sheaf.
\item Functoriality and adjunction are not new constructions after the universal property;
they are consequences of uniqueness of factorization.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
\textbf{Step 1: topology and the associated sheaf.}
Every $a\in\mathcal F_p$ has a representative $s\in\mathcal F(U)$ with $p\in U$, so the
sets $\widetilde s(U)$ cover $E_{\mathcal F}$.  If
$a=s_p=t_p\in\widetilde s(U)\cap\widetilde t(V)$, equality of germs gives an open
$W\subseteq U\cap V$ containing $p$ with $s|_W=t|_W$.  Hence
\[
a\in\widetilde{s|_W}(W)
\subseteq
\widetilde s(U)\cap\widetilde t(V).
\]
Thus the germ images are a basis.  On each one, $\pi$ is a homeomorphism onto $U$ with
inverse $x\mapsto s_x$.

Define $\mathcal F^+(U)$ to be the continuous sections $\sigma:U\to E_{\mathcal F}$ of
$\pi$.  If $U=\bigcup_iU_i$ and $\sigma_i\in\mathcal F^+(U_i)$ agree on overlaps, define
$\sigma(x)=\sigma_i(x)$ for $x\in U_i$.  This is well defined and is a section of $\pi$.
Since $\sigma|_{U_i}=\sigma_i$ and continuity is local, $\sigma$ is continuous.  Uniqueness
is equality of maps on a cover.  Therefore $\mathcal F^+$ is a sheaf.

\textbf{Step 2: the canonical morphism.}
For $s\in\mathcal F(U)$ define
\[
\theta_U(s)=\widetilde s,
\qquad
\widetilde s(p)=s_p.
\]
If $V\subseteq U$, then $(s|_V)_p=s_p$ for $p\in V$, so
\[
\widetilde{s|_V}=\widetilde s|_V.
\]
Hence the $\theta_U$ form a morphism of presheaves
$\theta:\mathcal F\to\mathcal F^+$.

\textbf{Step 3: stalk preservation.}
Define
\[
\Phi_p:(\mathcal F^+)_p\to\mathcal F_p,
\qquad
\Phi_p(\sigma_p)=\sigma(p).
\]
This is well defined because sections with the same germ agree near $p$ and therefore have
the same value in the fiber over $p$.

For surjectivity, let $a\in\mathcal F_p$ and choose $s\in\mathcal F(U)$ with $a=s_p$.
Then
\[
\Phi_p((\widetilde s)_p)=s_p=a.
\]
For injectivity, suppose $\Phi_p(\sigma_p)=\Phi_p(\tau_p)$.  Since continuous sections of
$\pi$ are locally represented by original sections, after shrinking choose
$s\in\mathcal F(V)$ and $t\in\mathcal F(W)$ with
$\sigma|_V=\widetilde s$ and $\tau|_W=\widetilde t$.  The equality
$\sigma(p)=\tau(p)$ says $s_p=t_p$, hence after further shrinking $s=t$.  Therefore
$\sigma_p=\tau_p$.  Thus $\Phi_p$ is an isomorphism.  Its inverse is exactly the map induced
on stalks by $\theta$.

\textbf{Step 4: universal property.}
Let $\mathcal H$ be a sheaf and let $u:\mathcal F\to\mathcal H$ be a presheaf morphism.
For $\sigma\in\mathcal F^+(U)$ choose an open cover $U=\bigcup_iU_i$ and
$s_i\in\mathcal F(U_i)$ with
\[
\sigma|_{U_i}=\widetilde{s_i}.
\]
Set $h_i=u(s_i)\in\mathcal H(U_i)$.  On $U_i\cap U_j$, the two sheafified sections have the
same germ at every point.  Therefore $s_i$ and $s_j$ have the same germ at every point of
the overlap, so $u(s_i)$ and $u(s_j)$ do too.  Since $\mathcal H$ is a sheaf, equality of all
germs implies equality of sections.  Thus the $h_i$ agree on overlaps and glue uniquely to
$h\in\mathcal H(U)$.  Define
\[
\bar u_U(\sigma)=h.
\]
Passing to a common refinement proves independence of the chosen local presentation.
Restrictions are respected by construction, so $\bar u$ is a sheaf morphism.  For an
original section $s$ one may use the one-set cover $U$, giving
\[
\bar u(\theta(s))=u(s).
\]
Hence $u=\bar u\theta$.

If $v:\mathcal F^+\to\mathcal H$ is another such factorization, every
$\sigma\in\mathcal F^+(U)$ is locally $\widetilde{s_i}$, and there
\[
v(\widetilde{s_i})=u(s_i)=\bar u(\widetilde{s_i}).
\]
The two sections agree on a cover, so $v(\sigma)=\bar u(\sigma)$.  Thus the factorization is
unique.

\textbf{Step 5: functoriality.}
For $f:\mathcal F\to\mathcal G$, the composite
\[
\mathcal F\xrightarrow{f}\mathcal G\xrightarrow{\theta_{\mathcal G}}\mathcal G^+
\]
has sheaf target.  By the universal property there is a unique
$f^+:\mathcal F^+\to\mathcal G^+$ satisfying
\[
f^+\theta_{\mathcal F}=\theta_{\mathcal G}f.
\]
Uniqueness immediately gives
\[
(\id_{\mathcal F})^+=\id_{\mathcal F^+},
\qquad
(gf)^+=g^+f^+.
\]
Taking stalks in the defining square gives
\[
(f^+)_p(\theta_{\mathcal F})_p
=(\theta_{\mathcal G})_pf_p.
\]
The two $\theta$-maps on stalks are the canonical isomorphisms established above, so under
those identifications $(f^+)_p=f_p$.

\textbf{Step 6: adjunction.}
For every sheaf $\mathcal H$, precomposition with $\theta_{\mathcal F}$ defines
\[
\Hom_{\Sh_X}(\mathcal F^+,\mathcal H)
\longrightarrow
\Hom_{\PreSh_X}(\mathcal F,i\mathcal H).
\]
The universal property says exactly that every map on the right has one and only one
preimage.  Therefore this map is a bijection.  Naturality in both variables follows from the
same uniqueness of factorization.  Hence the sheafification functor $a$ is left adjoint to
the inclusion $i$:
\[
a\dashv i.
\]
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The problem constructed a sheaf from nothing but germs and then proved that the construction
is forced by a universal property.  The crucial local invariant is
\[
(\mathcal F^+)_p\cong\mathcal F_p,
\]
while the crucial categorical invariant is the adjunction.  Existence, uniqueness,
functoriality, and preservation of stalks are therefore not separate facts but different
faces of one canonical repair process.

\paragraph{Alternative approaches.}
One may replace the espace \'{e}tal\'{e} construction by the standard plus construction:
first pass from $\mathcal F$ to compatible local families modulo refinement, obtaining a
separated presheaf, and then apply the construction once more to obtain a sheaf.  The
universal property proves that the resulting $\mathcal F^{++}$ is canonically isomorphic to
the sheaf $\mathcal F^+$ constructed above.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Repeat the construction for presheaves of sets, rings, and modules.  Then prove that
sheafification commutes with restriction to an open subset.  Finally, use the adjunction to
predict why quotient and cokernel sheaves in VI/15 are obtained by sheafifying the
corresponding objectwise presheaves.

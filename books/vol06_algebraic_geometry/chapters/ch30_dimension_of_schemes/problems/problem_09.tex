\begin{problem}[VI.30.P9: Normalization invariance]\label{prob:vi30-09}
Let \(X\) be an integral scheme and \(X^\nu\to X\) its normalization. Under the standard affine normalization construction, explain why dimension is unchanged.

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical bridge from VI/28--VI/29.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
First note a useful global lemma: for any scheme \(T\),
\[
\dim T=\sup\{\dim V:V\subseteq T\text{ affine open}\}.
\]
The inequality \(\ge\) is immediate. For the reverse inequality, take a chain
\(Z_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq Z_n\), choose \(z\in Z_0\) and an affine neighborhood \(V\) of \(z\). Each
\(Z_i\cap V\) is nonempty, irreducible, and closed in \(V\), and the inclusions remain strict: otherwise a proper
closed subset \(Z_i\) of the irreducible space \(Z_{i+1}\) would contain a nonempty open subset of \(Z_{i+1}\).

Now cover \(X\) by affine opens \(U=\Spec A\). Their inverse images in the normalization are
\[
\nu^{-1}(U)=\Spec\overline A.
\]
VI/29 gives
\[
\dim\overline A=\dim A
\]
because \(A\subseteq\overline A\) is integral. Applying the affine-cover supremum lemma to the cover of \(X\) and
its inverse-image cover of \(X^\nu\) yields
\[
\boxed{\dim X^\nu=\dim X}.
\]
\end{solution}
\fi

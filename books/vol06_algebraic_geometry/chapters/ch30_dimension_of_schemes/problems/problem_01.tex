\begin{problem}[VI.30.P1: AG8 Problem 2: nonempty opens of a variety]\label{prob:vi30-01}
Let \(X\) be a variety over \(k\) and let \(\varnothing\neq U\subseteq X\) be open. Prove that \(\dim U=\dim X\).

\textbf{Provenance.} Legacy: AG8-P2d, the global/open-dimension portion of Problem 2. The function-field setup is already canonical in VI/27; local dimension and codimension remain for VI/32 and VI/31.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Because \(X\) is finite type over a field, it is Noetherian. Hence every open subset is quasi-compact. The
nonempty open \(U\) is reduced and irreducible because these properties pass to nonempty opens of an integral
scheme. Cover \(U\) by finitely many affine opens of \(X\); their coordinate rings are finitely generated
\(k\)-algebras. Thus \(U\) is itself a variety.

The generic point of \(X\) lies in every nonempty open, so \(U\) and \(X\) have the same function field:
\[
K(U)=K(X).
\]
Applying the dimension theorem to both varieties gives
\[
\dim U
=
\operatorname{trdeg}_kK(U)
=
\operatorname{trdeg}_kK(X)
=
\dim X.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\dim U=\dim X}.
\]

This is exactly where finite type matters: for \(X=\Spec k[x,y]_{(x)}\), the nonempty dense open \(D(x)\) has
dimension \(0\) while \(X\) has dimension \(1\).
\end{solution}
\fi

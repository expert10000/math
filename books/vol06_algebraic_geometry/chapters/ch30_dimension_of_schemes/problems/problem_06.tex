\begin{problem}[VI.30.P6: Finite disjoint unions]\label{prob:vi30-06}
Prove \(\dim(X\sqcup Y)=\max\{\dim X,\dim Y\}\).

\textbf{Provenance.} Canonical component computation.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Both \(X\) and \(Y\) are open and closed in \(X\sqcup Y\). An irreducible subset cannot meet both components:
if it did, its intersections with the two open-and-closed components would decompose it into two nonempty proper
closed subsets. Hence every irreducible closed subset, and therefore every irreducible-closed chain, lies wholly in
one component.

Thus every chain has length at most \(\dim X\) or at most \(\dim Y\), giving
\[
\dim(X\sqcup Y)\le\max\{\dim X,\dim Y\}.
\]
Conversely chains in either component are chains in the union. Hence
\[
\boxed{\dim(X\sqcup Y)=\max\{\dim X,\dim Y\}}.
\]
\end{solution}
\fi

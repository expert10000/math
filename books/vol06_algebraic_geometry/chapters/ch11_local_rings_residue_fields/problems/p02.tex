\begin{problem}[Prime localization is the canonical local ring]
Let $A$ be a ring and $\mathfrak p\in\Spec A$.  Prove that $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is local, determine its unique maximal ideal, and characterize all of its units.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This construction is the algebraic heart of local geometry.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
A fraction $a/s$ has denominator outside $\mathfrak p$.  Ask when $a$ can also be used as a denominator.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Show $a/s$ is invertible iff $a\notin\mathfrak p$.

\begin{solution}
If $a\notin\mathfrak p$, then $s/a$ is an inverse of $a/s$.  If $a\in\mathfrak p$ and $a/s$ had inverse $b/t$, equality to $1$ would imply after clearing denominators that an element outside $\mathfrak p$ belongs to $(a)\subseteq\mathfrak p$, impossible.  Hence the nonunits are precisely the fractions with numerator in $\mathfrak p$, namely $\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}$.  Since the nonunits form a proper ideal, the local-ring criterion gives uniqueness of the maximal ideal.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Localization converts a prime ideal into the unique closed point of a local spectrum.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Repeat for localization at a maximal ideal and compare with the quotient $A/\mathfrak m$.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical synthesis problem based on the local-ring and residue-field corpus.

\begin{problem}[The local spectrum as generalizations]
Prove that the canonical map $\Spec A_{\mathfrak p}\to\Spec A$ identifies its source homeomorphically with the subspace
\[
\{\mathfrak q\in\Spec A:\mathfrak q\subseteq\mathfrak p\}.
\]
Interpret this set in specialization language.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
It explains geometrically what the local ring sees: exactly the points more generic than the chosen point.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the prime correspondence for localization at $A\setminus\mathfrak p$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
A prime is disjoint from $A\setminus\mathfrak p$ exactly when it is contained in $\mathfrak p$.

\begin{solution}
Prime ideals of $A_{\mathfrak p}$ correspond bijectively to primes $\mathfrak q$ of $A$ disjoint from $A\setminus\mathfrak p$, i.e. $\mathfrak q\subseteq\mathfrak p$.  Extension and contraction preserve inclusions and the standard localization correspondence preserves closed sets in the induced topology, giving a homeomorphism onto that subspace.  Since $\mathfrak q\subseteq\mathfrak p$ means $\mathfrak p$ is a specialization of $\mathfrak q$, these are precisely the generalizations of $\mathfrak p$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A local spectrum is organized around one closed point and all ways of moving generically away from it.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Draw the local spectra at $(x,y)$ and $(x)$ inside $\Spec k[x,y]$.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical synthesis problem based on the local-ring and residue-field corpus.

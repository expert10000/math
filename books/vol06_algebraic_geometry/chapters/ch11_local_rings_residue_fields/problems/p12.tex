\begin{problem}[When does an element vanish locally?]
Prove that for $a\in A$ and $\mathfrak p\in\Spec A$,
\[
a/1=0\text{ in }A_{\mathfrak p}
\iff
\exists s\notin\mathfrak p\text{ with }sa=0.
\]
Apply this to $A=k[x,y]/(xy)$ and the prime $(y)$ to show that $y$ vanishes in $A_{(y)}$ although $y\neq0$ in $A$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
It gives the precise algebraic meaning of a function being zero near a point without being zero globally.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the equality criterion for fractions in a localization.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
For the application, take $s=x$.

\begin{solution}
In $S^{-1}A$ with $S=A\setminus\mathfrak p$, equality $a/1=0/1$ holds iff some $s\in S$ satisfies $s(a\cdot1-0\cdot1)=sa=0$.  In $k[x,y]/(xy)$ at $(y)$, $x\notin(y)$ and $xy=0$, so $y/1=0$ locally.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Localization ignores phenomena supported entirely away from the chosen prime.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Describe the set of primes at which a fixed element $a$ becomes zero in the local ring.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical synthesis problem based on the local-ring and residue-field corpus.

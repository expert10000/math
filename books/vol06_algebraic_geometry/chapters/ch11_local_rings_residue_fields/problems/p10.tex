\begin{problem}[Local maps and residue-field extensions]
Let $\varphi:A\to B$, let $\mathfrak q\in\Spec B$, and put $\mathfrak p=\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)$.  Construct
\[
A_{\mathfrak p}\to B_{\mathfrak q},
\]
prove it is local, and derive the induced injective homomorphism
\[
\kappa(\mathfrak p)\hookrightarrow\kappa(\mathfrak q).
\]
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the local algebra carried by a morphism at a point and is fundamental for later fibers.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Elements outside $\mathfrak p$ map outside $\mathfrak q$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
After localizing, compare inverse images of the maximal ideals and then quotient.

\begin{solution}
For $a\notin\mathfrak p$, $\varphi(a)\notin\mathfrak q$, so $\varphi(a)$ is a unit in $B_{\mathfrak q}$.  The universal property gives $A_{\mathfrak p}\to B_{\mathfrak q}$.  A fraction $a/s$ maps into $\mathfrak qB_{\mathfrak q}$ iff $\varphi(a)\in\mathfrak q$, iff $a\in\mathfrak p$, so the inverse image of the maximal ideal is $\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}$.  Therefore the map is local and descends to a field homomorphism of residues.  It is injective because it is unital.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A point lying over another point comes with an extension of residue fields.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Compute the residue-field map for $k\to K$ and for evaluation $k[t]\to k$.

\paragraph{Provenance.}

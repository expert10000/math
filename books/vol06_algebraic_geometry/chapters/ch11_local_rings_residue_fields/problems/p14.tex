\begin{problem}[The cusp and its parametrizing line]
Let $A=k[t^2,t^3]$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m=(t^2,t^3)$.  Compare the local ring $A_{\mathfrak m}$ with $k[t]_{(t)}$.  Show they have the same residue field $k$, but $t\notin A_{\mathfrak m}$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This advanced local example previews normalization and shows that equal residue fields do not imply equal local rings.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Suppose $t=f(t^2,t^3)/g(t^2,t^3)$ with $g(0)\neq0$ and compare orders of vanishing.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Elements of $k[t^2,t^3]$ have no term of degree $1$.

\begin{solution}
Both local rings have residue field $k$ by evaluation at $t=0$.  There is an inclusion $A_{\mathfrak m}\hookrightarrow k[t]_{(t)}$.  If $t=f/g$ with $f,g\in k[t^2,t^3]$ and $g(0)\neq0$, then $tg=f$.  The left side has a nonzero term of degree $1$ because $g(0)\neq0$, whereas every element of $k[t^2,t^3]$ has zero coefficient of $t^1$.  Contradiction.  Hence the inclusion is strict.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The local ring detects a singular algebraic defect even when the point and residue field look ordinary.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Investigate which powers $t^n$ lie in $k[t^2,t^3]$ and relate this to the semigroup generated by $2$ and $3$.

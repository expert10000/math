\begin{problem}[Localizing a product ring]
Let $A=A_1\times A_2$ with both factors nonzero.  Classify the prime ideals of $A$ and show that localizing at a prime coming from $A_1$ kills the $A_2$ factor.  Deduce the result for $k\times k$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
It shows how localization isolates one connected component of a disconnected affine scheme.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the idempotents $e_1=(1,0)$ and $e_2=(0,1)$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Every prime contains exactly one of $e_1,e_2$.

\begin{solution}
Every prime of $A_1\times A_2$ is either $\mathfrak p_1\times A_2$ or $A_1\times\mathfrak p_2$.  At $\mathfrak p_1\times A_2$, the idempotent $e_1=(1,0)$ is outside the prime and becomes a unit.  Since $e_1e_2=0$, this forces $e_2=0$ in the localization, killing the second factor.  The localization is naturally $(A_1)_{\mathfrak p_1}$.  For $k\times k$, each localization is $k$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Global disconnectedness can disappear completely at a chosen local point.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Generalize to a finite product of rings.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical synthesis problem based on the local-ring and residue-field corpus.

\begin{problem}[Closed points over a finite field]
Let $f\in\FF_q[t]$ be monic irreducible of degree $d$.  Compute the local ring and residue field at $(f)$, show the residue field has $q^d$ elements, and explain why degree-$d$ irreducibles represent closed points that are not $\FF_q$-rational when $d>1$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
It makes residue degree concrete and connects local algebra to arithmetic geometry.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
$(f)$ is maximal because $\FF_q[t]$ is a PID and $f$ is irreducible.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Use the classification of finite fields.

\begin{solution}
The local ring is $\FF_q[t]_{(f)}$, with maximal ideal generated by $f$.  Since $(f)$ is maximal,
\[
\kappa((f))=\FF_q[t]/(f).
\]
This quotient has $q^d$ elements and is therefore isomorphic to $\FF_{q^d}$.  For $d=1$ the point is $\FF_q$-rational; for $d>1$ its residue field is a proper finite extension, so it is closed but not rational over the base field.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Closed points remember extension fields, not merely coordinate values in the base field.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Count degree-$d$ closed points of $\mathbb A^1_{\FF_q}$ for small $d$.

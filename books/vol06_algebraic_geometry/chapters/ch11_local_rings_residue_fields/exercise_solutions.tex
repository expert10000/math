\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E01.}
Assume every $a$ has $a$ or $1-a$ invertible.  If $\mathfrak m\neq\mathfrak n$ were maximal, then $\mathfrak m+\mathfrak n=R$, so choose $a\in\mathfrak m$, $b\in\mathfrak n$ with $a+b=1$.  Both $a$ and $1-a=b$ are nonunits, contradiction.  Hence the maximal ideal is unique.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E02.}
Every element is $f/g$ with $g(a)\neq0$.  It is a unit iff $f(a)\neq0$, with inverse $g/f$.  Therefore the nonunits are precisely the fractions with numerator divisible by $t-a$, namely $(t-a)k[t]_{(t-a)}$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E03.}
If $a\notin\mathfrak p$, then $s/a$ is an inverse of $a/s$.  Conversely, if $a/s$ is invertible and $a\in\mathfrak p$, clearing denominators from $(a/s)(b/t)=1$ forces an element outside $\mathfrak p$ to lie in $\mathfrak p$, contradiction.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E04.}
For $S=A\setminus\mathfrak p$, primes of $S^{-1}A$ correspond to primes $\mathfrak q$ of $A$ disjoint from $S$.  This disjointness is equivalent to $\mathfrak q\subseteq\mathfrak p$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E05.}
We have $A_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}\cong S^{-1}(A/\mathfrak p)$ for $S=A\setminus\mathfrak p$.  Since $A/\mathfrak p$ is a domain and the image of $S$ is exactly its nonzero elements, the localization is $\Frac(A/\mathfrak p)$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E06.}
At $(0)$, $k[t]_{(0)}=k(t)$ and the residue field is $k(t)$.  At $(t-a)$, the local ring consists of $f/g$ with $g(a)\neq0$, maximal ideal $(t-a)$ locally, and residue field $k$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E07.}
The local ring is $\RR[t]_{(t^2+1)}$.  Its maximal ideal is generated locally by $t^2+1$.  Since the prime is maximal, the residue field is $\RR[t]/(t^2+1)\cong\CC$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E08.}
$\ZZ_{(p)}$ consists of fractions $a/b$ with $p\nmid b$.  Such a fraction is a unit iff $p\nmid a$.  The unique maximal ideal is $p\ZZ_{(p)}$, and quotienting gives $\FF_p$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E09.}
Because $f$ is irreducible, $(f)$ is maximal and $\kappa((f))=\FF_q[t]/(f)$.  This field has $q^{\deg f}=q^d$ elements, so it is isomorphic to $\FF_{q^d}$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E10.}
The quotient $k[x,y]/(y-x^2)$ is $k[x]$, so the residue field is $\Frac(k[x])=k(x)$.  The local ring itself is $k[x,y]_{(y-x^2)}$; it is not a field.  Its unique maximal ideal is generated locally by $y-x^2$, and quotienting that local ring by its maximal ideal gives $k(x)$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E11.}
In $A_{(x,y)}$, neither $x$ nor $y$ becomes invertible because both lie in the maximal ideal.  They remain nonzero, while $xy=0$, so the local ring has zero divisors.  Quotient by $(x,y)$ gives $k$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E12.}
At $(x-a,y)$ with $a\neq0$, $x$ is outside the maximal ideal and hence is a unit.  The relation $xy=0$ gives $y=0$.  The remaining ring is $k[x]_{(x-a)}$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E13.}
At $(x)$, $y$ is inverted, so $x=0$ and the localization becomes $k[y]_{(0)}=k(y)$.  Similarly $A_{(y)}\cong k(x)$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E14.}
Every element is $a+b\varepsilon$.  It is a unit iff $a\neq0$, since $(a+b\varepsilon)^{-1}=a^{-1}-a^{-2}b\varepsilon$.  Thus the unique maximal ideal is $(\varepsilon)$ and the residue field is $k$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E15.}
$\ZZ/n\ZZ$ is local exactly when $n$ is a power of a single prime.  Hence $\ZZ/4\ZZ$ and $\ZZ/8\ZZ$ are local with maximal ideal generated by $2$ and residue field $\FF_2$; $\ZZ/6\ZZ$ has maximal ideals $(2)/(6)$ and $(3)/(6)$ and is not local.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E16.}
Because $f\notin\mathfrak p$, $f$ is already a unit in $A_{\mathfrak p}$.  Therefore $A\to A_{\mathfrak p}$ factors through $A_f$, and localizing $A_f$ at $\mathfrak pA_f$ inverts precisely all elements outside $\mathfrak p$.  The resulting maps are inverse isomorphisms.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E17.}
Let $S=A\setminus\mathfrak p$.  Then $(A/I)_{\mathfrak p/I}=S^{-1}(A/I)\cong S^{-1}A/S^{-1}I=A_{\mathfrak p}/IA_{\mathfrak p}$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E18.}
The maximal ideal of $A_{\mathfrak p}/IA_{\mathfrak p}$ is $\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}/IA_{\mathfrak p}$.  Quotienting by it gives $A_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}=\kappa_A(\mathfrak p)$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E19.}
By definition, $a/1=0/1$ in $S^{-1}A$ iff there exists $s\in S$ such that $s(a\cdot1-0\cdot1)=sa=0$.  Here $S=A\setminus\mathfrak p$.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E20.}
If $a\notin\mathfrak p$, then $\varphi(a)\notin\mathfrak q$.  Thus denominators used in $A_{\mathfrak p}$ map to units in $B_{\mathfrak q}$, giving the localized map.  Its inverse image of $\mathfrak qB_{\mathfrak q}$ is exactly $\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}$, so it is local.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E21.}
A local homomorphism maps $\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}$ into $\mathfrak qB_{\mathfrak q}$ and hence descends to $\kappa(\mathfrak p)\to\kappa(\mathfrak q)$.  The map is unital between fields, hence injective.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E22.}
The primes are $0\times k$ and $k\times0$.  At $0\times k$, the idempotent $(1,0)$ is outside the prime and becomes a unit, while $(0,1)$ is annihilated; the localization is $k$.  The other prime is symmetric.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E23.}
The quotient is $\ZZ[x]/(2,x-1)\cong\FF_2$, so the residue field is $\FF_2$.  In the local ring, a polynomial/integer expression is a unit exactly when its image mod $(2,x-1)$ is nonzero, equivalently when it does not lie in the maximal ideal.

\paragraph{Solution to VI.11.E24.}
For primes $\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak q$, every element outside $\mathfrak q$ is outside $\mathfrak p$, so the map $A_{\mathfrak q}\to A_{\mathfrak p}$ exists.  Applying this to $(0)\subset(x)\subset(x,y)$ gives $A_{(x,y)}\to A_{(x)}\to A_{(0)}$.  Each step toward a smaller prime inverts more elements.

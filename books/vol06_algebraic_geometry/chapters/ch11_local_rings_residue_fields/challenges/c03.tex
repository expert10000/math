\begin{problem}[Challenge: residue fields on an arithmetic curve]
Let $A=\ZZ[x]/(x^2+1)$.  Analyze the residue fields of closed points lying over an odd prime $p$.  Distinguish the cases in which $x^2+1$ splits or remains irreducible modulo $p$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
It combines residue fields, factorization modulo primes, and arithmetic fibers without yet requiring full fiber-product machinery.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Closed points over $p$ correspond to maximal ideals containing $p$ and an irreducible factor of $x^2+1$ modulo $p$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Recall that $-1$ is a quadratic residue mod $p$ exactly when $p\equiv1\pmod4$.

\begin{solution}
Modulo $p$, the ring becomes $\FF_p[x]/(x^2+1)$.  If $p\equiv1\pmod4$, then $x^2+1=(x-a)(x+a)$ with $a\in\FF_p$, giving two closed points with residue field $\FF_p$.  If $p\equiv3\pmod4$, the polynomial is irreducible, giving one closed point with residue field $\FF_{p^2}$.  The case $p=2$ is ramified/nonreduced and is excluded by hypothesis.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Residue fields record arithmetic splitting behavior of primes.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Analyze the exceptional prime $2$ and explain the appearance of nonreduced structure.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical synthesis problem based on the local-ring and residue-field corpus.

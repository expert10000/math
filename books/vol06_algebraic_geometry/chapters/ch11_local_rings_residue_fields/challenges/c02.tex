\begin{problem}[Challenge: minimal-prime localizations of a reduced ring]
Assume $A$ is reduced with finitely many minimal primes $\mathfrak p_1,\dots,\mathfrak p_r$.  Show each $A_{\mathfrak p_i}$ is a field.  Investigate the canonical map
\[
A\longrightarrow\prod_{i=1}^r A_{\mathfrak p_i}
\]
and prove it is injective.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This connects component generic points with a ring's total quotient behavior.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
In a reduced ring the intersection of minimal primes is zero.  Show the maximal ideal of $A_{\mathfrak p_i}$ is zero.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
An element of $\mathfrak p_i$ is killed after localization by an element outside $\mathfrak p_i$; use minimality or the zero-divisor structure carefully.

\begin{solution}
For a minimal prime $\mathfrak p$, primes of $A_{\mathfrak p}$ correspond to primes contained in $\mathfrak p$, so the only prime is $\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}$.  Localization preserves reducedness, hence the nilradical of $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is zero.  Since the nilradical is the intersection of all primes and there is only one prime, $\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}=0$.  Thus $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is a field.  Moreover the kernel of $A\to A_{\mathfrak p}$ is exactly $\mathfrak p$: it contains $\mathfrak p$ because $\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}=0$, while an element outside $\mathfrak p$ becomes a unit and cannot map to zero.  Therefore the kernel of the product map $A\to\prod_i A_{\mathfrak p_i}$ is $\bigcap_i\mathfrak p_i=\Nil(A)=0$, because $A$ is reduced.  Hence the product map is injective.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A reduced ring embeds into the product of the fields carried by the generic points of its components.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Relate this to the total quotient ring when the set of zero divisors is the union of minimal primes.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical synthesis problem based on the local-ring and residue-field corpus.

% VI42-DETAILED-CHALLENGE-01
\subsection*{Challenge VI.42.1}
\begin{solution}
Choose a nonzero rational section \(s\) of an invertible sheaf \(\mathcal L\), write \(s=f_i e_i\) in local frames, and use \(f_i/f_j\in\mathcal O^\times\) to define a Cartier divisor \(D_s\).  The transition functions show \(\mathcal L\simeq\mathcal O(D_s)\), proving surjectivity of \(\mathrm{CaCl}(X)\to\mathrm{Pic}(X)\).  If \(\mathcal O(D)\) is trivial, choose a global nowhere-vanishing frame and compare it with the canonical rational frame; the quotient is one rational function \(f\), giving \(D=\operatorname{div}(f)\), so the kernel is principal.  Finally, replacing \(s\) by \(fs\) changes \(D_s\) by \(\operatorname{div}(f)\).  This proves the isomorphism without appealing to cohomology.
\end{solution}

% VI42-DETAILED-CHALLENGE-02
\subsection*{Challenge VI.42.2}
\begin{solution}
Fix a cover and trivial frames.  A line bundle gives units \(g_{ij}\) satisfying \(g_{ij}g_{jk}=g_{ik}\).  A change of frames \(e_i'=u_i e_i\) changes the cocycle by \(g'_{ij}=u_i g_{ij}u_j^{-1}\).  Conversely a cocycle glues trivial rank-one modules, and two cocycles differing by such a coboundary yield isomorphic glued bundles.  Tensor product multiplies cocycles termwise.  Hence the group of line bundles trivialized by the cover is the multiplicative cocycle quotient, the concrete precursor of \(H^1(X,\mathcal O_X^\times)\).
\end{solution}

% VI42-DETAILED-CHALLENGE-03
\subsection*{Challenge VI.42.3}
\begin{solution}
For the cone \(X\), VI/41 gives \(\mathrm{Cl}(X)=\mathbb Z/2[L]\) using normality and codimension-one valuations.  VI/40 shows \(L\) is not Cartier at the vertex; thus the nonzero Weil class is not in the Picard image.  The remaining Picard classes on the affine cone are trivial, giving \(\mathrm{Pic}(X)=0\).  Remove the vertex: codimension-two invariance uses normal Weil divisor theory to keep \(\mathrm{Cl}(U)=\mathbb Z/2\), while regularity of \(U\) gives local factoriality and hence \(\mathrm{Pic}(U)\simeq\mathrm{Cl}(U)\).  This isolates the exact role of each hypothesis.
\end{solution}

% VI42-DETAILED-CHALLENGE-04
\subsection*{Challenge VI.42.4}
\begin{solution}
Method one: \(\mathbb P^n\) is regular, so \(\mathrm{Pic}\simeq\mathrm{Cl}\), and VI/41 gives \(\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb P^n)=\mathbb Z[H]\); the corresponding line bundle is \(\mathcal O(1)\).  Method two: on the standard cover \(U_i\), the twisting sheaf \(\mathcal O(d)\) has transition functions \((x_j/x_i)^d\).  One proves any line bundle can be normalized to one such exponent by comparing transition units and their orders along hyperplanes; the integer is the degree.  Both methods identify the same generator \(\mathcal O(1)\).
\end{solution}

% VI42-DETAILED-CHALLENGE-05
\subsection*{Challenge VI.42.5}
\begin{solution}
The map \(P\mapsto[\mathcal O_E(P-O)]\) always lands in degree zero.  Divisor theory already shows that for a line section \(P+Q+R\sim H\), and if \(O\) is a flex then \(H\sim3O\), yielding \([P-O]+[Q-O]+[R-O]=0\).  To obtain a genuine group law on points, VI/43 still needs two geometric facts: degree-one rigidity for injectivity of the Abel--Picard map, and a reduction argument proving every degree-zero class is \(P-O\).  Once those are supplied, Picard addition becomes the chord-and-tangent law.
\end{solution}

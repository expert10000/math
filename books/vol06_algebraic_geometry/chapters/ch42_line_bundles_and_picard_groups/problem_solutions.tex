% VI42-DETAILED-PROBLEM-01
\subsection*{Problem VI.42.1}
\begin{solution}
Glue \(\mathcal O_U\) and \(\mathcal O_V\) by frames \(e_U,e_V\) with \(e_U=ge_V\) on \(U\cap V\).  The cocycle condition is automatic for a two-set cover, so this produces an invertible sheaf.  It is trivial exactly when one can choose units \(u\in\mathcal O^\times(U)\), \(v\in\mathcal O^\times(V)\) so that, after replacing frames by \(u e_U\) and \(v e_V\), the transition becomes \(1\).  Equivalently \(u g v^{-1}=1\), so \(g=u^{-1}v\).  Thus triviality is precisely the coboundary condition.
\end{solution}

% VI42-DETAILED-PROBLEM-02
\subsection*{Problem VI.42.2}
\begin{solution}
Take \(H=V_+(x_0)\).  On \(U_0\) the divisor equation may be chosen as \(1\); on \(U_1\) it is \(x_0/x_1\).  Hence frames of \(\mathcal O(H)\) can be taken as \(1\) and \(x_1/x_0\).  On the overlap their ratio is \(x_0/x_1\) (or its inverse, depending on frame direction).  This is the standard transition function of \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)\).  Its exponent \(d\) similarly gives \(\mathcal O(d)\).
\end{solution}

% VI42-DETAILED-PROBLEM-03
\subsection*{Problem VI.42.3}
\begin{solution}
If \(e_i=g_{ij}e_j\), then \(e_i^{\otimes m}=g_{ij}^m e_j^{\otimes m}\) for \(m>0\).  For \(m=0\) the bundle is \(\mathcal O_X\), with transition \(1\).  For \(m<0\), define \(\mathcal L^{\otimes m}=(\mathcal L^\vee)^{\otimes(-m)}\); dualization replaces \(g_{ij}\) by \(g_{ij}^{-1}\), so the same formula \(g_{ij}^m\) holds for all integers.
\end{solution}

% VI42-DETAILED-PROBLEM-04
\subsection*{Problem VI.42.4}
\begin{solution}
Choose a nonzero vector \(s\in\mathcal L_\eta\) at the generic point.  On each trivializing \(U_i\) with frame \(e_i\), write \(s=f_i e_i\) with \(f_i\in K(X)^\times\).  If \(e_i=g_{ij}e_j\), equality of the same rational section gives \(f_j=f_i g_{ij}\), hence \(f_i/f_j\in\mathcal O^\times(U_{ij})\).  The \(f_i\) therefore define a Cartier divisor \(D\).  The transition functions of \(\mathcal O(D)\) are \(f_j/f_i=g_{ij}\), proving \(\mathcal L\simeq\mathcal O(D)\).
\end{solution}

% VI42-DETAILED-PROBLEM-05
\subsection*{Problem VI.42.5}
\begin{solution}
A line bundle does not select a rational trivialization.  If \(s\) and \(s'\) are two nonzero rational sections, then \(s'=fs\) for a unique \(f\in K(X)^\times\).  Their local equations differ by multiplication by \(f\), so
\[
\operatorname{div}(s')=\operatorname{div}(s)+\operatorname{div}(f).
\]
Thus the line bundle determines the Cartier divisor only modulo principal divisors, exactly the quotient \(\mathrm{CaCl}(X)\).
\end{solution}

% VI42-DETAILED-PROBLEM-06
\subsection*{Problem VI.42.6}
\begin{solution}
On the affine cone \(X\), the generator \(L\) of \(\mathrm{Cl}(X)\cong\mathbb Z/2\) is not Cartier at the vertex; hence it cannot arise from a line bundle on all of \(X\).  In fact \(\mathrm{Pic}(X)=0\).  On \(U=X\setminus\{o\}\), the singular point has been removed, \(U\) is regular and therefore locally factorial, and the same Weil class survives because codimension-two deletion does not change \(\mathrm{Cl}\).  Hence
\[
\mathrm{Pic}(U)\cong\mathrm{Cl}(U)\cong\mathbb Z/2.
\]
The change is purely a Cartier/local-freeness phenomenon.
\end{solution}

% VI42-DETAILED-PROBLEM-07
\subsection*{Problem VI.42.7}
\begin{solution}
Let \(H=V_+(x_0)\).  Since \(\mathcal O(d)\simeq\mathcal O(dH)\) and \(D_+(x_0)\cap H=\varnothing\), the divisor \(dH\) restricts to zero on \(D_+(x_0)\).  Therefore \(\mathcal O(d)\) restricts to the trivial bundle.  Explicitly \(x_0^d\) gives a nowhere-vanishing frame on this chart, with negative \(d\) handled by the dual frame.
\end{solution}

% VI42-DETAILED-PROBLEM-08
\subsection*{Problem VI.42.8}
\begin{solution}
Write \(s_i=f_i e\) in a local frame \(e\).  Since the two sections generate \(\mathcal L\), \(f_0,f_1\) never vanish simultaneously, so
\[
x\mapsto[f_0(x):f_1(x)]
\]
defines a morphism \(X\to\mathbb P^1\), independent of the frame.  The fiber over \([0:1]\) is the zero scheme of \(s_0\), and the fiber over \([1:0]\) is the zero scheme of \(s_1\).  When the sections are generically nonzero these are effective Cartier divisors with their natural multiplicities.
\end{solution}

% VI42-DETAILED-PROBLEM-09
\subsection*{Problem VI.42.9}
\begin{solution}
For a Dedekind domain every nonzero fractional ideal is an invertible rank-one \(A\)-module, and every rank-one projective module embeds into \(K=\operatorname{Frac}(A)\) as a fractional ideal after choosing a rational generator.  Tensor product corresponds to multiplication of fractional ideals.  Principal fractional ideals \(fA\) are free and hence give the trivial line bundle.  Consequently
\[
\mathrm{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}A)
\cong\{\text{fractional ideals}\}/\{\text{principal fractional ideals}\}.
\]
\end{solution}

% VI42-DETAILED-PROBLEM-10
\subsection*{Problem VI.42.10}
\begin{solution}
A linear form \(\ell\) is a global section of \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^n}(1)\) with zero divisor \(H\).  Pulling back gives a section \(f^*\ell\) of \(\mathcal L=f^*\mathcal O(1)\).  Provided this section is not identically zero on any relevant component, its zero divisor is precisely the Cartier pullback \(f^*H\).  Therefore
\[
\mathcal O_X(f^*H)\simeq f^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^n}(1)=\mathcal L.
\]
This is the line-bundle formulation of pulling back a hyperplane equation.
\end{solution}

% VI42-DETAILED-PROBLEM-11
\subsection*{Problem VI.42.11}
\begin{solution}
A hyperplane divisor on \(\mathbb P^1\) is a single degree-one point.  Since \(\mathcal O(d)\simeq\mathcal O(dH)\),
\[
\deg\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(d)=\deg(dH)=d.
\]
Thus the degree map identifies \(\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb P^1)\) with \(\mathbb Z\), and tensor product corresponds to addition of degrees.
\end{solution}

% VI42-DETAILED-PROBLEM-12
\subsection*{Problem VI.42.12}
\begin{solution}
For a degree-one point \(P\) and the chosen degree-one point \(O\), the divisor \(P-O\) has degree zero.  Hence
\[
\mathcal L_P=\mathcal O_E(P-O)
\]
lies in \(\ker(\deg)=\mathrm{Pic}^0(E)\).  The assignment depends on \(O\), and VI/43 supplies the geometric input—line sections and degree-one rigidity—showing that \(P\mapsto[\mathcal L_P]\) identifies the cubic group law with addition in \(\mathrm{Pic}^0(E)\).
\end{solution}

VI/40 attached an invertible sheaf \(\OO_X(D)\) to every Cartier divisor, and VI/41 passed from Weil divisors to the divisor class group
\[
  \Cl(X)=\Div(X)/\Prin(X).
\]
The present chapter develops the intrinsic object behind the Cartier construction: a line bundle, equivalently an invertible \(\OO_X\)-module.  Tensor product turns isomorphism classes of line bundles into the Picard group
\[
  \Pic(X).
\]
On an integral scheme, Cartier divisors modulo principal Cartier divisors recover this group:
\[
  \CaCl(X)\cong\Pic(X).
\]
On a normal Noetherian integral scheme this embeds naturally into \(\Cl(X)\), and on a locally factorial scheme the two groups agree.

The distinction is visible immediately on the quadric cone
\[
  X=\Spec k[x,y,z]/(xy-z^2).
\]
VI/41 computed
\[
  \Cl(X)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z
\]
and showed that the nonzero class is represented by the non-Cartier ruling \(L=V(x,z)\).  Consequently
\[
  \Pic(X)=0.
\]
After deleting the singular vertex, however, the punctured cone \(U\) is regular and locally factorial, so
\[
  \Pic(U)\cong\Cl(U)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]
Thus a codimension-two deletion can leave the Weil class group unchanged while changing the Picard group.

By the end of the chapter, the reader should be able to:

\item define line bundles and invertible \(\OO_X\)-modules and pass freely between the two viewpoints;
\item describe line bundles by transition functions \(g_{ij}\in\OO_X^\times(U_i\cap U_j)\);
\item recognize the cocycle and coboundary relations arising from changes of local frame;
\item construct tensor products and duals of line bundles;
\item define the Picard group and identify its identity and inverse;
\item pull back line bundles functorially along scheme morphisms;
\item construct the homomorphism \(D\mapsto\OO_X(D)\) from Cartier divisors to line bundles;
\item prove \(\CaCl(X)\cong\Pic(X)\) for integral schemes;

\item define degree and \(\Pic^0\) on smooth projective curves as preparation for plane cubics.
\end{itemize}

\section{Line bundles and invertible sheaves}

\begin{definition}[Invertible sheaf]\label{def:vi42-invertible-sheaf}
An \(\OO_X\)-module \(\mathcal L\) is \emph{invertible} if every point \(x\in X\) has an open neighborhood \(U\) such that
\[
  \mathcal L|_U\cong\OO_U.
\]
Equivalently, \(\mathcal L\) is locally free of rank one.
\end{definition}

A geometric line bundle is a morphism \(p:L\to X\) whose fibers are one-dimensional vector spaces and which is locally isomorphic to \(U\times\mathbb A^1\to U\).  Its sheaf of regular sections is invertible.  Conversely an invertible sheaf determines a line bundle by relative spectrum:
\[
  \mathbf V(\mathcal L^\vee)
  :=
  \underline{\Spec}_X\!\bigl(\operatorname{Sym}_{\OO_X}\mathcal L^\vee\bigr).
\]
For the purposes of divisor theory, the invertible-sheaf language is usually more efficient.

\begin{definition}[Local frame]\label{def:vi42-local-frame}
A \emph{local frame} for an invertible sheaf \(\mathcal L\) on \(U\subseteq X\) is a section
\[
  e\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal L)
\]
such that multiplication by \(e\) defines an isomorphism
\[
  \OO_U\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal L|_U.
\]
\end{definition}

If \(e_i\) and \(e_j\) are frames on \(U_i\) and \(U_j\), then on the overlap there is a unique unit
\[
  g_{ij}\in\OO_X^\times(U_i\cap U_j)
\]
such that
\[
  e_i=g_{ij}e_j.
\]

\section{Transition functions and cocycles}

The transition functions satisfy
\[
  g_{ii}=1,\qquad
  g_{ij}=g_{ji}^{-1},
\]
and on triple overlaps
\[
  g_{ij}g_{jk}=g_{ik}.
\]
The last identity is the cocycle condition.

\begin{theorem}[Gluing line bundles from units]\label{thm:vi42-gluing}
Let \(X=\bigcup_i U_i\) be an open cover and suppose units
\[
  g_{ij}\in\OO_X^\times(U_i\cap U_j)
\]
satisfy
\[
  g_{ii}=1,\qquad g_{ij}g_{jk}=g_{ik}.
\]
Then the trivial bundles \(\OO_{U_i}\) glue to an invertible sheaf \(\mathcal L\) with transition functions \(g_{ij}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On \(U_i\cap U_j\), identify a section \(a_j\) in the \(j\)-th trivialization with
\[
  a_i=g_{ij}^{-1}a_j
\]
in the \(i\)-th trivialization, equivalently choose local frames satisfying \(e_i=g_{ij}e_j\).  The cocycle identity guarantees that the identifications agree on triple overlaps.  The sheaf-gluing theorem then produces \(\mathcal L\), and each \(U_i\) remains a trivializing open.
\end{proof}

Changing frames does not change the isomorphism class.  If
\[
  e_i'=u_i e_i,\qquad u_i\in\OO_X^\times(U_i),
\]
then
\[
  g_{ij}'=u_i g_{ij}u_j^{-1}.
\]
Thus transition functions differing by such a family of local units define isomorphic line bundles.

\begin{remark}[Čech viewpoint]
For a fixed cover, line bundles are represented by multiplicative \(1\)-cocycles with values in \(\OO_X^\times\), modulo multiplicative coboundaries.  Later cohomology chapters package this as
\[
  \Pic(X)\cong H^1(X,\OO_X^\times),
\]
but the present chapter needs only the explicit gluing calculation.
\end{remark}

\section{Tensor products, duals, and the Picard group}

If \(\mathcal L\) and \(\mathcal M\) are invertible, then so is
\[
  \mathcal L\otimes_{\OO_X}\mathcal M.
\]
If \(e_i\) and \(f_i\) are local frames with transition functions \(g_{ij}\) and \(h_{ij}\), then \(e_i\otimes f_i\) has transition function
\[
  g_{ij}h_{ij}.
\]

The dual
\[
  \mathcal L^\vee
  :=
  \mathcal{H}om_{\OO_X}(\mathcal L,\OO_X)
\]
is invertible, with transition functions \(g_{ij}^{-1}\), and evaluation gives
\[
  \mathcal L\otimes\mathcal L^\vee\cong\OO_X.
\]

\begin{definition}[Picard group]\label{def:vi42-picard-group}
The \emph{Picard group} of \(X\) is
\[
  \Pic(X)
  :=
  \{\text{isomorphism classes of invertible }\OO_X\text{-modules}\},
\]
with group law
\[
  [\mathcal L]+[\mathcal M]
  :=
  [\mathcal L\otimes\mathcal M].
\]
The identity is \([\OO_X]\), and
\[
  -[\mathcal L]=[\mathcal L^\vee].
\]
\end{definition}

Associativity and commutativity follow from the corresponding canonical tensor-product isomorphisms.

\section{Pullback of line bundles}

Let \(f:Y\to X\) be a morphism.  For an invertible sheaf \(\mathcal L\) on \(X\), define
\[
  f^*\mathcal L
  :=
  f^{-1}\mathcal L\otimes_{f^{-1}\OO_X}\OO_Y.
\]
Since local freeness of rank one is preserved by pullback, \(f^*\mathcal L\) is invertible.

\begin{proposition}[Functoriality of the Picard group]\label{prop:vi42-pic-functoriality}
Every morphism \(f:Y\to X\) induces a homomorphism
\[
  f^*:\Pic(X)\longrightarrow\Pic(Y).
\]
Moreover,
\[
  \operatorname{id}_X^*=\operatorname{id}_{\Pic(X)},
  \qquad
  (f\circ g)^*\cong g^*f^*.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Pullback commutes canonically with tensor products:
\[
  f^*(\mathcal L\otimes\mathcal M)
  \cong
  f^*\mathcal L\otimes f^*\mathcal M.
\]
It also preserves the trivial bundle.  The functorial identities are the standard identities for pullback of modules.
\end{proof}

If \(\mathcal L\) is represented by transition functions \(g_{ij}\), then \(f^*\mathcal L\) is represented on \(f^{-1}(U_i)\) by the pulled-back units \(f^\#(g_{ij})\).

\section{Cartier divisors produce line bundles}

Let \(X\) be integral with function field \(K(X)\).  Suppose a Cartier divisor \(D\) is represented on \(U_i\) by local rational equations
\[
  f_i\in K(X)^\times
\]
such that
\[
  \frac{f_i}{f_j}\in\OO_X^\times(U_i\cap U_j).
\]
VI/40 defined
\[
  \OO_X(D)|_{U_i}
  =
  f_i^{-1}\OO_{U_i}
  \subset K(X).
\]
A local frame is \(e_i=f_i^{-1}\), and
\[
  e_i=\frac{f_j}{f_i}e_j.
\]
Thus the transition functions of \(\OO_X(D)\) are \(f_j/f_i\).

\begin{proposition}[Tensor law for divisor line bundles]\label{prop:vi42-divisor-tensor}
For Cartier divisors \(D,E\),
\[
  \OO_X(D+E)
  \cong
  \OO_X(D)\otimes\OO_X(E),
\]
and
\[
  \OO_X(-D)\cong\OO_X(D)^\vee.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If \(D\) and \(E\) have local equations \(f_i\) and \(g_i\), then \(D+E\) has equation \(f_ig_i\).  The local generator
\[
  (f_ig_i)^{-1}
\]
is exactly \(f_i^{-1}\otimes g_i^{-1}\), and the transition functions multiply.
\end{proof}

A principal Cartier divisor \(D=\operatorname{div}(f)\) gives
\[
  \OO_X(D)=f^{-1}\OO_X\cong\OO_X
\]
by multiplication by \(f\).  Hence \(D\mapsto\OO_X(D)\) factors through Cartier divisor classes.

\section{Cartier divisor classes equal the Picard group}

Write
\[
  \CaCl(X)
  :=
  \CaDiv(X)/\{\text{principal Cartier divisors}\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Cartier--Picard equivalence]\label{thm:vi42-cartier-picard}
Let \(X\) be an integral scheme.  Then
\[
  \boxed{
  \CaCl(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Pic(X),
  \qquad
  [D]\longmapsto[\OO_X(D)].
  }
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The tensor law makes the map a homomorphism, and principal divisors map to the identity.

For surjectivity, let \(\mathcal L\) be invertible.  At the generic point \(\eta\), the stalk
\[
  \mathcal L_\eta
\]
is a one-dimensional \(K(X)\)-vector space.  Choose a nonzero rational section \(s\).  On a trivializing open \(U_i\), choose a frame \(e_i\) and write
\[
  s=f_i e_i,\qquad f_i\in K(X)^\times.
\]
If \(e_i=g_{ij}e_j\), then
\[
  f_j=f_i g_{ij},
\]
so
\[
  \frac{f_i}{f_j}=g_{ij}^{-1}\in\OO_X^\times(U_i\cap U_j).
\]
Hence the \(f_i\) define a Cartier divisor \(D=\operatorname{div}(s)\).  The frames \(f_i^{-1}\) of \(\OO_X(D)\) have transition functions
\[
  \frac{f_j}{f_i}=g_{ij},
\]
so
\[
  \OO_X(D)\cong\mathcal L.
\]

For injectivity, suppose \(\OO_X(D)\cong\OO_X\).  Under a chosen trivialization, the canonical rational section \(1\) of \(\OO_X(D)\) becomes a rational function \(f\in K(X)^\times\).  Since the divisor of the canonical rational section is \(D\), we obtain
\[
  D=\operatorname{div}(f).
\]
Thus \(D\) is principal.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the theorem remembers]
A line bundle does not remember a specific Cartier divisor, only its class modulo principal divisors.  Choosing a nonzero rational section of \(\mathcal L\) chooses one divisor representative; multiplying the section by \(f\in K(X)^\times\) changes that representative by \(\operatorname{div}(f)\).
\end{remark}

\section{From the Picard group to the Weil class group}

Assume now that \(X\) is normal, Noetherian, and integral.  VI/40 constructed an injective map
\[
  \CaDiv(X)\hookrightarrow\Div(X)
\]
compatible with principal divisors.  Passing to quotient groups and using \cref{thm:vi42-cartier-picard} gives:

\begin{theorem}[Picard group inside the class group]\label{thm:vi42-pic-to-cl}
There is a natural injective homomorphism
\[
  \boxed{
  \Pic(X)\hookrightarrow\Cl(X).
  }
\]
Its image consists exactly of Weil divisor classes admitting a Cartier representative.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A Cartier divisor determines a Weil divisor.  If two Cartier divisors differ by a principal Cartier divisor, their Weil divisors differ by the same principal Weil divisor, so the map descends to classes.

If a Cartier class maps to zero in \(\Cl(X)\), its associated Weil divisor is principal.  By injectivity of the Cartier-to-Weil map on a normal Noetherian integral scheme, the Cartier divisor itself is principal.  Hence the induced map on classes is injective.

The image statement is immediate from the construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Locally factorial comparison]\label{cor:vi42-locally-factorial}
If \(X\) is normal, Noetherian, integral, and locally factorial, then
\[
  \boxed{\Pic(X)\cong\Cl(X).}
\]
In particular this holds for regular Noetherian integral schemes.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
On a locally factorial scheme every Weil divisor is Cartier.  Therefore every class in \(\Cl(X)\) lies in the image of \cref{thm:vi42-pic-to-cl}.  Regular local rings are UFDs, so regular Noetherian schemes are locally factorial.
\end{proof}

\section{Affine schemes and invertible modules}

For \(X=\Spec A\), quasi-coherent sheaves correspond to \(A\)-modules.  Under this correspondence, invertible sheaves correspond to finitely generated projective \(A\)-modules of rank one.

\begin{proposition}[Affine description]\label{prop:vi42-affine-pic}
There is a natural identification
\[
  \Pic(\Spec A)
  \cong
  \{\text{rank-one finitely generated projective }A\text{-modules}\}/\cong.
\]
\end{proposition}

An important warning is that affine does not mean Picard-trivial.  Polynomial affine space is trivial because its coordinate ring is factorial, but general affine schemes may have nonzero Picard group.

If \(A\) is a local ring, every finitely generated projective \(A\)-module is free.  Hence
\[
  \Pic(\Spec A)=0.
\]

For a Dedekind domain, every nonzero fractional ideal is invertible and
\[
  \Pic(\Spec A)
\]
is the usual ideal class group.

\section{Affine space}

\begin{theorem}[Picard group of affine space]\label{thm:vi42-affine-space}
For every field \(k\) and \(n\ge1\),
\[
  \boxed{\Pic(\AA^n_k)=0.}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The polynomial ring
\[
  k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]
\]
is a UFD, so VI/41 gives
\[
  \Cl(\AA^n_k)=0.
\]
Affine space is regular, hence locally factorial, and \cref{cor:vi42-locally-factorial} gives
\[
  \Pic(\AA^n_k)\cong\Cl(\AA^n_k)=0.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{The quadric cone: \(\Pic\) versus \(\Cl\)}

Let
\[
  X=\Spec A,
  \qquad
  A=k[x,y,z]/(xy-z^2),
\]
and let
\[
  L=V(x,z).
\]
VI/41 proved
\[
  \Cl(X)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z
\]
with nonzero generator \([L]\), and VI/40 proved that \(L\) is not Cartier at the vertex.

\begin{theorem}[Picard group of the affine quadric cone]\label{thm:vi42-cone-pic}
For the affine quadric cone,
\[
  \boxed{\Pic(X)=0}
\]
while
\[
  \boxed{\Cl(X)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:vi42-pic-to-cl}, \(\Pic(X)\) injects into the two-element group \(\Cl(X)\).  If the image were nonzero, it would contain \([L]\).  Then \([L]\) would admit a Cartier representative \(D\).  Since
\[
  [D]=[L],
\]
the difference \(L-D\) would be principal, hence Cartier.  Therefore \(L\) itself would be Cartier, contradicting the failure of local principality at the vertex.  Thus the image is zero and injectivity forces \(\Pic(X)=0\).
\end{proof}

Now remove the vertex \(o=(x,y,z)\):
\[
  U=X\setminus\{o\}.
\]
VI/41 showed that deleting this codimension-two point does not change the Weil class group:
\[
  \Cl(U)\cong\Cl(X)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]
The scheme \(U\) is regular, hence locally factorial.

\begin{corollary}[Picard group of the punctured cone]\label{cor:vi42-punctured-cone}
For the punctured quadric cone,
\[
  \boxed{
  \Pic(U)\cong\Cl(U)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
  }
\]
\end{corollary}

The nonzero line bundle on \(U\) is represented by the restriction
\[
  \OO_U(L|_U),
\]
because the ruling becomes Cartier after the singular vertex is removed.

\input{chapters/ch42_line_bundles_and_picard_groups/figures/figure_05}

\begin{warning}[Codimension two behaves differently for \(\Pic\)]
For normal schemes, removing a codimension-at-least-two subset leaves \(\Cl\) unchanged.  The example above shows that \(\Pic\) need not be unchanged:
\[
  \Pic(X)=0,
  \qquad
  \Pic(U)=\mathbb Z/2.
\]
The reason is that Cartierness is a local condition sensitive to the removed singular point.
\end{warning}

\section{Projective space and the twisting sheaves}

VI/36 introduced the twisting sheaves \(\OO_{\PP^n}(d)\).  Their tensor law is
\[
  \OO(a)\otimes\OO(b)\cong\OO(a+b),
\qquad
  \OO(d)^\vee\cong\OO(-d).
\]

Let \(H\subset\PP^n\) be a hyperplane.  VI/40 identified
\[
  \OO_{\PP^n}(H)\cong\OO_{\PP^n}(1),
\]
and VI/41 computed
\[
  \Cl(\PP^n_k)\cong\mathbb Z[H].
\]
Since projective space is regular, \cref{cor:vi42-locally-factorial} immediately gives:

\begin{theorem}[Picard group of projective space]\label{thm:vi42-pic-projective-space}
For \(n\ge1\),
\[
  \boxed{
  \Pic(\PP^n_k)\cong\mathbb Z,
  }
\]
generated by \([\OO_{\PP^n}(1)]\).  Under this isomorphism,
\[
  d\longleftrightarrow\OO_{\PP^n}(d).
\]
\end{theorem}

If \(F\) is a nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree \(d\), then its zero hypersurface satisfies
\[
  \OO_{\PP^n}(V_+(F))\cong\OO_{\PP^n}(d).
\]

\section{Regular sections and effective Cartier divisors}

Let \(\mathcal L\) be invertible on an integral scheme \(X\), and let
\[
  s\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal L)
\]
be nonzero at the generic point.  Choose a local frame \(e_i\) and write
\[
  s=f_i e_i,
  \qquad
  f_i\in\OO_X(U_i).
\]
Because \(X\) is integral and \(s\) is generically nonzero, each nonzero local coefficient \(f_i\) is a non-zero-divisor wherever the frame is defined.  The ideals \((f_i)\) glue to an effective Cartier divisor.

\begin{theorem}[Zero divisor of a section]\label{thm:vi42-section-divisor}
Let \(X\) be integral, \(\mathcal L\) invertible, and \(s\) a nonzero global section.  Then \(s\) determines an effective Cartier divisor
\[
  D=(s)_0
\]
and a canonical isomorphism
\[
  \boxed{\mathcal L\cong\OO_X(D)}
\]
carrying \(s\) to the canonical section of \(\OO_X(D)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(s=f_i e_i\).  On overlaps, if \(e_i=g_{ij}e_j\), then
\[
  f_j=f_i g_{ij},
\]
so the local equations \(f_i\) differ by units and define an effective Cartier divisor \(D\).  The transition function of \(\OO_X(D)\) between frames \(f_i^{-1}\) and \(f_j^{-1}\) is
\[
  \frac{f_j}{f_i}=g_{ij},
\]
the same as for \(\mathcal L\).  Under the induced isomorphism, the canonical section \(1=f_i(f_i^{-1})\) corresponds to \(f_i e_i=s\).
\end{proof}

Conversely every effective Cartier divisor produces \(\OO_X(D)\) with its canonical global section.  Thus effective Cartier divisors are line bundles together with a distinguished section satisfying the non-zero-divisor condition.

\section{Globally generated line bundles and projective maps}

An invertible sheaf \(\mathcal L\) is \emph{globally generated} if the evaluation map
\[
  \Gamma(X,\mathcal L)\otimes\OO_X\longrightarrow\mathcal L
\]
is surjective.

Suppose
\[
  s_0,\ldots,s_n\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal L)
\]
have no common zero.  On a trivializing open where \(e\) is a frame, write
\[
  s_i=f_i e.
\]
The ratios define
\[
  x\longmapsto[f_0(x):\cdots:f_n(x)].
\]
Changing the frame multiplies every \(f_i\) by the same unit, so the projective point is unchanged.

\begin{theorem}[Line bundle and sections define a projective morphism]\label{thm:vi42-sections-projective-map}
If \(s_0,\ldots,s_n\) generate \(\mathcal L\) at every point, they define a morphism
\[
  \varphi:X\longrightarrow\PP^n
\]
such that
\[
  \varphi^*\OO_{\PP^n}(1)\cong\mathcal L.
\]
Conversely, a morphism \(\varphi:X\to\PP^n\) pulls back the coordinate sections of \(\OO(1)\) to \(n+1\) global sections generating \(\varphi^*\OO(1)\).
\end{theorem}

This is the line-bundle formulation of the homogeneous-coordinate constructions from VI/38.  Very ample line bundles are those for which a suitable finite-dimensional space of sections gives a closed immersion.

\section{Restriction to open subsets}

If \(j:U\hookrightarrow X\) is open, restriction gives
\[
  j^*:\Pic(X)\longrightarrow\Pic(U).
\]
Line bundles can become trivial after deleting the support of a divisor.  In the locally factorial normal setting, the class-group localization sequence from VI/41 gives an efficient description.

\begin{proposition}[Restriction across divisors]\label{prop:vi42-restriction-divisors}
Let \(X\) be normal, Noetherian, integral, and locally factorial, and let
\[
  U=X\setminus\bigcup_{\alpha=1}^r Z_\alpha
\]
where the \(Z_\alpha\) are the codimension-one irreducible components of the complement.  Then
\[
  \bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^r\mathbb Z[Z_\alpha]
  \longrightarrow
  \Pic(X)
  \longrightarrow
  \Pic(U)
  \longrightarrow0
\]
is exact.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use \(\Pic\cong\Cl\) on \(X\) and \(U\), then apply the open-subset exact sequence for class groups from VI/41.
\end{proof}

For example,
\[
  \AA^1=\PP^1\setminus\{\infty\}.
\]
The class of \(\OO_{\PP^1}(1)\) is generated by the removed point and therefore becomes trivial on \(\AA^1\).

\section{Degree on smooth projective curves}

Let \(C\) be a smooth projective integral curve over a field.  Every Weil divisor is Cartier, so
\[
  \Pic(C)\cong\Cl(C).
\]
For a divisor
\[
  D=\sum_P n_P[P]
\]
over an algebraically closed field, define
\[
  \deg D=\sum_P n_P.
\]
More generally one weights closed points by residue-field degrees.

Principal divisors have degree zero, so degree descends to
\[
  \deg:\Pic(C)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z.
\]

\begin{definition}[Degree-zero Picard group]\label{def:vi42-pic-zero}
The subgroup
\[
  \Pic^0(C)
  :=
  \ker\!\bigl(\deg:\Pic(C)\to\mathbb Z\bigr)
\]
is the degree-zero Picard group.
\end{definition}

For \(\PP^1\),
\[
  \Pic(\PP^1)\cong\mathbb Z,
  \qquad
  \Pic^0(\PP^1)=0.
\]
The next chapter studies the first dramatically different case: a smooth plane cubic \(E\).  After choosing a rational point \(O\), one obtains
\[

\section{A practical dictionary}

The constructions of VI/40--VI/42 can be summarized as follows:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{object} & \text{local data} & \text{equivalence}\\
\hline
\text{Cartier divisor} & f_i\in K(X)^\times,\ f_i/f_j\in\OO^\times & \text{multiply globally by }f\\
\text{line bundle} & g_{ij}\in\OO^\times,\ g_{ij}g_{jk}=g_{ik} & g'_{ij}=u_i g_{ij}u_j^{-1}\\
\text{divisor line bundle} & g_{ij}=f_j/f_i & [D]\mapsto[\OO(D)]\\
\text{Weil class} & \sum n_Z[Z] & \text{mod principal divisors}
\end{array}
\]

On an integral scheme,
\[
  \CaCl(X)\cong\Pic(X).
\]
On a normal Noetherian integral scheme,
\[
  \Pic(X)\hookrightarrow\Cl(X).
\]
On a locally factorial one,
\[
  \Pic(X)\cong\Cl(X).
\]

\begin{problem}[Two-chart gluing]\label{prob:vi42-01}
Let \(X=U\cup V\) and \(g\in\OO_X^\times(U\cap V)\).  Construct the corresponding line bundle and determine when it is trivial.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[The standard bundle on \(\PP^1\)]\label{prob:vi42-02}
Using \(U_0=D_+(x_0)\) and \(U_1=D_+(x_1)\), write a transition function for \(\OO_{\PP^1}(1)\).

\begin{problem}[Recovering a Cartier divisor from a line bundle]\label{prob:vi42-04}

  \Pic(X)=0,\qquad \Pic(U)=\mathbb Z/2.

\begin{problem}[Hyperplane pullback]\label{prob:vi42-10}

\section{Challenges}

\begin{problem}[Challenge: Cartier--Picard without shortcuts]\label{chal:vi42-01}
Give a complete proof of \(\CaCl(X)\cong\Pic(X)\) for an integral scheme, including the construction of a rational section from the generic stalk and the verification that different rational sections change the divisor only by a principal divisor.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Challenge: local-to-global classification]\label{chal:vi42-02}
Starting from an open cover \(\{U_i\}\), prove directly that isomorphism classes of line bundles trivialized by the cover are multiplicative \(1\)-cocycles \(g_{ij}\in\OO^\times(U_{ij})\) modulo coboundaries \(u_i u_j^{-1}\), and show that tensor product corresponds to multiplication of cocycles.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Challenge: Picard versus class group on the cone]\label{chal:vi42-03}
For \(X=\Spec k[x,y,z]/(xy-z^2)\), reconstruct from first principles the chain
\[
  \Pic(X)=0,\qquad
  \Cl(X)=\mathbb Z/2,\qquad
  \Pic(X\setminus\{o\})=\mathbb Z/2.
\]
Identify exactly which implication uses normality, which uses local factoriality, and which uses codimension-two invariance of \(\Cl\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Challenge: projective space from two methods]\label{chal:vi42-04}
Compute \(\Pic(\PP^n_k)\) twice: first from \(\Pic\cong\Cl\) and the class-group computation of VI/41, and second directly from transition functions on the standard affine cover.  Compare the two generators.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Challenge: bridge to plane cubics]\label{chal:vi42-05}
Let \(E\subset\PP^2\) be a smooth plane cubic with \(O\in E(k)\).  Prove as much as possible, using only divisor and line-bundle methods developed so far, about the map
\[
  E(k)\longrightarrow\Pic^0(E),
  \qquad
  P\longmapsto[\OO_E(P-O)].
\]
Isolate precisely the additional geometric input needed in VI/43 to prove that this map realizes the chord-and-tangent group law.
\end{problem}

\section*{Bridge to VI/43}

The divisor and line-bundle arcs now meet:
\[
\boxed{
\text{Cartier divisors}/\text{principal}
\cong
\Pic(X)
\hookrightarrow
\Cl(X).
}
\]
For smooth varieties the last arrow is an isomorphism, so divisor classes and line bundles become interchangeable.  On smooth projective curves, degree splits the discussion into
\[
  \Pic(C)
  \xrightarrow{\deg}
  \mathbb Z,
  \qquad
  \Pic^0(C)=\ker(\deg).
\]
For \(\PP^1\), \(\Pic^0=0\).  VI/43 turns to a smooth plane cubic, where \(\Pic^0\) becomes a nontrivial geometric object and the divisor relation cut out by a line,
\[
  P+Q+R\sim 3O,
\]
will be converted into the chord-and-tangent group law.

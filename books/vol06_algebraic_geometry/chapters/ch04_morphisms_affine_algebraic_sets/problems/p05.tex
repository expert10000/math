% VI.04.P5
\subsection*{Problem VI.04.P5 --- Dense image and injective pullback}
\label{prob:vi04-p5}
\paragraph{Problem.}
For a morphism \(\varphi:X\to Y\), prove
\[
\ker\varphi^*=I_Y(\varphi(X)),
\qquad
\overline{\varphi(X)}=V_Y(\ker\varphi^*).
\]
Deduce that \(\varphi\) has dense image iff \(\varphi^*\) is injective.  Apply this to the
projection \(\mathbb A^2\to\mathbb A^1\) and, over an infinite field, to squaring
\(t\mapsto t^2\).
\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}  It gives a direct algebraic test for a topological
property of a map and is one of the most useful patterns in affine algebraic geometry.
\paragraph{Guided hints.}  First characterize the kernel by evaluation on \(\varphi(X)\),
then invoke the closure formula from VI/02.
\paragraph{Solution.}
For \(h\in k[Y]\),
\[
h\in\ker\varphi^*\iff h(\varphi(x))=0\ \forall x\in X,
\]
so the kernel is precisely \(I_Y(\varphi(X))\).  The closure formula gives the second
equality.  The image is dense iff its closure is all of \(Y\), equivalently iff its
vanishing ideal in \(k[Y]\) is zero, i.e. iff \(\varphi^*\) is injective.

For projection, \(t\mapsto x\) embeds \(k[t]\) into \(k[x,y]\), so the image is dense
(in fact surjective on points).  For squaring, \(u\mapsto t^2\) is injective over an
infinite field, so the image is Zariski dense even when it is not all rational points.
\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}  Kernel means ``equations lost by the map'';
those equations cut out exactly the closure of the image.
\paragraph{Extensions.}  Show that the coordinate ring of the image closure is
\(k[Y]/\ker\varphi^*\), hence is naturally isomorphic to the image subalgebra of
\(\varphi^*\).
\paragraph{Provenance.}  New synthesis problem built from the VI/02 closure formula and the
affine pullback correspondence.
\fi

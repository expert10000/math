\subsection*{Problem VI.04.P3 --- No nonconstant polynomial map to a smooth cubic}
\label{prob:vi04-p3}
\paragraph{Problem.}
Assume \(k\) is algebraically closed and \(\operatorname{char}(k)\neq2\).  Let
\[
E=V(y^2-x^3+x)\subseteq\mathbb A_k^2.
\]
Show that every morphism \(\mathbb A_k^1\to E\) is constant.
\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}  It shows how a geometric non-parametrization
statement can be proved entirely inside the UFD \(k[t]\).
\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}  Write the pullbacks of \(x,y\) as \(a,b\in k[t]\)
and factor \(b^2=a(a-1)(a+1)\).
\paragraph{Guided hints.}  \textbf{Hint 1.} The three factors are pairwise coprime unless
one is zero, in which case the map is already constant.  \textbf{Hint 2.} A product of
pairwise coprime elements in a UFD is a square only if each factor is a square up to a unit.
\textbf{Hint 3.} Use algebraic closedness to absorb the units into squares.
\paragraph{Solution.}
Let \(a=\varphi^*(x)\), \(b=\varphi^*(y)\).  Then
\[
b^2=a(a-1)(a+1).
\]
If one factor is zero, \(a\) and then \(b\) are constant.  Otherwise, because
\(\operatorname{char}(k)\neq2\), the three factors are pairwise coprime.  Unique
factorization and algebraic closedness give polynomials \(p,q\) with
\[
a=p^2,\qquad a-1=q^2.
\]
Thus \((p-q)(p+q)=1\).  Both factors are units of \(k[t]\), hence constants, so \(p,q\)
and therefore \(a\) are constant.  Then \(b\) is constant as well.
\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}  A polynomial parametrization would force three
pairwise coprime factors to be simultaneous squares, which collapses to a factorization of
\(1\).
\paragraph{Extensions.}  Compare with the singular cubic \(y^2=x^3\), which does admit the
nonconstant parametrization \(t\mapsto(t^2,t^3)\).
\paragraph{Provenance.}  Recovered from legacy DG4 affine Problem 10.  The field and
characteristic hypotheses implicit in the old solution are explicit here.

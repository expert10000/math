\subsection*{Problem VI.04.P1 --- The hyperbola is not the affine line}
\label{prob:vi04-p1}
\paragraph{Problem.}
Let \(H=V(xy-1)\subseteq\mathbb A_k^2\).  Show that \(H\not\cong\mathbb A_k^1\), and
determine every morphism \(\mathbb A_k^1\to H\).
\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}  The geometry is decided by a small ring-theoretic
invariant: units.  It is the cleanest first demonstration that two smooth-looking affine
curves can have different algebraic function theory.
\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}  Use \(k[H]\cong k[x,x^{-1}]\), compare unit
groups, and remember that a morphism to \(H\) sends the invertible coordinate \(x\) to a
unit in the source coordinate ring.
\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\textbf{Hint 1.} Compute the units of \(k[t]\).  \textbf{Hint 2.} Compute the units of
\(k[x,x^{-1}]\).  \textbf{Hint 3.} For a map \(\mathbb A^1\to H\), the pullback of \(x\)
must be invertible.
\paragraph{Solution.}
Since \(xy=1\),
\[
k[H]=k[x,y]/(xy-1)\cong k[x,x^{-1}].
\]
Its units are \(c x^n\) with \(c\in k^\times\), \(n\in\mathbb Z\), whereas
\(k[t]^\times=k^\times\).  An isomorphism of coordinate rings would preserve unit groups,
so \(H\not\cong\mathbb A^1\).

A morphism \(\varphi:\mathbb A^1\to H\) yields
\(\varphi^*:k[x,x^{-1}]\to k[t]\).  Since \(x\) is a unit, \(\varphi^*(x)=c\in k^\times\),
and then \(\varphi^*(y)=c^{-1}\).  Hence every morphism is constant:
\[
t\longmapsto(c,c^{-1}).
\]
\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}  Morphisms into \(H\cong\mathbb G_m\) are
controlled by invertible functions.  The affine line has no nonconstant invertible
polynomial functions.
\paragraph{Extensions.}  Replace \(\mathbb A^1\) by a general affine algebraic set \(X\)
and prove \(\operatorname{Mor}(X,H)\cong k[X]^\times\).
\paragraph{Provenance.}  Recovered from legacy DG4 affine Problem 7; both the
non-isomorphism and classification of maps are retained.
\fi

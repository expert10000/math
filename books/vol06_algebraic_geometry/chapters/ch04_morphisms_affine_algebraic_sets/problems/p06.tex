\subsection*{Problem VI.04.P6 --- Graphs are closed embeddings}
\label{prob:vi04-p6}
\paragraph{Problem.}
Let \(f:X\to Y\subseteq\mathbb A^m\) be a morphism.  Prove that its graph
\(\Gamma_f\subseteq X\times Y\) is closed and that
\[
\gamma_f:X\to X\times Y,\qquad x\mapsto(x,f(x))
\]
is a closed embedding.  Describe the induced map on coordinate rings.
\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}  Graphs convert an arbitrary morphism into a closed
subspace of a product and are a standard tool throughout geometry.
\paragraph{Guided hints.}  In ambient coordinates, impose \(y_i-f_i(x)=0\).  Projection to
\(X\) is an inverse on the graph.
\paragraph{Solution.}
Write \(f=(f_1,\dots,f_m)\).  Inside \(X\times Y\),
\[
\Gamma_f=V_{X\times Y}(y_1-f_1,\dots,y_m-f_m),
\]
so it is closed.  The first projection restricts to an inverse of \(\gamma_f\), proving
that \(\gamma_f\) is a closed embedding.  Under
\(k[X\times Y]\cong k[X]\otimes k[Y]\), its pullback is
\[
a\otimes b\longmapsto a\,f^*(b),
\]
which is surjective because \(a\otimes1\mapsto a\).
\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}  The graph equations are the algebraic encoding
of ``second coordinate equals the image of the first.''
\paragraph{Extensions.}  Use the graph to describe the equalizer of two morphisms.
\paragraph{Provenance.}  New structural problem.
\fi

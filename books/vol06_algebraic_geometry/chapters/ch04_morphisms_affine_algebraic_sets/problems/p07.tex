\subsection*{Problem VI.04.P7 --- All automorphisms of the affine line}
\label{prob:vi04-p7}
\paragraph{Problem.}
Prove that every automorphism of \(\mathbb A_k^1\) has the form
\[
t\longmapsto at+b,
\qquad a\in k^\times,\ b\in k.
\]
\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}  The affine correspondence turns a geometric
classification into a one-line degree argument in \(k[t]\).
\paragraph{Guided hints.}  An automorphism corresponds to a polynomial \(p(t)\) admitting a
polynomial inverse \(q(t)\).  Compare \(\deg(q\circ p)\) with \(1\).
\paragraph{Solution.}
Let \(\varphi(t)=p(t)\) and \(\psi(t)=q(t)\) be inverse polynomial maps.  Neither is
constant, and
\[
1=\deg t=\deg(q\circ p)=\deg q\,\deg p.
\]
Therefore \(\deg p=\deg q=1\), so \(p(t)=at+b\) with \(a\neq0\).  Conversely every such
map has polynomial inverse \(a^{-1}(t-b)\).
\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}  Polynomial degree is rigid under composition;
there is no room for a nonlinear polynomial automorphism in one variable.
\paragraph{Extensions.}  Contrast with \(\mathbb A^2\), where nonlinear polynomial
automorphisms such as \((x,y)\mapsto(x,y+x^2)\) exist.
\paragraph{Provenance.}  New problem extending the routine affine-linear exercise to a full
classification theorem.
\fi

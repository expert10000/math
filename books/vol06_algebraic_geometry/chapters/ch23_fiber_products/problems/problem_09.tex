\subsection*{VI.23.P9: Closed intersections by fiber product}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi23-p09}
Let $I,J\subseteq A$. Compute
\[
\Spec(A/I)\times_{\Spec A}\Spec(A/J).
\]
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
The computation proves that scheme-theoretic intersection is intrinsically a pullback.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate the requested statement into compatible maps from an arbitrary test scheme, and use uniqueness before choosing coordinates.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Apply the affine fiber-product formula.
\item Construct the multiplication map to $A/(I+J)$.
\item Check the inverse using the balancing relation.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
By the affine theorem,
\[
\Spec(A/I)\times_{\Spec A}\Spec(A/J)
\cong
\Spec\bigl((A/I)\otimes_A(A/J)\bigr).
\]
We identify the tensor product explicitly.  The map
\[
(A/I)\times(A/J)\to A/(I+J),\qquad
(\bar a,\bar b)\mapsto \overline{ab}
\]
is $A$-balanced and hence induces
\[
\theta:(A/I)\otimes_A(A/J)\to A/(I+J).
\]
Conversely, $a\mapsto(\bar a)\otimes1$ kills $I+J$: elements of $I$ vanish in the first factor,
and elements of $J$ may be moved by balancing to the second factor where they vanish.  Thus it
induces $A/(I+J)\to(A/I)\otimes_A(A/J)$.  The two maps are inverse on pure tensors and on the
class of every $a\in A$.

Therefore
\[
\Spec(A/I)\times_{\Spec A}\Spec(A/J)\cong\Spec A/(I+J).
\]
This recovers exactly the scheme-theoretic intersection from VI/22, including nilpotent
intersection structure rather than only the set $V(I)\cap V(J)$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A geometric assertion was reduced to a representability statement, so the canonical map was forced by universality.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Repeat the argument after restricting to affine opens and compare with the tensor-product formula.

\subsection*{VI.23.P1: The AG7 diagonal Cartesian square}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi23-p01}
Suppose $X\to S\to T$ and $Y\to S\to T$. Prove that
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
X\times_SY \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & S \arrow[d,"\Delta_{S/T}"]\\
X\times_TY \arrow[r] & S\times_TS
\end{tikzcd}
\]
is Cartesian.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This square is the cleanest expression of the idea that the diagonal imposes equality over the finer base $S$.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
First forget the map to $S$ and view a test point as a compatible pair over $T$.  Then ask what pulling back the diagonal forces.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Describe a $Q$-point of $X\times_TY$.
\item Describe a $Q$-point of $S\times_TS$.
\item Show that landing on the diagonal is equivalent to equality of the two maps to $S$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Let $P$ denote the pullback
\[
P=(X\times_TY)\times_{S\times_TS}S.
\]
We prove that $P$ has the universal property of $X\times_SY$.

Fix a test scheme $Q$.  A morphism $Q\to X\times_TY$ is the same as a pair
$a:Q\to X$, $b:Q\to Y$ whose composites to $T$ agree.  Such a pair induces a map
\[
Q\longrightarrow S\times_TS,
\qquad
q\longmapsto (f(a(q)),g(b(q)))
\]
scheme-theoretically.  To give in addition a map $c:Q\to S$ making the defining square for $P$
commute is exactly to require
\[
(fa,gb)=\Delta_{S/T}c=(c,c).
\]
Hence $fa=c=gb$.  The map $c$ is therefore forced, and the remaining datum is precisely a pair
$a:Q\to X$, $b:Q\to Y$ satisfying $fa=gb$ in $S$.

Thus, naturally in $Q$,
\[
\Hom(Q,P)
\cong
\Hom(Q,X)\times_{\Hom(Q,S)}\Hom(Q,Y).
\]
The right-hand side is the functor represented by $X\times_SY$.  By uniqueness of representing
objects, there is a unique projection-compatible isomorphism
\[
P\xrightarrow{\sim}X\times_SY.
\]
Under this identification the displayed square is Cartesian.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The diagonal converted the condition ``the two $S$-maps are equal'' into an ordinary fiber-product condition.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Use the same argument to construct equalizers as pullbacks of diagonals.

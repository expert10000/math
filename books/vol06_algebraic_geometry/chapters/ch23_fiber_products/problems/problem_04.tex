\subsection*{VI.23.P4: Uniqueness of fiber products}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi23-p04}
Given two fiber products $P$ and $P'$ of $X\to S\leftarrow Y$, construct the unique projection-compatible isomorphism $P\cong P'$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Fiber products are defined by a property rather than a chosen construction, so this uniqueness theorem is what makes every later canonical identification legitimate.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate the requested statement into compatible maps from an arbitrary test scheme, and use uniqueness before choosing coordinates.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Use the projections from one product as a compatible pair into the other.
\item Construct maps in both directions.
\item Compare each composite with the appropriate identity by its projections.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Let $(p_X,p_Y)$ and $(p_X',p_Y')$ be the projections from $P$ and $P'$.  Since
$f p_X=g p_Y$, the pair $(p_X,p_Y)$ is a compatible pair of maps from $P$ to $X$ and $Y$.
The universal property of $P'$ therefore gives a unique map
\[
u:P\to P'
\]
with $p_X'u=p_X$ and $p_Y'u=p_Y$.  Reversing the roles gives a unique
$v:P'\to P$ satisfying $p_Xv=p_X'$ and $p_Yv=p_Y'$.

Now $vu:P\to P$ has the same two projected components as $\id_P$:
\[
p_Xvu=p_X,\qquad p_Yvu=p_Y.
\]
Uniqueness in the universal property of $P$ forces $vu=\id_P$.  Similarly $uv=\id_{P'}$.
Thus $u$ is an isomorphism.

Finally, if $\tilde u:P\to P'$ is any projection-compatible map, it satisfies the same two
equations as $u$, so universality of $P'$ gives $\tilde u=u$.  Hence the isomorphism is unique
in the strongest chart/projection-compatible sense.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A geometric assertion was reduced to a representability statement, so the canonical map was forced by universality.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Repeat the argument after restricting to affine opens and compare with the tensor-product formula.

\subsection*{VI.23.P14: Equalizer as a pullback of the diagonal}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi23-p14}
Let $f,g:T\rightrightarrows X$ be morphisms over $S$. Show that the pullback of
$(f,g):T\to X\times_SX$ along $\Delta_{X/S}$ represents maps $Q\to T$ on which $f$ and $g$
become equal.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
The argument shows how a categorical equalizer is built from a diagonal and a fiber product.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate the requested statement into compatible maps from an arbitrary test scheme, and use uniqueness before choosing coordinates.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Form the indicated pullback.
\item Expand its $Q$-points.
\item Observe that the auxiliary map $Q\to X$ is forced.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Form
\[
E=T\times_{X\times_SX}X,
\]
where $T\to X\times_SX$ is $(f,g)$ and $X\to X\times_SX$ is the diagonal.  For a test scheme
$Q$, a map $Q\to E$ consists of maps
\[
h:Q\to T,\qquad q:Q\to X
\]
such that
\[
(fh,gh)=\Delta q=(q,q).
\]
Hence $fh=q=gh$.  The map $q$ is forced, so the datum is exactly a morphism
$h:Q\to T$ satisfying $fh=gh$.  Conversely every such $h$ determines $q=fh=gh$ and therefore
a unique $Q\to E$.

Thus $E$ represents the equalizer functor:
\[
\Hom(Q,E)=\{h\in\Hom(Q,T):fh=gh\}.
\]
In particular the scheme-theoretic equalizer is obtained by imposing equality through the
diagonal, not by merely taking the set of points on which the underlying continuous maps happen
to agree.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A geometric assertion was reduced to a representability statement, so the canonical map was forced by universality.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Repeat the argument after restricting to affine opens and compare with the tensor-product formula.

\subsection*{VI.23.P7: Symmetry}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi23-p07}
Construct the canonical isomorphism $X\times_SY\cong Y\times_SX$ and prove its square is the identity.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Symmetry is a coherence isomorphism forced by the universal property, not an extra structure placed on the product.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate the requested statement into compatible maps from an arbitrary test scheme, and use uniqueness before choosing coordinates.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Swap the projections.
\item Construct the reverse swap.
\item Use uniqueness to identify the double swap with the identity.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Let $P=X\times_SY$ with projections $p_X,p_Y$.  The swapped pair $(p_Y,p_X)$ is compatible
over $S$, hence induces a unique
\[
\tau:P\to Y\times_SX.
\]
Similarly the projections from $Y\times_SX$ induce a unique reverse flip $\sigma$.

The first and second projections of $\sigma\tau:P\to P$ are $p_X$ and $p_Y$.  The identity on
$P$ has the same components, so uniqueness gives $\sigma\tau=\id_P$.  Likewise
$\tau\sigma=\id$.  Thus $\tau$ is an isomorphism.

If one identifies $Y\times_SX$ with $P$ via this canonical map, applying the flip twice has the
original projections and hence is literally the identity.  No coordinate permutation has to be
chosen.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A geometric assertion was reduced to a representability statement, so the canonical map was forced by universality.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Repeat the argument after restricting to affine opens and compare with the tensor-product formula.

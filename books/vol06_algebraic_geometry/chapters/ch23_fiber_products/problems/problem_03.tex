\subsection*{VI.23.P3: Affine fiber product}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi23-p03}
Let $A\to B$ and $A\to C$ be ring maps. Prove
\[
\Spec B\times_{\Spec A}\Spec C\cong\Spec(B\otimes_AC).
\]
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This theorem is the computational engine for every later affine pullback, intersection, fiber, and base change.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Build the two projections from the canonical maps into the tensor product.  For the universal arrow, use the universal property of affine targets from VI/19 and then the universal property of $B\otimes_AC$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Construct the two canonical $A$-algebra maps into $B\otimes_AC$.
\item Translate a compatible pair $T\to\Spec B,\Spec C$ into maps to $\Gamma(T,\OO_T)$.
\item Use tensor-product uniqueness to prove uniqueness of the scheme morphism.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Set $P=\Spec(B\otimes_AC)$.  The canonical $A$-algebra maps
\[
B\to B\otimes_AC,\quad b\mapsto b\otimes1,
\qquad
C\to B\otimes_AC,\quad c\mapsto1\otimes c
\]
give morphisms $p_B:P\to\Spec B$ and $p_C:P\to\Spec C$.  Their composites to $\Spec A$ agree,
because $a\otimes1=1\otimes a$ in the tensor product.

Now let $T$ be any scheme with maps
\[
u:T\to\Spec B,\qquad v:T\to\Spec C
\]
whose composites to $\Spec A$ agree.  By the affine-target universal property from VI/19, these
maps correspond to ring homomorphisms
\[
\alpha:B\to\Gamma(T,\OO_T),\qquad
\beta:C\to\Gamma(T,\OO_T)
\]
whose restrictions to $A$ agree.  The tensor-product universal property therefore gives a unique
ring map
\[
\gamma:B\otimes_AC\to\Gamma(T,\OO_T),
\qquad
b\otimes c\mapsto\alpha(b)\beta(c).
\]
Again by VI/19, $\gamma$ gives a unique morphism $w:T\to P$.

By construction $p_Bw=u$ and $p_Cw=v$.  If $w'$ is another such morphism, its map on global
sections restricts to the same $\alpha$ and $\beta$, so the tensor-product universal property
forces the same $\gamma$; hence $w'=w$.  Therefore $P$ satisfies the full scheme-theoretic
fiber-product universal property.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The categorical pullback of affine schemes became the categorical pushout of rings, namely a tensor product.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Apply the formula to quotients, localizations, diagonals, and fibers.

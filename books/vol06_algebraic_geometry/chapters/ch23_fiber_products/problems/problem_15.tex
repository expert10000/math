\subsection*{VI.23.P15: Construction by affine gluing}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi23-p15}
Outline a proof that fiber products of arbitrary schemes exist using affine charts.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the existence theorem that upgrades the affine tensor-product construction to the entire category of schemes.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use three levels of locality: affine opens of the base, affine opens of their inverse images, and principal refinements on overlaps.  Let uniqueness provide the cocycle.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Choose affine covers of $S$, then of the inverse images in $X$ and $Y$.
\item Form all local tensor-product spectra.
\item On common principal refinements use localization compatibility.
\item Use uniqueness to prove cocycles, then VI/20 to glue.
\item Verify the global universal property locally on a cover of the test scheme.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Let $f:X\to S$ and $g:Y\to S$.  Choose an affine open cover
$S=\bigcup_iU_i$, with $U_i=\Spec A_i$.  The inverse images $f^{-1}(U_i)$ and $g^{-1}(U_i)$ are
open, so choose affine covers
\[
f^{-1}(U_i)=\bigcup_jV_{ij},\qquad V_{ij}=\Spec B_{ij},
\]
\[
g^{-1}(U_i)=\bigcup_kW_{ik},\qquad W_{ik}=\Spec C_{ik}.
\]
For every triple $(i,j,k)$ form the affine pullback
\[
P_{ijk}=V_{ij}\times_{U_i}W_{ik}
=\Spec(B_{ij}\otimes_{A_i}C_{ik}).
\]

We next compare two such pieces.  Their overlap is covered by opens obtained by simultaneously
shrinking the base and both factors to principal affine opens.  On such a common refinement,
say after inverting $h$ on the base and its images in the factors, the two coordinate
descriptions become
\[
(B_{ij}\otimes_{A_i}C_{ik})_h
\cong
(B_{ij})_h\otimes_{(A_i)_h}(C_{ik})_h.
\]
Thus both restricted pieces satisfy the same affine fiber-product universal property and are
canonically isomorphic.  On triple overlaps, any two composites of these transition isomorphisms
are compatible with the same two projections.  Uniqueness of fiber products forces the composites
to agree, giving the cocycle condition.

VI/20 therefore glues the $P_{ijk}$ to a scheme $P$.  Their local projections glue to morphisms
$p_X:P\to X$ and $p_Y:P\to Y$, and the equality $fp_X=gp_Y$ holds because it holds on the open
cover.

Finally let $T$ carry a compatible pair $a:T\to X$, $b:T\to Y$.  Cover $T$ by opens mapping into
some $V_{ij}$ and $W_{ik}$ over a common $U_i$.  On each such open the affine universal property
gives a unique map to $P_{ijk}$.  On overlaps the maps agree because both have the same two
projections; hence they glue to a global $T\to P$.  The same local uniqueness proves global
uniqueness.  Therefore $P$ is $X\times_SY$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Existence was assembled from affine tensor products, while uniqueness did most of the coherence work needed for gluing.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
This construction is the template for many later objects defined locally by universal properties.

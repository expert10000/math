\subsection*{VI.23.P16: Compatible affine refinements}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi23-p16}
Explain why shrinking $\Spec A$ to $D(f)$ and the factors to the corresponding inverse images
does not change the local fiber-product construction except by localization.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This identity is the algebraic compatibility that makes the global gluing construction possible.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate the requested statement into compatible maps from an arbitrary test scheme, and use uniqueness before choosing coordinates.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Identify the principal open in the product.
\item Compute the product after localizing all three rings.
\item Use the universal property of localization to construct inverse maps.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Let $\varphi:A\to B$ and $\psi:A\to C$.  Before shrinking, the local product ring is
$D=B\otimes_AC$.  The inverse image of $D(f)\subseteq\Spec A$ in the product is the principal
open defined by the common image of $f$ in $D$, so its ring is $D_f$.

On the other hand, shrinking the factors first gives
\[
\Spec B_{\varphi(f)}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\Spec C_{\psi(f)}
\]
over $\Spec A_f$, whose product has ring
\[
B_{\varphi(f)}\otimes_{A_f}C_{\psi(f)}.
\]
There is a canonical map
\[
(B\otimes_AC)_f
\longrightarrow
B_{\varphi(f)}\otimes_{A_f}C_{\psi(f)}
\]
induced by the localized factor maps.  Conversely the maps from $B$ and $C$ into
$(B\otimes_AC)_f$ send $f$ to a unit, so they factor through the two localized rings and produce
the inverse map.  Hence
\[
(B\otimes_AC)_f
\cong
B_{\varphi(f)}\otimes_{A_f}C_{\psi(f)}.
\]

Therefore restricting an affine fiber product to a common principal part is canonically the same
as first restricting all three schemes and then taking their fiber product.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A geometric assertion was reduced to a representability statement, so the canonical map was forced by universality.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Repeat the argument after restricting to affine opens and compare with the tensor-product formula.

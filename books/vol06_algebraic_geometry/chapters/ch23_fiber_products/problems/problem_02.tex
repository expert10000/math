\subsection*{VI.23.P2: The AG7 graph Cartesian square}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi23-p02}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism over $S$. Prove that
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
X \arrow[r,"f"] \arrow[d,"\Gamma_f"'] & Y \arrow[d,"\Delta_{Y/S}"]\\
X\times_SY \arrow[r,"h"'] & Y\times_SY
\end{tikzcd}
\]
is Cartesian.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Graphs are ubiquitous later; this proof shows that a graph is not an ad hoc ordered pair but a pullback of a diagonal.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
A map to the product has two components.  Pulling back the diagonal says that the two $Y$-components become equal.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Write the map $h$ by its two components.
\item Use the diagonal factorization criterion from Exercise VI.23.16.
\item Show the remaining datum is a single map $Q\to X$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Write $h:X\times_SY\to Y\times_SY$ for the map whose two components are
$f\circ p_X$ and $p_Y$.  These are compatible over $S$, so $h$ exists uniquely.

Let $P$ be the pullback of $h$ along $\Delta_{Y/S}$.  For a test scheme $Q$, a map
$Q\to X\times_SY$ is a compatible pair $(a,b)$ with $a:Q\to X$, $b:Q\to Y$.  To lift this map to
$P$ is to require that
\[
(fa,b):Q\to Y\times_SY
\]
factor through $\Delta_{Y/S}$.  By the defining property of the diagonal, this happens exactly
when
\[
b=fa.
\]
Once this equality holds, the map to the upper-right copy of $Y$ is forced.

Consequently a $Q$-point of $P$ is exactly a morphism $a:Q\to X$, and its induced map to
$X\times_SY$ is
\[
(a,fa)=\Gamma_f\circ a.
\]
Hence $\Hom(Q,P)\cong\Hom(Q,X)$ naturally in $Q$, so $P\cong X$.  The resulting identification
is exactly the displayed Cartesian square.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The graph was characterized as the locus where the free $Y$-coordinate equals $f$ of the $X$-coordinate.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
When separatedness is introduced later, combine this square with properties of the diagonal to study graphs.

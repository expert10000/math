\subsection*{VI.23.P11: Pullback of a closed immersion in the affine case}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi23-p11}
For $A\to B$ and $I\subseteq A$, compute
\[
\Spec B\times_{\Spec A}\Spec(A/I).
\]
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
The computation tracks equations through a pullback: an ideal $I$ becomes its extension $IB$.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate the requested statement into compatible maps from an arbitrary test scheme, and use uniqueness before choosing coordinates.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Compute $B\otimes_AA/I$.
\item Show it is $B/IB$.
\item Interpret the projection on spectra.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
The affine theorem gives
\[
\Spec B\times_{\Spec A}\Spec(A/I)
\cong\Spec(B\otimes_AA/I).
\]
Tensor the quotient map $A\to A/I$ with $B$.  The resulting map
\[
B\to B\otimes_AA/I,\qquad b\mapsto b\otimes1
\]
kills every element of the extended ideal $IB$.  Therefore it factors through $B/IB$.
Conversely multiplication
\[
B\times A/I\to B/IB,\qquad (b,\bar a)\mapsto b\varphi(a)\bmod IB
\]
is balanced and induces $B\otimes_AA/I\to B/IB$.  These maps are inverse.

Thus the product is
\[
\Spec(B/IB),
\]
and its projection to $\Spec B$ is the closed immersion induced by the quotient
$B\twoheadrightarrow B/IB$.  This is the affine computation underlying preservation of closed
immersions under base change, a theorem formulated systematically in VI/24.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A geometric assertion was reduced to a representability statement, so the canonical map was forced by universality.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Repeat the argument after restricting to affine opens and compare with the tensor-product formula.

\subsection*{VI.23.P12: The affine diagonal}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi23-p12}
Let $A\to B$. Identify the ring map corresponding to
$\Delta:\Spec B\to\Spec B\times_{\Spec A}\Spec B$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the coordinate formula behind the geometric diagonal and later separatedness criteria.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate the requested statement into compatible maps from an arbitrary test scheme, and use uniqueness before choosing coordinates.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Write the product ring.
\item Use the two identity components of the diagonal.
\item Invoke uniqueness of the tensor-product map.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
The product is $\Spec(B\otimes_AB)$.  Let
\[
i_1:B\to B\otimes_AB,\quad b\mapsto b\otimes1,
\qquad
i_2:B\to B\otimes_AB,\quad b\mapsto1\otimes b.
\]
The two composites of the diagonal with the projections are both $\id_{\Spec B}$.  Reversing
arrows, the ring map $\Delta^\#:B\otimes_AB\to B$ must satisfy
\[
\Delta^\#i_1=\id_B=\Delta^\#i_2.
\]
The tensor-product universal property says there is a unique $A$-algebra map with these
restrictions, namely
\[
\mu(b_1\otimes b_2)=b_1b_2.
\]
Thus $\Delta=\Spec(\mu)$.  Notice that $\mu$ is always surjective, a fact that later interacts
with the theory of separated morphisms.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A geometric assertion was reduced to a representability statement, so the canonical map was forced by universality.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Repeat the argument after restricting to affine opens and compare with the tensor-product formula.

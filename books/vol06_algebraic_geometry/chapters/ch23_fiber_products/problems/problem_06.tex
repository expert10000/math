\subsection*{VI.23.P6: Fiber product with the base}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi23-p06}
Show $X\times_SS\cong X$ when the second map is $\operatorname{id}_S$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the identity law for pullbacks and is repeatedly used to simplify diagrams.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate the requested statement into compatible maps from an arbitrary test scheme, and use uniqueness before choosing coordinates.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Construct $X\to X\times_SS$ from $(\id_X,f)$.
\item One composite is immediate.
\item Compare the two projections of the other composite.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Let $f:X\to S$.  Define
\[
\eta:X\to X\times_SS
\]
by the compatible pair $(\id_X,f)$.  Let $p_X:X\times_SS\to X$ be the first projection.
Then $p_X\eta=\id_X$.

For the other composite, the two components of $\eta p_X$ are
\[
p_X,\qquad f p_X.
\]
But the defining commutative square for $X\times_SS$ says that the second projection
$p_S$ is exactly $f p_X$.  Therefore $\eta p_X$ and $\id_{X\times_SS}$ have the same two
projections.  Uniqueness in the fiber-product universal property yields
$\eta p_X=\id_{X\times_SS}$.  Thus $p_X$ is the canonical isomorphism with inverse $\eta$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A geometric assertion was reduced to a representability statement, so the canonical map was forced by universality.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Repeat the argument after restricting to affine opens and compare with the tensor-product formula.

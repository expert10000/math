\subsection*{VI.23.P19: Functor of points}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:vi23-p19}
Prove that for every scheme $T$,
\[
(X\times_SY)(T)\cong X(T)\times_{S(T)}Y(T).
\]
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the most compact functorial expression of the fiber-product universal property.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate the requested statement into compatible maps from an arbitrary test scheme, and use uniqueness before choosing coordinates.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Expand the notation $X(T)$.
\item Identify the set-theoretic fiber product of Hom-sets.
\item Check compatibility with precomposition.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
By definition
\[
X(T)=\Hom(T,X),\qquad Y(T)=\Hom(T,Y),\qquad S(T)=\Hom(T,S).
\]
The universal property of $P=X\times_SY$ says exactly that composition with the two projections
induces a bijection
\[
\Hom(T,P)
\longrightarrow
\{(a,b)\in\Hom(T,X)\times\Hom(T,Y):fa=gb\}.
\]
The set on the right is the set-theoretic fiber product
$X(T)\times_{S(T)}Y(T)$.

To check naturality, let $r:T'\to T$.  A $T$-point $u$ is sent to $u r$.  Under the displayed
bijection this becomes
\[
(p_Xu\,r,\;p_Yu\,r),
\]
which is obtained by precomposing both components of the corresponding compatible pair with $r$.
Thus the bijection commutes with every change of test scheme.  Hence it is an isomorphism of
functors of points, not merely a separate bijection for each $T$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A geometric assertion was reduced to a representability statement, so the canonical map was forced by universality.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Repeat the argument after restricting to affine opens and compare with the tensor-product formula.

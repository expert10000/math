% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-01
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E1.}
The whole space \(\mathbb A^2_k\) is itself an irreducible component of \(\mathbb A^2_k\).  There is no strict
irreducible closed enlargement of it inside itself.  Hence
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{codim}(\mathbb A^2_k,\mathbb A^2_k)=0}.
\]

% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-02
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E2.}
Write
\[
\mathbb A^3_k=\Spec k[x,y,z].
\]
The closed subset \(V(x)\) corresponds to the prime ideal \((x)\).  The chain
\[
(0)\subsetneq(x)
\]
shows \(\operatorname{ht}(x)\ge1\), while the principal ideal theorem gives
\(\operatorname{ht}(x)\le1\).  Therefore
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{codim}(V(x),\mathbb A^3_k)=1}.
\]

% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-03
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E3.}
The closed subset \(V(x,y)\subseteq\mathbb A^3_k\) corresponds to the prime \((x,y)\).  We have
\[
(0)\subsetneq(x)\subsetneq(x,y),
\]
so its height is at least \(2\).  On the other hand
\[
k[x,y,z]/(x,y)\cong k[z]
\]
has dimension \(1\), while \(\dim k[x,y,z]=3\).  The polynomial-ring dimension formula gives height \(2\).  Hence
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{codim}(V(x,y),\mathbb A^3_k)=2}.
\]

% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-04
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E4.}
The origin corresponds to the maximal ideal
\[
\mathfrak m=(x,y,z).
\]
The coordinate chain
\[
(0)\subsetneq(x)\subsetneq(x,y)\subsetneq(x,y,z)
\]
has length \(3\).  Since \(\dim k[x,y,z]=3\), the height cannot be larger.  Therefore
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{codim}(\{0\},\mathbb A^3_k)=3}.
\]

% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-05
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E5.}
An irreducible component is maximal among irreducible closed subsets.  Thus if \(Y\) is an irreducible component
of \(X\), there is no strict chain
\[
Y\subsetneq Z
\]
with \(Z\) irreducible and closed in \(X\).  Hence
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{codim}(Y,X)=0}.
\]

% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-06
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E6.}
For \(A=k[x,y]\) and \(\mathfrak p=(x)\),
\[
\dim A_{\mathfrak p}=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p).
\]
The prime \((x)\) has height \(1\), since
\[
(0)\subsetneq(x)
\]
and the principal ideal theorem gives the matching upper bound.  Therefore
\[
\boxed{\dim A_{(x)}=1}.
\]

% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-07
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E7.}
For \(\mathfrak m=(x,y)\subset k[x,y]\),
\[
\dim A_{\mathfrak m}=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak m).
\]
The chain
\[
(0)\subsetneq(x)\subsetneq(x,y)
\]
has length \(2\), and \(\dim k[x,y]=2\).  Hence
\[
\boxed{\dim k[x,y]_{(x,y)}=2}.
\]

% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-08
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E8.}
Let \(\eta=\eta_Y\) be the generic point of the irreducible closed subset \(Y\).  The generic-point local-ring
formula gives
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{codim}(Y,X)=\dim\mathcal O_{X,\eta}}.
\]
Affinely, if \(\eta\leftrightarrow\mathfrak p\) in \(U=\Spec A\), both sides equal
\(\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)\).

% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-09
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E9.}
Every \(y\in Y\) is a specialization of \(\eta_Y\).  In an affine neighborhood of \(y\), this corresponds to an
inclusion of primes
\[
\mathfrak p_{\eta_Y}\subseteq\mathfrak p_y.
\]
Every prime chain ending at \(\mathfrak p_{\eta_Y}\) is therefore also a chain below \(\mathfrak p_y\).  Hence
\[
\dim\mathcal O_{X,y}\ge\dim\mathcal O_{X,\eta_Y}.
\]
Equality occurs at \(y=\eta_Y\), so the infimum is attained there.

% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-10
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E10.}
For an irreducible closed subvariety \(Y\subseteq X\),
\[
\dim Y+\operatorname{codim}(Y,X)=\dim X.
\]
Thus
\[
2+\operatorname{codim}(Y,X)=5,
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{codim}(Y,X)=3}.
\]

% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-11
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E11.}
If \(Y\subseteq X\) has codimension \(1\) and \(\dim X=7\), the variety additivity theorem gives
\[
\dim Y+1=7.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{\dim Y=6}.
\]

% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-12
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E12.}
A closed point has dimension \(0\).  If it lies on a curve variety \(X\), then \(\dim X=1\).  Therefore
\[
0+\operatorname{codim}(\{x\},X)=1,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{codim}(\{x\},X)=1}.
\]

0+\operatorname{codim}(\{x\},X)=4.

Therefore

\boxed{\operatorname{codim}(\{x\},X)=4}.

% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-14
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E14.}
The ambient product
\[
\mathbb A^2_k\times_k\mathbb A^2_k\cong\mathbb A^4_k
\]
has dimension \(4\).  The diagonal is isomorphic to \(\mathbb A^2_k\), hence has dimension \(2\).  Thus
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{codim}(\Delta,\mathbb A^2\times\mathbb A^2)=2}.
\]
Equivalently its prime ideal is \((x_1-y_1,x_2-y_2)\), of height \(2\).

% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-15
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E15.}
The coordinate subspace
\[
V(x_1,x_2,x_3)\subseteq\mathbb A^6_k
\]
has coordinate ring
\[
k[x_4,x_5,x_6],
\]
so it has dimension \(3\).  Since the ambient dimension is \(6\),
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{codim}=6-3=3}.
\]

\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E17.}
No, not for a genuine nonzero principal hypersurface in affine space.  Let \(A=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\) and let
\(0\ne f\in A\) be a nonunit.  Every irreducible component of \(V(f)\) corresponds to a prime minimal over

% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-18
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E18.}
In
\[
X=\Spec k\sqcup\mathbb A^1_k,
\]
the point component \(Y=\Spec k\) is an irreducible component of \(X\).  Hence
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{codim}(Y,X)=0}.
\]

% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-19
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E19.}
For the point component \(Y\),
\[
\dim Y=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\operatorname{codim}(Y,X)=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\dim Y+\operatorname{codim}(Y,X)=0},
\]
whereas \(\dim X=1\).  This is the AG8-P3c failure of dimension--codimension additivity.

% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-20
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E20.}
Assume
\[
Z\subseteq Y\subseteq X
\]
are irreducible closed subvarieties with
\[
\operatorname{codim}(Z,Y)=2,
\qquad
\operatorname{codim}(Y,X)=3.
\]
Codimension is additive along nested subvarieties of a variety, so
\[
\operatorname{codim}(Z,X)=2+3=\boxed{5}.
\]

\[

% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-22
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E22.}
The closed subset
\[
W=V(x)\cup V(y,z)\subseteq\mathbb A^3_k
\]
has two irreducible components.  The plane \(V(x)\) has codimension \(1\), while the line \(V(y,z)\) has
codimension \(2\).  By definition for a reducible closed subset,
\[
\operatorname{codim}(W,\mathbb A^3_k)
=
\inf\{1,2\}
=
\boxed{1}.
\]

% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-23
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E23.}
An irreducible closed subset \(Y\subseteq X\) has codimension zero exactly when no strictly larger irreducible
closed subset contains it.  This is exactly maximality among irreducible closed subsets.  Hence
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{codim}(Y,X)=0\iff Y\text{ is an irreducible component of }X}.
\]

% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-24
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E24.}
Codimension-one irreducible closed subsets are the geometric supports from which prime Weil divisors are later
built, under the hypotheses of the divisor chapters.  VI/31 records only the codimension-one geometry.  The
valuation order, principal divisors, divisor groups, Cartier divisors, and divisor class groups are deferred to
the later divisor arc.

% VI31-DETAILED-EXERCISE-26
\paragraph{Solution VI.31.E26.}
The invariant
\[
\dim X
\]
is absolute: it measures maximal chains of irreducible closed subsets anywhere in \(X\).  By contrast,
\[
\operatorname{codim}(Y,X)
\]
is relative to the embedding \(Y\subseteq X\): it measures chains beginning at \(Y\) and moving upward through
irreducible closed subsets of \(X\).  The same abstract \(Y\) can therefore have different codimensions in
different ambient schemes.

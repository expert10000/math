\begin{problem}[VI.31.P2: AG8 Problem 3(c): failure of dimension--codimension additivity]\label{prob:vi31-02}
Let \(X=\Spec k\sqcup\mathbb A^1_k\) and let \(Y=\Spec k\) be the zero-dimensional component. Show that
\[
\dim Y+\operatorname{codim}(Y,X)\neq\dim X.
\]

\textbf{Provenance.} Legacy: AG8-P3c, the exact part-(c) counterexample split from AG8 Problem 3. Part (d) is VI.30.P2; part (a) remains reserved for VI/32.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
The subset \(Y=\Spec k\) is one of the two open-and-closed irreducible components of \(X\). Since an
irreducible component is maximal among irreducible closed subsets,
\[
\operatorname{codim}(Y,X)=0.
\]
Also
\[
\dim Y=0.
\]
The dimension of a disjoint union is the maximum of the component dimensions, so
\[
\dim X
=
\max\{\dim\Spec k,\dim\mathbb A^1_k\}
=
\max\{0,1\}
=
1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\dim Y+\operatorname{codim}(Y,X)=0+0=0\ne1=\dim X.
\]
Thus the variety additivity theorem is not a formal identity for arbitrary schemes. The obstruction here is
already the presence of irreducible components of different dimensions.
\end{solution}
\fi

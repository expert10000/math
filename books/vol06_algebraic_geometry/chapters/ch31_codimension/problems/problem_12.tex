\begin{problem}[VI.31.P12: Codimension zero]\label{prob:vi31-12}
Prove that an irreducible closed subset \(Y\subseteq X\) has codimension zero exactly when it is an irreducible component of \(X\).
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
By definition,
\[
\operatorname{codim}(Y,X)
\]
is the supremum of lengths of chains
\[
Y=Z_n\subsetneq Z_{n-1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq Z_0
\]
of irreducible closed subsets of \(X\).

If the codimension is zero, then no chain of positive length begins at \(Y\). In particular there is no
irreducible closed subset \(Z\) with
\[
Y\subsetneq Z.
\]
Thus \(Y\) is maximal among irreducible closed subsets, which is exactly the definition of an irreducible
component.

Conversely, if \(Y\) is an irreducible component, maximality rules out every strict irreducible enlargement.
Hence every admissible chain has length zero and
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{codim}(Y,X)=0}.
\]
\end{solution}
\fi

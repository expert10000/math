\begin{problem}[VI.31.P19: Why equations do not equal codimension]\label{prob:vi31-19}
Give examples showing that the number of generators of an ideal can exceed the codimension of its zero set.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
A basic example is
\[
I=(x,xy,xz)\subseteq k[x,y,z].
\]
Since \(xy,xz\in(x)\),
\[
I=(x).
\]
Thus
\[
V(I)=V(x),
\]
a plane in \(\mathbb A^3\) of codimension \(1\), although the displayed generating list contains three equations.

Even when the chosen generators are not literally repetitions, the geometric zero set depends only on the
radical:
\[
V(I)=V(\sqrt I).
\]
Hence redundant generators, algebraic consequences of other equations, and non-minimal presentations can make
the number of equations much larger than codimension. Codimension is controlled by the heights of the minimal
primes of \(\sqrt I\), not by the cardinality of a chosen generating set.
\end{solution}
\fi

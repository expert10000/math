\begin{problem}[VI.31.P19: Why equations do not equal codimension]\label{prob:vi31-19}
Give examples showing that the number of generators of an ideal can exceed the codimension of its zero set.
\end{problem}
\begin{solution}
For $(x,xy,xz)\subset k[x,y,z]$, the radical is $(x)$, so the zero set has codimension one despite three generators. Redundant generators and non-complete-intersection ideals prevent equation counting from being a definition.
\end{solution}

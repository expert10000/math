\begin{problem}[VI.31.P8: Redundant equations]\label{prob:vi31-08}
Compute the codimension of \(V(x,xy)\subseteq\mathbb A^2_k\) and explain why counting equations fails.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
The ideal generated by the two displayed equations is
\[
(x,xy)=(x),
\]
because \(xy\in(x)\). Hence
\[
V(x,xy)=V(x).
\]
The latter is a coordinate line in \(\mathbb A^2_k\), so
\[
\operatorname{codim}(V(x,xy),\mathbb A^2_k)=1.
\]

Thus two equations have produced codimension one. The reason is that the second equation contributes no new
geometric condition. More generally, codimension is determined by chains of prime ideals, or equivalently by
the heights of the relevant minimal primes, not by the size of a chosen generating set.
\end{solution}
\fi

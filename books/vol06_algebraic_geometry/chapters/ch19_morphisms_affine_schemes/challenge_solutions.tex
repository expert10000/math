\paragraph{Challenge VI.19.C1 -- The affine anti-equivalence as an equivalence of categories.}
\begin{solution}
Define
\[
\Spec:(\mathbf{CRing})^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{AffSch}
\]
by \(A\mapsto\Spec A\) and, for a morphism \(A\to B\) in \(\mathbf{CRing}\), by the affine morphism \(\Spec B\to\Spec A\).  Functoriality is VI.19.P3.  In the opposite direction define
\[
\Gamma:\mathbf{AffSch}\to(\mathbf{CRing})^{\mathrm{op}}
\]
by \(X\mapsto\Gamma(X,\OO_X)\).  If \(f:X\to Y\), the pullback of global functions is a ring map \(\Gamma(Y,\OO_Y)\to\Gamma(X,\OO_X)\), hence a morphism \(\Gamma(X)\to\Gamma(Y)\) in the opposite category.

For a ring \(A\), VI/18 gives the canonical isomorphism
\[
\eta_A:A\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gamma(\Spec A,\OO_{\Spec A}).
\]
This is natural: for \(\varphi:A\to B\), the square with \(\eta_A,\eta_B\) commutes because the global pullback of \(\Spec(\varphi)\) is exactly \(\varphi\).

For an affine scheme \(X\), choose any presentation \(X\cong\Spec A\).  Affine reconstruction gives a canonical morphism
\[
\varepsilon_X:X\longrightarrow\Spec\Gamma(X,\OO_X)
\]
corresponding under VI.19.P1 to the identity homomorphism of \(\Gamma(X,\OO_X)\).  It is an isomorphism because, after identifying \(X\) with \(\Spec A\), its global pullback is the canonical isomorphism \(\Gamma(X)\cong A\).  Naturality follows from uniqueness in the affine Hom correspondence: for \(f:X\to Y\), both routes in the square
\[
X\longrightarrow\Spec\Gamma(X),\qquad
Y\longrightarrow\Spec\Gamma(Y)
\]
have the same pullback on global sections.

Thus \(\eta\) and \(\varepsilon\) are natural isomorphisms, so \(\Spec\) and \(\Gamma\) are quasi-inverse equivalences.  This is precisely the statement that affine schemes form the category opposite to commutative rings.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{Challenge VI.19.C2 -- Endomorphisms of the dual-number point.}
\begin{solution}
Let \(A=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\).  A \(k\)-algebra endomorphism \(\varphi:A\to A\) is determined by \(\varphi(\varepsilon)\), and this image must square to zero.  Write an arbitrary element as \(a+b\varepsilon\).  Its square is
\[
(a+b\varepsilon)^2=a^2+2ab\varepsilon.
\]
If this is zero, then \(a^2=0\) in the field \(k\), so \(a=0\).  Hence every square-zero element is of the form \(c\varepsilon\), and conversely \((c\varepsilon)^2=0\).  Therefore
\[
\varphi_c(\varepsilon)=c\varepsilon,
\qquad c\in k,
\]
classifies all \(k\)-algebra endomorphisms.

Composition satisfies \(\varphi_c\circ\varphi_d=\varphi_{cd}\).  Thus \(\varphi_c\) is invertible exactly when \(c\in k^\times\), with inverse \(\varphi_{c^{-1}}\).  The case \(c=0\) is the reduction map on the infinitesimal direction and is not an automorphism.

The spectrum of \(A\) has the single prime \((\varepsilon)\), because the nilradical is \((\varepsilon)\) and the quotient is the field \(k\).  Consequently every affine-scheme endomorphism induced by \(\varphi_c\) has the same underlying continuous map: the identity of a one-point space.  They are nevertheless different scheme morphisms whenever the scalars \(c\) differ, because their pullbacks on global sections differ.  This explicitly exhibits the infinitesimal information carried by the structure sheaf.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{Challenge VI.19.C3 -- Image closure and nilpotent kernels.}
\begin{solution}
For \(f=\Spec(\varphi):\Spec B\to\Spec A\), VI.19.P13 gives
\[
\overline{f(\Spec B)}=V(\ker\varphi).
\]
This closed set is all of \(\Spec A\) exactly when every prime of \(A\) contains \(\ker\varphi\).  The intersection of all primes is the nilradical \(\sqrt{(0)_A}\), so density is equivalent to
\[
\ker\varphi\subseteq\sqrt{(0)_A}.
\]

For an injective dense example take \(\mathbb Z\hookrightarrow\mathbb Q\).  The kernel is zero, so the induced map \(\Spec\mathbb Q\to\Spec\mathbb Z\) has dense image.  Its image is the generic point \((0)\), whose closure is all of \(\Spec\mathbb Z\).

For a noninjective dense example take
\[
k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\longrightarrow k,
\qquad \varepsilon\longmapsto0.
\]
Its kernel \((\varepsilon)\) is nilpotent, so the image is dense; indeed both spectra have one point.

For a nondense example take the quotient \(k[x]\to k[x]/(x)\cong k\).  Its kernel \((x)\) is not contained in the nilradical of the reduced ring \(k[x]\).  The image of \(\Spec k\) is the closed point \((x)\), and its closure is \(V(x)\), not all of \(\mathbb A^1_k\).  These examples show that density detects the radical of the kernel, not injectivity itself.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{Challenge VI.19.C4 -- Global functions as maps and equations.}
\begin{solution}
Put\[
R=\mathbb Z[t_1,\ldots,t_n].
\]
The sections \(s_1,\ldots,s_n\in\Gamma(X,\OO_X)\) determine, by the polynomial-ring universal property, a unique ring map
\[
\alpha:R\longrightarrow\Gamma(X,\OO_X),
\qquad t_i\longmapsto s_i.
\]
VI.19.P1 converts \(\alpha\) into a unique locally-ringed-space morphism
\[
f:X\longrightarrow\Spec R.
\]
For a point \(x\), the image prime is
\[
f(x)=\{P\in R:P(s_1,\ldots,s_n)_x\in\mathfrak m_x\}.
\]

Now let \(I\subseteq R\).  By the quotient factorization criterion VI.19.P15, the morphism \(f\) factors through \(\Spec(R/I)\) exactly when
\[
I\subseteq\ker\alpha.
\]
But \(P\in\ker\alpha\) means precisely that the global section obtained by substitution vanishes:
\[
P(s_1,\ldots,s_n)=0\in\Gamma(X,\OO_X).
\]
Therefore the factorization exists exactly when every polynomial relation \(P\in I\) vanishes after substituting the chosen global functions.

When this condition holds, the quotient universal property produces a unique map \(R/I\to\Gamma(X,\OO_X)\), so VI.19.P1 gives a unique factorization through \(\Spec(R/I)\).  This is the affine algebraic form of saying that a tuple of functions lands in the locus cut out by a system of equations.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{Challenge VI.19.C5 -- From affine compatibility to the gluing boundary.}
\begin{solution}
Write \(X=D(f)\cup D(g)\subseteq\Spec A\).  By the sheaf axiom for \(\OO_X\), global sections form the equalizer
\[
\Gamma(X,\OO_X)
\longrightarrow A_f\times A_g
\rightrightarrows A_{fg}.
\]
Suppose the two local morphisms to \(Y=\Spec B\) correspond to ring maps
\[
\alpha_f:B\to A_f,
\qquad
\alpha_g:B\to A_g,
\]
and assume their composites to \(A_{fg}\) agree.  For each \(b\in B\), the pair
\[
(\alpha_f(b),\alpha_g(b))
\]
lies in the equalizer, so there is a unique section \(\alpha(b)\in\Gamma(X,\OO_X)\) restricting to those two elements.  Because addition and multiplication in the equalizer are componentwise, uniqueness shows
\[
\alpha(b+b')=\alpha(b)+\alpha(b'),\qquad
\alpha(bb')=\alpha(b)\alpha(b'),\qquad
\alpha(1)=1.
\]
Hence \(\alpha:B\to\Gamma(X,\OO_X)\) is a unique ring homomorphism producing both local maps.  VI.19.P1 then gives the corresponding unique morphism \(X\to Y\) when \(X\) is regarded as the existing locally ringed space.

What remains for VI/20 is a different and stronger gluing theorem.  There one begins with separate spaces or affine schemes and specified isomorphisms between open pieces satisfying a cocycle condition; one must construct the global topological space, construct and glue the sheaves, verify the locally ringed condition, and prove the resulting universal property.  The present argument only glues morphisms/functions on an already existing space with an already existing sheaf.  Thus it reaches exactly the boundary of, but does not consume, the general scheme-gluing machinery.
\end{solution}

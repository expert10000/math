\paragraph{VI.19.E1. Direction reversal.} The induced map is $\Spec B\to\Spec A$, $\mfp\mapsto\varphi^{-1}(\mfp)$.  Prime ideals live in the target ring of $\varphi$, so contraction sends them back to $A$, reversing arrows.

\paragraph{VI.19.E2. Contraction is prime.} If $ab\in\varphi^{-1}(\mfp)$, then $\varphi(a)\varphi(b)\in\mfp$.  Since $\mfp$ is prime, one factor lies in $\mfp$, so $a$ or $b$ lies in the contraction.  Also $1$ is not in the contraction.

\paragraph{VI.19.E3. Principal-open inverse image.} For $\mfp\in\Spec B$, $\mfp$ lies over $D(a)$ iff $a\notin\varphi^{-1}(\mfp)$ iff $\varphi(a)\notin\mfp$ iff $\mfp\in D(\varphi(a))$.

\paragraph{VI.19.E4. Continuity.} Inverse images of basis opens $D(a)$ are the open sets $D(\varphi(a))$, so the spectrum map is continuous.

\paragraph{VI.19.E5. Localized section map.} It is $r/a^n\mapsto\varphi(r)/\varphi(a)^n$.  It exists because $\varphi(a)$ is invertible in $B_{\varphi(a)}$.

\paragraph{VI.19.E6. Stalk map.} The map is $a/s\mapsto\varphi(a)/\varphi(s)$.  If $s\notin\mfq$, then $\varphi(s)\notin\mfp$, so the denominator is invertible in $B_{\mfp}$.

\paragraph{VI.19.E7. Locality of the stalk map.} The preimage of $\mfp B_{\mfp}$ consists of fractions whose numerator lies in $\mfq$, hence is exactly $\mfq A_{\mfq}$, the maximal ideal of $A_{\mfq}$.

\paragraph{VI.19.E8. Identity functoriality.} Contraction along the identity fixes every prime.  On every principal open the localized map $A_a\to A_a$ is the identity, hence so is the sheaf map.

\paragraph{VI.19.E9. Composition.} For $\mfp\in\Spec C$, $(\psi\varphi)^{-1}(\mfp)=\varphi^{-1}(\psi^{-1}(\mfp))$.  The localized section maps also compose, so the locally-ringed-space morphisms agree.

\paragraph{VI.19.E10. Polynomial projection.} The contraction is $(t-a)$, so $(a,b)$ maps to $a$.

\paragraph{VI.19.E11. Squaring map.} The contraction is $(t-a^2)$, so the point $a$ maps to $a^2$.

\paragraph{VI.19.E12. Localization image.} Primes of $A_f$ correspond bijectively by contraction to primes of $A$ not containing $f$, which are exactly the points of $D(f)$.

\paragraph{VI.19.E13. Quotient image.} Primes of $A/I$ correspond bijectively to primes of $A$ containing $I$.  Under contraction this is exactly $V(I)$.

\paragraph{VI.19.E14. Reduction map.} Every prime contains $\sqrt{(0)}$, so quotienting by it preserves the prime spectrum and Zariski topology.  But $A\to A_{\mathrm{red}}$ need not be an isomorphism, so by affine anti-equivalence the schemes need not be isomorphic.

\paragraph{VI.19.E15. Maps to affine line.} Every map is determined by $b=\varphi(t)$, and each $b\in B$ defines the evaluation homomorphism $p(t)\mapsto p(b)$.  Hence maps $\Spec B\to\Spec\mathbb Z[t]$ correspond to global functions in $B$.

\paragraph{VI.19.E16. Maps to affine $n$-space.} A map is determined by the images $b_i$ of the variables, and every tuple gives a unique map.  Contravariantly this gives morphisms $\Spec B\to\A^n_k$.

\paragraph{VI.19.E17. Isomorphism criterion.} A ring inverse induces a scheme inverse by contravariance.  Conversely a scheme inverse induces an inverse ring map on global sections.  Thus the two notions coincide for affine schemes.

\paragraph{VI.19.E18. Residue fields.} The local map $A_{\mfq}\to B_{\mfp}$ sends $\mfq A_{\mfq}$ into $\mfp B_{\mfp}$ and is local, hence descends to a unital map of residue fields $\kappa(\mfq)\to\kappa(\mfp)$.

\paragraph{VI.19.E19. Dense image criterion.} The intersection of the contracted primes is $\varphi^{-1}(\sqrt{(0)_B})=\sqrt{\ker\varphi}$.  Therefore the smallest closed set containing the image is $V(\sqrt{\ker\varphi})=V(\ker\varphi)$.

\paragraph{VI.19.E20. Dense but noninjective.} Take $k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\to k$ with $\varepsilon\mapsto0$.  Its kernel $(\varepsilon)$ is nilpotent, so $V(\ker)=\Spec A$ and the one-point image is dense, while the ring map is not injective.

\paragraph{VI.19.E21. Factor through a quotient.} A factorization $\Spec B\to\Spec(A/I)\to\Spec A$ corresponds to a factorization $A\to A/I\to B$, which exists exactly when $I$ maps to zero, i.e. $I\subseteq\ker\alpha$.

\paragraph{VI.19.E22. Factor through a principal open.} Contravariantly a factorization through $\Spec A_f$ is a factorization of $\alpha$ through $A_f$.  Such a ring map exists uniquely exactly when $\alpha(f)$ is invertible in $B$.

\paragraph{VI.19.E23. Same continuous map, different scheme maps.} The ring endomorphisms $\varepsilon\mapsto\varepsilon$ and $\varepsilon\mapsto0$ are distinct.  The spectrum has only one point, so both induce the identity continuous map, but their sheaf maps differ on global sections.

\paragraph{VI.19.E24. Recover a morphism from global sections.} Both morphisms correspond under the bijection $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{AffSch}}(\Spec B,\Spec A)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Ring}}(A,B)$ to the same ring homomorphism, hence they are equal.

\paragraph{VI.19.E25. Universal property for a locally ringed source.} The ring map $\mathbb Z[t]\to\Gamma(X,\OO_X)$ sends $t$ to $s$. VI.19.P1 gives the unique morphism. At $x$, its image prime is $\{p(t):p(s)_x\in\mfm_x\}$, and the pullback on a principal open is obtained by localizing wherever the corresponding polynomial in $s$ is a unit.

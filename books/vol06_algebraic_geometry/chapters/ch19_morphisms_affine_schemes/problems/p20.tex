\begin{problem}[Naturality of the universal property]\label{prob:vi19-p20}
Show the bijection of VI.19.P1 is natural in both $X$ and $A$: precomposition by a locally-ringed-space morphism corresponds to pulling global sections forward along it, and a ring map $A\to A'$ corresponds to postcomposition with $\Spec A'\to\Spec A$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Naturality is what turns a useful bijection into a structural universal property.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Write both sides using pullbacks of global sections and use functoriality.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item For $g:X'\to X$, compare $f\circ g$ with $A\to\Gamma(X)\to\Gamma(X')$.
\item For $u:A\to A'$, compare $X\to\Spec A'\to\Spec A$ with $A\to A'\to\Gamma(X)$.
\item Use uniqueness from VI.19.P1.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
% VI19 full-solutions refinement v1
Write the universal-property bijection as
\[
\Phi_{X,A}:\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{LRS}}(X,\Spec A)
\xrightarrow{\sim}
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Ring}}(A,\Gamma(X,\OO_X)).
\]
First check naturality in the source.  Let \(g:X'\to X\) and let \(f:X\to\Spec A\) correspond to \(\alpha:A\to\Gamma(X,\OO_X)\).  The composite \(f\circ g\) has pullback on global sections
\[
A\xrightarrow{\alpha}\Gamma(X,\OO_X)
\xrightarrow{g^\sharp}\Gamma(X',\OO_{X'}).
\]
Thus precomposition by \(g\) on the geometric Hom-set corresponds exactly to postcomposition with the global pullback \(g^\sharp\) on the ring Hom-set.

Now check naturality in the ring.  Let \(u:A\to A'\), so contravariance gives \(\Spec A'\to\Spec A\).  If \(h:X\to\Spec A'\) corresponds to \(\beta:A'\to\Gamma(X,\OO_X)\), then the composite
\[
X\xrightarrow{h}\Spec A'\xrightarrow{\Spec u}\Spec A
\]
has global pullback
\[
A\xrightarrow{u}A'\xrightarrow{\beta}\Gamma(X,\OO_X).
\]
Hence postcomposition geometrically corresponds to precomposition by \(u\) algebraically.

These calculations prove the two naturality squares commute.  Equivalently, the universal property identifies the Hom functors themselves, not just isolated Hom-sets.  Uniqueness in VI.19.P1 ensures that equality of the global pullbacks forces equality of the resulting locally-ringed-space morphisms.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The universal property respects every relevant composition, so it identifies whole Hom functors rather than isolated sets.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Use naturality to recover the affine anti-equivalence categorically.

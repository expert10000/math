\begin{problem}[Quotients and their underlying closed images]\label{prob:vi19-p08}
Let $I\subseteq A$. Prove that $A\to A/I$ induces an injective spectrum map with image $V(I)$ and describe the map on local rings at a prime containing $I$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the morphism-level precursor to closed subschemes without prematurely consuming VI/22.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the correspondence between primes of $A/I$ and primes of $A$ containing $I$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Write a prime of $A/I$ as $\mfp/I$.
\item Contract it to $A$.
\item Localize the quotient map at $\mfp$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
% VI19 full-solutions refinement v1
Let \(\pi:A\to A/I\) be the quotient map.  Every prime of \(A/I\) is uniquely of the form \(\mathfrak p/I\) with \(\mathfrak p\supseteq I\), and contraction sends \(\mathfrak p/I\) back to \(\mathfrak p\).  Thus
\[
\Spec(A/I)\longrightarrow\Spec A
\]
is injective with image \(V(I)\).  Closed subsets of \(\Spec(A/I)\) are \(V(J/I)\) for ideals \(J\supseteq I\), and these correspond to \(V(J)\subseteq V(I)\), so the map is a homeomorphism onto \(V(I)\) with its subspace topology.

Fix \(\mathfrak p\supseteq I\).  The stalk of the target at \(\mathfrak p\) is \(A_{\mathfrak p}\), while the source stalk at \(\mathfrak p/I\) is
\[
(A/I)_{\mathfrak p/I}.
\]
Localization commutes with quotient, so there is a canonical isomorphism
\[
(A/I)_{\mathfrak p/I}\cong A_{\mathfrak p}/IA_{\mathfrak p}.
\]
The stalk map is therefore the quotient homomorphism
\[
A_{\mathfrak p}\twoheadrightarrow A_{\mathfrak p}/IA_{\mathfrak p}.
\]
It is local, since the maximal ideal maps onto \(\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}/IA_{\mathfrak p}\).  This completely describes the affine morphism, while the closed-subscheme terminology is left for VI/22.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Quotienting functions restricts the underlying spectrum to primes containing the quotient ideal and simultaneously changes the local rings.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Study the reduction quotient $I=\sqrt{(0)}$.

\begin{problem}[Morphisms preserve specialization]\label{prob:vi19-p18}
Let $f=\Spec(\varphi):\Spec B\to\Spec A$. Prove that if $\mfp\subseteq\mfp'$ in $B$, then $f(\mfp)\subseteq f(\mfp')$ in $A$, and interpret this as preservation of specialization order.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This connects ring-theoretic contraction with the topology of generic and closed points.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Contraction preserves inclusion.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Apply $\varphi^{-1}$ to the inclusion of primes.
\item Recall that $\mfp' $ lies in the closure of $\{\mfp\}$ exactly when $\mfp\subseteq\mfp'$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
% VI19 full-solutions refinement v1
Let \(\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak p'\) be prime ideals of \(B\).  Inverse images preserve inclusions, so
\[
\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak p)
\subseteq
\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak p').
\]
Since \(f=\Spec(\varphi)\), this is precisely
\[
f(\mathfrak p)\subseteq f(\mathfrak p').
\]

To interpret this topologically, recall that in \(\Spec B\) the closure of the point \(\{\mathfrak p\}\) is
\[
\overline{\{\mathfrak p\}}=V(\mathfrak p)
=\{\mathfrak r:\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak r\}.
\]
Thus \(\mathfrak p'\) is a specialization of \(\mathfrak p\) exactly when \(\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak p'\).  The inclusion just proved shows that \(f(\mathfrak p')\) is then a specialization of \(f(\mathfrak p)\).  Hence affine morphisms preserve the specialization preorder.  This is consistent with the general topological fact that every continuous map sends the closure of a point into the closure of its image, but here the statement is seen directly as monotonicity of contraction of primes.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The specialization order on spectra is directly compatible with contraction of primes.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Apply this to generic points under dominant affine morphisms.

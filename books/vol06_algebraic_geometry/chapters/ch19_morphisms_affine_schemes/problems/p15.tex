\begin{problem}[Factorization through a quotient]\label{prob:vi19-p15}
Let $\alpha:A\to B$ and $I\subseteq A$. Prove the associated morphism $\Spec B\to\Spec A$ factors through $\Spec(A/I)$ if and only if $I\subseteq\ker\alpha$, and prove uniqueness of the factorization.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the affine algebraic factorization test underlying later closed-subscheme theory.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate the diagram by the affine Hom correspondence.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A geometric factorization reverses to $A\to A/I\to B$.
\item Use the quotient universal property.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
% VI19 full-solutions refinement v1
Let \(f:\Spec B\to\Spec A\) correspond to \(\alpha:A\to B\).  Suppose first that \(f\) factors as
\[
\Spec B\xrightarrow{h}\Spec(A/I)\xrightarrow{j}\Spec A.
\]
Under affine anti-equivalence this diagram reverses to a factorization
\[
A\xrightarrow{\pi}A/I\xrightarrow{\beta}B
\]
with \(\alpha=\beta\circ\pi\).  Every element of \(I=\ker\pi\) is therefore killed by \(\alpha\), so \(I\subseteq\ker\alpha\).

Conversely, assume \(I\subseteq\ker\alpha\).  The quotient universal property gives a unique ring homomorphism
\[
\overline\alpha:A/I\to B,
\qquad a+I\longmapsto\alpha(a),
\]
with \(\alpha=\overline\alpha\circ\pi\).  Applying \(\Spec\) gives the required factorization
\[
\Spec B\xrightarrow{\Spec(\overline\alpha)}\Spec(A/I)
\xrightarrow{\Spec(\pi)}\Spec A.
\]
Uniqueness of \(\overline\alpha\) implies uniqueness of the affine morphism by the Hom correspondence.  Hence the factorization exists uniquely exactly when \(I\subseteq\ker\alpha\).  VI/22 will reinterpret the same algebraic criterion in closed-subscheme language.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Quotient factorization is a purely algebraic condition on pulled-back functions.

\begin{problem}[Factorization through a quotient]\label{prob:vi19-p15}
Let $\alpha:A\to B$ and $I\subseteq A$. Prove the associated morphism $\Spec B\to\Spec A$ factors through $\Spec(A/I)$ if and only if $I\subseteq\ker\alpha$, and prove uniqueness of the factorization.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the affine algebraic factorization test underlying later closed-subscheme theory.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate the diagram by the affine Hom correspondence.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A geometric factorization reverses to $A\to A/I\to B$.
\item Use the quotient universal property.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
A factorization
\[
\Spec B\longrightarrow\Spec(A/I)\longrightarrow\Spec A
\]
corresponds contravariantly to a ring factorization
\[
A\longrightarrow A/I\longrightarrow B.
\]
By the universal property of the quotient, such a factorization exists exactly when $I\subseteq\ker\alpha$, and then the map $A/I\to B$ is uniquely determined.  Affine anti-equivalence therefore gives existence and uniqueness of the geometric factorization.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Quotient factorization is a purely algebraic condition on pulled-back functions.

\begin{problem}[Affine-scheme isomorphisms are ring isomorphisms]\label{prob:vi19-p11}
Prove $\Spec A\cong\Spec B$ as affine schemes if and only if $A\cong B$ as rings, and show a given affine morphism is an isomorphism iff its global pullback is.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This turns geometric isomorphism testing into algebra.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the affine Hom correspondence and global recovery.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A ring inverse gives a reversed scheme inverse.
\item A scheme inverse gives inverse maps on global sections.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
% VI19 full-solutions refinement v1
Suppose first that \(\varphi:A\to B\) is a ring isomorphism, with inverse \(\psi:B\to A\).  Contravariant functoriality gives
\[
\Spec(\psi\circ\varphi)=\Spec(\varphi)\circ\Spec(\psi)=\operatorname{id}_{\Spec A}
\]
and similarly
\[
\Spec(\psi)\circ\Spec(\varphi)=\operatorname{id}_{\Spec B}.
\]
Thus \(\Spec(\varphi):\Spec B\to\Spec A\) is an affine-scheme isomorphism.

Conversely, let \(f:\Spec B\to\Spec A\) be an affine-scheme isomorphism with inverse \(g\).  Taking global sections reverses arrows and gives ring maps
\[
A\xrightarrow{f^\sharp}B,
\qquad
B\xrightarrow{g^\sharp}A.
\]
Functoriality of pullback implies
\[
g^\sharp\circ f^\sharp=\operatorname{id}_A,
\qquad
f^\sharp\circ g^\sharp=\operatorname{id}_B.
\]
Hence \(f^\sharp:A\to B\) is a ring isomorphism.  The same argument shows more generally that a given affine morphism is an isomorphism if and only if its global pullback is an isomorphism.  Therefore \(\Spec A\cong\Spec B\) as affine schemes exactly when \(A\cong B\) as rings.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Affine geometry remembers the entire coordinate ring, including nilpotents, through its sheaf.

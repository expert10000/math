\begin{problem}[Localization as an open affine morphism]\label{prob:vi19-p07}
For $f\in A$, analyze the morphism induced by $A\to A_f$ and prove it identifies $\Spec A_f$ with the open affine subscheme $D(f)$ from VI/18.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This reconnects the localization theorem of VI/18 with the new morphism language.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the prime correspondence and compare the sheaf map with VI.18.P1.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Find the image on points.
\item Check it is a homeomorphism onto $D(f)$.
\item Compare localized section maps.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
The map sends $\mfq\in\Spec A_f$ to its contraction in $A$.  Localization gives a bijection from primes of $A_f$ to primes of $A$ avoiding $f$, namely $D(f)$.  On a basic open $D(g/1)$ the section map identifies
\[
A_g\longrightarrow (A_f)_{g/1}
\]
with restriction to $D(fg)$; after identifying the source chart as in VI.18.P1, this is exactly the inclusion morphism $D(f)\hookrightarrow\Spec A$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The geometric inclusion of a principal affine chart is contravariantly the algebraic operation of inverting one element.

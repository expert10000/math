\begin{problem}[Localization as an open affine morphism]\label{prob:vi19-p07}
For $f\in A$, analyze the morphism induced by $A\to A_f$ and prove it identifies $\Spec A_f$ with the open affine subscheme $D(f)$ from VI/18.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This reconnects the localization theorem of VI/18 with the new morphism language.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the prime correspondence and compare the sheaf map with VI.18.P1.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Find the image on points.
\item Check it is a homeomorphism onto $D(f)$.
\item Compare localized section maps.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
% VI19 full-solutions refinement v1
Consider the localization homomorphism \(\lambda:A\to A_f\).  Prime correspondence for localization says that contraction defines a bijection
\[
\Spec A_f\xrightarrow{\sim}\{\mathfrak p\in\Spec A:f\notin\mathfrak p\}=D(f),
\]
with inverse \(\mathfrak p\mapsto\mathfrak pA_f\).  The correspondence is a homeomorphism onto \(D(f)\), because a basic open \(D(g/1)\subseteq\Spec A_f\) maps to
\[
D(g)\cap D(f)=D(fg).
\]

Now compare the sheaf maps.  On \(D(g)\subseteq\Spec A\), the affine morphism induced by \(\lambda\) pulls sections back by
\[
A_g\longrightarrow(A_f)_{g/1}.
\]
Localization is associative, giving \((A_f)_{g/1}\cong A_{fg}\), which is exactly the ring of sections on the corresponding open \(D(fg)\subseteq D(f)\).  These identifications commute with restrictions.  On stalks, for \(\mathfrak p\in D(f)\),
\[
(A_f)_{\mathfrak pA_f}\cong A_{\mathfrak p}.
\]
Hence the morphism \(\Spec A_f\to\Spec A\) is precisely the inclusion of the open affine subscheme \(D(f)\) established in VI.18.P1.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The geometric inclusion of a principal affine chart is contravariantly the algebraic operation of inverting one element.

\begin{problem}[Functoriality of $\Spec$]\label{prob:vi19-p03}
For $A\xrightarrow{\varphi}B\xrightarrow{\psi}C$, prove $\Spec(\psi\circ\varphi)=\Spec(\varphi)\circ\Spec(\psi)$ as morphisms of locally ringed spaces, and prove the identity law.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Without functoriality, the ring--geometry dictionary would not respect composition.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Check first on primes, then on localized section maps.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Contract a prime of $C$ twice.
\item Compare $A_a\to C_{\psi(\varphi(a))}$ in one and two steps.
\item Do the same for identity maps.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
% VI19 full-solutions refinement v1
Let \(A\xrightarrow{\varphi}B\xrightarrow{\psi}C\).  For \(\mathfrak p\in\Spec C\), contraction gives
\[
(\psi\circ\varphi)^{-1}(\mathfrak p)
=\varphi^{-1}(\psi^{-1}(\mathfrak p)).
\]
Thus the point map of \(\Spec(\psi\circ\varphi)\) equals the composite point map \(\Spec(\varphi)\circ\Spec(\psi)\).  This also identifies the inverse image of a principal open \(D(a)\) with \(D(\psi(\varphi(a)))\).

It remains to compare sheaf maps.  On \(D(a)\), the direct pullback is
\[
A_a\to C_{\psi(\varphi(a))},
\qquad
r/a^n\longmapsto
\psi(\varphi(r))/\psi(\varphi(a))^n.
\]
The two-stage pullback first sends \(r/a^n\) to \(\varphi(r)/\varphi(a)^n\in B_{\varphi(a)}\), then applies the localization of \(\psi\); the result is exactly the same fraction in \(C_{\psi(\varphi(a))}\).  Since principal opens form a basis, the sheaf morphisms coincide.  Passing to stalks gives the same equality there as well.

For \(\operatorname{id}_A\), contraction fixes every prime, each localized map \(A_a\to A_a\) is the identity, and hence every stalk map is the identity.  Therefore \(\Spec\) satisfies both composition and identity laws and is a contravariant functor.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Composition of geometric maps is just composition of algebra maps read backwards.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Use the result to translate a commutative ring diagram into a reversed commutative diagram of affine schemes.

\begin{problem}[Two different affine morphisms with the same point map]\label{prob:vi19-p17}
Let $A=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$. Show that the ring endomorphisms $\varepsilon\mapsto\varepsilon$ and $\varepsilon\mapsto0$ induce the same continuous map $\Spec A\to\Spec A$ but different affine-scheme morphisms.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This isolates why a morphism must include the sheaf pullback, not just a continuous point map.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
The spectrum has one point; compare global pullbacks.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Determine $\Spec A$.
\item Use the affine Hom correspondence to distinguish the morphisms.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
The only prime ideal of $A$ is $(\varepsilon)$, so $\Spec A$ is a one-point space.  Every continuous endomorphism of this space is therefore the identity.  Both ring endomorphisms induce that same point map.  However, the induced maps on global sections are the original ring endomorphisms, one fixing $\varepsilon$ and the other killing it.  Since affine morphisms are determined by their global pullbacks, the two scheme morphisms are distinct.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Infinitesimal directions live in the structure sheaf and can change under a morphism without moving any topological point.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Classify all endomorphisms of the dual-number affine scheme over a field.

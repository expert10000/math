\begin{problem}[Two different affine morphisms with the same point map]\label{prob:vi19-p17}
Let $A=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$. Show that the ring endomorphisms $\varepsilon\mapsto\varepsilon$ and $\varepsilon\mapsto0$ induce the same continuous map $\Spec A\to\Spec A$ but different affine-scheme morphisms.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This isolates why a morphism must include the sheaf pullback, not just a continuous point map.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
The spectrum has one point; compare global pullbacks.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Determine $\Spec A$.
\item Use the affine Hom correspondence to distinguish the morphisms.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
% VI19 full-solutions refinement v1
Let \(A=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\).  Its unique prime ideal is \((\varepsilon)\): every prime contains the nilpotent \(\varepsilon\), and \(A/(\varepsilon)\cong k\) is a field.  Hence \(\Spec A\) is a one-point topological space, and its only continuous self-map is the identity.

Now consider the two \(k\)-algebra endomorphisms
\[
\varphi_1:A\to A,\quad\varepsilon\mapsto\varepsilon,
\qquad
\varphi_0:A\to A,\quad\varepsilon\mapsto0.
\]
Both contract the unique prime \((\varepsilon)\) to itself, so both induced affine-scheme morphisms have the same underlying continuous point map.

They are nevertheless distinct as morphisms of affine schemes.  Their pullbacks on global sections are exactly \(\varphi_1\) and \(\varphi_0\), and these differ on the nonzero nilpotent \(\varepsilon\).  By the affine Hom correspondence, distinct ring maps give distinct affine morphisms.  Equivalently, the two sheaf maps on the unique nonempty open differ.  This example shows explicitly why a scheme morphism must include a morphism of structure sheaves in addition to a continuous map of spaces.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Infinitesimal directions live in the structure sheaf and can change under a morphism without moving any topological point.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Classify all endomorphisms of the dual-number affine scheme over a field.

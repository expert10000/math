\begin{problem}[Projection $\A^2\to\A^1$]\label{prob:vi19-p05}
Let $k[t]\to k[x,y]$ send $t$ to $x$. Describe the induced map of spectra, its action on closed $k$-points, the inverse image of $D(t)$, and the map on the stalk at $(x-a,y-b)$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This connects the abstract spectrum construction to a familiar coordinate projection.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use contraction and the principal-open formula.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Contract $(x-a,y-b)$.
\item Pull back $D(t)$.
\item Localize at the corresponding primes.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
The induced morphism is $f:\Spec k[x,y]\to\Spec k[t]$.  For a closed point $(x-a,y-b)$, contraction consists of polynomials vanishing at $a$, hence is $(t-a)$.  Thus $(a,b)\mapsto a$.  Also
\[
f^{-1}(D(t))=D(x).
\]
At the chosen point the local map is
\[
k[t]_{(t-a)}\longrightarrow k[x,y]_{(x-a,y-b)},\qquad t\longmapsto x,
\]
which is local because $(t-a)$ maps into $(x-a,y-b)$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Classical polynomial maps are recovered exactly from coordinate-ring homomorphisms.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Replace $t\mapsto x$ by $t\mapsto p(x,y)$.

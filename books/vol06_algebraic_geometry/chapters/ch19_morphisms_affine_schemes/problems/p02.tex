\begin{problem}[Construct the affine morphism from a ring map]\label{prob:vi19-p02}
Let $\varphi:A\to B$. Construct $\Spec B\to\Spec A$ as a morphism of locally ringed spaces, including the point map, maps on principal-open sections, and maps on stalks.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the explicit affine form of the construction used abstractly in VI.19.P1.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use contraction on primes, $A_a\to B_{\varphi(a)}$ on principal opens, and $A_{\varphi^{-1}(\mfp)}\to B_{\mfp}$ on stalks.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Contract a prime of $B$.
\item Compute inverse images of $D(a)$.
\item Localize $\varphi$.
\item Check the maximal ideals on stalks.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
% VI19 full-solutions refinement v1
Define the point map
\[
f:\Spec B\to\Spec A,
\qquad
f(\mathfrak p)=\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak p).
\]
The contraction is prime, so this is well defined.  For every \(a\in A\),
\[
f^{-1}(D(a))=D(\varphi(a)),
\]
so inverse images of a basis of opens are open and \(f\) is continuous.

On \(D(a)\subseteq\Spec A\), the section ring is \(A_a\); on its inverse image \(D(\varphi(a))\), the section ring is \(B_{\varphi(a)}\).  Since \(\varphi(a)\) is invertible in the latter ring, localization gives
\[
\varphi_a:A_a\to B_{\varphi(a)},
\qquad
\frac r{a^n}\longmapsto\frac{\varphi(r)}{\varphi(a)^n}.
\]
If \(D(ab)\subseteq D(a)\), both routes from \(A_a\) to \(B_{\varphi(ab)}\) send the same fraction to the same fraction.  Thus the \(\varphi_a\) commute with restrictions and define a sheaf morphism \(f^\sharp:\OO_{\Spec A}\to f_*\OO_{\Spec B}\).

Finally fix \(\mathfrak p\in\Spec B\) and put \(\mathfrak q=\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak p)\).  Passing to stalks gives
\[
A_{\mathfrak q}\to B_{\mathfrak p},
\qquad a/s\longmapsto\varphi(a)/\varphi(s).
\]
The inverse image of \(\mathfrak pB_{\mathfrak p}\) is \(\mathfrak qA_{\mathfrak q}\): the image of \(a/s\) lies in the target maximal ideal exactly when \(\varphi(a)\in\mathfrak p\), equivalently \(a\in\mathfrak q\).  Hence the stalk map is local.  Therefore \((f,f^\sharp)\) is a morphism of locally ringed spaces.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Contraction controls the topology while localization supplies exactly the compatible function pullbacks needed for a scheme morphism.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Apply the construction to quotient, localization, and polynomial maps.

\begin{problem}[Factorization through a distinguished open]\label{prob:vi19-p16}
Let $\alpha:A\to B$ and $f\in A$. Prove the morphism $\Spec B\to\Spec A$ factors through $D(f)=\Spec A_f$ if and only if $\alpha(f)$ is a unit in $B$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the geometric form of the localization universal property.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Translate the factorization to a ring map out of $A_f$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Use VI.18.P1 to identify $D(f)$ with $\Spec A_f$.
\item Apply the universal property of localization.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
% VI19 full-solutions refinement v1
By VI.18.P1, the distinguished open \(D(f)\subseteq\Spec A\) is canonically the affine scheme \(\Spec A_f\), and its inclusion corresponds contravariantly to the localization map \(A\to A_f\).  Therefore a factorization
\[
\Spec B\longrightarrow D(f)=\Spec A_f\longrightarrow\Spec A
\]
is equivalent, under affine anti-equivalence, to a ring factorization
\[
A\longrightarrow A_f\longrightarrow B
\]
of \(\alpha:A\to B\).

The universal property of localization states that a ring map \(A_f\to B\) extending \(\alpha\) exists if and only if the element \(\alpha(f)\) is invertible in \(B\).  When it exists it is unique and is given explicitly by
\[
\frac a{f^n}\longmapsto\alpha(a)\alpha(f)^{-n}.
\]
Thus the geometric factorization exists uniquely exactly when \(\alpha(f)\in B^\times\).

Geometrically this says that the map lands in the nonvanishing locus of \(f\) precisely when the pulled-back function \(f\) is nowhere vanishing, equivalently a unit on the affine source.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A map lands in the nonvanishing locus of $f$ exactly when the pullback of $f$ is everywhere invertible.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Prove the analogous statement for a general locally ringed space using VI.19.P1.

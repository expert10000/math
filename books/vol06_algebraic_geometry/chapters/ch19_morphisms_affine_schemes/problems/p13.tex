\begin{problem}[Closure of the image]\label{prob:vi19-p13}
For $\varphi:A\to B$, prove $\overline{\operatorname{im}(\Spec B\to\Spec A)}=V(\ker\varphi)$ and deduce a dense-image criterion.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This separates topological density from injectivity of the ring map.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
The ideal of the image is the intersection of all contracted primes.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Compute $\bigcap_{\mfp\in\Spec B}\varphi^{-1}(\mfp)$.
\item Use the nilradical theorem.
\item Translate the resulting radical ideal to a closed set.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
The ideal of elements of $A$ lying in every image prime is
\[
\bigcap_{\mfp\in\Spec B}\varphi^{-1}(\mfp)
=\varphi^{-1}\!\left(\bigcap_{\mfp\in\Spec B}\mfp\right)
=\varphi^{-1}(\sqrt{(0)_B}).
\]
An element $a$ lies in this ideal iff some power $\varphi(a)^n$ is zero, equivalently $a^n\in\ker\varphi$, so the ideal is $\sqrt{\ker\varphi}$.  Therefore the smallest closed set containing the image is
$V(\sqrt{\ker\varphi})=V(\ker\varphi)$.  The image is dense iff $V(\ker\varphi)=\Spec A$, equivalently $\ker\varphi\subseteq\sqrt{(0)_A}$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Nilpotent kernel is invisible to the underlying spectral topology but not to the scheme morphism.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Apply to the reduction map $A\to A_{\mathrm{red}}$.

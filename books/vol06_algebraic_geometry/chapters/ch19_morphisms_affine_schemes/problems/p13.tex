\begin{problem}[Closure of the image]\label{prob:vi19-p13}
For $\varphi:A\to B$, prove $\overline{\operatorname{im}(\Spec B\to\Spec A)}=V(\ker\varphi)$ and deduce a dense-image criterion.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This separates topological density from injectivity of the ring map.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
The ideal of the image is the intersection of all contracted primes.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Compute $\bigcap_{\mfp\in\Spec B}\varphi^{-1}(\mfp)$.
\item Use the nilradical theorem.
\item Translate the resulting radical ideal to a closed set.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
% VI19 full-solutions refinement v1
Let \(f=\Spec(\varphi):\Spec B\to\Spec A\).  A closed subset \(V(J)\subseteq\Spec A\) contains the image of \(f\) exactly when every \(a\in J\) lies in every contracted prime \(\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak p)\).  Hence the ideal of all functions vanishing on the image is
\[
\begin{aligned}
I(f(\Spec B))
&=\bigcap_{\mathfrak p\in\Spec B}\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak p)\\
&=\varphi^{-1}\!\left(\bigcap_{\mathfrak p\in\Spec B}\mathfrak p\right)\\
&=\varphi^{-1}(\sqrt{(0)_B}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Now \(a\in\varphi^{-1}(\sqrt{(0)_B})\) iff \(\varphi(a)^n=0\) for some \(n\), iff \(a^n\in\ker\varphi\), iff \(a\in\sqrt{\ker\varphi}\).  Thus the ideal is \(\sqrt{\ker\varphi}\).

The closure of a subset of a spectrum is the closed set defined by its vanishing ideal, so
\[
\overline{f(\Spec B)}
=V(\sqrt{\ker\varphi})
=V(\ker\varphi).
\]
Therefore the image is dense exactly when \(V(\ker\varphi)=\Spec A\), equivalently when every prime of \(A\) contains \(\ker\varphi\).  Since the intersection of all primes is \(\sqrt{(0)_A}\), this is equivalent to
\[
\boxed{\ker\varphi\subseteq\sqrt{(0)_A}.}
\]
Thus density sees only the kernel modulo nilpotents, not literal injectivity.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Nilpotent kernel is invisible to the underlying spectral topology but not to the scheme morphism.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Apply to the reduction map $A\to A_{\mathrm{red}}$.

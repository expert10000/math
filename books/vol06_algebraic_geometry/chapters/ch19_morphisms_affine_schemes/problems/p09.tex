\begin{problem}[Residue fields under affine morphisms]\label{prob:vi19-p09}
Let $f=\Spec(\varphi):\Spec B\to\Spec A$. For $\mfp\in\Spec B$, prove the local stalk map induces a field homomorphism $\kappa(f(\mfp))\to\kappa(\mfp)$ and compute it for an evaluation map $k[t]\to K$, $t\mapsto a$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
A scheme morphism transports not only points but also their fields of values.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Quotient the local stalk map by maximal ideals.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Set $\mfq=\varphi^{-1}(\mfp)$.
\item Use locality to descend $A_\mfq\to B_\mfp$.
\item For evaluation, identify the kernel and residue fields.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
% VI19 full-solutions refinement v1
Let \(f=\Spec(\varphi):\Spec B\to\Spec A\), fix \(\mathfrak p\in\Spec B\), and put \(\mathfrak q=\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak p)\).  The morphism gives a local homomorphism
\[
A_{\mathfrak q}\longrightarrow B_{\mathfrak p}.
\]
Since it is local, \(\mathfrak qA_{\mathfrak q}\) maps into \(\mathfrak pB_{\mathfrak p}\).  Passing to quotients therefore gives
\[
\kappa(\mathfrak q)
=A_{\mathfrak q}/\mathfrak qA_{\mathfrak q}
\longrightarrow
B_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak pB_{\mathfrak p}
=\kappa(\mathfrak p).
\]
This is a unital map of fields and hence is injective.

For the evaluation map \(k[t]\to K\), \(t\mapsto a\), the source \(\Spec K\) has the unique point \((0)\), whose image is the kernel \(\mathfrak q\) of evaluation.  If \(a\in k\), then \(\mathfrak q=(t-a)\), so
\[
\kappa(\mathfrak q)=k[t]/(t-a)\cong k,
\qquad
\kappa((0))=K.
\]
The induced residue-field map is the given inclusion of constants \(k\to K\).  More generally, when \(a\) is algebraic over \(k\), the image prime is generated by the minimal polynomial of \(a\), and the residue field embeds as \(k(a)\hookrightarrow K\).
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Residue-field maps encode arithmetic information invisible in the underlying continuous map.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Use a non-rational point of $\Spec k[t]$.

\paragraph{VI.20.E1. Identity transition.} The identity transition makes $x\sim x$ automatic and lets $\varphi_{ik}=\varphi_{jk}\varphi_{ij}$ be read without exceptional cases when indices coincide.

\paragraph{VI.20.E2. Symmetry of the relation.} If $y=\varphi_{ij}(x)$, then $x=\varphi_{ji}(y)$, so $y\sim x$.

\paragraph{VI.20.E3. Transitivity.} If $y=\varphi_{ij}(x)$ and $z=\varphi_{jk}(y)$, then the cocycle gives $z=\varphi_{ik}(x)$ on the relevant triple overlap, hence $x\sim z$.

\paragraph{VI.20.E4. Open chart image.} Its inverse image in $X_j$ is $U_{ji}$, open by hypothesis.  Therefore the definition of the quotient topology makes $\psi_i(X_i)$ open.

\paragraph{VI.20.E5. Chart injectivity.} Two points in the same chart can be equivalent only through transition chains whose cocycle composite is $\varphi_{ii}=\operatorname{id}$; hence they coincide.

\paragraph{VI.20.E6. Overlap identity.} A class lies in both images precisely when representatives $x\in X_i$, $y\in X_j$ are related, which means $x\in U_{ij}$ and $y=\varphi_{ij}(x)$.

\paragraph{VI.20.E7. Componentwise restrictions.} Restriction commutes with each $\varphi_{ij}^{\sharp}$, so an equality on $V_i\cap U_{ij}$ restricts to the corresponding equality on any smaller open.

\paragraph{VI.20.E8. Sheaf locality.} If two compatible families agree on an open cover of $V$, then their $i$th components agree on the induced cover of $V_i$ by uniqueness in the sheaf $\OO_{X_i}$; hence all components agree.

\paragraph{VI.20.E9. Sheaf gluing.} Local compatible families glue componentwise in every $\OO_{X_i}$.  The overlap equalities hold locally and therefore globally by the sheaf uniqueness axiom, producing a compatible global family.

\paragraph{VI.20.E10. Stalk identification.} Since $\psi_i$ identifies $X_i$ with the open neighborhood $\psi_i(X_i)$ and the restricted sheaf is $\OO_{X_i}$, taking direct limits over neighborhoods gives the stated stalk isomorphism.

\paragraph{VI.20.E11. Locality.} Every point lies in some chart, and its stalk is isomorphic to a local ring $\OO_{X_i,x_i}$; therefore every glued stalk is local.

\paragraph{VI.20.E12. Scheme condition.} The chart images form an open cover, and each is isomorphic to the scheme $X_i$.  Hence every point has an affine neighborhood inherited from a chart.

\paragraph{VI.20.E13. Two-chart global sections.} A global section is exactly a pair $(s_U,s_V)$ whose two restrictions to $U\cap V$ agree, so $\Gamma(X,\OO_X)=\ker[\Gamma(U)\times\Gamma(V)\rightrightarrows\Gamma(U\cap V)]$ in the set/ring equalizer sense.

\paragraph{VI.20.E14. Doubled origin sections.} Compatibility says $p=q$ after localization.  Since $k[t]\hookrightarrow k[t,t^{-1}]$ is injective, $p=q$, so global sections are canonically $k[t]$.

\paragraph{VI.20.E15. Doubled origin non-affine.} If $X$ were affine, VI/18 would give $X\cong\Spec\Gamma(X,\OO_X)\cong\Spec k[t]$, which has one origin, while $X$ has two distinct origins.  Contradiction.

\paragraph{VI.20.E16. Inversion overlap.} The overlap rings are $k[t,t^{-1}]$ and $k[u,u^{-1}]$; the isomorphism sends $u\mapsto t^{-1}$ and has inverse $t\mapsto u^{-1}$.

\paragraph{VI.20.E17. Common distinguished refinement.} Choose $D(f_0)\subseteq U\cap V$ around $x$.  Inside $V$, choose $D(g)\subseteq D(f_0)$ around $x$.  Since $D(f_0)\cong\Spec A_{f_0}$, refine $D(g)$ near $x$ by a distinguished open $D(h/1)$ of $A_{f_0}$; in $U$ this is $D(f_0h)$.  Rename it $W$ and, if needed, refine symmetrically so it is distinguished in both charts.

\paragraph{VI.20.E18. Common overlap ring.} VI/18 gives $\Gamma(W,\OO_W)\cong A_f$ from the first chart and $\Gamma(W,\OO_W)\cong B_g$ from the second, hence a canonical isomorphism $A_f\cong B_g$.

\paragraph{VI.20.E19. Compatible maps.} If $x\in X_i$ and $y=\varphi_{ij}(x)$ represent the same point, compatibility gives $f_i(x)=f_j(y)$.  Thus $f([x])$ is well-defined; the sheaf maps glue similarly on the open chart cover.

\paragraph{VI.20.E20. Uniqueness of a glued map.} The continuous maps agree on an open cover, hence globally.  The sheaf morphisms also agree on the cover and therefore glue uniquely, so the morphisms coincide.

\paragraph{VI.20.E21. Reconstruct from affine cover.} Take $U_{ij}=U_i\cap U_j$ as an open subscheme of $U_i$ and $\varphi_{ij}$ the identity map onto the same intersection viewed in $U_j$.  The cocycle is automatic, and the resulting gluing maps canonically to $X$ chartwise, hence is an isomorphism.

\paragraph{VI.20.E22. Disjoint union as gluing.} With $U_{ij}=\varnothing$ for $i\ne j$, no cross-chart points or sections are identified, so the quotient and sheaf are exactly the disjoint-union scheme.

\paragraph{VI.20.E23. Full-overlap gluing.} The quotient has one copy of each equivalence class and either chart maps isomorphically onto the whole glued scheme; hence the result is canonically isomorphic to $X_1$ and $X_2$.

\paragraph{VI.20.E24. Triple cocycle.} Following the transition $1\to2\to3$ would identify a point with $\varphi_{23}\varphi_{12}(x)$, while the direct $1\to3$ prescription identifies it with $\varphi_{13}(x)$.  If these differ, the prescribed chart embeddings cannot realize all transitions simultaneously.

\paragraph{VI.20.E25. Inversion gluing global sections.} A compatible pair gives a Laurent polynomial belonging to both $k[t]$ and $k[t^{-1}]$.  Such a Laurent polynomial has neither positive nor negative powers, hence is constant.  Therefore the global section ring is $k$.

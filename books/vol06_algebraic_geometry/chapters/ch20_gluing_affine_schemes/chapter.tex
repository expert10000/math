\section*{Purpose and learning goals}

Affine schemes are local building blocks, not the whole subject.  The passage from affine
geometry to general scheme geometry is made by gluing compatible local models along open
subschemes.  The central theorem of this chapter is the full legacy AG3 Problem 14: a family
of schemes equipped with compatible open-overlap isomorphisms can be assembled into a scheme,
unique up to unique isomorphism.

A second legacy problem belongs here as well.  AG5 Problem 2 proves that if two affine open
subschemes $U=\Spec A$ and $V=\Spec B$ lie in one scheme, then $U\cap V$ can be covered by
open affines that are distinguished in both $U$ and $V$.  This is exactly the local algebraic
refinement needed to make gluing computations explicit.

After this chapter, the reader should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
\item state gluing data and the inverse/cocycle compatibility conditions;
\item construct the quotient topological space underlying a glued scheme;
\item prove each original chart embeds homeomorphically as an open subset;
\item define the glued structure sheaf by compatible families of local sections;
\item verify the sheaf axiom and identify the stalks with chart stalks;
\item prove the resulting locally ringed space is a scheme;
\item prove uniqueness up to unique isomorphism;
\item use the universal mapping property for morphisms out of a glued scheme;
\item reduce two-chart affine gluing to ring isomorphisms on localized coordinate rings;
\item construct the doubled-origin line and a two-chart model later recognized as $\PP^1$;
\item recover a scheme from any affine open cover;
\item refine intersections of affine opens by common distinguished affine opens;
\item compute global sections of a two-chart gluing as an equalizer;
\item distinguish gluing from later closed-subscheme, fiber-product, and base-change constructions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Gluing data}

Let $\{X_i\}_{i\in I}$ be schemes.  For each pair $i,j$, choose an open subscheme
$U_{ij}\subseteq X_i$ and an isomorphism
\[
\varphi_{ij}:U_{ij}\xrightarrow{\sim}U_{ji}.
\]
It is convenient to put $U_{ii}=X_i$ and $\varphi_{ii}=\operatorname{id}_{X_i}$.
The gluing conditions are
\[
\varphi_{ji}=\varphi_{ij}^{-1}
\]
and, on every triple overlap where both sides are defined,
\[
\varphi_{ik}=\varphi_{jk}\circ\varphi_{ij}.
\]
Equivalently, $\varphi_{ij}$ must carry the part of $U_{ij}$ meeting $U_{ik}$ onto the
corresponding part of $U_{ji}$ meeting $U_{jk}$, and the transition maps satisfy the cocycle law.

\input{chapters/ch20_gluing_affine_schemes/figures/vi20_cocycle.tex}

\section{The quotient set and topology}

Start from the disjoint union of underlying sets
\[
Y=\coprod_{i\in I}X_i.
\]
For $x\in X_i$ and $y\in X_j$, declare $x\sim y$ exactly when $x\in U_{ij}$ and
$y=\varphi_{ij}(x)$.  Identity, inverse, and cocycle conditions make this an equivalence relation.
Define
\[
X=Y/{\sim}
\]
and let $\psi_i:X_i\to X$ be the quotient maps.

A subset $V\subseteq X$ is declared open precisely when every $\psi_i^{-1}(V)$ is open in $X_i$.
This final topology is designed so that all chart maps are continuous.  Moreover,
\[
\psi_j^{-1}(\psi_i(X_i))=U_{ji},
\]
so $\psi_i(X_i)$ is open, and $\psi_i:X_i\to\psi_i(X_i)$ is a homeomorphism.
The images cover $X$, and
\[
\psi_i(X_i)\cap\psi_j(X_j)=\psi_i(U_{ij})=\psi_j(U_{ji}).
\]

\input{chapters/ch20_gluing_affine_schemes/figures/vi20_quotient_space.tex}

\section{Gluing the structure sheaf}

For an open set $V\subseteq X$, put $V_i=\psi_i^{-1}(V)$.  Define
\[
\OO_X(V)=
\left\{(s_i)_i\in\prod_i\OO_{X_i}(V_i):
 s_i|_{V_i\cap U_{ij}}
 =\varphi_{ij}^{\sharp}\bigl(s_j|_{V_j\cap U_{ji}}\bigr)
 \text{ for all }i,j
\right\}.
\]
Restrictions are componentwise.  Because each $\OO_{X_i}$ is a sheaf and the transition maps
satisfy the cocycle law, compatible local families glue uniquely; hence $\OO_X$ is a sheaf.
On the chart $\psi_i(X_i)$ the restriction of $\OO_X$ identifies canonically with $\OO_{X_i}$.

\input{chapters/ch20_gluing_affine_schemes/figures/vi20_sheaf_equalizer.tex}

\section{Local rings and the scheme condition}

If $x\in X$, choose a chart $\psi_i(X_i)$ containing it and a point $x_i\in X_i$ with
$\psi_i(x_i)=x$.  The chart identification gives
\[
\OO_{X,x}\cong\OO_{X_i,x_i}.
\]
The ring on the right is local, so the glued ringed space is locally ringed.  Since the opens
$\psi_i(X_i)$ cover $X$ and are schemes, $X$ is a scheme.  Thus gluing is genuinely local:
all stalk and affine-neighborhood questions may be checked inside one chart.

\input{chapters/ch20_gluing_affine_schemes/figures/vi20_stalk_chart.tex}

\section{Uniqueness and the universal mapping property}

Suppose $X$ and $X'$ are two gluings of the same data.  On every chart, the identity of $X_i$
produces an isomorphism between the corresponding open subsets of $X$ and $X'$.  These chartwise
isomorphisms agree on overlaps, hence glue to a unique isomorphism $X\xrightarrow{\sim}X'$.
Therefore the gluing is unique up to unique isomorphism compatible with all charts.

A useful equivalent form is the mapping property.  If $Y$ is any scheme, then giving a morphism
$f:X\to Y$ is equivalent to giving morphisms $f_i:X_i\to Y$ such that
\[
f_i|_{U_{ij}}=f_j\circ\varphi_{ij}
\]
on every overlap.  Thus morphisms out of a glued scheme are exactly compatible families of
morphisms out of its charts.

\section{Two-chart affine gluing}

Let $X_1=\Spec A$ and $X_2=\Spec B$.  Suppose principal opens
$D(f)\subseteq X_1$ and $D(g)\subseteq X_2$ are identified by an affine-scheme isomorphism.
By VI/18--VI/19 this is equivalently a ring isomorphism
\[
B_g\xrightarrow{\sim}A_f
\]
with the direction reversed geometrically.  Gluing the two affine schemes along these opens
produces a scheme covered by two affine charts.

This is the basic computational model: topology says which points are identified; localization
says what the functions are on the overlap; the isomorphism of localized rings tells how functions
transform between charts.

\input{chapters/ch20_gluing_affine_schemes/figures/vi20_two_affines.tex}

\section{The doubled-origin line}

Take two copies $X_1=X_2=\A^1_k=\Spec k[t]$ and glue
\[
D(t)\subseteq X_1\qquad\text{to}\qquad D(t)\subseteq X_2
\]
by the identity.  Every nonzero point is identified across the two charts, but the two origins are
not.  The result is the affine line with doubled origin.

Its global sections are the equalizer
\[
\Gamma(X,\OO_X)=
\{(p,q)\in k[t]\times k[t]:p=q\text{ in }k[t,t^{-1}]\}
\cong k[t].
\]
Nevertheless the glued scheme is not $\Spec k[t]$: its underlying space has two distinct origins.
Thus a scheme covered by affine opens need not itself be affine.

\input{chapters/ch20_gluing_affine_schemes/figures/vi20_doubled_origin.tex}

\section{A two-chart projective-line model}

Take $X_0=\Spec k[t]$ and $X_1=\Spec k[u]$.  Identify $D(t)$ with $D(u)$ by
\[
u\longmapsto t^{-1}
\]
at the level of overlap rings $k[u,u^{-1}]\cong k[t,t^{-1}]$.  Gluing produces a scheme covered
by two affine lines.  Later projective geometry will identify this model canonically with $\PP^1_k$;
here its role is simply to show that non-affine global geometry can emerge from elementary affine
charts and localization.

\input{chapters/ch20_gluing_affine_schemes/figures/vi20_projective_line_model.tex}

\section{Intersections of affine open subschemes}

Let $X$ be a scheme and let
\[
U=\Spec A,\qquad V=\Spec B
\]
be affine opens.  For every $x\in U\cap V$, the basis theorem for distinguished opens lets us
choose a distinguished neighborhood inside $U$ contained in $U\cap V$, then refine inside $V$,
and finally refine once more so the same open is distinguished in both charts.  Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
U\cap V=\bigcup_\lambda W_\lambda,
\qquad
W_\lambda=D(f_\lambda)\subseteq U
=D(g_\lambda)\subseteq V.
}
\]
This is legacy AG5 Problem 2 and is the practical bridge from abstract overlap isomorphisms to
localization computations.

\input{chapters/ch20_gluing_affine_schemes/figures/vi20_common_distinguished_refinement.tex}

\section{Reconstructing a scheme from an affine cover}

Let $X$ be any scheme with affine open cover $X=\bigcup_i U_i$.  On $U_i\cap U_j$, use the identity
as the transition isomorphism between the two copies of the overlap.  The cocycle condition is
automatic, so the gluing theorem reconstructs a scheme canonically isomorphic to $X$.
Thus the slogan ``schemes are obtained by gluing affine schemes'' is literally a reconstruction
theorem, not merely a heuristic.

\section{Global sections as an equalizer}

For a two-open cover $X=U\cup V$, the sheaf axiom yields
\[
0\longrightarrow\Gamma(X,\OO_X)
\longrightarrow\Gamma(U,\OO_X)\times\Gamma(V,\OO_X)
\rightrightarrows\Gamma(U\cap V,\OO_X),
\]
where the two arrows are the two restrictions.  Hence global sections are pairs of local functions
that agree on the overlap.  This formula is especially effective when $U,V$, and their overlap are
affine principal opens.

\section{Common pitfalls}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Pairwise identifications are not enough: triple-overlap cocycle compatibility is essential.
\item The quotient topology alone does not make a scheme; the structure sheaf must also be glued.
\item The sheaf is not a direct product of chart sheaves: sections must satisfy overlap compatibility.
\item The chart maps are open embeddings because inverse images of chart images are the prescribed open overlaps.
\item A glued scheme can be non-affine even when every chart and overlap is affine.
\item An isomorphism of overlap spaces is insufficient; one needs an isomorphism of schemes, including the sheaves.
\item When affines overlap inside a scheme, the whole intersection need not be one principal open, but it has a cover by opens principal in both charts.
\item AG3 Problem 13, the finality of $\Spec\ZZ$ and structure morphism, remains reserved for VI/21.
\item Closed subschemes remain centered in VI/22; quotient-ring examples here are used only when needed to understand open-chart gluing.
\item Fiber products and base change are different universal constructions and remain reserved for VI/23--VI/24.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Summary}

Compatible open-subscheme isomorphisms satisfying inverse and cocycle laws determine a scheme
obtained from the disjoint union of the charts by quotienting points and gluing functions.  Each
chart survives as an open subscheme, the stalks are the original chart stalks, and the result is
unique up to unique isomorphism.  This is AG3 Problem 14, now recovered uniquely as VI.20.P1.

The corpus audit also recovers AG5 Problem 2 as VI.20.P2: intersections of affine opens possess a
cover by affine opens distinguished in both charts.  This turns abstract overlap geometry into
explicit localization algebra and closes VI/20 without consuming AG3 Problem 13, which remains
reserved for VI/21.

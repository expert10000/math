\begin{problem}[Two-chart gluing theorem]\label{prob:vi20-p03}
Specialize VI.20.P1 to two schemes and prove the construction directly, including the overlap and sheaf formulas.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
The two-chart case is the workhorse for explicit examples.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
There are no nontrivial triple-overlap constraints beyond inverse compatibility.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item There are no nontrivial triple-overlap constraints beyond inverse compatibility.
\end{enumerate}

% VI20_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINEMENT P03
\begin{solution}
Let $X_1$ and $X_2$ be schemes, let $U_{12}\subseteq X_1$ and $U_{21}\subseteq X_2$ be open, and let $\varphi:U_{12}\xrightarrow{\sim}U_{21}$ with inverse $\varphi^{-1}$.  Form the disjoint union $X_1\amalg X_2$ and identify each $x\in U_{12}$ with $\varphi(x)$.  Because there are only two charts, inverse compatibility is enough to make this an equivalence relation.

Let $X$ be the quotient and let $\psi_i:X_i\to X$ be the quotient maps.  A subset $V\subseteq X$ is open iff both $\psi_i^{-1}(V)$ are open.  Then
\[
\psi_2^{-1}(\psi_1(X_1))=U_{21},\qquad
\psi_1^{-1}(\psi_2(X_2))=U_{12},
\]
so both chart images are open.  No two distinct points of the same chart are identified, hence each $\psi_i$ is a homeomorphism onto its image, and
\[
\psi_1(X_1)\cap\psi_2(X_2)=\psi_1(U_{12})=\psi_2(U_{21}).
\]

For $V\subseteq X$ open, put $V_i=\psi_i^{-1}(V)$ and define
\[
\OO_X(V)=\{(s_1,s_2):s_i\in\OO_{X_i}(V_i),\ s_1|_{U_{12}\cap V_1}=\varphi^\sharp(s_2|_{U_{21}\cap V_2})\}.
\]
Locality and gluing hold componentwise because the two chart sheaves are sheaves.  On each chart image this sheaf restricts to the original chart sheaf.  Therefore the stalk at a point represented in $X_i$ is $\OO_{X_i,x_i}$, which is local, and the quotient is a scheme covered by the two original charts.  The uniqueness statement follows by gluing the identity maps on the two chart images, exactly as in the general gluing theorem.
\end{solution}


\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The general theorem reduces to a transparent pairwise construction.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Apply it to two affine lines.

\begin{problem}[Two-chart projective-line model]\label{prob:vi20-p08}
Glue $\Spec k[t]$ and $\Spec k[u]$ along punctured lines using $u=t^{-1}$. Describe the points and overlap functions.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This previews projective geometry using only affine gluing.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
The overlap rings are Laurent polynomial rings.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The overlap rings are Laurent polynomial rings.
\end{enumerate}

% VI20_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINEMENT P08
\begin{solution}
Take
\[
X_0=\Spec k[t],\qquad X_1=\Spec k[u].
\]
Their punctured opens are $D(t)=\Spec k[t,t^{-1}]$ and $D(u)=\Spec k[u,u^{-1}]$.  The ring isomorphism
\[
k[u,u^{-1}]\xrightarrow{\sim}k[t,t^{-1}],\qquad u\longmapsto t^{-1}
\]
has inverse $t\mapsto u^{-1}$ and therefore defines an isomorphism $D(t)\xrightarrow{\sim}D(u)$ of affine schemes.  Glue the two affine lines using this transition.

The origins $(t)$ and $(u)$ do not belong to the overlap, so both survive as distinct points.  Every point of the punctured first chart is identified with the corresponding point of the second chart through reciprocal coordinates.  At the level of regular functions, a function $q(u)$ on the second chart restricts on the overlap to the Laurent polynomial $q(t^{-1})$ in first-chart coordinates.  More generally, all overlap restrictions are compared by the substitution $u=t^{-1}$.

Thus the result is a scheme covered by two affine lines with transition function $u=1/t$.  No identification with projective space is required for the gluing construction itself; that interpretation belongs to the later projective chapters.
\end{solution}


\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A global compact-looking object emerges from two elementary affine charts.

\begin{problem}[Gluing is local on the source]\label{prob:vi20-p19}
Prove a morphism from a glued scheme is uniquely determined by its restrictions to any open cover refining the original charts.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is a flexible local-to-global principle for later chapters.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Refine the chart cover and use sheaf uniqueness.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Refine the chart cover and use sheaf uniqueness.
\end{enumerate}

% VI20_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINEMENT P19
\begin{solution}
Let $X$ be a glued scheme and let $\{V_\alpha\}$ be any open cover refining the original chart cover.  Suppose two morphisms $f,g:X\to Y$ have equal restrictions to every $V_\alpha$.  Each original chart image is covered by those $V_\alpha$ lying inside it.  Since morphisms of locally ringed spaces are local on the source, $f$ and $g$ agree on each original chart.  The uniqueness part of the gluing mapping property then gives $f=g$ globally.

Conversely, suppose morphisms $f_\alpha:V_\alpha\to Y$ agree on all pairwise intersections.  On each original chart $X_i$, the restrictions of the relevant $f_\alpha$ form a compatible family on an open cover of $X_i$.  Their point maps and sheaf pullbacks glue to a unique morphism $f_i:X_i\to Y$.  On the original chart overlaps, the $f_i$ agree because this can be checked on the refining cover.  The gluing mapping property now produces a unique global morphism $X\to Y$.

Thus construction and equality of morphisms may be checked on any sufficiently fine open refinement, not only on the original atlas.
\end{solution}


\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Morphisms can be constructed and compared on arbitrarily fine affine neighborhoods.

\begin{problem}[Why the cocycle is necessary]\label{prob:vi20-p18}
Construct an abstract three-chart scenario showing that pairwise inverse maps without the cocycle need not define the prescribed gluing.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
The cocycle is structural, not cosmetic.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Track one point around a triple overlap.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Track one point around a triple overlap.
\end{enumerate}

% VI20_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINEMENT P18
\begin{solution}
Take three charts with a common nonempty overlap and pairwise inverse isomorphisms $\varphi_{12},\varphi_{23},\varphi_{13}$.  Suppose there exists a point $x$ in the triple overlap such that
\[
\varphi_{13}(x)\neq\varphi_{23}(\varphi_{12}(x)).
\]
The pairwise prescriptions require $x\sim\varphi_{13}(x)$ directly.  They also require
\[
x\sim\varphi_{12}(x)\sim\varphi_{23}(\varphi_{12}(x)).
\]
Any equivalence relation containing the prescribed identifications is transitive, so it forces the two distinct points
$\varphi_{13}(x)$ and $\varphi_{23}(\varphi_{12}(x))$ of the third chart to become equivalent.

But a valid gluing must embed the third chart injectively as an open subspace.  Identifying two distinct points already lying in the same chart contradicts that requirement.  Hence arbitrary pairwise inverse identifications do not suffice.  The cocycle equation
\[
\varphi_{13}=\varphi_{23}\circ\varphi_{12}
\]
on triple overlaps is exactly the coherence condition that prevents this collapse and makes the prescribed chart embeddings simultaneously realizable.
\end{solution}


\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Triple coherence is exactly what allows pairwise data to globalize.

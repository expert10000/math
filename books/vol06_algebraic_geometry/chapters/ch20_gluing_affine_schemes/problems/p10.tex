\begin{problem}[Associativity of finite gluing]\label{prob:vi20-p10}
Show that gluing three charts in two stages gives a scheme uniquely isomorphic to the one-step gluing, provided the original cocycle data are respected.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Staged constructions should not depend on arbitrary parenthesization.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}

% VI20_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINEMENT P10
\begin{solution}
Suppose three schemes $X_1,X_2,X_3$ are equipped with overlap isomorphisms satisfying the cocycle condition.  Let $X$ be their one-step gluing.  Alternatively, first glue $X_1$ and $X_2$ to obtain $X_{12}$; the original cocycle data identify the appropriate open subset of $X_{12}$ with the prescribed overlap in $X_3$, so one may glue $X_{12}$ to $X_3$ and obtain a staged gluing $X'$.

Both $X$ and $X'$ contain open copies of $X_1,X_2,X_3$.  On every pairwise overlap these copies are identified by exactly the original transition maps, because the staged construction used restrictions of the same data.  Therefore the identity maps of the three charts form a compatible family of morphisms from the chart cover of $X$ to $X'$.  By the gluing mapping property they give a unique morphism $F:X\to X'$.  Reversing the roles gives $G:X'\to X$.

The composites $GF$ and $FG$ restrict to the identity on every original chart.  Uniqueness in the mapping property forces them to be identity morphisms globally.  Thus $F$ is the unique chart-compatible isomorphism.  Hence finite gluing is associative up to the unique canonical isomorphism required by the original transition data.
\end{solution}


\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The gluing theorem is canonical enough to ignore construction order.

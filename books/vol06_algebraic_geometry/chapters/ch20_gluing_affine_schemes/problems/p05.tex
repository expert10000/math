\begin{problem}[Reconstruct a scheme from an affine cover]\label{prob:vi20-p05}
Prove every scheme is canonically recovered by gluing the members of any affine open cover along their intersections.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This makes the slogan 'schemes are glued affine schemes' precise.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use identity transitions on literal intersections.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Use identity transitions on literal intersections.
\end{enumerate}

% VI20_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINEMENT P05
\begin{solution}
Let $X=\bigcup_iU_i$ be an affine open cover.  For each pair put $U_{ij}=U_i\cap U_j$, viewed as an open subscheme of $U_i$, and let
\[
\varphi_{ij}:U_{ij}\longrightarrow U_{ji}
\]
be the identity on the underlying open subscheme of $X$.  The inverse law and triple cocycle are automatic because all transition maps are restrictions of the identity of $X$.

Let $X'$ be the scheme obtained by gluing these affine charts.  The inclusions $j_i:U_i\hookrightarrow X$ are compatible on overlaps, so the gluing mapping property gives a unique morphism
\[
F:X'\longrightarrow X
\]
whose restriction to the $i$th chart is $j_i$.  On each chart image of $X'$, $F$ is an isomorphism onto the open set $U_i\subseteq X$.  These chart images cover $X'$ and the $U_i$ cover $X$.  Hence $F$ is a homeomorphism locally on an open cover and its sheaf map is an isomorphism locally on the same cover.  Therefore $F$ is an isomorphism of schemes.

The construction is canonical relative to the chosen cover: it uses only the literal intersections and identity transitions.  Thus every scheme is recovered by gluing the members of any affine open cover along their actual overlaps.
\end{solution}


\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
An affine atlas is complete reconstruction data.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Replace the affine cover by any open cover.

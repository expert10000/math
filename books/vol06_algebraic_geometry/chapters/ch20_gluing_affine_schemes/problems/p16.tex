\begin{problem}[Gluing with an empty overlap]\label{prob:vi20-p16}
Prove gluing two schemes along the empty opens gives their disjoint union as a scheme.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This checks an extreme boundary case of P1.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
No points or sections are forced to agree across charts.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item No points or sections are forced to agree across charts.
\end{enumerate}

% VI20_FULL_SOLUTION_REFINEMENT P16
\begin{solution}
Take two schemes $X_1,X_2$ and use the empty opens $U_{12}=U_{21}=\varnothing$ as the overlap.  In the quotient construction there are no cross-chart pairs to identify, so the underlying set is $X_1\amalg X_2$.  The final topology says a set is open precisely when its intersections with both components are open, which is the usual disjoint-union topology.

For an open set $V=V_1\amalg V_2$, the compatibility condition in the glued sheaf is imposed only on the empty overlap and is therefore vacuous.  Hence
\[
\OO_X(V)=\OO_{X_1}(V_1)\times\OO_{X_2}(V_2).
\]
These are exactly the sections of the standard disjoint-union sheaf.  The stalk at a point is the stalk from the unique component containing it.  Thus the gluing construction gives the scheme-theoretic disjoint union $X_1\amalg X_2$.
\end{solution}


\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Disjoint unions are gluing with no overlap.

Generalize to arbitrary families.

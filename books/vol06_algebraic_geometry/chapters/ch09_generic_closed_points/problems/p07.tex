\begin{problem}[Generic and closed points of $\Spec\ZZ$]
Describe all points of $\Spec\ZZ$, their specialization relations, and their closures.
Prove that the set of closed points
\[
\{(p):p\text{ prime}\}
\]
is dense in $\Spec\ZZ$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Arithmetic spectra make generic-to-closed specialization visible without coordinates.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
The prime ideals of $\ZZ$ are $(0)$ and $(p)$.  To prove density, show every nonempty
principal open contains a closed point.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
For $0\neq n\in\ZZ$,
\[
D(n)=\{(0)\}\cup\{(p):p\nmid n\}.
\]

\begin{solution}
The points are
\[
(0),\qquad (p)\text{ for prime }p.
\]
The zero ideal is generic because $\ZZ$ is a domain:
\[
\overline{\{(0)\}}=\Spec\ZZ.
\]
Each $(p)$ is maximal and therefore closed:
\[
\overline{\{(p)\}}=\{(p)\}.
\]
The only nontrivial specialization relations are
\[
(0)\leadsto(p).
\]

Let $C$ be the set of closed points.  Every nonempty open contains a nonempty principal
open.  For nonzero $n$,
\[
D(n)=\{(0)\}\cup\{(p):p\nmid n\}.
\]
Only finitely many primes divide $n$, so some prime $p$ does not divide $n$, and then
$(p)\in D(n)\cap C$.  Hence every nonempty open meets $C$, proving
\[
\overline C=\Spec\ZZ.
\]
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A dense subset can consist entirely of closed points while still accumulating to a generic
point.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Show that deleting finitely many closed prime-number points leaves a dense open subset.

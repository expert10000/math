\begin{problem}[Generic points under maps of spectra]
Let $\varphi:A\to B$ induce
\[
f=\varphi^\ast:\Spec B\to\Spec A.
\]
\begin{enumerate}
\item Prove that $f$ preserves specialization.
\item More generally, let $X$ be an irreducible topological space with generic point
$\eta$ and let $g:X\to Y$ be continuous.  Prove that $g(\eta)$ is the generic point of
$\overline{g(X)}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Generic points make closure of images computable from a single distinguished point.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Contraction preserves inclusion.  Continuous maps send specializations to
specializations.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
For any $x\in X$, use $x\in\overline{\{\eta\}}$.

\begin{solution}
If $\mathfrak q_1\subseteq\mathfrak q_2$ in $\Spec B$, then
\[
\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q_1)\subseteq\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q_2).
\]
Hence a specialization relation in $\Spec B$ maps to a specialization relation in
$\Spec A$.

Now let $X$ be irreducible with generic point $\eta$.  Every $x\in X$ satisfies
\[
x\in\overline{\{\eta\}}.
\]
Continuity implies
\[
g(x)\in\overline{\{g(\eta)\}}.
\]
Thus
\[
g(X)\subseteq\overline{\{g(\eta)\}}.
\]
Taking closures gives
\[
\overline{g(X)}
\subseteq
\overline{\{g(\eta)\}}.
\]
The reverse inclusion holds because $g(\eta)\in g(X)$.  Therefore
\[
\overline{g(X)}
=
\overline{\{g(\eta)\}},
\]
so $g(\eta)$ is generic for the closure of the image.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
An irreducible source has one point carrying the closure data of the whole source, and
continuity preserves that role in the image.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Apply the result to the map induced by $k[t]\to k[x]$, $t\mapsto x^2$.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New problem extending the contravariant spectrum map developed in VI/07.

\begin{problem}[Generic and closed points of the affine line over an arbitrary field]
Let $k$ be a field.  Classify the points of $\Spec k[t]$, identify the generic point and
all closed points, and determine which closed points are $k$-rational.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
The affine line already shows that ``closed'' and ``rational over the base field'' are
different notions.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use that $k[t]$ is a PID.  Its nonzero prime ideals are generated by irreducible
polynomials.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
For an irreducible $f$, inspect $k[t]/(f)$.

\begin{solution}
Since $k[t]$ is a PID and a domain, its prime ideals are
\[
(0)
\]
and
\[
(f)
\]
for irreducible polynomials $f\in k[t]$.  The zero ideal is generic because
\[
\overline{\{(0)\}}=\Spec k[t].
\]
Every nonzero prime $(f)$ is maximal because $k[t]/(f)$ is a field, so these are exactly
the closed points.

A closed point $(f)$ is $k$-rational exactly when its quotient field is $k$, which happens
exactly when $\deg f=1$.  Thus higher-degree irreducibles define closed but non-rational
points.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Closed points remember finite algebraic extensions of the base field even when the
underlying classical $k$-point set does not display them.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Specialize the answer to $k=\CC$, $\RR$, and $\FF_p$.

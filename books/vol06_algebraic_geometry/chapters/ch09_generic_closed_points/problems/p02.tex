\begin{problem}[Specialization chain: plane, line, point]
Let $A=k[x,y]$.  Analyze the chain
\[
(0)\subset(x)\subset(x,y).
\]
Compute the closure of each point, identify every specialization relation among the three,
and explain the corresponding geometry.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This chain is the smallest concrete model of how scheme-theoretic points encode nested
geometric loci.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use
\[
\overline{\{\mathfrak p\}}=V(\mathfrak p)
\]
and remember that inclusion of primes means specialization.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Compute $V(0)$, $V(x)$, and $V(x,y)$ separately.

\begin{solution}
Since $A$ is a domain,
\[
\overline{\{(0)\}}=V(0)=\Spec k[x,y].
\]
Thus $(0)$ is the generic point of the plane.

Next,
\[
\overline{\{(x)\}}=V(x),
\]
the vertical line $x=0$.  The quotient
\[
k[x,y]/(x)\cong k[y]
\]
is a domain, so $(x)$ is the generic point of this irreducible line.

Finally,
\[
\overline{\{(x,y)\}}=V(x,y)=\{(x,y)\}
\]
because $(x,y)$ is maximal.  Thus it is the closed origin.

The containments
\[
(0)\subset(x)\subset(x,y)
\]
give the specialization chain
\[
(0)\leadsto(x)\leadsto(x,y).
\]
Transitivity also makes $(x,y)$ a specialization of $(0)$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
One prime chain simultaneously records the whole plane, an irreducible subvariety, and a
closed point.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Replace $(x)$ by an arbitrary irreducible polynomial prime $(f)$ and choose a maximal ideal
containing it.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New synthesis problem built from the generic-point and specialization theory retained from
legacy Algebraic Geometry 1.

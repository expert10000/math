\begin{problem}[Closed points and maximal ideals]
Prove that a point $\mathfrak p\in\Spec A$ is closed if and only if $\mathfrak p$ is
maximal.  Show also that if $\mathfrak p$ is not maximal, then every maximal ideal
$\mathfrak m\supsetneq\mathfrak p$ is a proper specialization of $\mathfrak p$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Closed points are the bridge back from prime spectra to familiar point-set geometry.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
The closure is the set of all primes containing $\mathfrak p$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
A prime is maximal exactly when it has no strictly larger proper ideal, hence no larger
prime.

\begin{solution}
The closure formula gives
\[
\overline{\{\mathfrak p\}}
=
\{\mathfrak q\in\Spec A:\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak q\}.
\]
This equals the singleton $\{\mathfrak p\}$ iff there is no prime properly containing
$\mathfrak p$.  Since every proper ideal is contained in a maximal ideal, a nonmaximal
prime lies in some maximal ideal $\mathfrak m\supsetneq\mathfrak p$.  Hence
\[
\mathfrak p\text{ closed}
\iff
\mathfrak p\text{ maximal}.
\]
For such a larger maximal ideal,
\[
\mathfrak p\subsetneq\mathfrak m
\]
means exactly that $\mathfrak m$ is a proper specialization of $\mathfrak p$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A point fails to be closed precisely when it still has room to specialize.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Characterize rings for which every point of the spectrum is closed.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
Canonical expansion of the closed-point theorem in legacy Algebraic Geometry 1.

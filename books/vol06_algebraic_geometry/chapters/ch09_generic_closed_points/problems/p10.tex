\begin{problem}[A nonreduced irreducible spectrum]
Let
\[
A=k[x,y]/(x^2).
\]
Determine the nilradical, prove $\Spec A$ is irreducible, identify its generic point, and
explain why the generic point is not $(0)$.  Compare the underlying spectra of $A$ and
$A_{\mathrm{red}}$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
It separates the topological notion of a generic point from the algebraic presence of
nilpotents.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
The quotient by $(\bar x)$ is $k[y]$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Show every nilpotent maps to zero in the reduced quotient.

\begin{solution}
The class $\bar x$ is nilpotent, so
\[
(\bar x)\subseteq\sqrt{(0)}.
\]
The quotient
\[
A/(\bar x)\cong k[y]
\]
is reduced, so every nilpotent element of $A$ lies in $(\bar x)$.  Hence
\[
\sqrt{(0)}=(\bar x).
\]
Since $k[y]$ is a domain, $(\bar x)$ is prime.  Therefore $\Spec A$ is irreducible and its
generic point is
\[
\eta=(\bar x).
\]
The zero ideal is not prime because
\[
\bar x^2=0,\qquad \bar x\neq0.
\]
Finally,
\[
A_{\mathrm{red}}=A/(\bar x)\cong k[y],
\]
and quotienting by the nilradical induces a homeomorphism
\[
\Spec A_{\mathrm{red}}\cong\Spec A.
\]
Thus the same topological generic point is represented by $(0)$ in the reduced ring and by
$(\bar x)$ in the nonreduced ring.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The topology detects the radical prime defining the irreducible space but forgets the
nilpotent thickening around it.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Generalize to $k[x,y]/(x^n)$ for $n\ge2$.

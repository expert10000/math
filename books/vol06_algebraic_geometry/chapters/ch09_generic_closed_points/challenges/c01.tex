\begin{problem}[Challenge: density of closed points for finite-type algebras]
Let $k$ be a field and let $A$ be a finitely generated $k$-algebra.  Using the weak
Nullstellensatz (or Zariski's lemma), prove that the closed points are dense in $\Spec A$.
More generally, prove they are dense in every closed subset $V(I)$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this challenge matters.}
It explains why classical closed points are often sufficient for testing functions even
though the spectrum also contains generic and intermediate points.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
It is enough to show every nonempty principal open contains a maximal ideal.  If $D(f)$ is
nonempty, then $A_f\neq0$ and $A_f$ is again a finitely generated $k$-algebra.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Choose a maximal ideal of $A_f$ and contract it to $A$.  Use Zariski's lemma to show the
contraction is maximal.

\begin{solution}
Let $D(f)\subseteq\Spec A$ be nonempty.  Then $A_f\neq0$.  Since
\[
A_f\cong A[T]/(fT-1),
\]
the ring $A_f$ is again a finitely generated $k$-algebra.  Choose a maximal ideal
$\mathfrak n\subset A_f$.  Its contraction
\[
\mathfrak m=\mathfrak n\cap A
\]
does not contain $f$, so $\mathfrak m\in D(f)$.

Moreover
\[
A/\mathfrak m
\hookrightarrow
A_f/\mathfrak n
\]
and the right side is a field finitely generated as a $k$-algebra.  By Zariski's lemma it
is a finite algebraic extension of $k$.  The contraction correspondence for localization
shows $\mathfrak m$ is maximal in $A$.  Hence every nonempty principal open contains a
closed point.  Since principal opens form a basis, closed points are dense in $\Spec A$.

For a closed subset $V(I)$, apply the same argument to the finitely generated algebra
$A/I$ and use
\[
\Spec(A/I)\cong V(I).
\]
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Finite-type spectra are Jacobson spaces: closed points are topologically abundant even
though they are only the maximal primes.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
When $k$ is algebraically closed, reformulate the result as density of classical
$k$-points in every affine algebraic set.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New challenge synthesizing the weak Nullstellensatz with the legacy closed-point
discussion.

\subsection*{Problem VI.06.P5 --- A hierarchy of loci in three-space}
\label{prob:vi-06-p5}

\paragraph{Problem.}
In \(A=k[x,y,z]\), consider
\[
(0)\subset (x)\subset(x,y)\subset(x,y,z).
\]
\begin{enumerate}
\item Prove that each ideal is prime and that the final one is maximal.
\item Assuming \(k\) algebraically closed, identify all four zero loci.
\item Explain how this chain previews specialization and dimension.
\end{enumerate}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Prime chains are the algebraic skeleton behind nested geometric loci and, later, Krull
dimension.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Compute the four quotient rings.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
The quotients are polynomial rings in three, two, one, and zero variables.

\paragraph{Solution.}
We have
\[
A/(0)=k[x,y,z],\quad A/(x)=k[y,z],\quad A/(x,y)=k[z],\quad A/(x,y,z)=k.
\]
The first three are domains and the last is a field, proving primality/maximality.  The loci
are respectively \(\mathbb A^3\), the plane \(x=0\), the \(z\)-axis, and the origin.

Thus each strict prime inclusion corresponds to a strict geometric specialization to a
smaller irreducible locus.  Later, the maximum lengths of such chains measure dimension.

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A chain of prime ideals simultaneously records incidence and codimension growth.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Construct the analogous chain of length \(r\) in \(k[x_1,\dots,x_r]\).

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical synthesis preparing VI/29 on Krull dimension.
\fi

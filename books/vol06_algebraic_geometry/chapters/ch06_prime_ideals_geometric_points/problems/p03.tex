\subsection*{Problem VI.06.P3 --- Maximal ideals and classical points}
\label{prob:vi-06-p3}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Assume \(k\) is algebraically closed.  Using the weak Nullstellensatz, prove that the map
\[
a=(a_1,\dots,a_n)
\longmapsto
\mathfrak m_a=(x_1-a_1,\dots,x_n-a_n)
\]
is a bijection from \(\mathbb A_k^n\) to the maximal ideals of
\(k[x_1,\dots,x_n]\).  Then explain why this bijection fails if one replaces an
algebraically closed field by \(\mathbb R\).

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
It isolates exactly what remains of ordinary point geometry inside the larger prime-ideal
picture.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Evaluation maps, the weak Nullstellensatz, and the example \((t^2+1)\subset\mathbb R[t]\).

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Every point ideal is the kernel of evaluation.  Conversely, if \(\mathfrak m\) is maximal,
look at the images of the coordinate functions in the field
\(k[x_1,\dots,x_n]/\mathfrak m\).

\paragraph{Solution.}
Evaluation at \(a\) is surjective with kernel \(\mathfrak m_a\), so each point gives a
maximal ideal.  Different points give different kernels.

Conversely, if \(\mathfrak m\) is maximal, the quotient is a field finitely generated as a
\(k\)-algebra.  The weak Nullstellensatz identifies it with \(k\).  Let \(a_i\) be the image
of \(x_i\).  Then \(x_i-a_i\in\mathfrak m\) for all \(i\), so
\[
\mathfrak m_a\subseteq\mathfrak m.
\]
Both are maximal, hence equal.

Over \(\mathbb R\), \((t^2+1)\) is maximal but is not \((t-a)\) for any real \(a\).  Thus
maximal ideals can encode closed points with residue fields larger than the base field.

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
When $k$ is algebraically closed, closed points coincide with ordinary coordinate points.
Outside that setting, the prime/maximal-ideal language is strictly richer.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Classify the maximal ideals of \(\mathbb R[t]\) and relate irreducible quadratics to
conjugate pairs of complex roots.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical synthesis from the point/maximal-ideal material distributed throughout the
legacy corpus.

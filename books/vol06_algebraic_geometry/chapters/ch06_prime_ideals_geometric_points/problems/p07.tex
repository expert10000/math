% Problem dossier: VI.06.P7
\subsection*{Problem VI.06.P7 --- Pulling prime points backward along a ring map}
\label{prob:vi-06-p7}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Let \(\varphi:A\to B\) be a unital ring homomorphism and let \(\mathfrak q\subset B\) be
prime.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Prove that \(\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)\) is prime in \(A\).
\item Apply this to the map \(k[x,y]\to k[t]\) given by \(x\mapsto t\), \(y\mapsto t^2\)
and the prime \((t-a)\subset k[t]\).
\item Compute the inverse image and interpret the corresponding point on the parabola.
\end{enumerate}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
When schemes are introduced, ring maps reverse direction to geometric maps.  The inverse
image of prime ideals is the point-level mechanism behind that reversal.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Prime ideals and kernels of evaluation.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
For (1), apply \(\varphi\) to a product.  For (2), a polynomial lies in the inverse image
exactly when its restriction \(f(t,t^2)\) vanishes at \(a\).

\paragraph{Solution.}
If \(uv\in\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)\), then
\(\varphi(u)\varphi(v)\in\mathfrak q\), so one factor lies in \(\mathfrak q\) and one of
\(u,v\) lies in the inverse image.  Thus the inverse image is prime.

For the specified map, the inverse image of \((t-a)\) is the kernel of evaluation at
\((a,a^2)\), namely
\[
(x-a,\,y-a^2).
\]
This is the maximal ideal of the point \((a,a^2)\) on the parabola \(y=x^2\).

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Prime ideals behave functorially under inverse image.  This is why a ring homomorphism will
later determine a continuous map between spectra in the opposite direction.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Compute the inverse image of \((0)\subset k[t]\); show that it is \((y-x^2)\), the prime
ideal of the entire parabola.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical bridge problem connecting VI/04 morphisms to the future spectrum functor.
\fi

% Problem dossier: VI.06.P6
\subsection*{Problem VI.06.P6 --- One real prime that becomes two complex points}
\label{prob:vi-06-p6}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Let
\[
A=\mathbb R[t],\qquad \mathfrak p=(t^2+1).
\]
\begin{enumerate}
\item Prove that \(\mathfrak p\) is maximal.
\item Show that \(V_{\mathbb R}(t^2+1)=\varnothing\) as a classical real zero set.
\item Extend scalars to \(\mathbb C\) and prove that the extended ideal is no longer prime.
\item Identify the two complex maximal ideals that appear.
\end{enumerate}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This problem gives a concrete reason to abandon the idea that geometry consists only of
base-field coordinate tuples.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Irreducible polynomials in a PID and factorization over \(\mathbb C\).

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Use \(\mathbb R[t]/(t^2+1)\cong\mathbb C\), then factor
\(t^2+1=(t-i)(t+i)\).

\paragraph{Solution.}
The quotient \(\mathbb R[t]/(t^2+1)\) is isomorphic to \(\mathbb C\), hence is a field,
so \(\mathfrak p\) is maximal.  The equation has no real root, so its classical real zero
set is empty.

After extending coefficients,
\[
\mathbb C[t]/(t^2+1)
\cong
\mathbb C[t]/((t-i)(t+i)).
\]
The ideal is not prime because the product of the two nonzero residue classes vanishes.
Since \((t-i)\) and \((t+i)\) are comaximal, the Chinese remainder theorem gives
\[
\mathbb C[t]/(t^2+1)\cong\mathbb C\times\mathbb C.
\]
Thus the real closed prime point splits into the two complex closed points
\((t-i)\) and \((t+i)\).

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Prime behavior can change under extension of the base field.  This is the elementary seed
of geometric irreducibility and base change.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Analyze an irreducible cubic over a non-algebraically closed field after passing to a
splitting field.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
Recovered from the legacy base-field counterexample material, but placed here only as a
motivating prime-as-point example; systematic base change is reserved for VI/24.
\fi

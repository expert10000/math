% Problem dossier: VI.06.P2
\subsection*{Problem VI.06.P2 --- The zero ideal as a generic geometric point}
\label{prob:vi-06-p2}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Let \(k\) be a field.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Prove that \((0)\subset k[x]\) is prime but not maximal.
\item Show that every nonzero prime ideal of \(k[x]\) is maximal.
\item If \(k\) is algebraically closed, identify the ordinary closed point corresponding
to each nonzero prime.
\item Explain why \((0)\) should represent the whole affine line generically rather than
one coordinate point.
\end{enumerate}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the smallest example in which prime ideals force a new kind of geometric point.  A
closed coordinate point is maximal; the zero prime is different and encodes the whole
irreducible line.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
The quotient-domain criterion, the PID structure of \(k[x]\), and the weak Nullstellensatz
when \(k\) is algebraically closed.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Use \(k[x]/(0)\cong k[x]\).  For nonzero primes use that every ideal is principal and a
prime generator is irreducible.

\paragraph{Solution.}
Since \(k[x]\) is a domain, \((0)\) is prime.  It is not maximal because its quotient is
\(k[x]\), not a field.

Every nonzero prime is \((f)\) with \(f\) irreducible.  In a PID generated by one
irreducible element, \((f)\) is maximal because the quotient is a field.  If \(k\) is
algebraically closed, \(f=x-a\) for some \(a\in k\), so the nonzero primes are exactly the
point ideals \((x-a)\).

The ideal \((0)\) imposes no polynomial equation.  Its associated irreducible locus is
\[
V(0)=\mathbb A_k^1.
\]
Hence it naturally represents the whole line at a generic location, not any particular
closed coordinate value.

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The distinction between prime and maximal is precisely the distinction between generic
irreducible information and closed-point information.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Generalize the zero-prime discussion to any irreducible affine algebraic set with coordinate
ring a domain.

% Problem dossier: VI.06.P4
\subsection*{Problem VI.06.P4 --- Prime correspondence for a closed algebraic subset}
\label{prob:vi-06-p4}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Let \(A=k[x,y]\) and \(I=(xy)\).  Set \(B=A/I\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item Prove that primes of \(B\) correspond to primes of \(A\) containing \((xy)\).
\item Show that every such prime contains \(x\) or \(y\).
\item Identify the two minimal primes of \(B\).
\item Assuming \(k\) algebraically closed, interpret the result geometrically on the
crossing axes.
\end{enumerate}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the first complete computation in which quotient algebra, prime ideals and
irreducible components line up exactly.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
The prime correspondence theorem and the factorization \(xy\).

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Use the quotient map \(A\to B\).  For a prime \(\mathfrak p\supset(xy)\), primality forces
one factor into \(\mathfrak p\).

\paragraph{Solution.}
By the quotient correspondence, the primes of \(B\) are \(\mathfrak p/(xy)\) for primes
\(\mathfrak p\subset A\) containing \((xy)\).  Since \(xy\in\mathfrak p\), either
\(x\in\mathfrak p\) or \(y\in\mathfrak p\).  The minimal choices are \((x)\) and \((y)\),
whose images in \(B\) are \((\bar x)\) and \((\bar y)\).

Geometrically, these are the generic prime points of the two irreducible components
\(V(x)\) and \(V(y)\).  Maximal ideals such as \((x,y-a)\) or \((x-b,y)\) represent closed
points on the axes, and \((x,y)\) represents their intersection.

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The reducible closed set does not need one generic point.  It has one minimal prime for each
irreducible component.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Repeat the analysis for \(k[x,y]/(xy(x-y))\).

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical problem combining legacy quotient/prime examples with the component theory of
VI/05.
\fi

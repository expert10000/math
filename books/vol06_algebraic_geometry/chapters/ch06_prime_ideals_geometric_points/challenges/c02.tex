\subsection*{Challenge VI.06.C2 --- Arbitrarily long prime chains in polynomial rings}
\label{chal:vi-06-c2}

\paragraph{Problem.}
For \(r\ge1\), prove that
\[
(0)\subsetneq(x_1)\subsetneq(x_1,x_2)\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq(x_1,\dots,x_r)
\]
is a strict chain of prime ideals in \(k[x_1,\dots,x_r]\).  Interpret the corresponding
chain of irreducible affine loci and explain why this suggests a numerical notion of
dimension.

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Krull dimension, developed later, is built from exactly these prime chains.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Quotient by the first \(j\) variables and identify the remaining polynomial ring.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
The quotient by \((x_1,\dots,x_j)\) is \(k[x_{j+1},\dots,x_r]\), a domain.

\paragraph{Solution.}
For each \(j<r\),
\[
k[x_1,\dots,x_r]/(x_1,\dots,x_j)
\cong k[x_{j+1},\dots,x_r],
\]
a domain.  The final quotient is \(k\), so every ideal in the chain is prime and the final
one maximal.  Strictness follows because \(x_{j+1}\) is not in the previous ideal.

Geometrically the loci are
\[
\mathbb A^r\supset\mathbb A^{r-1}\supset\cdots\supset\mathbb A^0,
\]
embedded as coordinate subspaces.  The number of strict prime inclusions measures how many
independent geometric specializations are possible, foreshadowing Krull dimension.

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Dimension emerges from the partial order on prime points, not from a metric notion of size.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Compare this chain with prime chains in quotient rings defining hypersurfaces.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical challenge preparing VI/29.
\fi

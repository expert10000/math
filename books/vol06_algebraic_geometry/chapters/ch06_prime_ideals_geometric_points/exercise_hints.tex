\paragraph{Hint for Exercise~\ref{ex:vi06-prime-quotient}.} Translate $ab\in\mathfrak p$ into $(a+\mathfrak p)(b+\mathfrak p)=0$ in the quotient.

\paragraph{Hint for Exercise~\ref{ex:vi06-maximal-prime}.} Use that a quotient by a maximal ideal is a field. For the counterexample use $(0)\subset k[t]$.

\paragraph{Hint for Exercise~\ref{ex:vi06-zero-ideal}.} Apply the quotient-domain criterion to $A/(0)\cong A$.

\paragraph{Hint for Exercise~\ref{ex:vi06-point-ideal}.} Subtract $f(a)$ and use the multivariable remainder identity, or invoke the kernel of evaluation.

\paragraph{Hint for Exercise~\ref{ex:vi06-k-t-primes}.} Use that $k[t]$ is a PID and a nonzero principal prime generator must be irreducible.

\paragraph{Hint for Exercise~\ref{ex:vi06-real-line}.} Irreducibles over $\mathbb R$ have degree one or two; irreducible quadratics have negative discriminant.

\paragraph{Hint for Exercise~\ref{ex:vi06-chain-plane}.} Compute the quotients by the three ideals; then use $I\subseteq J\Rightarrow V(J)\subseteq V(I)$.

\paragraph{Hint for Exercise~\ref{ex:vi06-order-reversal}.} One direction is elementary. For the converse use $I(V(\mathfrak p))=\mathfrak p$ for prime $\mathfrak p$ over algebraically closed $k$.

\paragraph{Hint for Exercise~\ref{ex:vi06-xy-primes}.} Since $xy\in\mathfrak p$ and $\mathfrak p$ is prime, one factor belongs to $\mathfrak p$.

\paragraph{Hint for Exercise~\ref{ex:vi06-quotient-correspondence}.} Use $(A/I)/(\mathfrak p/I)\cong A/\mathfrak p$ and inverse images under the quotient map.

\paragraph{Hint for Exercise~\ref{ex:vi06-irred-prime}.} Reuse the prime-ideal criterion from VI/05; for the reverse direction combine primality with Nullstellensatz.

\paragraph{Hint for Exercise~\ref{ex:vi06-maximal-points}.} The weak Nullstellensatz says the residue field of a maximal ideal in a finitely generated $k$-algebra is $k$.

\paragraph{Hint for Exercise~\ref{ex:vi06-Z-six}.} Prime ideals of $\mathbb Z$ are $(0)$ and $(p)$ for prime numbers $p$; containment reverses divisibility of generators.

\paragraph{Hint for Exercise~\ref{ex:vi06-radical-not-prime}.} Use $(x)\cap(y)=(xy)$ in $k[x,y]$ and the fact that an intersection of radical ideals is radical.

\paragraph{Hint for Exercise~\ref{ex:vi06-minimal-primes-cross}.} Use the quotient correspondence from primes of $k[x,y]$ containing $(xy)$.

\paragraph{Hint for Exercise~\ref{ex:vi06-preimage-prime}.} If $ab$ maps into $\mathfrak q$, then $\varphi(a)\varphi(b)\in\mathfrak q$.

\paragraph{Hint for Exercise~\ref{ex:vi06-products-ideals}.} If $I\not\subseteq\mathfrak p$, choose $a\in I\setminus\mathfrak p$ and test $ab$ for $b\in J$.

\paragraph{Hint for Exercise~\ref{ex:vi06-complex-splitting}.} Over $\mathbb C$, $t^2+1=(t-i)(t+i)$, so the quotient has zero divisors; alternatively use the Chinese remainder theorem.

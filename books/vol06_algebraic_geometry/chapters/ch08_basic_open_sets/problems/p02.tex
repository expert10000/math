\begin{problem}[Radical control of principal-open inclusion]
For $f,g\in A$, prove
\[
D(f)\subseteq D(g)
\iff
f\in\sqrt{(g)}.
\]
Deduce
\[
D(f)=D(g)
\iff
\sqrt{(f)}=\sqrt{(g)}.
\]
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Principal opens look element-dependent, but topologically they remember only the radicals
of the principal ideals.  This is the exact criterion needed whenever two different
fractions or equations produce the same open region.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Take complements and use the order reversal between ideals and closed sets.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Translate $D(f)\subseteq D(g)$ into $V(g)\subseteq V(f)$, then apply the closed-set
criterion from VI/07.

\begin{solution}
We have
\[
D(f)\subseteq D(g)
\iff
V(g)\subseteq V(f).
\]
For closed sets of a spectrum,
\[
V(J)\subseteq V(I)
\iff
\sqrt I\subseteq\sqrt J.
\]
Putting $I=(f)$ and $J=(g)$ gives
\[
D(f)\subseteq D(g)
\iff
\sqrt{(f)}\subseteq\sqrt{(g)}.
\]
Since $f\in\sqrt{(f)}$, the latter is equivalent to $f\in\sqrt{(g)}$.  Equivalently,
some power $f^N$ lies in $(g)$.

Applying this in both directions gives
\[
D(f)=D(g)
\iff
\sqrt{(f)}=\sqrt{(g)}.
\]
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Open inclusion reverses closed inclusion, which reverses ideal inclusion once more.  The
net result is a radical divisibility criterion.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Determine precisely when $D(f)=D(fg)$ and when $D(f)\subseteq D(fg)$.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical synthesis built from the legacy principal-open and radical material in
\emph{Theory of Algebraic Geometry 1}.

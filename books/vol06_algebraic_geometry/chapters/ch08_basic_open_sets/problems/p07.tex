\begin{problem}[Nested localization and nested principal opens]
Let $f,g\in A$.  Prove the canonical isomorphism
\[
(A_f)_{g/1}\cong A_{fg}.
\]
Under $\Spec A_f\cong D_A(f)$, identify $D_{A_f}(g/1)$ inside $\Spec A$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the algebraic calculus of shrinking affine neighborhoods.  It explains why finite
intersections of principal opens remain principal.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use universal properties, or construct the maps explicitly on fractions.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
If $fg$ is a unit in a commutative ring, then both $f$ and $g$ are units.

\begin{solution}
The composite map $A\to(A_f)_{g/1}$ makes both $f$ and $g$ invertible.  Conversely, if a
map $A\to B$ makes $fg$ invertible, then
\[
f\cdot\bigl(g(fg)^{-1}\bigr)=1,
\qquad
g\cdot\bigl(f(fg)^{-1}\bigr)=1,
\]
so both $f$ and $g$ are invertible.  Therefore $A_{fg}$ and $(A_f)_{g/1}$ satisfy the same
universal property, giving a canonical isomorphism.

Under the prime correspondence,
\[
D_{A_f}(g/1)
\]
consists of primes $\mathfrak p$ of $A$ that avoid both $f$ and $g$.  Hence it corresponds
to
\[
D_A(f)\cap D_A(g)=D_A(fg).
\]
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Shrinking twice is the same as inverting the product once.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Generalize to finitely many elements $f_1,\dots,f_r$.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical problem expanding the legacy one-element localization calculus.

\begin{problem}[Principal opens in product rings]
Let $R=A\times B$ and $(a,b)\in R$.  Under the standard homeomorphism
\[
\Spec(A\times B)\cong\Spec A\sqcup\Spec B,
\]
prove
\[
D_R(a,b)
\cong
D_A(a)\sqcup D_B(b).
\]
Compute the localization $R_{(1,0)}$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Product rings provide the cleanest model of disconnected affine geometry, and principal
opens interact with the two components exactly componentwise.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Every prime of $A\times B$ is either $\mathfrak p\times B$ or $A\times\mathfrak q$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Test whether $(a,b)$ belongs to each of the two types of prime ideals.

\begin{solution}
A prime of the first type is $\mathfrak p\times B$.  We have
\[
(a,b)\in\mathfrak p\times B
\iff a\in\mathfrak p.
\]
Hence the primes of this component that lie in $D_R(a,b)$ are exactly those corresponding
to $D_A(a)$.  Similarly, for a prime $A\times\mathfrak q$,
\[
(a,b)\in A\times\mathfrak q
\iff b\in\mathfrak q,
\]
so the surviving primes are $D_B(b)$.  This proves
\[
D_R(a,b)\cong D_A(a)\sqcup D_B(b).
\]

For $e=(1,0)$, localization forces $e$ to be $1$.  But $1-e=(0,1)$ then becomes $0$, so the
entire $B$-factor is killed.  Explicitly,
\[
(A\times B)_{(1,0)}\cong A.
\]
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A principal open can select one connected component by inverting its characteristic
idempotent.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Compute $D(0,1)$ and $D(a,0)$ and compare the corresponding localizations.

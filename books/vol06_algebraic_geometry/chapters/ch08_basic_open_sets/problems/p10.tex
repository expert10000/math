\begin{problem}[Finite covers and algebraic certificates]
Let $f_1,\dots,f_r\in A$.  Prove that the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\Spec A=D(f_1)\cup\cdots\cup D(f_r)$;
\item $(f_1,\dots,f_r)=A$;
\item there exist $a_1,\dots,a_r\in A$ such that
\[
a_1f_1+\cdots+a_rf_r=1.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
A finite open cover has a concrete algebraic certificate.  Later sheaf arguments on affine
schemes repeatedly exploit exactly such identities.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
A proper ideal lies in a maximal ideal, and every maximal ideal is prime.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
The cover fails precisely when there is a prime containing every $f_i$.

\begin{solution}
The complement of the union is
\[
V(f_1,\dots,f_r).
\]
Thus (i) is equivalent to $V(f_1,\dots,f_r)=\varnothing$.  If the ideal
$I=(f_1,\dots,f_r)$ were proper, it would lie in a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, and then
$\mathfrak m\in V(I)$, contradiction.  Hence (i) implies $I=A$.  Conversely, if $I=A$,
no proper prime contains $I$, so $V(I)=\varnothing$ and the principal opens cover.
Therefore (i) and (ii) are equivalent.

Finally $I=A$ exactly means $1\in I$, which by definition is equivalent to the existence of
coefficients $a_i$ satisfying
\[
1=\sum_i a_if_i.
\]
Thus all three conditions are equivalent.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Covering every prime is the same as generating the unit ideal.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Apply the criterion to $D(t)$ and $D(1-t)$ in $\Spec k[t]$ and to
$D(x),D(y),D(1-x-y)$ in $\Spec k[x,y]$.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical synthesis of the finite-cover consequences of the legacy basis theorem.

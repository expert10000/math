\begin{problem}[The general principal-open cover criterion]
Let $f\in A$ and let $\{g_\lambda\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}$ be any family of elements of $A$.
Prove
\[
D(f)\subseteq\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}D(g_\lambda)
\]
if and only if
\[
f\in\sqrt{(g_\lambda:\lambda\in\Lambda)}.
\]
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This converts an arbitrary topological cover into one explicit algebraic statement.  It is
the engine behind quasi-compactness and finite principal-open refinements.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
The complement of a union of principal opens is the common closed set of all the
$g_\lambda$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Take complements inside $\Spec A$ and then use the radical description of closed-set
containment.

\begin{solution}
Taking complements gives
\[
D(f)\subseteq\bigcup_\lambda D(g_\lambda)
\iff
\bigcap_\lambda V(g_\lambda)\subseteq V(f).
\]
But
\[
\bigcap_\lambda V(g_\lambda)
=
V(g_\lambda:\lambda\in\Lambda).
\]
Therefore the inclusion is equivalent to
\[
V(g_\lambda:\lambda\in\Lambda)\subseteq V(f).
\]
By the closed-set/radical correspondence this holds iff
\[
f\in\sqrt{(g_\lambda:\lambda\in\Lambda)}.
\]
Equivalently, $f^N$ belongs to the ideal generated by the $g_\lambda$ for some $N$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A cover is equivalent to saying that no prime can simultaneously contain every $g_\lambda$
while avoiding $f$.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Set $f=1$ and obtain the criterion for a family of principal opens to cover the whole
spectrum.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical problem consolidating the cover argument implicit in the legacy basis proof.

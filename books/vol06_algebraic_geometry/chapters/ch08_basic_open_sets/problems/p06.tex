\begin{problem}[Arithmetic principal opens]
For $0\neq n\in\ZZ$, describe $D(n)\subseteq\Spec\ZZ$ explicitly and prove
\[
D(n)\cong\Spec\ZZ[1/n].
\]
Explain what happens when $n$ is replaced by $n^m$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Arithmetic localization gives a concrete geometric picture of inverting primes: the
closed points dividing the denominator are removed.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
The prime ideals of $\ZZ$ are $(0)$ and $(p)$, and $(p)$ contains $n$ iff $p\mid n$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
List the primes that avoid $n$, then invoke P5.

\begin{solution}
The generic point $(0)$ does not contain nonzero $n$.  A closed prime $(p)$ contains $n$
iff $p\mid n$.  Therefore
\[
D(n)=\{(0)\}\cup\{(p):p\nmid n\}.
\]
By P5,
\[
D(n)\cong\Spec\ZZ_n=\Spec\ZZ[1/n].
\]
Algebraically, the rational primes dividing $n$ become units in $\ZZ[1/n]$, so the
corresponding closed points disappear.

Since $D(n^m)=D(n)$ for every $m\ge1$, replacing $n$ by a positive power changes neither
the open subset nor the set of rational primes inverted.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The topology of $\Spec\ZZ$ turns denominator arithmetic into deletion of prime-number
points.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Describe $D(30)$ explicitly and compare $\ZZ[1/30]$ with successive localizations at
$2$, $3$, and $5$.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical arithmetic synthesis from the legacy localization examples.

\begin{problem}[Challenge: algebraic gluing on a finite principal cover]
Suppose
\[
\Spec A=D(f_1)\cup\cdots\cup D(f_r).
\]
For each $i$ let $s_i\in A_{f_i}$, and assume the images of $s_i$ and $s_j$ in
$A_{f_if_j}$ agree for every pair $i,j$.  Prove that there exists a unique $a\in A$ whose
image in every $A_{f_i}$ is $s_i$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this challenge matters.}
This is the algebraic core of the sheaf gluing property on an affine spectrum.  It previews
VI/17 without requiring the full structure-sheaf formalism.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Because the cover is finite, clear denominators uniformly.  After raising the $f_i$ to one
large common power, the agreement relations can be made literal equalities in $A$.  The
powers still generate the unit ideal.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Represent all $s_i$ as $a_i/f_i^n$ for one common $n$.  Increase $n$ until
\[
f_j^n a_i=f_i^n a_j
\]
holds in $A$ for all $i,j$.  Then choose $b_i$ with $\sum_i b_if_i^n=1$.

\begin{solution}
Since there are finitely many indices, choose $n$ so that each section can be written
\[
s_i=\frac{a_i}{f_i^n}.
\]
Agreement of $s_i$ and $s_j$ in $A_{f_if_j}$ means that for some $m_{ij}$,
\[
(f_if_j)^{m_{ij}}
\bigl(f_j^n a_i-f_i^n a_j\bigr)=0.
\]
Choose $M\ge m_{ij}$ for all pairs and replace
\[
n\leftarrow n+M,
\qquad
a_i\leftarrow a_i f_i^M.
\]
The represented fractions do not change, and now
\[
f_j^n a_i=f_i^n a_j
\]
holds in $A$ for every pair.

Because the $D(f_i)$ cover $\Spec A$, the $D(f_i^n)$ also cover.  Hence
\[
(f_1^n,\dots,f_r^n)=A.
\]
Choose $b_1,\dots,b_r\in A$ with
\[
\sum_i b_i f_i^n=1.
\]
Define
\[
a=\sum_i b_i a_i.
\]
Fix $k$.  Multiplying by $f_k^n$ gives
\[
f_k^n a
=
\sum_i b_i f_k^n a_i
=
\sum_i b_i f_i^n a_k
=a_k.
\]
Therefore in $A_{f_k}$,
\[
a=\frac{a_k}{f_k^n}=s_k.
\]
So $a$ glues the family.

For uniqueness, suppose $a\in A$ maps to zero in every $A_{f_i}$.  For each $i$ some
power $f_i^{N_i}$ annihilates $a$.  Choose a common $N$ with $f_i^N a=0$ for all $i$.
The opens $D(f_i^N)=D(f_i)$ still cover, so $(f_1^N,\dots,f_r^N)=A$.  Write
$1=\sum_i c_i f_i^N$.  Then
\[
a=\sum_i c_i f_i^N a=0.
\]
Thus the glued element is unique.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Pairwise agreement of localized data can be cleared of denominators and recombined using a
unit-ideal identity.  This is the affine sheaf condition in purely algebraic form.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Rephrase the result as exactness of
\[
0\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow\prod_i A_{f_i}
\rightrightarrows
\prod_{i,j}A_{f_if_j}.
\]

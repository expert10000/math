\begin{problem}[Challenge: clopen principal opens and idempotents]
Suppose $D(f)\subseteq\Spec A$ is both open and closed.  Prove that there exists an
idempotent $e\in A$ such that
\[
D(f)=D(e).
\]
Conversely, prove that $D(e)$ is clopen for every idempotent $e$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this challenge matters.}
It links the localizing language of VI/08 back to the product-decomposition and connectedness
language of VI/05.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Use the idempotent--clopen correspondence for affine spectra.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
For the easy direction, use $e(1-e)=0$ to show $D(e)=V(1-e)$.

\begin{solution}
If $e^2=e$, then $e(1-e)=0$.  For every prime $\mathfrak p$, primality implies that
$e\in\mathfrak p$ or $1-e\in\mathfrak p$, and both cannot occur.  Therefore
\[
e\notin\mathfrak p
\iff
1-e\in\mathfrak p,
\]
so
\[
D(e)=V(1-e).
\]
Thus $D(e)$ is both open and closed.

Conversely, the idempotent--clopen theorem for spectra states that every clopen subset of
$\Spec A$ is $D(e)$ for a unique idempotent $e$ (equivalently, for the complementary pair
$e,1-e$).  Applying that theorem to the clopen set $D(f)$ gives an idempotent $e$ with
\[
D(f)=D(e).
\]
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A principal open can be a whole connected component only when, topologically, it is governed
by an idempotent rather than by an ordinary vanishing condition.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Show that if $A=A_1\times A_2$ then $D(1,0)$ and $D(0,1)$ are the two clopen components.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New challenge joining the retained connectedness/idempotent material of VI/05 to the
principal-open calculus of VI/08.

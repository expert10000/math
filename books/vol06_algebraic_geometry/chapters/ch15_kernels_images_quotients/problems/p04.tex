\begin{problem}[Smallest subsheaf containing the presheaf image]\label{prob:vi15-p4}
Show that $\im\varphi$ is the smallest subsheaf of $\mathcal G$ containing $\im(\varphi_U)$ for every open $U$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Sheafification can be characterized by a minimality property inside the target.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Translate the sheaf statement to local representatives or stalks whenever possible.  Keep track of whether the construction is sectionwise or requires sheafification.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Intersect all subsheaves containing the presheaf image, or use the local-image description.

\begin{solution}
The image sheaf contains every sectionwise image, since a section that is globally $\varphi(f)$ is certainly locally of that form.

Let $\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal G$ be any subsheaf with
\[
\im(\varphi_U)\subseteq\mathcal S(U)
\]
for every open $U$. Take $g\in(\im\varphi)(U)$. By definition there is an open cover $U=\bigcup_iV_i$ and $f_i\in\mathcal F(V_i)$ such that
\[
g|_{V_i}=\varphi_{V_i}(f_i).
\]
Hence each $g|_{V_i}$ belongs to $\mathcal S(V_i)$. These local sections agree on overlaps because they are restrictions of the same section $g$ of $\mathcal G$. Since $\mathcal S$ is a sheaf, they glue to a section $s\in\mathcal S(U)$. Under the inclusion into $\mathcal G$, the section $s$ and $g$ have the same restrictions to the cover, so locality in $\mathcal G$ gives $s=g$. Thus $g\in\mathcal S(U)$.

Therefore $\im\varphi$ is contained in every subsheaf containing the presheaf image, and hence is the smallest such subsheaf.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Formulate the same statement for sheaves of modules.  Where meaningful, test the statement on a discrete space and then on the exponential-map example to see the difference between sectionwise and local behavior.

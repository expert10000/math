\begin{problem}[Restriction commutes with image]\label{prob:vi15-p13}
Prove $(\im\varphi)|_U\cong\im(\varphi|_U)$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
The image construction is itself local.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Translate the sheaf statement to local representatives or stalks whenever possible.  Keep track of whether the construction is sectionwise or requires sheafification.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Use the local-lift description or stalks.

\begin{solution}
Use the local-lift description. Let $V\subseteq U$ and $g\in\mathcal G(V)$. The section $g$ lies in $((\im\varphi)|_U)(V)$ exactly when every point of $V$ has a neighborhood $W\subseteq V$ on which $g|_W=\varphi_W(f)$ for some $f\in\mathcal F(W)$. But this is exactly the definition of membership in $\im(\varphi|_U)(V)$. Thus the two subsheaves of $\mathcal G|_U$ have identical sections on every open $V\subseteq U$, and hence are equal.

Equivalently, both have stalk $\im\varphi_x$ at each $x\in U$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The image construction is itself local.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Formulate the same statement for sheaves of modules.  Where meaningful, test the statement on a discrete space and then on the exponential-map example to see the difference between sectionwise and local behavior.

Compare VI.15.P1 and VI.15.P2, the two preserved corpus problems.  VI/16 will reorganize these constructions into general exact-sequence language.

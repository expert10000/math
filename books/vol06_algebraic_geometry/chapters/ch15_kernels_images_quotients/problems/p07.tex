\begin{problem}[Cokernel universal property]\label{prob:vi15-p7}
Prove the universal property of $\coker\varphi$ directly from the objectwise cokernel presheaf and sheafification.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Cokernels are colimit constructions, so the sheafification step is essential.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Translate the sheaf statement to local representatives or stalks whenever possible.  Keep track of whether the construction is sectionwise or requires sheafification.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Factor maps sectionwise first, then invoke the universal property of sheafification.

\begin{solution}
Let
\[
Q^{\mathrm{pre}}(U)=\mathcal G(U)/\im\varphi_U
\]
be the cokernel presheaf and let $q^{\mathrm{pre}}:\mathcal G\to Q^{\mathrm{pre}}$ be the quotient map. By definition
\[
\coker\varphi=(Q^{\mathrm{pre}})^\#.
\]
Suppose $h:\mathcal G\to\mathcal H$ satisfies $h\varphi=0$. Define
\[
\widetilde h_U([g])=h_U(g).
\]
If $g-g'=\varphi_U(f)$, then $h_U(g-g')=(h\varphi)_U(f)=0$, so the formula is well defined. The maps commute with restrictions, giving a presheaf morphism $Q^{\mathrm{pre}}\to\mathcal H$.

Since $\mathcal H$ is already a sheaf, the universal property of sheafification yields a unique morphism
\[
\bar h:\coker\varphi\to\mathcal H
\]
whose composite with $q$ is $h$. If another morphism has the same property, both induce the same map from $Q^{\mathrm{pre}}$ to $\mathcal H$, so uniqueness of sheafification forces them to coincide. Hence $\coker\varphi$ has the desired universal property.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Cokernels are colimit constructions, so the sheafification step is essential.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Formulate the same statement for sheaves of modules.  Where meaningful, test the statement on a discrete space and then on the exponential-map example to see the difference between sectionwise and local behavior.

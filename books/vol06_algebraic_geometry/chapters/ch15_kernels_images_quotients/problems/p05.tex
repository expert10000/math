\begin{problem}[A strict image-presheaf example]\label{prob:vi15-p5}
For the exponential morphism $\mathcal C^0_{\mathbb R}\to\mathcal C^0_{S^1}$ on $S^1$, prove that the image sheaf is the whole target but the identity circle map is not in the image of global sections.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the standard concrete witness that image presheaf and image sheaf differ.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Translate the sheaf statement to local representatives or stalks whenever possible.  Keep track of whether the construction is sectionwise or requires sheafification.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Use local arguments and winding number/covering-space lifting globally.

\begin{solution}
Let
\[
E:\mathcal C^0_{\mathbb R}\longrightarrow\mathcal C^0_{S^1},\qquad E(f)=e^{2\pi if}.
\]
Fix a point $x$ and a circle-valued continuous section $g$ near $x$. After shrinking around $x$, the image of $g$ lies in an open arc of $S^1$ on which the covering map $p:\mathbb R\to S^1$, $p(t)=e^{2\pi it}$, admits a continuous inverse. Composing $g$ with that inverse gives a local real-valued lift. Thus every target section is locally in the image, and
\[
\im E=\mathcal C^0_{S^1}
\]
as sheaves.

Now consider the global section $g:S^1\to S^1$, $g(z)=z$. Suppose $g=E(f)$ for a continuous $f:S^1\to\mathbb R$. Put $F(t)=f(e^{2\pi it})$ on $[0,1]$. Then
\[
e^{2\pi iF(t)}=e^{2\pi it},
\]
so $F(t)-t$ is integer-valued. It is continuous, hence constant, say $k$. Therefore $F(1)-F(0)=1$. But $e^{2\pi i0}=e^{2\pi i1}$, so $F(0)=F(1)$ because both are the value of the same function $f$ at the same point. Contradiction. Hence the identity section is not in the global sectionwise image.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
This is the standard concrete witness that image presheaf and image sheaf differ.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Formulate the same statement for sheaves of modules.  Where meaningful, test the statement on a discrete space and then on the exponential-map example to see the difference between sectionwise and local behavior.

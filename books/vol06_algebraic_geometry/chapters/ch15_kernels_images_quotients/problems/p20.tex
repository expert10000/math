\begin{problem}[Compare two subsheaves by quotients]\label{prob:vi15-p20}
If $\mathcal F'\subseteq\mathcal F''\subseteq\mathcal F$, construct and prove the natural isomorphism $(\mathcal F/\mathcal F')/(\mathcal F''/\mathcal F')\cong\mathcal F/\mathcal F''$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Successive quotient sheaves satisfy the same local algebra as ordinary quotients.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Translate the sheaf statement to local representatives or stalks whenever possible.  Keep track of whether the construction is sectionwise or requires sheafification.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Take stalks and apply the third isomorphism theorem for abelian groups.

\begin{solution}
The quotient map $q'':\mathcal F\to\mathcal F/\mathcal F''$ kills $\mathcal F'$, so it factors through
\[
\alpha:\mathcal F/\mathcal F'\to\mathcal F/\mathcal F''.
\]
The subsheaf $\mathcal F''/\mathcal F'$ lies in $\ker\alpha$, hence the quotient universal property yields
\[
\bar\alpha:(\mathcal F/\mathcal F')/(\mathcal F''/\mathcal F')
\longrightarrow
\mathcal F/\mathcal F''.
\]
At $x$ this becomes
\[
(\mathcal F_x/\mathcal F'_x)/(\mathcal F''_x/\mathcal F'_x)
\longrightarrow
\mathcal F_x/\mathcal F''_x.
\]
This is the ordinary third isomorphism theorem for abelian groups, and therefore an isomorphism. Hence every stalk map $\bar\alpha_x$ is an isomorphism, so $\bar\alpha$ is an isomorphism of sheaves.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Successive quotient sheaves satisfy the same local algebra as ordinary quotients.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Formulate the same statement for sheaves of modules.  Where meaningful, test the statement on a discrete space and then on the exponential-map example to see the difference between sectionwise and local behavior.

\begin{problem}[Skyscraper quotients]\label{prob:vi15-p15}
For $B\subseteq A$, prove $i_x(A)/i_x(B)\cong i_x(A/B)$ and compute all stalks.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Skyscraper sheaves reduce quotient algebra to one group calculation.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Translate the sheaf statement to local representatives or stalks whenever possible.  Keep track of whether the construction is sectionwise or requires sheafification.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Only the stalk at $x$ contributes.

\begin{solution}
Let $\mathcal A=i_x(A)$ and $\mathcal B=i_x(B)$. By the quotient-stalk theorem,
\[
(\mathcal A/\mathcal B)_y\cong\mathcal A_y/\mathcal B_y.
\]
At $y=x$, this is $A/B$. At every $y\neq x$, both stalks are zero and the quotient is zero. Thus $\mathcal A/\mathcal B$ and $i_x(A/B)$ have the same stalks.

The group quotient $A\to A/B$ induces a natural morphism
\[
\mathcal A/\mathcal B\to i_x(A/B).
\]
Its stalk at $x$ is the identity of $A/B$, and away from $x$ it is the identity of the zero group. Hence it is a stalkwise isomorphism and therefore
\[
i_x(A)/i_x(B)\cong i_x(A/B).
\]
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Skyscraper sheaves reduce quotient algebra to one group calculation.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Formulate the same statement for sheaves of modules.  Where meaningful, test the statement on a discrete space and then on the exponential-map example to see the difference between sectionwise and local behavior.

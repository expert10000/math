\begin{problem}[Legacy AG1 Exercise 5: identify the image sheaf]\label{prob:vi15-p1}
Let
\[
f:\mathcal F\longrightarrow\mathcal G
\]
be a morphism of sheaves of abelian groups on a topological space $X$.  Begin with the
presheaf image
\[
\operatorname{Im}^{\mathrm{pre}}(f)(U)=\im\bigl(f_U:\mathcal F(U)\to\mathcal G(U)\bigr)
\]
and let $\im f$ denote its sheafification.  Prove that $\im f$ can be naturally identified
with the subsheaf $\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal G$ defined by
\[
\mathcal H(U)=\left\{s\in\mathcal G(U):
\begin{array}{l}
\text{for every }p\in U\text{ there is a neighborhood }V\subseteq U\text{ of }p\\
\text{and }t\in\mathcal F(V)\text{ such that }s|_V=f_V(t)
\end{array}\right\}.
\]
Deduce that
\[
(\im f)_p=\im(f_p)
\]
for every $p\in X$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the principal legacy image-sheaf problem in the corpus.  It isolates the precise
place where sheaf algebra differs from objectwise algebra: being a global image is too rigid,
whereas being \emph{locally} an image is the correct sheaf condition.  The result is used in
exactness, quotient constructions, and later cohomological arguments.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Separate the proof into four tasks: show $\mathcal H$ is a subpresheaf of $\mathcal G$; show
it is a sheaf; compare the presheaf image with $\mathcal H$ through sheafification; and then
translate the local description into germs.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $s$ is locally $f(t)$ on $U$ and $W\subseteq U$, intersect every lifting neighborhood
with $W$.
\item To glue sections of $\mathcal H$, first glue them in the ambient sheaf $\mathcal G$.
Local liftability of the result is checked inside one member of the cover.
\item There is a natural map $\operatorname{Im}^{\mathrm{pre}}(f)\to\mathcal H$.  Use the
universal property of sheafification.
\item Conversely, every section of $\mathcal H$ is locally represented by a section of the
presheaf image.  That is exactly the local description of sheafification.
\item For stalks, shrink until equality of germs becomes equality of actual sections.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
\textbf{Step 1: $\mathcal H$ is a subpresheaf.}
Let $s\in\mathcal H(U)$ and $W\subseteq U$.  For $p\in W$, choose $V\subseteq U$ and
$t\in\mathcal F(V)$ with $p\in V$ and $s|_V=f_V(t)$.  Then $V\cap W$ is a neighborhood of
$p$ in $W$ and
\[
(s|_W)|_{V\cap W}=f_{V\cap W}(t|_{V\cap W}).
\]
Thus $s|_W\in\mathcal H(W)$.

\textbf{Step 2: $\mathcal H$ is a sheaf.}
Let $U=\bigcup_iU_i$ and suppose $s_i\in\mathcal H(U_i)$ are compatible.  Since
$\mathcal G$ is a sheaf, they glue uniquely to $s\in\mathcal G(U)$.  Fix $p\in U$ and
choose $i$ with $p\in U_i$.  Since $s_i$ is locally in the image, there are
$p\in V\subseteq U_i$ and $t\in\mathcal F(V)$ such that $s_i|_V=f_V(t)$.  But
$s|_V=s_i|_V$, so $s$ is locally in the image at $p$.  Hence $s\in\mathcal H(U)$.
Uniqueness is inherited from $\mathcal G$.

\textbf{Step 3: identify $\mathcal H$ with the sheaf image.}
Every globally image section is locally image, so there is a presheaf morphism
\[
\operatorname{Im}^{\mathrm{pre}}(f)\longrightarrow\mathcal H.
\]
Since $\mathcal H$ is a sheaf, it factors uniquely through
$(\operatorname{Im}^{\mathrm{pre}}(f))^+=\im f$.  Conversely, by definition, every section
of $\mathcal H$ has an open cover on which it belongs to the presheaf image.  These local
image sections determine a section of the sheafification.  The two constructions are inverse
locally and hence globally.  Therefore $\im f\cong\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal G$.

\textbf{Step 4: stalks.}
If a germ $s_p$ lies in $(\im f)_p$, choose a representative $s$ that is locally in the
image.  After shrinking around $p$, $s=f(t)$, so $s_p=f_p(t_p)$.  Thus
$(\im f)_p\subseteq\im f_p$.  Conversely, if $s_p=f_p(t_p)$, represent the germs by
sections and shrink until $s=f(t)$.  Then $s$ is locally in the image and
$s_p\in(\im f)_p$.  Hence
\[
(\im f)_p=\im(f_p).
\]
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The global image condition was replaced by a local lifting condition.  Sheafification adds
exactly those sections that can be built from local image sections.  Consequently the image
is completely visible on stalks even when the map on global sections is not surjective.

\paragraph{Alternative approach.}
Define $\mathcal H$ first and prove directly that it is the smallest subsheaf of $\mathcal G$
containing every sectionwise image.  This minimality gives the same identification with the
sheafification of the presheaf image.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Apply the result to the exponential map of continuous-function sheaves and to the derivative
map on a circle.  In both cases the image sheaf is the target although the map on global
sections fails to be onto.

\begin{problem}[A quotient descent datum]\label{prob:vi15-p19}
Given an open cover $U=\bigcup U_i$, sections $s_i\in\mathcal F(U_i)$, and differences $s_i-s_j\in\mathcal F'(U_i\cap U_j)$, prove the classes $[s_i]$ glue to a section of $\mathcal F/\mathcal F'$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Quotient sheaves convert controlled disagreement into compatible local data.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Translate the sheaf statement to local representatives or stalks whenever possible.  Keep track of whether the construction is sectionwise or requires sheafification.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
The quotient classes agree on overlaps even when the representatives do not.

\begin{solution}
Let $q:\mathcal F\to\mathcal F/\mathcal F'$ be the quotient morphism and define
\[
\bar s_i=q(s_i)\in(\mathcal F/\mathcal F')(U_i).
\]
On $U_i\cap U_j$,
\[
s_i-s_j\in\mathcal F'(U_i\cap U_j).
\]
Since $q$ kills $\mathcal F'$, we get
\[
\bar s_i|_{U_i\cap U_j}=\bar s_j|_{U_i\cap U_j}.
\]
Thus the $\bar s_i$ are compatible sections of the quotient sheaf. By the gluing axiom there is a unique
\[
\bar s\in(\mathcal F/\mathcal F')(U)
\]
with $\bar s|_{U_i}=\bar s_i$.

The original $s_i$ need not glue in $\mathcal F$: their differences are only required to lie in $\mathcal F'$, not to vanish. This is precisely the extra global section that the quotient sheaf is allowed to contain.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Quotient sheaves convert controlled disagreement into compatible local data.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Formulate the same statement for sheaves of modules.  Where meaningful, test the statement on a discrete space and then on the exponential-map example to see the difference between sectionwise and local behavior.

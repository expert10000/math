\begin{problem}[Direct image and kernels]\label{prob:vi15-p14}
For a continuous map $f:X\to Y$, prove $f_*(\ker\varphi)=\ker(f_*\varphi)$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Right-adjoint behavior is already visible without category theory.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Translate the sheaf statement to local representatives or stalks whenever possible.  Keep track of whether the construction is sectionwise or requires sheafification.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Evaluate on an open $V\subseteq Y$: both sides are the same kernel on $f^{-1}V$.

\begin{solution}
For every open $V\subseteq Y$,
\[
(f_*\ker\varphi)(V)
=(\ker\varphi)(f^{-1}V)
=\ker\bigl(\mathcal F(f^{-1}V)\to\mathcal G(f^{-1}V)\bigr).
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\ker(f_*\varphi)(V)
=\ker\bigl((f_*\mathcal F)(V)\to(f_*\mathcal G)(V)\bigr),
\]
and by definition of direct image this is the same kernel. The identifications are compatible with restriction in $V$, so
\[
f_*(\ker\varphi)=\ker(f_*\varphi)
\]
as sheaves.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Formulate the same statement for sheaves of modules.  Where meaningful, test the statement on a discrete space and then on the exponential-map example to see the difference between sectionwise and local behavior.

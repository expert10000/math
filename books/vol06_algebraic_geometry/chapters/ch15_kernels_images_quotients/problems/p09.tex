\begin{problem}[Coimage equals image]\label{prob:vi15-p9}
Prove $\mathcal F/\ker\varphi\xrightarrow{\sim}\im\varphi$ and identify the map on every stalk.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the structural reason the category of abelian sheaves behaves like an abelian category.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Translate the sheaf statement to local representatives or stalks whenever possible.  Keep track of whether the construction is sectionwise or requires sheafification.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Use the ordinary first isomorphism theorem on each stalk.

\begin{solution}
Because $\varphi$ kills $\ker\varphi$, the quotient universal property gives a unique morphism
\[
\alpha:\mathcal F/\ker\varphi\to\mathcal G
\]
with $\alpha q=\varphi$. By construction the image of $\alpha$ lies in the image sheaf, so there is a canonical map
\[
\bar\alpha:\mathcal F/\ker\varphi\to\im\varphi.
\]
At $x$, the quotient- and image-stalk formulas identify $\bar\alpha_x$ with
\[
\mathcal F_x/\ker(\varphi_x)\longrightarrow\im(\varphi_x),\qquad [a]\mapsto\varphi_x(a).
\]
The ordinary first isomorphism theorem for abelian groups says this map is an isomorphism. Hence every stalk map $\bar\alpha_x$ is an isomorphism. By the stalkwise isomorphism criterion for sheaves,
\[
\mathcal F/\ker\varphi\xrightarrow{\sim}\im\varphi.
\]
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
This is the structural reason the category of abelian sheaves behaves like an abelian category.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Formulate the same statement for sheaves of modules.  Where meaningful, test the statement on a discrete space and then on the exponential-map example to see the difference between sectionwise and local behavior.

\begin{problem}[Quotient universal property]\label{prob:vi15-p8}
Let $\mathcal F'\subseteq\mathcal F$. Prove that morphisms $\mathcal F/\mathcal F'\to\mathcal H$ are naturally equivalent to morphisms $\mathcal F\to\mathcal H$ vanishing on $\mathcal F'$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This characterizes the quotient independently of any chosen construction.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Translate the sheaf statement to local representatives or stalks whenever possible.  Keep track of whether the construction is sectionwise or requires sheafification.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Build the factorization on the quotient presheaf, then sheafify.

\begin{solution}
Let $q:\mathcal F\to\mathcal F/\mathcal F'$ be the quotient morphism. Any morphism $u:\mathcal F/\mathcal F'\to\mathcal H$ gives $uq:\mathcal F\to\mathcal H$, and $uq$ vanishes on $\mathcal F'$ because $q$ does.

Conversely, suppose $h:\mathcal F\to\mathcal H$ vanishes on $\mathcal F'$. On the quotient presheaf define
\[
\widetilde h_U:\mathcal F(U)/\mathcal F'(U)\to\mathcal H(U),\qquad [s]\mapsto h_U(s).
\]
If $s-s'\in\mathcal F'(U)$, then $h_U(s-s')=0$, so the map is well defined. It is compatible with restrictions. Because $\mathcal H$ is a sheaf, the universal property of sheafification gives a unique
\[
\bar h:\mathcal F/\mathcal F'\to\mathcal H
\]
with $\bar hq=h$.

The two assignments are inverse by uniqueness. Therefore
\[
\Hom(\mathcal F/\mathcal F',\mathcal H)
\cong
\{h:\mathcal F\to\mathcal H\mid h|_{\mathcal F'}=0\}
\]
naturally in $\mathcal H$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Formulate the same statement for sheaves of modules.  Where meaningful, test the statement on a discrete space and then on the exponential-map example to see the difference between sectionwise and local behavior.

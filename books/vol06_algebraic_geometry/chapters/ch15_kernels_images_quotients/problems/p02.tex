\begin{problem}[Legacy AG2 Problem 8: quotient sheaves and short exact sequences]\label{prob:vi15-p2}
Let $\mathcal F'\subseteq\mathcal F$ be a subsheaf of a sheaf of abelian groups.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Define $\mathcal F/\mathcal F'$ as the sheafification of the quotient presheaf
$U\mapsto\mathcal F(U)/\mathcal F'(U)$.  Show that the natural map
\[
q:\mathcal F\longrightarrow\mathcal F/\mathcal F'
\]
has kernel $\mathcal F'$ and is surjective in the sheaf-theoretic sense, giving
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal F'\longrightarrow\mathcal F\xrightarrow{q}
\mathcal F/\mathcal F'\longrightarrow0.
\]
\item Conversely, suppose
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal F'\xrightarrow{i}\mathcal F\xrightarrow{p}
\mathcal F''\longrightarrow0
\]
is a short exact sequence of sheaves.  Show that $\mathcal F'$ is isomorphic to a subsheaf
of $\mathcal F$ and that $\mathcal F''$ is naturally isomorphic to the quotient of
$\mathcal F$ by that subsheaf.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the principal legacy quotient-sheaf problem.  Its two directions say that quotient
sheaves produce short exact sequences and, conversely, every short exact sequence is a
quotient sequence after identifying the first term with its image.  It therefore connects
the constructions of this chapter with the exact-sequence language of VI/16.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Use stalks for the quotient calculation:
\[
(\mathcal F/\mathcal F')_x\cong\mathcal F_x/\mathcal F'_x.
\]
The quotient map on each stalk is the ordinary group quotient.  For the converse, replace
$\mathcal F'$ by its image subsheaf in $\mathcal F$ and compare the quotient with
$\mathcal F''$ stalkwise.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item First prove the quotient-stalk isomorphism.
\item The kernel of $q_x$ is $\mathcal F'_x$.  Translate this back to equality of subsheaves.
\item Sheaf-surjectivity of $q$ means every stalk map $q_x$ is onto, or equivalently every
quotient section is locally represented by an $\mathcal F$-section.
\item In the converse, identify $\mathcal F'$ with $\im i\subseteq\mathcal F$.
\item The map $p$ kills this subsheaf, so it induces
$\mathcal F/\im i\to\mathcal F''$.  Check the induced map on every stalk.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
\textbf{Part I.}
Let
\[
Q^{\mathrm{pre}}(U)=\mathcal F(U)/\mathcal F'(U),
\qquad
\mathcal F/\mathcal F'=(Q^{\mathrm{pre}})^\#.
\]
The quotient maps $\mathcal F(U)\to Q^{\mathrm{pre}}(U)$ are compatible with restrictions,
so after sheafification they give $q:\mathcal F\to\mathcal F/\mathcal F'$.
For each $x$,
\[
(\mathcal F/\mathcal F')_x\cong\mathcal F_x/\mathcal F'_x,
\]
and $q_x$ is the ordinary quotient map.  Therefore $q_x$ is surjective and
\[
\ker q_x=\mathcal F'_x.
\]
The inclusion $\mathcal F'\subseteq\ker q$ is clear.  Since both subsheaves have the same
stalk at every point, they are equal.  Thus $\ker q=\mathcal F'$.  Surjectivity of every
$q_x$ means every germ of the quotient has a local lift, exactly the sheaf-theoretic
surjectivity required.  Hence
\[
0\to\mathcal F'\to\mathcal F\to\mathcal F/\mathcal F'\to0
\]
is exact.

\textbf{Part II.}
Suppose
\[
0\to\mathcal F'\xrightarrow{i}\mathcal F\xrightarrow{p}\mathcal F''\to0
\]
is exact.  Since $i$ is a monomorphism, it identifies $\mathcal F'$ with the subsheaf
$\mathcal I:=\im i\subseteq\mathcal F$.  Because $p\circ i=0$, the map $p$ kills
$\mathcal I$ and therefore factors through a morphism
\[
\alpha:\mathcal F/\mathcal I\longrightarrow\mathcal F''.
\]
At $x$, exactness gives
\[
\ker p_x=\im i_x=\mathcal I_x
\]
and $p_x$ is onto.  The ordinary first isomorphism theorem yields
\[
\mathcal F_x/\mathcal I_x\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal F''_x.
\]
But the left side is $(\mathcal F/\mathcal I)_x$, so every $\alpha_x$ is an isomorphism.
Hence $\alpha$ is an isomorphism of sheaves.  Thus
\[
\mathcal F'\cong\mathcal I\subseteq\mathcal F,
\qquad
\mathcal F''\cong\mathcal F/\mathcal I.
\]
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Quotients are determined locally by ordinary quotient groups.  The sheafification step does
not alter those local quotients, but it repairs the global gluing behavior.  Consequently
short exact quotient sequences have exactly the algebra expected on every stalk.

\paragraph{Alternative approach.}
Use the universal property of the quotient sheaf instead of stalks: any morphism out of
$\mathcal F$ that kills $\mathcal F'$ factors uniquely through $\mathcal F/\mathcal F'$.
The converse then follows by applying this factorization to $p$ and constructing an inverse
locally.  The stalk proof is shorter and exposes the local algebra directly.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Repeat the construction for sheaves of modules and for a sheaf of ideals
$\mathcal I\subseteq\mathcal O_X$.  The quotient $\mathcal O_X/\mathcal I$ is the local
algebraic prototype for a closed subscheme.

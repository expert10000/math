\begin{problem}[Holomorphic logarithms]\label{prob:vi15-p18}
For $\exp:\mathcal O\to\mathcal O^\times$ on $\mathbb C^\times$, prove local surjectivity and show the global section $z$ has no holomorphic logarithm.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Analytic continuation gives another natural source of image-sheaf enlargement.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Translate the sheaf statement to local representatives or stalks whenever possible.  Keep track of whether the construction is sectionwise or requires sheafification.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Use a small simply connected disk for local logarithms; integrate $dz/z$ or use winding number globally.

\begin{solution}
Let $g$ be a nowhere-zero holomorphic function near $x\in\mathbb C^\times$. Shrink to a simply connected disk $D$ on which $g$ has no zeros. Then $g'/g$ is holomorphic on $D$ and has a primitive $h_0$. The function $ge^{-h_0}$ has derivative zero, hence is a nonzero constant $c$. Choose one logarithm of $c$ and add it to $h_0$ to obtain $h$ with $e^h=g$. Thus the exponential morphism is locally surjective.

Suppose the global section $z$ had a holomorphic logarithm $h$ on $\mathbb C^\times$, so $e^h=z$. Differentiating gives
\[
h'(z)=\frac1z.
\]
The derivative of a globally defined holomorphic function integrates to zero around every closed curve, but
\[
\int_{|z|=1}\frac{dz}{z}=2\pi i\neq0.
\]
Contradiction. Hence $z$ is locally but not globally in the exponential image.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Formulate the same statement for sheaves of modules.  Where meaningful, test the statement on a discrete space and then on the exponential-map example to see the difference between sectionwise and local behavior.

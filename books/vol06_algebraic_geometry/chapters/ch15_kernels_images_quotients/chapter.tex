\section*{Purpose and learning goals}

Sheafification repaired a presheaf without changing its germs.  We now need that repair in
ordinary algebraic constructions.  If
\[
\varphi:\F\longrightarrow\G
\]
is a morphism of sheaves of abelian groups, the sectionwise kernel is already a sheaf, but
the sectionwise image, cokernel, and quotient need not be.  Their correct sheaf-theoretic
versions are obtained by imposing a local condition or, equivalently, by sheafifying the
corresponding presheaves.

This distinction is small in notation and fundamental in practice.  A section may be locally
in the image of a morphism on every neighborhood and still have no single global preimage.
Thus the image sheaf can be strictly larger on an open set than the image of the map on that
open set.  Quotients exhibit the same phenomenon.

The Volume VI legacy-problem ledger is the starting point for this reconstruction.  The two
principal numbered corpus items are AG1 Exercise 5, on identifying the image sheaf with the
subsheaf of locally liftable sections, and AG2 Problem 8, on quotient sheaves and the resulting
short exact sequence.  Both are preserved as full detailed dossiers, VI.15.P1 and VI.15.P2.
The preferred reorganized sheaf notes are merged with them and with the chapter theory rather
than counted as duplicate problems.

After this chapter, the reader should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
\item compute the kernel sheaf sectionwise and prove its universal property;
\item distinguish the presheaf image from the image sheaf;
\item characterize image-sheaf sections by local liftability;
\item define cokernel and quotient sheaves by sheafification;
\item prove the stalk identities
\[
(\ker\varphi)_x=\ker(\varphi_x),\quad
(\im\varphi)_x=\im(\varphi_x),\quad
(\coker\varphi)_x=\coker(\varphi_x);
\]
\item prove
\[
(\F/\F')_x\cong\F_x/\F'_x
\]
for every subsheaf $\F'\subseteq\F$;
\item use the universal properties of kernels, quotients, and cokernels;
\item explain why a sheaf epimorphism need not be surjective on sections over every open set;
\item identify the canonical coimage-to-image map and understand why it is an isomorphism for
sheaves of abelian groups;
\item keep general exactness and the stalkwise exactness criterion for VI/16 while using the
legacy quotient problem here as a motivating application.
\end{itemize}

\section{Kernels are computed sectionwise}

Let $\varphi:\F\to\G$ be a morphism of sheaves of abelian groups on $X$.
For every open $U\subseteq X$ define
\[
(\ker\varphi)(U):=\ker\bigl(\varphi_U:\F(U)\to\G(U)\bigr).
\]
Restrictions preserve kernels because $\varphi$ commutes with restriction maps.

\begin{theorem}[Kernel sheaf]
The presheaf $U\mapsto\ker(\varphi_U)$ is a sheaf.  With its natural inclusion
$\ker\varphi\hookrightarrow\F$, it is the categorical kernel of $\varphi$ in the category
of sheaves of abelian groups.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $U=\bigcup_iU_i$ and $s_i\in\ker(\varphi)(U_i)$ are compatible.  Since $\F$ is a
sheaf, they glue uniquely to $s\in\F(U)$.  On every $U_i$,
\[
\varphi_U(s)|_{U_i}=\varphi_{U_i}(s_i)=0.
\]
By locality in $\G$, $\varphi_U(s)=0$, so $s\in\ker(\varphi)(U)$.  Uniqueness is inherited
from $\F$.  For the universal property, any $h:\Hcal\to\F$ with $\varphi h=0$ has every
$h_U$ landing in $\ker(\varphi_U)$, giving a unique factorization through the kernel sheaf.
\end{proof}

\section{The image presheaf and the local image sheaf}

The most tempting definition is
\[
\operatorname{Im}^{\mathrm{pre}}(\varphi)(U)
:=\im\bigl(\varphi_U:\F(U)\to\G(U)\bigr).
\]
This is a subpresheaf of $\G$, but it need not be a sheaf.  The obstruction is global:
compatible target sections may have local preimages without those preimages being capable of
forming one global source section.

\begin{definition}[Image sheaf]
The \emph{image sheaf} of $\varphi$ is
\[
\im\varphi:=\bigl(\operatorname{Im}^{\mathrm{pre}}(\varphi)\bigr)^\#.
\]
Equivalently, it is the following subsheaf of $\G$:
\[
(\im\varphi)(U)=
\left\{g\in\G(U):
\begin{array}{l}
\text{for every }x\in U\text{ there is an open }V\subseteq U\text{ with }x\in V\\
\text{and }f\in\F(V)\text{ such that }g|_V=\varphi_V(f)
\end{array}
\right\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Locally in the image]
The locally liftable sections above form a subsheaf of $\G$, and this subsheaf is canonically
isomorphic to the sheafification of the image presheaf.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Restriction preserves local liftability.  Compatible locally liftable sections glue in
$\G$; local liftability of the glued section is checked point by point inside one member of
the cover.  Thus they form a subsheaf $\Hcal\subseteq\G$.  The presheaf image maps into
$\Hcal$.  Since $\Hcal$ is a sheaf, the map factors through the sheafification.  Conversely,
every section of $\Hcal$ is locally represented by a section of the presheaf image, which is
exactly the local description of its sheafification.  The two maps are inverse locally and
hence globally.
\end{proof}

\section{Images on stalks}

\begin{theorem}[Image stalk]
For every $x\in X$ there is a canonical equality of subgroups of $\G_x$,
\[
(\im\varphi)_x=\im(\varphi_x:\F_x\to\G_x).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A germ in the image-sheaf stalk is represented near $x$ by a section that is locally in the
image, so after shrinking it is literally $\varphi(f)$ and its germ lies in $\im\varphi_x$.
Conversely, if $g_x=\varphi_x(f_x)$, choose representatives and shrink until the equality of
germs becomes equality of sections.  Then the representative of $g_x$ is locally in the
image, so $g_x\in(\im\varphi)_x$.
\end{proof}

Together with the sectionwise definition of the kernel,
\[
(\ker\varphi)_x=\ker(\varphi_x).
\]
This is the first indication that sheaf algebra is ordinary algebra when viewed through one
stalk at a time.

\section{Quotient presheaves and quotient sheaves}

Let $\F'\subseteq\F$ be a subsheaf.  Sectionwise algebra gives a quotient presheaf
\[
\Qcal^{\mathrm{pre}}(U)=\F(U)/\F'(U).
\]
It can fail to be a sheaf, so the correct quotient is its sheafification.

\begin{definition}[Quotient sheaf]
\[
\F/\F':=\bigl(U\longmapsto\F(U)/\F'(U)\bigr)^\#.
\]
The composite
\[
\F\longrightarrow\Qcal^{\mathrm{pre}}\longrightarrow\F/\F'
\]
is the canonical quotient morphism $q$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Stalks of a quotient]
For every $x\in X$ there is a natural isomorphism
\[
(\F/\F')_x\cong\F_x/\F'_x.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Sheafification preserves stalks, so the left side is the stalk of the quotient presheaf.
Filtered colimits of abelian groups are exact, hence localization to the stalk commutes with
the cokernel of $\F'(U)\to\F(U)$.  Concretely, a germ of a quotient section is represented
by $[s]\in\F(U)/\F'(U)$ and is sent to the class of $s_x$ in $\F_x/\F'_x$.  Equality of
classes is verified after shrinking, which proves injectivity as well as surjectivity.
\end{proof}

\section{Cokernels}

For a general morphism $\varphi:\F\to\G$, define the cokernel presheaf by
\[
U\longmapsto\coker(\varphi_U)=\G(U)/\im(\varphi_U).
\]
Its sheafification is the cokernel sheaf:
\[
\coker\varphi:=
\bigl(U\longmapsto\coker(\varphi_U)\bigr)^\#.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\coker\varphi\cong\G/\im\varphi.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Cokernel universal property]
If $h:\G\to\Hcal$ satisfies $h\varphi=0$, then there is a unique morphism
$\bar h:\coker\varphi\to\Hcal$ with $h=\bar h\circ q$.  Moreover,
\[
(\coker\varphi)_x\cong\coker(\varphi_x).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Sectionwise, $h_U$ kills $\im\varphi_U$ and therefore factors through the cokernel presheaf.
The target is already a sheaf, so the universal property of sheafification gives a unique
factorization through its sheafification.  Stalk preservation and the filtered-colimit
calculation above give the stalk formula.
\end{proof}

\section{The quotient by a subsheaf}

The quotient map $q:\F\to\F/\F'$ has stalk map
\[
q_x:\F_x\longrightarrow\F_x/\F'_x.
\]
Hence it is locally onto in the precise sheaf-theoretic sense, and its kernel sheaf is
$\F'$.  This yields the short sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\F'\longrightarrow\F\longrightarrow\F/\F'\longrightarrow0.
\]
The full theory of exact sequences and the criterion for exactness on all stalks are the
subject of VI/16.  Here the sequence is retained because it is the mathematical center of
legacy AG2 Problem 8.

\section{Sheaf surjectivity is local, not sectionwise}

A morphism $\varphi:\F\to\G$ can have image sheaf equal to $\G$ while
\[
\F(U)\longrightarrow\G(U)
\]
is not surjective for some open $U$.  Equivalently, every target germ can lift while some
global target section has no global lift.

The basic model is the exponential map of function sheaves.  On a circle, every circle-valued
continuous function has a local real-valued argument, but a degree-one map has no global
continuous argument.  Thus local liftability and global liftability are genuinely different.

\input{chapters/ch15_kernels_images_quotients/figures/vi15_local_global_lift.tex}

\begin{example}[Quotient sheaf]\label{ex:vi15-expand-03}
Similarly, the naive quotient presheaf $U\mapsto G(U)/F(U)$ can fail gluing.  The cokernel in sheaves is its sheafification, while stalks compute the expected module quotient.
\end{example}

\section{Coimage and image}

Define the coimage by
\[
\Coim(\varphi):=\F/\ker\varphi.
\]
There is a canonical morphism
\[
\Coim(\varphi)\longrightarrow\im\varphi.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Coimage equals image for sheaves of abelian groups]
The canonical morphism
\[
\F/\ker\varphi\xrightarrow{\sim}\im\varphi
\]
is an isomorphism.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At $x$ it becomes the ordinary first-isomorphism-theorem map
\[
\F_x/\ker(\varphi_x)\xrightarrow{\sim}\im(\varphi_x).
\]
A morphism of sheaves that is an isomorphism on every stalk is an isomorphism.  The latter
criterion was established in VI/13; VI/16 will place it into the general language of exactness.
\end{proof}

\section{Restriction and locality of the constructions}

If $j:U\hookrightarrow X$ is open, restriction commutes with all four constructions:
\[
(\ker\varphi)|_U\cong\ker(\varphi|_U),\qquad
(\im\varphi)|_U\cong\im(\varphi|_U),
\]
\[
(\coker\varphi)|_U\cong\coker(\varphi|_U),\qquad
(\F/\F')|_U\cong(\F|_U)/(\F'|_U).
\]
For kernels this is sectionwise.  For the others it follows either from the local
description or from sheafification commuting with restriction.

\section{Common pitfalls}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The sectionwise image $U\mapsto\im\varphi_U$ is a presheaf, not necessarily a sheaf.
\item The sectionwise quotient $U\mapsto\F(U)/\F'(U)$ is a presheaf, not necessarily a sheaf.
\item ``Surjective morphism of sheaves'' does not mean surjective on sections over every open
set; the correct notion is local liftability, equivalently surjectivity on stalks.
\item Kernels are different: they are already computed sectionwise because the sheaf axiom
passes to a subgroup defined by an equation.
\item Do not replace $\F/\F'$ by a sectionwise quotient in later exact-sequence arguments
unless an independent reason shows that quotient presheaf is already a sheaf.
\item The identity $\coker\varphi\cong\G/\im\varphi$ uses the \emph{image sheaf}, not merely
the image presheaf.
\item General stalkwise exactness belongs to VI/16.  The stalk computations here are the
algebraic ingredients for that theorem.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Summary}

For a morphism of sheaves $\varphi:\F\to\G$, kernels are already local enough to be
computed sectionwise, while images, quotients, and cokernels generally require
sheafification:
\[
(\ker\varphi)(U)=\ker\varphi_U,
\qquad
\im\varphi=(U\mapsto\im\varphi_U)^\#,
\]
\[
\F/\F'=(U\mapsto\F(U)/\F'(U))^\#,
\qquad
\coker\varphi=(U\mapsto\coker\varphi_U)^\#.
\]
At every point the constructions become ordinary algebra:
\[
(\ker\varphi)_x=\ker\varphi_x,
\quad
(\im\varphi)_x=\im\varphi_x,
\quad
(\coker\varphi)_x=\coker\varphi_x,
\quad
(\F/\F')_x=\F_x/\F'_x.
\]
AG1 Exercise 5 and AG2 Problem 8 are preserved here as VI.15.P1 and VI.15.P2.  The updated
Volume VI ledger records that migration explicitly so neither is lost when VI/16 develops
the general theory of exact sequences.

\subsection*{Proofs}

\begin{exercise}[Kernel sheaf]\label{exr:vi15-expand-06}
Prove: Prove the presheaf kernel is a sheaf.
\end{exercise}
\begin{hint}
Work directly from sheaf kernels, images, cokernels, and stalkwise algebra.
\end{hint}
\begin{solution}
Compatible local kernel sections glue in $F$, and their images glue to zero in $G$, so the global glue stays in the kernel.
\end{solution}

\begin{exercise}[Stalk exactness]\label{exr:vi15-expand-07}
Prove: Prove taking stalks commutes with kernels and cokernels for sheaves of modules.
\end{exercise}
\begin{hint}
Work directly from sheaf kernels, images, cokernels, and stalkwise algebra.
\end{hint}
\begin{solution}
Stalks are filtered colimits, which are exact in modules.
\end{solution}

\begin{exercise}[Image stalk]\label{exr:vi15-expand-08}
Prove: Prove the stalk of the sheaf image equals the image of the stalk map.
\end{exercise}
\begin{hint}
Work directly from sheaf kernels, images, cokernels, and stalkwise algebra.
\end{hint}
\begin{solution}
Sheafification preserves stalks, and the presheaf image germ condition is local.
\end{solution}

\begin{exercise}[Quotient stalk]\label{exr:vi15-expand-09}
Prove: Prove $(G/F)_x\cong G_x/F_x$ for a subsheaf of modules.
\end{exercise}
\begin{hint}
Work directly from sheaf kernels, images, cokernels, and stalkwise algebra.
\end{hint}
\begin{solution}
Use exactness of stalks on $0\to F\to G\to G/F\to0$.
\end{solution}

\subsection*{Applications and investigations}

\begin{exercise}[Local exactness]\label{exr:vi15-expand-13}
Why are stalks efficient for kernel/image questions?
\end{exercise}
\begin{hint}
Translate the question into sheaf kernels, images, cokernels, and stalkwise algebra.
\end{hint}
\begin{solution}
They turn sheaf operations into ordinary module operations at each point.
\end{solution}

\begin{exercise}[Quotient geometry]\label{exr:vi15-expand-14}
What does a quotient sheaf encode?
\end{exercise}
\begin{hint}
Translate the question into sheaf kernels, images, cokernels, and stalkwise algebra.
\end{hint}
\begin{solution}
It measures local sections modulo a subsheaf, with gluing repaired sheaf-theoretically.
\end{solution}

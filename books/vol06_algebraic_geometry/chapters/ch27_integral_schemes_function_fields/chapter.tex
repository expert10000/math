\chapter{Integral Schemes and Function Fields}\label{ch:vi27-integral-function-fields}

The finiteness conditions of VI/26 tell us when a scheme is built from finitely many algebraic coordinates.  We now
isolate the class on which one can speak unambiguously about a single field of rational functions.  An integral
scheme is simultaneously reduced and irreducible.  Its unique generic point, constructed in VI/09, carries a
field as its local ring; that field is the function field of the entire scheme.

\section{Integral schemes}
\begin{definition}[Integral scheme]\label{def:vi27-integral}
A scheme $X$ is \emph{integral} if it is nonempty, reduced, and irreducible.  Equivalently, every nonempty affine
open $U=\Spec A\subseteq X$ has $A$ an integral domain.
\end{definition}

The nonempty qualifier is important when one phrases the condition using rings of sections: the zero ring on the
empty open is not treated as an integral domain.

\begin{theorem}[Reduced plus irreducible]\label{thm:vi27-reduced-irreducible}
For a nonempty scheme $X$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $X$ is integral;
\item $X$ is reduced and irreducible;
\item every nonempty affine open $\Spec A\subseteq X$ has $A$ a domain;
\item for every nonempty open $U\subseteq X$, the ring $\OO_X(U)$ is a domain.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\section{The generic point revisited}
An integral scheme is irreducible, so by VI/09 it has a unique generic point $\eta$ with
\[
\overline{\{\eta\}}=X.
\]
Every nonempty open subset contains $\eta$.  Consequently every nonempty open subset of an integral scheme is
again integral and dense.

\begin{proposition}[Generic point on affine charts]\label{prop:vi27-generic-affine}
If $X$ is integral and $U=\Spec A\subseteq X$ is nonempty affine, then $A$ is a domain and the generic point
$\eta$ corresponds to the prime ideal $(0)\subset A$.
\end{proposition}

\section{The function field}
\begin{definition}[Function field]\label{def:vi27-function-field}
Let $X$ be integral with generic point $\eta$.  Its \emph{function field} is
\[
K(X):=\OO_{X,\eta}.
\]
\end{definition}

Because $\eta$ is the zero prime on every nonempty affine chart $U=\Spec A$, localization gives
\[
K(X)\cong A_{(0)}=\Frac(A).
\]
Thus the field is independent of the affine chart used to compute it.

\begin{theorem}[Affine computation of $K(X)$]\label{thm:vi27-affine-function-field}
For every nonempty affine open $U=\Spec A\subseteq X$ of an integral scheme,
\[
K(X)\cong\Frac(A).
\]
If $V=\Spec B$ is another nonempty affine open, then $\Frac(A)$ and $\Frac(B)$ are canonically identified through
$\OO_{X,\eta}$.
\end{theorem}

\section{Rational functions as germs on dense opens}
A rational function may be represented by a pair $(U,s)$ where $U\subseteq X$ is nonempty open and
$s\in\OO_X(U)$.  Two representatives $(U,s)$ and $(V,t)$ are equivalent if they agree on some nonempty open
subset of $U\cap V$.  On an integral scheme this equivalence class is exactly a germ at the generic point and
hence an element of $K(X)$.

\begin{proposition}[Sections embed in the function field]\label{prop:vi27-sections-embed}
For every nonempty open $U\subseteq X$ of an integral scheme, restriction to the generic stalk induces an
injective ring map
\[
\OO_X(U)\hookrightarrow K(X).
\]
Hence two regular functions on $U$ that agree on a nonempty open subset are equal on all of $U$.
\end{proposition}

\section{Regularity of a rational function at a point}
After identifying $\OO_{X,x}$ with a subring of $K(X)$, a rational function $r\in K(X)$ is \emph{regular at $x$}
when $r\in\OO_{X,x}$.  On an affine chart $U=\Spec A$ with $x\leftrightarrow\mathfrak p$, writing
$r=a/b\in\Frac(A)$, this means that $r$ admits a representation with denominator not in $\mathfrak p$.

\begin{proposition}[Regular locus]\label{prop:vi27-regular-locus}
For $r\in K(X)$, the set of points at which $r$ is regular is open and contains the generic point.  In particular,
every rational function is represented by an honest regular function on some dense open subset.
\end{proposition}

\section{Dominant morphisms and function fields}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism of integral schemes.  If $f$ is dominant, then $f(\eta_X)=\eta_Y$.  Passing to the
local maps at generic points yields a homomorphism of fields
\[
f^*:K(Y)\longrightarrow K(X).
\]
Every unital map of fields is injective.

\begin{theorem}[Contravariance under dominance]\label{thm:vi27-dominant-field-map}
A dominant morphism $f:X\to Y$ of integral schemes induces a canonical field embedding
\[
f^*:K(Y)\hookrightarrow K(X).
\]
Composition of dominant morphisms corresponds to composition of the induced embeddings in the opposite direction.
\end{theorem}

\section{Standard function fields}
For affine $n$-space over a field $k$,
\[
K(\mathbb A^n_k)=k(t_1,\dots,t_n).
\]
For an irreducible affine hypersurface $X=\Spec k[x_1,\dots,x_n]/(F)$ with $(F)$ prime, the function field is the
fraction field of its coordinate ring.  For the parabola $y=x^2$, this fraction field is $k(x)$ even though the
chosen affine presentation uses two coordinates.

\input{chapters/ch27_integral_schemes_function_fields/figures/figure_07}

\section{Finite-type integral schemes and transcendence degree}
Suppose $X$ is integral and of finite type over $k$.  If $U=\Spec A\subseteq X$ is nonempty affine, then $A$ is a
finitely generated $k$-domain and
\[
K(X)=\Frac(A).
\]
Therefore $\operatorname{trdeg}_kK(X)$ is a chart-independent invariant.  The AG8-P2c legacy slice uses exactly
this invariant.  The deeper theorem
\[
\dim X=\operatorname{trdeg}_kK(X)
\]
belongs to the dimension arc and is proved canonically in VI/30; VI/27 records the function-field side without
consuming that later proof.

\input{chapters/ch27_integral_schemes_function_fields/figures/figure_08}

\section{What integrality does and does not say}
Integrality is global: having all local rings domains does not by itself force irreducibility.  For example,
$\Spec k\amalg\Spec k$ has domain local rings at every point but is not irreducible.  Conversely, an irreducible
scheme may fail to be integral if it is nonreduced, as with $\Spec k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$.

The affine scheme
\[
\Spec k[x,y]/(xy)
\]
is reduced but reducible, hence not integral.  This is the exact counterexample reserved from AG6-P6c.

\section{Boundary with normalization and dimension}
VI/27 stops at the field of rational functions and its basic functoriality.  VI/28 will enlarge an integral scheme
inside its own function field by integral closure, producing the normalization.  VI/29--VI/32 then study chains of
prime ideals, dimension, codimension, and local geometry.  Rational maps and birational geometry will use the
language developed here later in the volume.

\section{Chapter synthesis}
An integral scheme has one generic point and one ambient field of rational functions.  Every nonempty affine chart
is a domain, every nonempty open set is dense, and every regular section embeds into the same field $K(X)$.  This
single field is what makes later normalization and birational constructions possible: local coordinate rings can
all be compared inside one common algebraic object.

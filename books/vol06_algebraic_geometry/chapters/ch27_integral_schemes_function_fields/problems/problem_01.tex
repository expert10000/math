\begin{problem}[Legacy AG5-P3: integral iff reduced and irreducible]\label{prob:vi27-p01}
Let $X$ be a nonempty scheme.  Prove the legacy theorem that $X$ is integral if and only if it is reduced and irreducible.  Reconcile the source formulation using domains of sections on nonempty opens with the affine-local formulation.
\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{Provenance.} Numbered legacy AG5-P3; full theorem preserved.
\end{problem}
\begin{solution}
If $X$ is integral, every nonempty affine open has domain coordinate ring, hence is reduced and irreducible; these local statements imply $X$ is reduced, and two disjoint nonempty opens would give a product ring of sections with zero divisors.  Conversely, if $X$ is reduced and irreducible, every nonempty affine open $U=\Spec A$ is reduced and irreducible.  Thus the nilradical of $A$ is both zero and prime, so $A$ is a domain.  Finally, for a nonempty open $W$, cover by affine domains and use irreducibility: if $st=0$ with nonzero $s,t\in\OO_X(W)$, the nonvanishing loci of $s$ and $t$ are nonempty opens and must intersect, where a local domain would force one germ to vanish, a contradiction.  Hence $\OO_X(W)$ is a domain.
\end{solution}

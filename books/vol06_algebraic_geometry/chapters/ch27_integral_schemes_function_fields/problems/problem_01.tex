\begin{problem}[Legacy AG5-P3: integral iff reduced and irreducible]\label{prob:vi27-p01}
Let \(X\) be a nonempty scheme. Prove the legacy theorem that \(X\) is integral if and only if it is reduced and irreducible. Reconcile the source formulation using domains of sections on nonempty opens with the affine-local formulation.
\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{Provenance.} Numbered legacy AG5-P3; full theorem preserved.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
If \(X\) is integral, reducedness and irreducibility are part of the definition. Let
\(U=\Spec A\subseteq X\) be nonempty affine. Then \(U\) is reduced and irreducible. Reducedness gives
\(\sqrt{(0)}=(0)\), while irreducibility of \(\Spec A\) says \(\sqrt{(0)}\) is prime. Hence \((0)\) is prime and
\(A\) is a domain.

Conversely, assume \(X\) is irreducible and has an affine cover by spectra of domains. Every such chart is reduced,
so \(X\) is reduced because reducedness is local. Thus \(X\) is integral.

Now let \(W\subseteq X\) be nonempty. We prove \(\mathcal O_X(W)\) is a domain. Suppose \(st=0\). If both \(s\)
and \(t\) were nonzero, each would be nonzero on some nonempty affine subopen. On an affine-domain chart the
nonvanishing locus of a nonzero section contains a nonempty principal open. Irreducibility forces the two
nonvanishing opens to meet, but on a common affine domain chart the product of two nonzero functions cannot be
zero. Contradiction. Hence one of \(s,t\) is zero. Therefore all three formulations agree.
\end{solution}
\fi

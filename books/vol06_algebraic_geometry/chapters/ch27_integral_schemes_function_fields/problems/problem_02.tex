\begin{problem}[Legacy AG6-P6c: a scheme that is not integral]\label{prob:vi27-p02}
Give an explicit scheme that is not integral and explain exactly which part of integrality fails.
\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{Provenance.} AG6 Problem 6, integral-scheme subpart; other subparts remain in their assigned chapters.
\end{problem}
\begin{solution}
Take $X=\Spec k[x,y]/(xy)$.  The classes of $x$ and $y$ are nonzero and have product zero, so the coordinate ring is not a domain.  Geometrically $X=V(x)\cup V(y)$ is reducible.  The ideal $(xy)$ is radical in $k[x,y]$, so $X$ is reduced; thus the failure is irreducibility (equivalently the domain condition), not reducedness.
\end{solution}

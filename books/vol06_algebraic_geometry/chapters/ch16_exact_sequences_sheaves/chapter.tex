\section*{Purpose and learning goals}

The preceding chapters built the local algebra of sheaves: germs and stalks, sheafification,
and the correct kernel, image, quotient, and cokernel constructions.  Exact sequences are the
language that assembles those constructions into a reusable calculus.

For sheaves of abelian groups, exactness is fundamentally local.  A sequence
\[
\cdots\longrightarrow\F_{i-1}\xrightarrow{d_{i-1}}\F_i
\xrightarrow{d_i}\F_{i+1}\longrightarrow\cdots
\]
is exact precisely when every induced sequence of stalks is exact.  This theorem is the
principal legacy problem AG1 Exercise 6 and is preserved in full as VI.16.P1.  The second
principal corpus problem, AG2 Problem 7, proves that a morphism of sheaves is an isomorphism
exactly when it is injective and surjective in the sheaf-theoretic sense; it is preserved as
VI.16.P2.  The preferred reorganized sheaf notes contain parallel treatments and are merged
into the theory and these dossiers rather than counted as duplicate problems.

After this chapter, the reader should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
\item define exactness at a term using the image and kernel sheaves;
\item prove and use the stalkwise exactness criterion;
\item recognize monomorphisms and epimorphisms by their stalk maps;
\item distinguish a sheaf epimorphism from surjectivity on sections over every open set;
\item work with short exact sequences and quotient presentations;
\item prove that restriction to an open subset preserves exactness;
\item understand the left exactness of the section functor without prematurely developing
cohomology;
\item package failure of exactness into homology sheaves;
\item use local lifting arguments in continuous, smooth, and holomorphic examples;
\item read exactness diagrams stalkwise and perform basic diagram chases.
\end{itemize}

\section{Exactness at a term}

Let
\[
\F_{i-1}\xrightarrow{d_{i-1}}\F_i\xrightarrow{d_i}\F_{i+1}
\]
be morphisms of sheaves of abelian groups.

\begin{definition}[Exactness at a term]
The sequence is \emph{exact at $\F_i$} if
\[
\im(d_{i-1})=\ker(d_i)
\]
as subsheaves of $\F_i$.
\end{definition}

This equality automatically implies
\[
d_i\circ d_{i-1}=0.
\]
The converse is weaker: $d_i d_{i-1}=0$ gives only
\[
\im(d_{i-1})\subseteq\ker(d_i).
\]
Exactness says that every local solution of $d_i(s)=0$ is locally produced by the preceding
map.

\input{chapters/ch16_exact_sequences_sheaves/figures/vi16_exact_middle.tex}

\begin{remark}[Local meaning of the image]
Because $\im(d_{i-1})$ is the sheaf image, a section $s\in\F_i(U)$ belongs to it exactly when
every $x\in U$ has a neighborhood $V\subseteq U$ and a section
$t\in\F_{i-1}(V)$ with
\[
d_{i-1}(t)=s|_V.
\]
Thus exactness of sheaves means \emph{local exactness of sections}, not necessarily exactness
of every sequence of section groups.
\end{remark}

\section{The stalkwise exactness theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Stalkwise criterion for exactness]\label{thm:vi16-stalkwise-exact}
A sequence of sheaves of abelian groups is exact if and only if, for every $x\in X$, the
induced sequence of stalks is exact.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that the sheaf sequence is exact at $\F_i$.  Then
\[
\im(d_{i-1})=\ker(d_i).
\]
Taking the stalk at $x$ and using the identities established in VI/15,
\[
(\im d_{i-1})_x=\im((d_{i-1})_x),\qquad
(\ker d_i)_x=\ker((d_i)_x),
\]
gives exactness at $(\F_i)_x$.

Conversely, suppose every stalk sequence is exact.  Since
$(d_i)_x(d_{i-1})_x=0$ for every $x$, the morphism $d_i d_{i-1}$ has zero stalk map
everywhere and hence is zero.  Therefore
$\im(d_{i-1})\subseteq\ker(d_i)$.

Let $s\in\ker(d_i)(U)$ and $x\in U$.  Exactness of the stalk sequence gives a germ
$t_x\in(\F_{i-1})_x$ satisfying
\[
(d_{i-1})_x(t_x)=s_x.
\]
Choose a representative $t\in\F_{i-1}(V)$ on a neighborhood $V$ of $x$.  Equality of germs
allows us to shrink $V$ so that
\[
d_{i-1}(t)=s|_V.
\]
Hence $s$ is locally in the image of $d_{i-1}$ at every point, so
$s\in(\im d_{i-1})(U)$.  Thus the reverse inclusion holds and the sequence is exact.
\end{proof}

\section{Monomorphisms, epimorphisms, and isomorphisms}

For a morphism $f:\F\to\G$, the following conditions are equivalent:
\[
\ker f=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
f_x:\F_x\to\G_x\text{ is injective for all }x
\]
and these are also equivalent to injectivity of
$f_U:\F(U)\to\G(U)$ for every open $U$.

Surjectivity is subtler.

\begin{theorem}[Local lifting criterion for epimorphisms]\label{thm:vi16-epi-local}
For $f:\F\to\G$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\im f=\G$;
\item $\coker f=0$;
\item every stalk map $f_x$ is surjective;
\item every section $g\in\G(U)$ is locally liftable: each $x\in U$ has a neighborhood
$V\subseteq U$ and $s\in\F(V)$ such that $f(s)=g|_V$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

A morphism satisfying these conditions is called surjective as a morphism of sheaves, or an
epimorphism in this abelian category.  It need not be surjective on $\F(U)\to\G(U)$ for a
large open set $U$.

\input{chapters/ch16_exact_sequences_sheaves/figures/vi16_local_lift.tex}

\begin{corollary}[Stalkwise isomorphism criterion]
A morphism $f:\F\to\G$ is an isomorphism if and only if every $f_x$ is an isomorphism.
Equivalently, $f$ is an isomorphism if and only if it is both injective and surjective as a
morphism of sheaves.
\end{corollary}

\section{Short exact sequences}

\begin{definition}[Short exact sequence]
A sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\F'\xrightarrow{i}\F\xrightarrow{q}\F''\longrightarrow0
\]
is short exact if it is exact at each of the three nonzero terms.
\end{definition}

Equivalently, $i$ identifies $\F'$ with $\ker q$, the map $q$ is locally surjective, and
\[
\F''\cong\F/\F'.
\]
By the stalkwise criterion, short exactness is equivalent to exactness of
\[
0\longrightarrow\F'_x\longrightarrow\F_x\longrightarrow\F''_x\longrightarrow0
\]
for every $x$.

\input{chapters/ch16_exact_sequences_sheaves/figures/vi16_short_exact.tex}

\section{Restriction preserves exactness}

Let $j:U\hookrightarrow X$ be the inclusion of an open subset.  Restriction of a sheaf to
$U$ does not change the stalks at points of $U$:
\[
(\F|_U)_x\cong\F_x\qquad(x\in U).
\]
Therefore restricting an exact sequence of sheaves on $X$ yields an exact sequence on $U$.
This is one of the most useful manifestations of locality: exactness can be checked after
passing to a sufficiently small neighborhood of every point.

\input{chapters/ch16_exact_sequences_sheaves/figures/vi16_restriction_exact.tex}

\section{Sections are left exact, but not generally exact}

Let
\[
0\longrightarrow\F'\longrightarrow\F\longrightarrow\F''\longrightarrow0
\]
be short exact.  For every open $U\subseteq X$, the induced sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\F'(U)\longrightarrow\F(U)\longrightarrow\F''(U)
\]
is exact at the first two terms.  The final map need not be surjective.

\begin{theorem}[Left exactness of sections]\label{thm:vi16-left-exact-sections}
For every open $U$, the functor $\Gamma(U,-)$ preserves kernels and therefore sends a short
exact sequence of sheaves of abelian groups to a left exact sequence of abelian groups.
\end{theorem}

The failure of the last map to be surjective measures a genuinely global obstruction.  The
systematic study of that failure belongs to VI/48; here we use it only as a warning and as a
source of examples.

\section{Homology sheaves}

Suppose $d_i d_{i-1}=0$.  Define the $i$th homology sheaf of the complex by
\[
\HH_i:=\ker(d_i)/\im(d_{i-1}).
\]
Because quotients are sheaf quotients, this is again a sheaf.

\begin{theorem}[Homology is computed on stalks]
For every $x\in X$,
\[
(\HH_i)_x\cong
\frac{\ker((d_i)_x)}{\im((d_{i-1})_x)}.
\]
Consequently, the complex is exact at $\F_i$ if and only if $\HH_i=0$.
\end{theorem}

\section{Common pitfalls}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Exactness of sheaves is equality of the \emph{sheaf image} and the kernel sheaf.
\item A sheaf epimorphism need not be surjective on sections over every open set.
\item Exactness on stalks is equivalent to exactness of sheaves; exactness of global-section
sequences is stronger at the right end.
\item The relation $d_i d_{i-1}=0$ is necessary for exactness but is not sufficient.
\item If a section lies in the kernel, exactness gives local preimages; those preimages need
not glue globally.
\item Restriction to an open subset preserves exactness, while direct image need not preserve
surjectivity.
\item Do not consume AG2 Problem 9 here: the failure of right exactness of $\Gamma(U,-)$ is
only previewed; the corpus problem is reserved for VI/48.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Summary}

The central theorem of this chapter is
\[
\boxed{
\text{a sequence of sheaves is exact}
\iff
\text{every sequence of stalks is exact}.}
\]
It converts sheaf-theoretic exactness into ordinary exactness of abelian groups, while the
local-image construction explains why a surjective sheaf morphism can fail to be surjective
on global sections.  Short exact sequences are therefore locally ordinary short exact
sequences, restrictions preserve exactness, and homology sheaves detect the precise locus of
failure of exactness.

The Volume VI ledger records AG1 Exercise 6 as VI.16.P1 and AG2 Problem 7 as VI.16.P2.  Their
parallel variants in the preferred notes and repeated theorem statements are merged rather
than duplicated.  AG2 Problem 9 remains reserved for VI/48.

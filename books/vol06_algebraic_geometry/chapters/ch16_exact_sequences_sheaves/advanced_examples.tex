\begin{example}[Advanced: Holomorphic exponential sequence]
On a complex manifold,
\[
0\to 2\pi i\,\underline{\mathbb Z}\to\mathcal O
\xrightarrow{\exp}\mathcal O^\times\to1
\]
is exact: a nowhere-zero holomorphic function has a local logarithm.  On $\mathbb C^\times$, the function $z$ has no global holomorphic logarithm, so global surjectivity fails.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced: The de Rham complex]
On a smooth manifold, the Poincare lemma says that
\[
0\to\underline{\mathbb R}\to\mathcal C^\infty\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^1\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^2\xrightarrow{d}\cdots
\]
is locally exact.  Hence it is an exact complex of sheaves in positive degrees, although global closed forms need not be globally exact.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced: A closed one-form on the circle]
The form $d\theta$ on $S^1$ is locally $df$ but has no global smooth primitive.  Thus $d:\mathcal C^\infty\to\Omega^1$ is a sheaf epimorphism on a one-manifold even when the map on global sections is not onto.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced: Direct image is only left exact]
For $f:X\to Y$, $(f_*\F)(V)=\F(f^{-1}V)$.  Because sections are left exact, $f_*$ preserves kernels and injections.  It need not preserve a sheaf epimorphism: taking $Y$ to be a point turns $f_*$ into global sections, where the exponential example gives a counterexample.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced: Inverse image preserves exactness]
For sheaves of abelian groups and a continuous map $f:X\to Y$, the stalk identity
\[
(f^{-1}\G)_x\cong\G_{f(x)}
\]
shows immediately that $f^{-1}$ carries exact sequences on $Y$ to exact sequences on $X$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced: Sheafification is exact for abelian groups]
An objectwise exact sequence of presheaves of abelian groups remains exact after sheafification.  Indeed, stalks are filtered colimits, filtered colimits of abelian groups are exact, and sheafification does not change stalks.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced: Direct sums preserve exactness]
If for every $\lambda$ the sequence
$0\to\F'_{\lambda}\to\F_{\lambda}\to\F''_{\lambda}\to0$ is exact, then the direct-sum sequence is exact.  At each stalk this is the direct sum of exact sequences of abelian groups.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced: Homology sheaf and the failure locus]
For a complex $\F^{\bullet}$, the support of $\HH^i(\F^{\bullet})$ is exactly the set of points at which the stalk complex fails to be exact in degree $i$.  Exactness is therefore encoded geometrically by the vanishing locus of a sheaf.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced: Exactness on the Sierpinski space]
On the Sierpinski space $\{\eta,x\}$ with opens $\varnothing,\{x\},\{\eta,x\}$, a sheaf can be encoded by a restriction map $A\to B$.  A sequence of such sheaves is exact exactly when the induced sequences at the two stalks are exact, making the stalkwise theorem completely explicit.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced: A locally split quotient]
If a short exact sequence admits local splittings $s_i:\F''|_{U_i}\to\F|_{U_i}$, then locally
$\F|_{U_i}\cong\F'|_{U_i}\oplus\F''|_{U_i}$.  The splittings need not agree on overlaps, so local splitting is weaker than global splitting.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced: An ideal-sheaf exact sequence - preview]
For a sheaf of ideals $\mathcal I\subseteq\mathcal O_X$ one has
\[
0\to\mathcal I\to\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal O_X/\mathcal I\to0.
\]
At a point $x$ this becomes the ordinary exact quotient sequence
$0\to\mathcal I_x\to\mathcal O_{X,x}\to\mathcal O_{X,x}/\mathcal I_x\to0$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced: Exactness can be tested after shrinking]
To prove exactness at $\G$, it is enough to show: whenever $g\in\G(U)$ maps to zero and $x\in U$, there is a neighborhood $V$ of $x$ and a preceding section mapping to $g|_V$.  No global lifting argument is required.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced: Five-lemma arguments become stalkwise]
Given a commutative diagram with exact rows, any statement such as the short five lemma can be proved on each stalk using ordinary group theory and then promoted back to a sheaf isomorphism by the stalkwise isomorphism criterion.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Advanced: Global lifting obstruction as a cocycle preview]
Given local lifts $s_i$ of a quotient section, the differences $s_i-s_j$ lie in the kernel sheaf on overlaps.  Their failure to be removable by changing the lifts is the prototype of a cohomological obstruction.  VI/48 develops this systematically.
\end{example}

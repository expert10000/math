\begin{problem}[Legacy AG2 Problem 7: injective and surjective implies isomorphism]\label{prob:vi16-p02}
Let $\varphi:\F\to\G$ be a morphism of sheaves of abelian groups.  Prove that $\varphi$ is an isomorphism if and only if it is injective and surjective in the sheaf-theoretic sense.  In the nontrivial direction, construct the inverse on sections by lifting target germs locally, prove the local lifts agree on overlaps using injectivity, glue them, and verify compatibility with restrictions.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the second principal legacy problem for VI/16.  It turns the stalkwise notions of injective and surjective into an actual inverse sheaf morphism and exposes the role of local lifting plus gluing.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Surjectivity gives a preimage germ at every point.  Represent these germs locally.  Injectivity forces two local lifts of the same target section to agree on overlaps.  The sheaf axiom then glues the inverse.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The easy direction follows by taking stalks of an inverse.
\item Given $t\in\G(U)$, lift $t_x$ through $\varphi_x$ for each $x$.
\item Shrink so that the chosen representative really maps to $t$ on a neighborhood.
\item On overlaps, apply injectivity to the difference of two lifts.
\item Glue and show the resulting inverse maps commute with restrictions.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
If $\varphi$ is an isomorphism, its inverse induces an inverse on every stalk, so the stalk maps are both injective and surjective.

Conversely, assume every $\varphi_x$ is injective and surjective.  Let $U$ be open and $t\in\G(U)$.  For each $x\in U$, choose a germ $s_x\in\F_x$ with
$\varphi_x(s_x)=t_x$.  Represent $s_x$ by a section $s_i\in\F(U_i)$ on a neighborhood
$U_i$ of $x$, shrinking until
\[
\varphi(s_i)=t|_{U_i}.
\]
On an overlap $U_i\cap U_j$, the two sections have the same image.  Their difference lies in
$\ker\varphi(U_i\cap U_j)$.  Since the stalk maps are injective, the kernel sheaf is zero,
so $s_i=s_j$ on the overlap.  The $s_i$ therefore glue uniquely to $s\in\F(U)$, and
$\varphi(s)=t$.

Uniqueness follows from injectivity: if $\varphi(s)=\varphi(s')$, then $s-s'$ lies in the
zero kernel.  Thus every $\varphi_U$ is bijective.  The inverse maps are compatible with
restriction because the original maps are, so they assemble to a morphism
$\psi:\G\to\F$ with $\psi\varphi=\id$ and $\varphi\psi=\id$.  Hence $\varphi$ is an
isomorphism.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Stalkwise bijectivity supplies local inverses, injectivity makes them compatible, and the sheaf gluing axiom turns them into one global inverse.  This is the archetypal local-to-global proof for morphisms of sheaves.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
The same pattern proves many diagram lemmas stalkwise.  For sheaves of sets, the local lifting/gluing argument still proves the stalkwise isomorphism criterion, although additive language disappears.

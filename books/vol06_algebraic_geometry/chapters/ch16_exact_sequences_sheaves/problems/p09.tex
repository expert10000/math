\begin{problem}[Exactness is preserved by restriction]\label{prob:vi16-p09}
Prove that if a sequence is exact on $X$, then its restriction to every open $U$ is exact.  Prove conversely that exactness can be checked on an open cover.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This formalizes the slogan that exactness is local on the base.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Use unchanged stalks under restriction.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item For $x\in U$, compare the restricted and original stalks.
\item For the converse, choose a cover member containing $x$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Restriction preserves the stalk at each point of the open set, so an exact sequence restricts to a sequence with exact stalks.  Conversely, if the sequence is exact on an open cover, then for any $x$ choose a cover member containing it; the original stalk sequence equals the exact restricted stalk sequence there.  Hence all stalks are exact and the original sequence is exact.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Exactness can be proved after arbitrary local shrinking.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Formulate a basis version using neighborhoods drawn from a fixed basis.

\paragraph{Related problems.}
VI.16.P3 is the local lifting form of the same principle.

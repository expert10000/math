\begin{problem}[The holomorphic exponential sequence]\label{prob:vi16-p05}
For a complex manifold $X$, prove the local exactness of $0\to2\pi i\underline{\mathbb Z}\to\mathcal O\xrightarrow{\exp}\mathcal O^\times\to1$, and show why $z\in\mathcal O^\times(\mathbb C^\times)$ has no global holomorphic logarithm.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This transports the local/global distinction into complex geometry.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Use local holomorphic logarithms and the integral of $dz/z$ around a loop.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A nonzero holomorphic germ has a logarithm after shrinking.
\item Identify the exponential kernel.
\item Integrate $d\log z=dz/z$ around the unit circle.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Local logarithms give stalkwise surjectivity of $\exp$.  Its kernel consists of locally constant functions with values in $2\pi i\mathbb Z$.  Hence the sequence is exact by stalks.  If $z=e^h$ globally on $\mathbb C^\times$, then $dh=dz/z$; integrating around the unit circle gives $0$ on the left and $2\pi i$ on the right, a contradiction.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Exactness is local while the period of $dz/z$ is a global obstruction.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Relate the obstruction to winding number and later to first cohomology.

\begin{problem}[Support of the failure of exactness]\label{prob:vi16-p11}
Let $\HH_i$ be a homology sheaf.  Prove that the complex is exact on $X\setminus\Supp\HH_i$, and that every point of $\Supp\HH_i$ is a point where the stalk complex fails to be exact.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
It converts an algebraic defect into a subset of the base space.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Use the definition of support by nonzero stalks.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Outside the support the homology stalk vanishes.
\item Apply P10 and restrict to the complement.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
By definition, $x\notin\Supp\HH_i$ means $(\HH_i)_x=0$.  P10 identifies this with vanishing of the ordinary stalk homology.  Hence the stalk complex is exact at every point of the complement, so the restricted sheaf complex is exact there.  Conversely, a point in the support has nonzero homology stalk and therefore is a failure point.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The support of homology localizes where an exactness theorem breaks.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Later use this viewpoint for coherent sheaves and singular loci.

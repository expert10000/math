\begin{problem}[Homology sheaves detect exactness]\label{prob:vi16-p10}
For a complex of sheaves define $\HH_i=\ker d_i/\im d_{i-1}$.  Prove $(\HH_i)_x$ is the ordinary homology of the stalk complex and deduce that exactness at degree $i$ is equivalent to $\HH_i=0$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
It packages the failure of exactness into a geometric object with a support.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Use the VI/15 stalk formulas for kernel, image, and quotient.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Take the stalk of the quotient.
\item Replace stalks of kernel and image by kernel and image of stalk maps.
\item A sheaf vanishes iff all its stalks vanish.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Using the quotient-stalk theorem,
\[
(\HH_i)_x\cong(\ker d_i)_x/(\im d_{i-1})_x
\cong\ker((d_i)_x)/\im((d_{i-1})_x).
\]
Thus $\HH_i$ has zero stalk at $x$ exactly when the stalk complex is exact there.  The sheaf $\HH_i$ is zero iff every stalk is zero, so $\HH_i=0$ iff the original complex is exact at $\F_i$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Homological algebra on sheaves is ordinary homological algebra performed pointwise, with the resulting homology reassembled into a sheaf.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Study $\Supp\HH_i$ as the failure locus.

\paragraph{Related problems.}
VI.16.P11 and the de Rham examples.

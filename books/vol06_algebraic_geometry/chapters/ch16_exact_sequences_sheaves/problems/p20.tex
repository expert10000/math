\begin{problem}[Local lifts and the global obstruction]\label{prob:vi16-p20}
Let $0\to\F'\to\F\xrightarrow{q}\F''\to0$ be short exact and $t\in\F''(U)$.  Choose local lifts $s_i\in\F(U_i)$.  Prove $s_i-s_j$ lies in $\F'(U_i\cap U_j)$ and derive the precise condition for the local lifts to be adjustable to one global lift.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This gives the exact mechanism behind the failure of right exactness of global sections without yet introducing cohomology groups.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Differences of lifts are killed by $q$.  Changing a lift by a kernel section changes the overlap differences by a coboundary-shaped expression.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Compute $q(s_i-s_j)$.
\item Write adjusted lifts as $s_i-a_i$ with $a_i\in\F'(U_i)$.
\item They glue iff $(s_i-s_j)=(a_i-a_j)$ on each overlap.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Because $q(s_i)=q(s_j)=t$ on overlaps,
\[
q(s_i-s_j)=0,
\]
so exactness identifies $s_i-s_j$ with a section of $\F'$ on $U_i\cap U_j$.  Replace $s_i$ by $s_i-a_i$ with $a_i\in\F'(U_i)$.  The adjusted lifts agree on overlaps exactly when
\[
(s_i-s_j)-(a_i-a_j)=0.
\]
Therefore a global lift exists precisely when the overlap differences can be written in this form.  If so, the adjusted lifts glue in $\F$ and the glued section maps to $t$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The obstruction is entirely encoded by kernel-valued overlap differences.  This is the elementary precursor of the connecting morphism in sheaf cohomology.

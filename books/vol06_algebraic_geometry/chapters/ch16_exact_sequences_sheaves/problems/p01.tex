\begin{problem}[Legacy AG1 Exercise 6: exactness is stalkwise]\label{prob:vi16-p01}
Show that a sequence of sheaves
\[
\cdots\to\F_{i-1}\xrightarrow{d_{i-1}}\F_i\xrightarrow{d_i}\F_{i+1}\to\cdots
\]
is exact if and only if, for every $p\in X$, the corresponding stalk sequence
\[
\cdots\to(\F_{i-1})_p\xrightarrow{(d_{i-1})_p}(\F_i)_p
\xrightarrow{(d_i)_p}(\F_{i+1})_p\to\cdots
\]
is exact.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the principal legacy exactness problem in the corpus.  It is the theorem that makes sheaf homological algebra workable: a categorical equality of subsheaves can be tested by ordinary algebra at each point.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Use the VI/15 identities for stalks of kernels and image sheaves.  In the reverse direction, a germ preimage must be represented by a section and then equality of germs must be converted into equality after shrinking.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item For the forward direction, take stalks of $\im d_{i-1}=\ker d_i$.
\item Use $(\im d)_p=\im(d_p)$ and $(\ker d)_p=\ker(d_p)$.
\item For the converse, first show $d_i d_{i-1}=0$ because all its stalk maps vanish.
\item Given $s\in\ker d_i(U)$, lift the germ $s_p$ through $(d_{i-1})_p$ and shrink until the representative maps to $s$.
\item Conclude that $s$ is locally in the image, hence belongs to the image sheaf.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Assume first that the sequence of sheaves is exact at $\F_i$.  Then
\[
\im(d_{i-1})=\ker(d_i).
\]
Taking the stalk at $p$ and using the kernel/image stalk formulas gives
\[
\im((d_{i-1})_p)=\ker((d_i)_p),
\]
so the stalk sequence is exact.

Conversely, assume every stalk sequence is exact.  Then
$(d_i)_p(d_{i-1})_p=0$ for every $p$, hence $d_i d_{i-1}=0$ as a sheaf morphism.  Thus
$\im(d_{i-1})\subseteq\ker(d_i)$.

For the reverse inclusion, let $s\in\ker(d_i)(U)$ and fix $p\in U$.  Its germ
$s_p$ lies in $\ker((d_i)_p)$, which equals $\im((d_{i-1})_p)$.  Choose
$t_p\in(\F_{i-1})_p$ with $(d_{i-1})_p(t_p)=s_p$.  Represent $t_p$ by
$t\in\F_{i-1}(V)$ for some neighborhood $V$ of $p$.  Equality of germs means that, after
shrinking $V$ if necessary,
\[
d_{i-1}(t)=s|_V.
\]
Thus $s$ is locally in the image at every point of $U$.  By the definition of the image
sheaf, $s\in(\im d_{i-1})(U)$.  Therefore
$\ker(d_i)\subseteq\im(d_{i-1})$, proving equality and exactness.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The proof has exactly one genuinely sheaf-theoretic move: a stalk preimage is converted to a local section and the neighborhood is shrunk.  Everything else is ordinary algebra applied to germs.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Use the theorem to prove the sheaf versions of the short five lemma and snake lemma.  It also immediately implies that exactness is local on the base space.

\paragraph{Related problems.}

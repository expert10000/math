\begin{problem}[Split short exact sequences]\label{prob:vi16-p13}
Prove that if $0\to\F'\xrightarrow{i}\F\xrightarrow{q}\F''\to0$ admits a morphism $s:\F''\to\F$ with $qs=\id$, then $\F\cong\F'\oplus\F''$.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Split exact sequences are the simplest exact sequences and provide a useful normal form.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Construct the map $(a,b)\mapsto i(a)+s(b)$ and an explicit inverse.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item For $u\in\F$, subtract $s(q(u))$ to land in $\ker q=\im i$.
\item Use injectivity of $i$ for uniqueness.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
Define $\Phi:\F'\oplus\F''\to\F$ by $\Phi(a,b)=i(a)+s(b)$.  For $u\in\F$, the section $u-s(q(u))$ lies in $\ker q=\im i$, so uniquely equals $i(a)$.  This defines $\Psi(u)=(a,q(u))$.  Direct calculation gives $\Phi\Psi=\id$ and $\Psi\Phi=\id$, and all constructions commute with restriction.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
A splitting turns an extension into a direct sum globally.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Compare local splittings that fail to glue to a global splitting.

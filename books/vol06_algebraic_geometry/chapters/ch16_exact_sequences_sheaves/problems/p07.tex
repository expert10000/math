\begin{problem}[Direct image is left exact]\label{prob:vi16-p07}
For a continuous map $f:X\to Y$, prove that $f_*$ preserves kernels and is left exact.  Give a counterexample to preservation of epimorphisms.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
It shows how exactness behaves under one of the basic sheaf functors.

\paragraph{Useful ideas.}
Evaluate on opens $V\subseteq Y$ and use left exactness of sections on $f^{-1}V$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Write $(f_*\F)(V)=\F(f^{-1}V)$.
\item Compute the kernel sectionwise.
\item For failure of epimorphisms, take $Y$ a point and reuse the exponential sequence.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}
For a morphism $u:\F\to\G$,
\[
(f_*\ker u)(V)=\ker u(f^{-1}V)=\ker((f_*u)_V),
\]
so $f_*$ preserves kernels.  Hence it sends a short exact sequence to a left exact one.  If $Y$ is a point, $f_*$ is global sections on $X$.  Taking $X=S^1$ and the exponential sheaf epimorphism shows that $f_*$ need not preserve surjectivity.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Right adjoint behavior and the local/global obstruction meet in one concrete computation.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Compare with the exactness of inverse image in P8.

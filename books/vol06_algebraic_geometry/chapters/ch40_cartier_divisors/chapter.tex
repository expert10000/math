\chapter{Cartier Divisors}
\label{ch:vi40-cartier-divisors}
\providecommand{\CaDiv}{\operatorname{CaDiv}}
\providecommand{\Div}{\operatorname{Div}}
\providecommand{\KX}{\mathcal{K}_X}

VI/39 encoded codimension-one geometry by Weil divisors.  That construction is global and cycle-theoretic: one records the order of a rational function along each prime divisor.  Cartier divisors start from the opposite direction.  They are given locally by one rational equation, with two equations considered equivalent when they differ by a nowhere-vanishing regular function.

This local formulation is both more flexible and more functorial.  It makes sense on schemes that need not be normal, it remembers when a codimension-one closed subscheme is locally principal, and it produces an invertible sheaf almost automatically.  The price is an important technical point: pullback is not defined for every morphism unless the pulled-back local equations remain invertible in the total quotient sheaf.

The migration ledger assigns three canonical rules to VI/40: the AG9 Cartier-divisor section and its theorem-like and exercise/problem descendants.  The later AG10 divisor/Picard/line-bundle block is mapped to VI/39 only as compare-and-migrate overlap.  Accordingly, this chapter uses the AG9 mapping as provenance and develops the canonical Cartier theory afresh, while leaving divisor class groups to VI/41 and the full Picard-group equivalence to VI/42.

\section*{Learning goals}
By the end of the chapter, the reader should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
\item construct the sheaf of total quotient rings \(\KX\) and interpret \(\KX^\times/\OO_X^\times\);
\item describe a Cartier divisor by compatible local rational equations \((U_i,f_i)\);
\item add, negate, restrict, and recognize principal Cartier divisors;
\item characterize effective Cartier divisors as locally principal closed subschemes cut out by non-zero-divisors;
\item identify the ideal sheaf of an effective Cartier divisor as an invertible sheaf;
\item state exactly when a morphism admits pullback of a Cartier divisor and compute ramification multiplicities in examples;
\item construct the Cartier-to-Weil map on normal Noetherian integral schemes and prove compatibility with principal divisors;
\item recognize locally factorial and regular schemes as settings in which every Weil divisor is Cartier;
\item exhibit a normal singular scheme carrying a Weil divisor that is not Cartier;
\item construct \(\OO_X(D)\) from local equations and identify \(\OO_X(-D)\) with the ideal sheaf of an effective Cartier divisor;
\item explain the first correspondence between Cartier divisors and invertible sheaves equipped with a rational trivialization.
\end{itemize}

\]

\section{The total quotient sheaf}
Let \(X\) be a locally Noetherian scheme.  On an open set \(U\subseteq X\), call a section \(s\in\OO_X(U)\) \emph{regular} in the ring-theoretic sense if multiplication by \(s\) is injective, equivalently if \(s\) is a non-zero-divisor on \(\OO_X(U)\).  Localizing at all such sections gives the total quotient ring.

\begin{definition}[Sheaf of total quotient rings]\label{def:vi40-total-quotient}
The \emph{sheaf of total quotient rings} \(\KX\) is the sheaf associated to the presheaf
\[
 U\longmapsto Q(\OO_X(U)),
\]
where \(Q(A)\) denotes the localization of a ring \(A\) at its multiplicative set of non-zero-divisors.  We write \(\KX^\times\) for its sheaf of units.
\end{definition}

On an integral scheme \(X\), every nonzero regular function is a non-zero-divisor and \(\KX\) is the constant sheaf of rational functions with value \(K(X)\) on every nonempty sufficiently small open set.  Thus the general language specializes to the familiar function field.

\begin{example}[Integral affine scheme]\label{ex:vi40-affine-total-quotient}
If \(X=\Spec A\) with \(A\) a domain, then
\[
 \Gamma(X,\KX)=\Frac(A).
\]
For \(A=k[x,y]\), the rational function \((x^2+y)/(xy-1)\) is a global section of \(\KX\) even though it is not regular everywhere on \(X\).
\end{example}

\begin{example}[A reducible ring]\label{ex:vi40-reducible-total-quotient}
For \(A=k[x,y]/(xy)\), both \(x\) and \(y\) are zero-divisors and therefore are not inverted in \(Q(A)\).  This already signals why a principal ideal generated by \(x\) need not define an effective Cartier divisor.
\end{example}

\section{Cartier divisors as a quotient sheaf}
\begin{definition}[Cartier divisor]\label{def:vi40-cartier}
A \emph{Cartier divisor} on \(X\) is a global section
\[
 D\in\Gamma\!\left(X,\KX^\times/\OO_X^\times\right).
\]
The abelian group of Cartier divisors is denoted
\[
 \CaDiv(X):=\Gamma\!\left(X,\KX^\times/\OO_X^\times\right),
\]
where multiplication of rational functions is written additively on divisors.
\end{definition}

The quotient by \(\OO_X^\times\) expresses the geometric principle that multiplying a local equation by a unit does not change its zero or pole locus.

\begin{proposition}[Local-equation description]\label{prop:vi40-local-equations}
A Cartier divisor can equivalently be described by an open cover \(X=\bigcup_i U_i\) and sections
\[
 f_i\in\Gamma(U_i,\KX^\times)
\]
such that on every overlap
\[
 \frac{f_i}{f_j}\in\Gamma(U_i\cap U_j,\OO_X^\times).
\]
Two such collections define the same divisor exactly when, after passing to a common refinement, their corresponding equations differ by units.
\end{proposition}

\input{chapters/ch40_cartier_divisors/figures/figure_02}

\begin{example}[A point on the projective line]\label{ex:vi40-point-p1}
Let \(D=[\infty]\) on \(\PP^1_k\).  On \(U_0=\PP^1\setminus\{\infty\}\) take the local equation \(f_0=1\).  On the chart \(U_\infty\) with coordinate \(u=1/t\), take \(f_\infty=u\).  On the overlap, \(u\) is a unit, so these equations define a Cartier divisor supported at infinity.
\end{example}

\section{Group operations and restriction}
Suppose \(D\) has local equations \(f_i\) and \(E\) has local equations \(g_i\) on a common refinement.  Define
\[
 D+E\quad\text{by}\quad f_ig_i,
 \qquad
 -D\quad\text{by}\quad f_i^{-1}.
\]
Because ratios of corresponding equations are units, these operations are independent of all choices.

\begin{proposition}[Restriction]\label{prop:vi40-restriction}
For every open subset \(j:U\hookrightarrow X\), restriction of local equations defines a homomorphism
\[
 j^*:\CaDiv(X)\longrightarrow\CaDiv(U).
\]
If a divisor is supported entirely in \(X\setminus U\), its restriction may become zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{example}[Forgetting infinity]\label{ex:vi40-restrict-infinity}
The Cartier divisor \([\infty]\) on \(\PP^1\) restricts to zero on \(\AA^1=\PP^1\setminus\{\infty\}\), because its local equation there is the unit \(1\).
\end{example}

\section{Principal Cartier divisors}
\begin{definition}[Principal Cartier divisor]\label{def:vi40-principal}
A global rational unit \(f\in\Gamma(X,\KX^\times)\) determines the \emph{principal Cartier divisor}
\[
 (f)_{\mathrm{Cart}}\in\CaDiv(X)
\]
represented by the single local equation \(f\).  Two global rational units determine the same principal Cartier divisor exactly when their quotient is a global regular unit.
\end{definition}

Thus there is an exact sequence at the left end
\[
 \Gamma(X,\OO_X^\times)
 \longrightarrow
 \Gamma(X,\KX^\times)
 \longrightarrow
 \CaDiv(X),
\]
whose second arrow is \(f\mapsto(f)_{\mathrm{Cart}}\).  We postpone the quotient of Cartier divisors by principal Cartier divisors to VI/41.

\begin{example}[The coordinate on \(\PP^1\)]\label{ex:vi40-principal-p1}
For the rational coordinate \(t\) on \(\PP^1\), the principal Cartier divisor \((t)_{\mathrm{Cart}}\) is locally represented by \(t\).  Under the Cartier-to-Weil map developed below, it becomes
\[
 [0]-[\infty].
\]
\end{example}

\begin{example}[A unit gives zero]\label{ex:vi40-unit-zero}
On \(\Spec k[x,x^{-1}]\), the function \(x\) is a global unit.  Hence \((x)_{\mathrm{Cart}}=0\), matching the VI/39 observation that \(\operatorname{div}_{\GG_m}(x)=0\).
\end{example}

\section{Effective Cartier divisors}
The local equations of a general Cartier divisor may have poles.  Effectivity means that they can be chosen to be genuine regular non-zero-divisors.

\begin{definition}[Effective Cartier divisor]\label{def:vi40-effective}
A Cartier divisor \(D\) is \emph{effective} if it admits local equations \(f_i\in\OO_X(U_i)\) such that each \(f_i\) is a non-zero-divisor.  The corresponding closed subschemes
\[
 V(f_i)\subseteq U_i
\]
glue to a closed subscheme, also denoted \(D\subseteq X\).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Ideal-sheaf characterization]\label{thm:vi40-effective-ideal}
A closed subscheme \(D\hookrightarrow X\) is an effective Cartier divisor if and only if its ideal sheaf \(\IIcal_D\) is locally generated by one non-zero-divisor.  In this case \(\IIcal_D\) is an invertible \(\OO_X\)-module of rank one.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If \(D\cap U_i=V(f_i)\) with \(f_i\) a non-zero-divisor, then \(\IIcal_D|_{U_i}=f_i\OO_{U_i}\).  Multiplication by \(f_i\) gives an isomorphism \(\OO_{U_i}\xrightarrow{\sim}f_i\OO_{U_i}\), so the ideal is locally free of rank one.  Conversely, if the ideal is locally generated by one non-zero-divisor, those generators define compatible effective Cartier local equations because two generators of the same invertible ideal differ by a unit.
\end{proof}

\input{chapters/ch40_cartier_divisors/figures/figure_03}

\begin{example}[Coordinate hyperplane]\label{ex:vi40-affine-hyperplane}
On \(\AA^n_k=\Spec k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\), the equation \(x_1=0\) cuts out an effective Cartier divisor.  Since the coordinate ring is a domain, \(x_1\) is a non-zero-divisor.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[A principal closed subscheme that is not Cartier]\label{ex:vi40-zero-divisor-warning}
Let
\[
 X=\Spec k[x,y]/(xy).
\]
The closed subscheme \(V(x)\) is principal as an ideal, but \(x\) is a zero-divisor because \(xy=0\).  Hence \(V(x)\) is \emph{not} an effective Cartier divisor.  Local principality alone is insufficient; the generator must be a non-zero-divisor.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[A doubled Cartier divisor]\label{ex:vi40-double}
If \(D\) is effective with local equation \(f\), then \(2D\) is effective with local equation \(f^2\).  Scheme-theoretically it is the thickening cut out by \((f^2)\), not merely the same reduced support counted twice informally.
\end{example}

\section{Support and the exact sequence of an effective divisor}
For an effective Cartier divisor \(i:D\hookrightarrow X\), the defining ideal gives the exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow\OO_X(-D)
 \longrightarrow\OO_X
 \longrightarrow i_*\OO_D
 \longrightarrow0,
\]
where \(\OO_X(-D)\) is initially notation for \(\IIcal_D\).  The invertible-sheaf construction below will show that this notation is compatible with arbitrary Cartier divisors.

\begin{proposition}[Support]\label{prop:vi40-support-effective}
For an effective Cartier divisor with local equations \(f_i\), its underlying support is
\[
 |D|=\{x\in X:(f_i)_x\notin\OO_{X,x}^\times\}.
\]
It is nowhere dense in every irreducible component on which the equation is defined by a non-zero-divisor.
\end{proposition}

\section{Pullback: the correct domain of definition}
Cartier divisors are more pullback-friendly than Weil divisors, but not completely automatic.

\begin{definition}[Defined pullback]\label{def:vi40-pullback-defined}
Let \(g:Y\to X\) be a morphism and let \(D\) have local equations \((U_i,f_i)\).  We say that \(g^*D\) is \emph{defined} if each pulled-back equation
\[
 g^\#(f_i)|_{g^{-1}(U_i)}
\]
represents a unit of \(\mathcal K_Y\).  Then these equations define a Cartier divisor \(g^*D\) on \(Y\).
\end{definition}

This condition is independent of the chosen local equations because multiplication by a regular unit remains multiplication by a regular unit after pullback.

\begin{proposition}[Two useful sufficient criteria]\label{prop:vi40-pullback-criteria}
The pullback \(g^*D\) is defined in each of the following situations.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \(X\) and \(Y\) are integral, \(g\) is dominant, and \(D\) is any Cartier divisor.
\item \(Y\) is locally Noetherian, \(D\) is effective, and no associated point of \(Y\) maps into \(|D|\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For (a), a dominant morphism of integral schemes induces an inclusion of function fields on the generic point, so a nonzero rational equation remains a nonzero rational function.  For (b), an effective local equation is a non-zero-divisor exactly when it avoids the associated primes in the locally Noetherian setting.  If no associated point maps into the support, its pullback avoids all associated primes of \(Y\), hence remains a non-zero-divisor.
\end{proof}

\input{chapters/ch40_cartier_divisors/figures/figure_04}

\begin{example}[Ramification multiplicity]\label{ex:vi40-ramification}
Let \(g:\AA^1_u\to\AA^1_t\) be defined by \(t=u^e\), and let \(D=V(t)\).  Then
\[
 g^*D=V(u^e)=e\,V(u)
\]
as Cartier divisors.  Pullback records multiplicity automatically.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Why pullback can fail]\label{ex:vi40-pullback-fails}
Let \(D=V(t)\) on \(\AA^1_t\), and let \(g:\Spec k\to\AA^1\) be the point \(t=0\).  The local equation \(t\) pulls back to \(0\in k\), which is not a unit of the total quotient ring \(k\).  Thus \(g^*D\) is not a Cartier divisor on the point.  Geometrically, the entire source lies inside the divisor.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Functoriality where defined]\label{prop:vi40-pullback-functorial}
Whenever all relevant pullbacks are defined,
\[
 g^*(D+E)=g^*D+g^*E,
 \qquad
 g^*(f)_{\mathrm{Cart}}=(g^*f)_{\mathrm{Cart}},
\]
and for \(Z\xrightarrow{h}Y\xrightarrow{g}X\),
\[
 (g\circ h)^*D=h^*(g^*D).
\]
\end{proposition}

\section{The Cartier-to-Weil map}
Return now to the setting of VI/39: let \(X\) be a normal Noetherian integral scheme.  Every codimension-one local ring is then a DVR.

\begin{construction}[From Cartier to Weil]\label{con:vi40-cartier-weil}
Let \(D\) be represented near the generic point \(\eta_Z\) of a prime divisor \(Z\) by a local equation \(f\).  Define
\[
 n_Z(D):=\operatorname{ord}_Z(f).
\]
If \(f'\) is another local equation, then \(f'/f\) is a unit, hence has order zero.  Therefore \(n_Z(D)\) is well defined.  Set
\[
 [D]_{\mathrm W}:=\sum_{Z\in X^{(1)}}n_Z(D)[Z].
\]
\end{construction}

\begin{theorem}[Cartier-to-Weil comparison]\label{thm:vi40-cartier-weil}
For a normal Noetherian integral scheme \(X\), the preceding construction defines an injective group homomorphism
\[
 \CaDiv(X)\hookrightarrow\Div(X).
\]
It sends principal Cartier divisors to principal Weil divisors:
\[
 [(f)_{\mathrm{Cart}}]_{\mathrm W}=\operatorname{div}_X(f).
\]
If \(D\) is effective Cartier, then \([D]_{\mathrm W}\) is effective Weil.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Additivity follows from additivity of each DVR valuation, and compatibility with principal divisors is immediate from the definitions.  Effectivity gives regular non-zero-divisor local equations, so their codimension-one orders are nonnegative.

For injectivity, work affine-locally on \(U=\Spec A\) with \(A\) normal Noetherian domain.  If every codimension-one order of a local equation \(f\in\Frac(A)^\times\) is zero, then both \(f\) and \(f^{-1}\) lie in every height-one localization \(A_{\mathfrak p}\).  Normality gives
\[
 A=\bigcap_{\height(\mathfrak p)=1}A_{\mathfrak p}\subseteq\Frac(A),
\]
so \(f,f^{-1}\in A\), hence \(f\in A^\times\).  Thus the Cartier divisor is locally zero and therefore globally zero.
\end{proof}

\input{chapters/ch40_cartier_divisors/figures/figure_05}

\begin{example}[The hyperplane in projective space]\label{ex:vi40-projective-hyperplane}
Let \(H=V_+(x_0)\subset\PP^n_k\).  On \(U_i=D_+(x_i)\), a Cartier equation is
\[
 f_i=\frac{x_0}{x_i}.
\]
On overlaps,
\[
 \frac{f_i}{f_j}=\frac{x_j}{x_i}
\]
is a unit.  The associated Weil divisor has coefficient one along the prime hyperplane \(H\).
\end{example}

\section{When Weil and Cartier differ}
The comparison map need not be surjective.  A prime Weil divisor may fail to be locally principal at a singular point.

\begin{definition}[Locally factorial]\label{def:vi40-locally-factorial}
A locally Noetherian integral scheme \(X\) is \emph{locally factorial} if every local ring \(\OO_{X,x}\) is a UFD.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Locally factorial criterion]\label{thm:vi40-factorial-weil-cartier}
If \(X\) is locally factorial and Noetherian integral, every Weil divisor on \(X\) is Cartier.  In particular, every regular Noetherian integral scheme is locally factorial, hence every Weil divisor is Cartier.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A prime divisor is locally represented by a height-one prime ideal.  In a UFD every height-one prime is principal, generated by an irreducible non-zero-divisor.  These local equations make the prime divisor Cartier, and finite integer sums preserve the property.  A regular local ring is a UFD, giving the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A normal singular surface with a non-Cartier Weil divisor]\label{ex:vi40-a1-singularity}
Let
\[
 X=\Spec k[x,y,z]/(xy-z^2)
\]
and let \(P=V(x,z)\).  VI/39 computed
\[
 \operatorname{div}(x)=2P.
\]
Thus \(2P\) is Cartier, indeed principal.  But \(P\) itself is not Cartier at the vertex \((x,y,z)\): its ideal \((x,z)\) is not principal in the local ring there.  Hence the Cartier-to-Weil map is not surjective.  This example foreshadows a nontrivial divisor class in VI/41.
\end{example}

\section{The invertible sheaf associated to a Cartier divisor}
A Cartier divisor automatically produces a line bundle.  This is the bridge to VI/42.

\begin{construction}[The sheaf associated to a Cartier divisor]\label{con:vi40-oxd}
Let \(D\) have local equations \(f_i\in\Gamma(U_i,\KX^\times)\).  Define a subsheaf of \(\KX\) by
\[
 \OO_X(D)|_{U_i}:=f_i^{-1}\OO_{U_i}.
\]
On overlaps,
\[
 f_i^{-1}\OO=f_j^{-1}\OO
\]
because \(f_i/f_j\) is a unit.  Thus the local modules glue to a globally defined invertible sheaf \(\OO_X(D)\).
\end{construction}

Each \(f_i^{-1}\) is a local generator, so \(\OO_X(D)\) is locally free of rank one.

\begin{proposition}[Basic identities]\label{prop:vi40-oxd-identities}
For Cartier divisors \(D,E\), there are canonical isomorphisms
\[
 \OO_X(D+E)\cong\OO_X(D)\otimes\OO_X(E),
 \qquad
 \OO_X(-D)\cong\OO_X(D)^\vee.
\]
If \(D=(f)_{\mathrm{Cart}}\) is principal, multiplication by \(f\) identifies
\[
 \OO_X(D)\cong\OO_X.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{example}[Effective divisor and its ideal]\label{ex:vi40-effective-ideal-linebundle}
If \(D\) is effective with local equation \(f_i\), then
\[
 \OO_X(-D)|_{U_i}=f_i\OO_{U_i}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \boxed{\OO_X(-D)=\IIcal_D}
\]
and \(\OO_X(D)=\IIcal_D^{-1}\).  The familiar ideal sheaf of a hypersurface is therefore the negative of its divisor line bundle.
\end{example}

\section{The canonical rational section}
Inside \(\OO_X(D)\subseteq\KX\), the rational function \(1\) defines a canonical rational section \(s_D\).  Relative to the local generator \(e_i=f_i^{-1}\), one has
\[
 1=f_i e_i.
\]
Thus the local coefficient of \(s_D\) is exactly the Cartier equation \(f_i\).

\begin{proposition}[Divisor of the canonical rational section]\label{prop:vi40-canonical-section}
The canonical rational section \(s_D=1\) of \(\OO_X(D)\) has Cartier divisor \(D\).  If \(D\) is effective, then \(s_D\) is a regular global section and its zero subscheme is precisely \(D\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{example}[The hyperplane bundle]\label{ex:vi40-hyperplane-bundle}
For the hyperplane \(H\subset\PP^n_k\), the construction gives
\[
 \OO_{\PP^n}(H)\cong\OO_{\PP^n}(1).
\]
The homogeneous coordinate \(x_0\) becomes the canonical section whose zero divisor is \(H\).  VI/42 will generalize this relationship between divisors, line bundles, and sections.
\end{example}

\section{Cartier divisors and rationally trivialized line bundles}
The previous construction can be reversed once a line bundle is equipped with a rational trivialization.

\begin{proposition}[First line-bundle correspondence]\label{prop:vi40-rational-trivialization}
Let \(\mathcal L\) be an invertible sheaf on \(X\) and suppose \(s\) is a rational section that generates \(\mathcal L\otimes_{\OO_X}\KX\).  Choose local generators \(e_i\) of \(\mathcal L\) and write
\[
 s=f_i e_i,
 \qquad f_i\in\Gamma(U_i,\KX^\times).
\]
Then the \(f_i\) define a Cartier divisor \(\operatorname{div}(s)\).  Moreover
\[
 \mathcal L\cong\OO_X(\operatorname{div}(s)).
\]
Changing \(s\) by a global rational unit changes the divisor by a principal Cartier divisor.
\end{proposition}

This is the first appearance of the bridge
\[
 \boxed{\text{Cartier divisors modulo principal divisors}\ \longleftrightarrow\ \text{invertible sheaves with isomorphism classes}},
\]
but the quotient and its relation to \(\Pic(X)\) belong to VI/41--VI/42.

\section{Worked examples}
\begin{example}[A divisor on \(\AA^2\)]\label{ex:vi40-worked-affine}
On \(X=\AA^2_k\), the polynomial \(f=x^2y\) defines the principal effective Cartier divisor \((f)\).  Under Cartier-to-Weil,
\[
 [(f)]_{\mathrm W}=2V(x)+V(y).
\]
Its ideal sheaf is \((x^2y)\), and \(\OO_X((f))\cong\OO_X\) because the divisor is principal.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Difference of two effective Cartier divisors]\label{ex:vi40-difference}
On \(\PP^1\), let \(D=[0]\) and \(E=[\infty]\).  Then \(D-E\) is Cartier and principal:
\[
 D-E=(t)_{\mathrm{Cart}}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \OO_{\PP^1}(D-E)\cong\OO_{\PP^1}.
\]
This is the line-bundle shadow of the relation \([0]\sim[\infty]\).
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Pulling back a hyperplane]\label{ex:vi40-pullback-hyperplane}
Let \(\phi:\PP^n\to\PP^m\) be a morphism defined by homogeneous forms \(F_0,\ldots,F_m\) of common degree \(d\) with no base points.  If \(H_j=V_+(y_j)\subset\PP^m\) and \(F_j\not\equiv0\), then
\[
 \phi^*H_j=V_+(F_j)
\]
as an effective Cartier divisor, with multiplicities determined by the factorization of \(F_j\).  Correspondingly,
\[
 \phi^*\OO_{\PP^m}(1)\cong\OO_{\PP^n}(d).
\]
\end{example}

\begin{example}[A smooth curve]\label{ex:vi40-smooth-curve}
On a smooth integral curve, every closed point is an effective Cartier divisor because its local ring is a DVR.  Hence every Weil divisor is Cartier.  The distinction between the two theories appears only when singularities or higher-dimensional local factoriality enter.
\end{example}

\begin{problem}[Local equations on \(\PP^1\)]\label{prob:vi40-01}
Construct explicit Cartier local equations for \([a]-[\infty]\) on \(\PP^1\).

Describe the effective Cartier divisor \(2V(x)\subset\AA^2\) scheme-theoretically.

\begin{problem}[Pullback and multiplicity]\label{prob:vi40-03}
Let \(g:\AA^1_u\to\AA^1_t\) be \(t=u^3(u-1)^2\).  Pull back \(V(t)\).
\end{problem}

Let \(Y=V(t)\subset\AA^2_{t,u}\) and let \(D=V(t)\) on \(\AA^2\).  Explain why \(D|_Y\) is not a Cartier pullback.
\end{problem}
\begin{problem}[Cartier-to-Weil on a UFD]\label{prob:vi40-05}
Let \(A\) be a UFD and \(f=p_1^{a_1}\cdots p_r^{a_r}/q_1^{b_1}\cdots q_s^{b_s}\in\Frac(A)^\times\).  Compute the associated Weil divisor.
\end{problem}
\begin{problem}[Why \(P\) on the quadric cone is not Cartier]\label{prob:vi40-06}
For \(X=\Spec k[x,y,z]/(xy-z^2)\) and \(P=V(x,z)\), explain the obstruction at the vertex.
\end{problem}

\end{problem}
\begin{problem}[Hyperplane pullback under the Veronese map]\label{prob:vi40-08}

\end{problem}
\begin{problem}[Cartier divisor from a line-bundle section]\label{prob:vi40-09}

\end{problem}

\section{Challenges}
\begin{problem}[Challenge: codimension-one injectivity]\label{chal:vi40-01}
Give a complete affine proof that the Cartier-to-Weil map is injective on a normal Noetherian integral scheme, including the identity \(A=\bigcap_{\height\mathfrak p=1}A_{\mathfrak p}\) inside \(\Frac(A)\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Challenge: associated points and pullback]\label{chal:vi40-02}
Prove carefully that if an effective Cartier divisor \(D\subset X\) contains no image of an associated point of a locally Noetherian scheme \(Y\), then its pullback along \(Y\to X\) is effective Cartier.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Challenge: the quadric cone]\label{chal:vi40-03}
For \(X=\Spec k[x,y,z]/(xy-z^2)\), prove directly that \((x,z)\) is not principal at the vertex and that \(2P=\operatorname{div}(x)\).  Interpret the result as the first evidence for torsion in a divisor class group.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Challenge: projective hypersurfaces]\label{chal:vi40-04}
Let \(F\) be a nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree \(d\) on \(\PP^n_k\).  Construct the effective Cartier divisor \(V_+(F)\) from local equations, prove \(\OO(V_+(F))\cong\OO(d)\), and track factor multiplicities when \(F\) is reducible.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Challenge: rational sections]\label{chal:vi40-05}
Formulate and prove the equivalence between Cartier divisors on an integral scheme and isomorphism classes of pairs \((\mathcal L,s)\), where \(\mathcal L\) is invertible and \(s\) is a nonzero rational section generating \(\mathcal L\otimes K(X)\).  Identify how multiplying \(s\) by \(f\in K(X)^\times\) changes the divisor.
\end{problem}

\section*{Bridge to VI/41}
Cartier divisors now have three equivalent-looking faces that will be separated and compared in the next chapters:
\[
\boxed{
\text{local rational equations}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{Cartier divisor}
\longrightarrow
\OO_X(D)
}
\]
and, on normal schemes,
\[
\boxed{
\text{Cartier divisor}
\hookrightarrow
\text{Weil divisor}.
}
\]
VI/41 will quotient Weil divisors by principal divisors to construct the divisor class group and measure the failure of factoriality.  VI/42 will then return to the invertible-sheaf side and identify the corresponding Picard group.

\begin{problem}[Localization is finite type]\label{prob:vi26-p17}
Show \(A\to A_f\) is a finite-type ring map and interpret geometrically.
\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{Provenance.} Canonical VI/26 dossier developed from the audited AG5/AG6 finiteness theory streams; no additional numbered legacy problem is claimed.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Consider
\[
A[t]\longrightarrow A_f,\qquad t\longmapsto f^{-1}.
\]
The map is surjective: every fraction \(a/f^n\) is the image of \(at^n\).  Its kernel contains
\(ft-1\), and the quotient \(A[t]/(ft-1)\) already makes \(f\) invertible.  By the universal property
of localization,
\[
A_f\cong A[t]/(ft-1).
\]
Thus \(A_f\) is generated by one element as an \(A\)-algebra, so \(A\to A_f\) is finite type.

Geometrically,
\[
\Spec A_f=D(f)\hookrightarrow\Spec A
\]
is the principal open immersion.  Hence principal open immersions are finite-type morphisms, even
though they are usually not finite morphisms.
\end{solution}

\begin{problem}[Noetherian schemes have quasi-compact opens]\label{prob:vi26-p20}
Show that every open subset of a Noetherian topological space is quasi-compact, and explain the scheme-theoretic consequence.
\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{Provenance.} Canonical VI/26 dossier developed from the audited AG5/AG6 finiteness theory streams; no additional numbered legacy problem is claimed.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Let \(T\) be a Noetherian topological space and let \(U\subseteq T\) be open.  We prove every open
cover of \(U\) has a finite subcover.

Let \(U=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}U_\lambda\), with each \(U_\lambda\) open in \(T\).  Consider the
set of finite unions of members of the cover:
\[
W_F=\bigcup_{\lambda\in F}U_\lambda,\qquad F\subseteq\Lambda\text{ finite}.
\]
If no \(W_F\) equals \(U\), choose a strictly increasing chain of such finite unions by repeatedly
adding a cover member that contains a point not yet covered:
\[
W_{F_1}\subsetneq W_{F_2}\subsetneq W_{F_3}\subsetneq\cdots.
\]
But a Noetherian space satisfies the ascending chain condition on open subsets, so such a chain
cannot exist.  Therefore some finite \(F\) satisfies \(W_F=U\), and \(U\) is quasi-compact.

Thus every open subset of a Noetherian scheme is quasi-compact.  In scheme language this means, for
example, that any open subscheme of a Noetherian scheme is quasi-compact as a space; combined with
local Noetherianity, every open subscheme is again Noetherian.
\end{solution}

\begin{problem}[Composition]\label{prob:vi26-p07}
Prove that composites of locally finite type morphisms are locally finite type, and composites of finite-type morphisms are finite type.
\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{Provenance.} Canonical VI/26 dossier developed from the audited AG5/AG6 finiteness theory streams; no additional numbered legacy problem is claimed.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Let \(X\xrightarrow f Y\xrightarrow g Z\) be locally finite type.  Fix \(x\in X\).  After choosing
and shrinking affine neighborhoods, we may arrange
\[
x\in\Spec C\subseteq X,\qquad
f(x)\in\Spec B\subseteq Y,\qquad
g(f(x))\in\Spec A\subseteq Z,
\]
with \(B\) finitely generated over \(A\) and \(C\) finitely generated over \(B\).  Write
\[
B=A[b_1,\ldots,b_m],\qquad C=B[c_1,\ldots,c_n].
\]
Then
\[
C=A[b_1,\ldots,b_m,c_1,\ldots,c_n],
\]
so the composite is locally finite type.

If both morphisms are finite type, they are also quasi-compact.  Quasi-compact morphisms are stable
under composition: the inverse image under \(g\circ f\) of an affine target open is the inverse image
under \(f\) of the quasi-compact space obtained under \(g\), hence is quasi-compact.  Therefore the
composite is locally finite type and quasi-compact, so finite type.
\end{solution}
\fi

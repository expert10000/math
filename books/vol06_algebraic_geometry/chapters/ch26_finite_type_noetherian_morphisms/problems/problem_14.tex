\begin{problem}[Finite type over Noetherian]\label{prob:vi26-p14}
If \(Y\) is Noetherian and \(X\to Y\) is finite type, prove \(X\) Noetherian.
\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{Provenance.} Canonical VI/26 dossier developed from the audited AG5/AG6 finiteness theory streams; no additional numbered legacy problem is claimed.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Problem~13 shows first that \(X\) is locally Noetherian, because a Noetherian scheme is in particular
locally Noetherian.

It remains to prove \(X\) quasi-compact.  The base \(Y\) is quasi-compact, and a finite-type
morphism is quasi-compact.  By definition of a quasi-compact morphism, the inverse image of the
quasi-compact space \(Y\) is quasi-compact:
\[
X=f^{-1}(Y).
\]
Therefore \(X\) is both locally Noetherian and quasi-compact.  By definition, \(X\) is a Noetherian
scheme.
\end{solution}

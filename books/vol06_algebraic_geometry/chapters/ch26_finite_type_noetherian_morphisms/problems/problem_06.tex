\begin{problem}[Restriction to open subschemes]\label{prob:vi26-p06}
Show that restricting a locally finite type morphism to an open subscheme of the target remains locally finite type.  Show the analogous statement for an open restriction of the source.
\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{Provenance.} Canonical VI/26 dossier developed from the audited AG5/AG6 finiteness theory streams; no additional numbered legacy problem is claimed.
\end{problem}

\ifdefined\FullProblemDossiers
\begin{solution}
Let \(f:X\to Y\) be locally finite type.

First let \(V\subseteq Y\) be open.  The restricted morphism
\[
f^{-1}(V)\longrightarrow V
\]
is locally finite type because the property can be checked on every affine open of the target:
if \(W=\Spec A\subseteq V\), then \(W\) is also an affine open of \(Y\), and the required
finite-type affine cover of \(f^{-1}(W)\) is already available.

Now let \(U\subseteq X\) be open and consider \(f|_U:U\to Y\).  Around \(x\in U\), choose affine
charts
\[
x\in W=\Spec B\subseteq X,\qquad f(x)\in V=\Spec A\subseteq Y
\]
with \(B\) finitely generated over \(A\).  The open \(U\cap W\) contains a distinguished
neighborhood \(D(g)\) of \(x\).  Its coordinate ring is \(B_g\), and
\[
B_g\cong B[t]/(gt-1)
\]
is finitely generated over \(B\), hence over \(A\).  Such distinguished neighborhoods cover \(U\),
so the source restriction remains locally finite type.
\end{solution}
\fi

\begin{problem}[Challenge: the full tower of infinitesimal thickenings]
For $A_n=k[t]/(t^n)$, $n\ge1$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item prove all spectra are the same one-point space;
\item compute the powers of the nilradical and the associated graded ring
\[
\operatorname{gr}_{(t)}(A_n)=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}(t)^j/(t)^{j+1};
\]
\item show $A_n$ and $A_m$ are nonisomorphic for $n\ne m$;
\item describe the natural quotient tower
\[
\cdots\twoheadrightarrow A_4\twoheadrightarrow A_3\twoheadrightarrow A_2\twoheadrightarrow A_1=k;
\]
\item explain how the tower records increasingly high-order infinitesimal information while
      never adding a point.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
It turns the doubled point into an entire hierarchy of infinitesimal neighborhoods.
\begin{solution}
The unique prime is $(\bar t)$ for every $n$.  Its powers satisfy
\[
(\bar t)^j=(\bar t^j),\qquad 0\le j<n,
\]
and $(\bar t)^n=0$.  Each graded piece $(\bar t^j)/(\bar t^{j+1})$ is one-dimensional
over $k$ for $0\le j<n$, so
\[
\operatorname{gr}_{(t)}(A_n)
\cong k\oplus k\bar t\oplus\cdots\oplus k\bar t^{n-1}
\]
as a graded $k$-vector space, with the evident truncated multiplication.  The nilpotency
index of the maximal ideal is $n$, so it distinguishes the rings.  The natural maps are
reduction modulo successively smaller powers of $t$.  Each map leaves the unique prime point
unchanged while deleting one layer of nilpotent structure.
\end{solution}

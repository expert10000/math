\begin{problem}[Challenge: a one-point spectrum with non-nilpotent nilradical]
Let
\[
A=k[x_1,x_2,x_3,\dots]/(x_1^2,x_2^3,x_3^4,\dots).
\]
Show that every $\bar x_i$ is nilpotent and that
\[
\Nil(A)=(\bar x_1,\bar x_2,\dots).
\]
Prove that $\Spec A$ consists of a single point but that $\Nil(A)$ is not a nilpotent ideal.
\end{problem}
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
It separates ``nil ideal'' from ``nilpotent ideal'' and shows how much infinitesimal depth a
single topological point can carry in a non-Noetherian ring.
\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Every prime contains every $\bar x_i$, so every prime contains their ideal.  The quotient by
that ideal is $k$.  To prove the ideal is not nilpotent, find a nonzero product of arbitrarily
many distinct variables.
\begin{solution}
Each $\bar x_i$ is nilpotent because $\bar x_i^{i+1}=0$, so the ideal
$N=(\bar x_1,\bar x_2,\dots)$ is contained in $\Nil(A)$.  Conversely $A/N\cong k$ is
reduced, so $N=\Nil(A)$.  Every prime contains $N$, and $N$ itself is maximal with quotient
$k$, hence $\Spec A=\{N\}$.
For every $r$, the product
\[
\bar x_1\bar x_2\cdots\bar x_r
\]
is nonzero: the defining ideal kills only monomials containing $x_i^{i+1}$ for some $i$,
and this squarefree product contains none.  Therefore $N^r\ne0$ for all $r$, so $N$ is not
nilpotent.
\end{solution}
\paragraph{Extension.}
Compare with the theorem that the nilradical of a Noetherian ring is nilpotent.

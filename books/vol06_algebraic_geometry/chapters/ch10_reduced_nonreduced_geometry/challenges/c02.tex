\begin{problem}[Challenge: classify spectrum-preserving quotients]
Let $I\subseteq A$.  Prove that the canonical map
\[
\Spec(A/I)\to\Spec A
\]
is a homeomorphism onto all of $\Spec A$ if and only if $I\subseteq\Nil(A)$.  Strengthen
the statement by proving that for every $f\in A$ the corresponding basic opens agree under
this homeomorphism:
\[
D_{A/I}(\bar f)\longleftrightarrow D_A(f).
\]
Then explain why the largest such quotient is the reduction $A\to A_{\mathrm{red}}$.
\end{problem}
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
It characterizes reduction purely by the topology of prime spectra.
\begin{solution}
The quotient-spectrum map identifies $\Spec(A/I)$ with $V(I)$.  It is onto all of
$\Spec A$ exactly when every prime contains $I$, i.e.
\[
I\subseteq\bigcap_{\mathfrak p}\mathfrak p=\Nil(A).
\]
Under the quotient correspondence, $\bar f\notin\mathfrak p/I$ exactly when
$f\notin\mathfrak p$, so principal opens correspond.  Among all ideals contained in every
prime, the largest is their intersection $\Nil(A)$; quotienting by it gives the maximal
spectrum-preserving quotient, namely $A_{\mathrm{red}}$.
\end{solution}
\paragraph{Extension.}
Ask which additional sheaf-theoretic data is needed to distinguish two such quotients.

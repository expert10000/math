\begin{problem}[When a quotient leaves the whole spectrum unchanged]
Let $I\subseteq A$.  Prove that the quotient map induces a homeomorphism
\[
\Spec(A/I)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Spec A
\]
onto the whole spectrum if and only if
\[
I\subseteq\Nil(A).
\]
Show that nilpotence of the ideal $I$ is sufficient but not necessary in arbitrary rings.
\end{problem}
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
It characterizes exactly which quotient information is completely invisible to prime-spectrum
topology.
\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
The image of $\Spec(A/I)$ in $\Spec A$ is $V(I)$.
\paragraph{Guided hints.}
$V(I)=\Spec A$ exactly when every prime contains $I$.
\begin{solution}
The quotient-spectrum map identifies $\Spec(A/I)$ homeomorphically with $V(I)$.  Therefore
it is a homeomorphism onto all of $\Spec A$ exactly when
\[
V(I)=\Spec A.
\]
This means every prime contains $I$, hence
\[
I\subseteq\bigcap_{\mathfrak p\in\Spec A}\mathfrak p=\Nil(A).
\]
Conversely this containment makes every prime contain $I$, so $V(I)=\Spec A$.
If $I$ is nilpotent then certainly every element of $I$ is nilpotent, so the condition holds.
Challenge VI.10.C1 constructs a nil ideal that is not nilpotent, showing the converse fails
without Noetherian hypotheses.
\end{solution}
\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The nilradical is exactly the largest ideal one may quotient out without changing the prime
spectrum at all.
\paragraph{Extensions.}
In a Noetherian ring, prove that the nilradical is nilpotent.
\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical formulation of the ``nilpotents are topologically invisible'' legacy theme.

\begin{problem}[Reduction commutes with localization]
Let $S\subseteq A$ be multiplicatively closed.  Prove
\[
S^{-1}(A_{\mathrm{red}})\cong(S^{-1}A)_{\mathrm{red}}.
\]
Then take
\[
A=k[x,y]/(x^2,xy)
\]
and compute the reductions of $A$, $A_y$, and $A_x$ whenever the localization is nonzero.
\end{problem}
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
It shows that removing nilpotents is compatible with restricting to open subsets.
\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Localization commutes with quotient and with the nilradical.
\paragraph{Guided hints.}
For $A_y$, invert $y$ in the relation $xy=0$.  For $A_x$, remember $x^2=0$.
\begin{solution}
We have
\[
S^{-1}(A/\Nil(A))\cong S^{-1}A/S^{-1}\Nil(A).
\]
The nilradical of $S^{-1}A$ is $S^{-1}\Nil(A)$, so this is $(S^{-1}A)_{\mathrm{red}}$.
For the example, $\Nil(A)=(\bar x)$ and $A_{\mathrm{red}}\cong k[y]$.
Localizing at $y$ forces $\bar x=0$, so
\[
A_y\cong k[y,y^{-1}],
\]
already reduced.  Localizing at $x$ gives the zero ring because $x$ is nilpotent and is
forced to become invertible; equivalently $1=x^{-2}x^2=0$.  Its spectrum is empty.
\end{solution}
\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Nilpotent structure may be supported on a closed subset and disappear on its complement.
\paragraph{Extensions.}
Generalize from one element to an arbitrary multiplicative system.
\paragraph{Provenance.}
New problem from the localization selector assigned to VI/10's structural consequences.

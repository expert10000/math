\begin{problem}[Reduced before base change, nonreduced after base change]
Let
\[
k=\FF_p(s),\qquad A=k[x]/(x^p-s).
\]
Prove that $x^p-s$ is irreducible over $k$, so $A$ is a field and hence reduced.  Let
$K=\FF_p(\alpha)$ with $\alpha^p=s$.  Prove
\[
A\otimes_kK\cong K[x]/((x-\alpha)^p)
\]
and conclude that the base-changed algebra is nonreduced.
\end{problem}
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
It is the first example where reducedness fails under a natural geometric operation.  This
forces the later notion of geometric reducedness.
\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
If $s$ were a $p$th power in $\FF_p(s)$, compare valuations at the irreducible polynomial
$s$.  In characteristic $p$, $(x-\alpha)^p=x^p-\alpha^p$.
\paragraph{Guided hints.}
The polynomial $X^p-s$ has a root in an extension exactly when $s$ is a $p$th power; in
characteristic $p$ any nontrivial factor would force the purely inseparable root to have
smaller degree.
\begin{solution}
The element $s$ is not a $p$th power in $k=\FF_p(s)$: the $s$-adic valuation of a $p$th
power is divisible by $p$, while $v_s(s)=1$.  Let $\alpha$ be a root of $X^p-s$ in an
algebraic closure.  In characteristic $p$ the extension $k(\alpha)/k$ is purely
inseparable, and the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ has degree either $1$ or $p$ because
it divides $X^p-s$.  The degree cannot be $1$, since that would put $\alpha$ in $k$ and
make $s=\alpha^p$ a $p$th power in $k$.  Therefore $[k(\alpha):k]=p$, so the degree-$p$
polynomial $X^p-s$ is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ and is irreducible.  Thus $A$
is a field.
After extending to $K=k(\alpha)$ with $\alpha^p=s$,
\[
A\otimes_kK
\cong K[x]/(x^p-s)
=K[x]/(x^p-\alpha^p)
=K[x]/((x-\alpha)^p).
\]
The nonzero class of $x-\alpha$ is nilpotent, so the tensor product is nonreduced.
\end{solution}
\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The original algebra was reduced only relative to the imperfect base field.  Enlarging the
field exposed a hidden repeated geometric point.

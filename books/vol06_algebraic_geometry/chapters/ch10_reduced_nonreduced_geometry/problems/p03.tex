\begin{problem}[Universal property and idempotence of reduction]
Prove that reduction is universal among maps from $A$ to reduced rings.  Then prove
\[
(A_{\mathrm{red}})_{\mathrm{red}}\cong A_{\mathrm{red}}.
\]
Finally, show that if $A\to B$ is an isomorphism on reductions, then every map from either
ring to a reduced ring factors through the same reduced quotient up to canonical isomorphism.
\end{problem}
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Reduction is not an ad hoc quotient: it is a canonical reflector from rings to reduced rings.
\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Nilpotents map to nilpotents, and reduced rings have no nonzero nilpotents.
\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Apply the universal property first to the quotient $A\to A_{\mathrm{red}}$, then to
$A_{\mathrm{red}}$ itself.
\begin{solution}
Any map $\varphi:A\to R$ with $R$ reduced kills $\Nil(A)$, hence factors uniquely through
$A/\Nil(A)$.  Since $A_{\mathrm{red}}$ is already reduced, its nilradical is zero and
therefore
\[
(A_{\mathrm{red}})_{\mathrm{red}}
=A_{\mathrm{red}}/0
\cong A_{\mathrm{red}}.
\]
If $A_{\mathrm{red}}\cong B_{\mathrm{red}}$, the universal factorization of maps from $A$
or $B$ to a reduced target passes through these canonically isomorphic reductions.
\end{solution}
\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Passing to the reduction discards exactly the information that no reduced target can detect.
\paragraph{Extensions.}
Rephrase the theorem categorically as a left adjoint to the inclusion of reduced rings into
all commutative rings.
\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical problem synthesized from the reduced-quotient theory in legacy AG1 and CA13.

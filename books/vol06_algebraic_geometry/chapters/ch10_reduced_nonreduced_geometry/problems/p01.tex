\begin{problem}[Legacy problem: a scheme that is not reduced]
Give an explicit affine scheme that is not reduced.  For your example:
\begin{enumerate}
\item exhibit a nonzero nilpotent element;
\item compute its reduction;
\item describe its underlying prime spectrum;
\item explain why the topology alone does not reveal the nilpotent.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the minimal counterexample that forces scheme theory beyond ordinary algebraic sets.
A one-point space can carry nontrivial algebraic thickness.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use the dual numbers $k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$.  Every prime ideal contains every
nilpotent element.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Show $(\bar\varepsilon)$ is the only prime ideal.  Then quotient by the nilradical.

\begin{solution}
Take
\[
A=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\]
The class $\bar\varepsilon$ is nonzero and satisfies $\bar\varepsilon^2=0$, so $A$ is
not reduced.  Every prime ideal contains $\bar\varepsilon$, and $(\bar\varepsilon)$ is
maximal because
\[
A/(\bar\varepsilon)\cong k.
\]
Hence
\[
\Spec A=\{(\bar\varepsilon)\}
\]
is a one-point space.  The nilradical is $(\bar\varepsilon)$, so
\[
A_{\mathrm{red}}=A/(\bar\varepsilon)\cong k.
\]
Thus $\Spec A$ and $\Spec k$ are the same one-point topological space, but the first ring
contains the extra nilpotent $\bar\varepsilon$.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The topological spectrum sees which elements lie in prime ideals.  Since every nilpotent lies
in every prime, nilpotents cannot create new points.  They change the structure ring instead.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Repeat the analysis for $k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^n)$ for arbitrary $n\ge2$.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
Preserves the reducedness subproblem of legacy \emph{Theory of Algebraic Geometry 6},
Problem 6 (``give an example of a scheme which is not reduced'').  The remaining subproblems
of that mixed legacy problem concern different properties and are routed to their canonical
chapters rather than duplicated here.

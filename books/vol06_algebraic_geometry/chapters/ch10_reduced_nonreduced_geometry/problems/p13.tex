\begin{problem}[Reduction preserves idempotents and connected components]
Prove that the quotient map
\[
A\to A_{\mathrm{red}}
\]
induces a bijection between idempotents of $A$ and idempotents of $A_{\mathrm{red}}$.
Conclude that $\Spec A$ and $\Spec A_{\mathrm{red}}$ have the same clopen decompositions
and the same connected components.
\end{problem}
\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
It connects the nilpotent story of VI/10 with the idempotent/connectedness dictionary of
VI/05.
\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
VI/05 proved that idempotents of a ring correspond bijectively to clopen subsets of its
spectrum.  Reduction gives a homeomorphism of spectra.
\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Transport a clopen subset through the spectrum homeomorphism and use the idempotent-clopen
correspondence on both sides.
\begin{solution}
Reduction gives a homeomorphism
\[
h:\Spec A_{\mathrm{red}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Spec A.
\]
Homeomorphisms induce bijections on clopen subsets.  By VI/05, idempotents of $A$ correspond
bijectively to clopens of $\Spec A$, and idempotents of $A_{\mathrm{red}}$ correspond to
clopens of $\Spec A_{\mathrm{red}}$.  Composing these three bijections gives a bijection
between idempotents.  Connected components are determined by maximal clopen decompositions,
so they are unchanged by reduction.
\end{solution}
\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Nilpotent thickness can change local algebra, but not the decomposition of the underlying
space into connected pieces.
\paragraph{Extensions.}
Give a direct algebraic lifting proof for idempotents modulo nilpotent ideals.
\paragraph{Provenance.}
New synthesis problem connecting VI/05 with the reduction theorem of VI/10.

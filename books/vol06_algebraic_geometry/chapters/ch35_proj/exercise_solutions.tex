\textbf{Exercise 1.} The irrelevant ideal is \(S_+=(x,y,z)\), and points of Proj are homogeneous primes not containing \(S_+\).\par

\textbf{Exercise 2.} The ideal is homogeneous and the quotient is \(k[y]\), a domain. It misses \(y\), so it does not contain \(S_+\).\par

\textbf{Exercise 3.} A prime contains \(IJ\) iff it contains \(I\) or \(J\). Restricting to projectively relevant homogeneous primes gives the formula.\par

\textbf{Exercise 4.} This is immediate from the definition of ideal containment.\par

\textbf{Exercise 5.} A prime misses \(fg\) exactly when it misses both \(f\) and \(g\).\par

\textbf{Exercise 6.} Every Proj point misses at least one generator, hence lies in the corresponding standard open.\par

\textbf{Exercise 7.} The space has the single point \((0)\), and \(S_{(x)}=k\), so the scheme is \(\Spec k\).\par

\textbf{Exercise 8.} Every prime contains \(\varepsilon\), hence contains \(S_+\); therefore Proj is empty.\par

\textbf{Exercise 9.} It is \(k[y/x,z/x]\), a polynomial ring in two variables.\par

\textbf{Exercise 10.} The ring is \(k[t,t^{-1}]\) with \(t=y/x\).\par

\textbf{Exercise 11.} Homogeneous primes of \(S/I\) correspond to homogeneous primes of \(S\) containing \(I\), and the irrelevant-ideal condition is preserved.\par

\textbf{Exercise 12.} They are fractions \(a/b\) with homogeneous \(a,b\) of equal degree and \(b\notin\mathfrak p\), modulo the usual localization relation.\par

\textbf{Exercise 13.} If \(a\notin\mathfrak p\), then \(b/a\) is an inverse of \(a/b\). Thus every element outside the stated ideal is a unit, proving it is the unique maximal ideal.\par

\textbf{Exercise 14.} By definition \(\Gamma(D_+(f),\widetilde M)=M_{(f)}=(M_f)_0\).\par

\textbf{Exercise 15.} Choose \(f\notin\mathfrak p\). The affine stalk of the module sheaf from \(M_{(f)}\) is its localization at the corresponding prime, which is exactly the balanced homogeneous localization \(M_{(\mathfrak p)}\).\par

\textbf{Exercise 16.} On every \(D_+(f)\), both sheaves have sections \(S_{(f)}\), with the same localization restrictions. Hence they are isomorphic.\par

\textbf{Exercise 17.} VI/34 gives exactness of \(M\mapsto M_{(f)}\). Thus the sheaf sequence is exact on an affine basis, equivalently on stalks.\par

\textbf{Exercise 18.} They are \(S(d)_{(f)}\) on \(D_+(f)\).\par

\textbf{Exercise 19.} The projectively relevant primes are \((x)\) and \((y)\). Each has a \(\Spec k\) chart and the points are disjoint.\par

\textbf{Exercise 20.} The degree-zero chart ring is \(k[t]/(t^2)\).\par

\textbf{Exercise 21.} Both degree-zero chart rings are \(k[y/x]\), giving the local form of Veronese invariance.\par

\textbf{Exercise 22.} Under \((x,y)\mapsto(\lambda x,\lambda y)\), the two terms scale by \(\lambda\) and \(\lambda^2\), so vanishing is not invariant under common scaling.\par

\textbf{Exercise 23.} Intersecting \(V(J)\) with the homogeneous-prime locus is the same as imposing all homogeneous components of elements of \(J\), hence gives some \(V_+(I)\). The converse is immediate.\par

\textbf{Exercise 24.} The opens \(D_+(x_i)\) cover, and
\[
\Gamma(D_+(x_i),\OO)
\cong k[x_0/x_i,\ldots,\widehat{x_i/x_i},\ldots,x_n/x_i]
\cong k[t_1,\ldots,t_n].
\]
VI/36 studies their global geometry.\par

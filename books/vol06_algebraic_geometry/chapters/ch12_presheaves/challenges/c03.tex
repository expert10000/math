\begin{problem}[How much does a basis remember?]
Let $\mathcal B$ be a basis for $X$.  Construct examples showing that restriction to $\mathcal B$ does not reconstruct arbitrary presheaves.  Then formulate a locality/gluing condition on basis data that suggests why sheaves behave differently.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this challenge matters.}
It pushes the presheaf formalism beyond routine restriction calculations and exposes the
categorical or affine structure that later sheaf theory will exploit.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Translate topology into a poset of opens and use functoriality or the universal property
suggested in the statement.

\begin{solution}
Use the basis-warning problem for the failure.  For the positive direction, demand that sections on an open be determined by compatible sections on a basis cover and that every compatible basis family glue uniquely.  These are exactly the sheaf axioms expressed using basis covers, foreshadowing the basis criterion for sheaves.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{Extension.}
Formulate the same construction in an arbitrary category rather than only
$\operatorname{Open}(X)$.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical challenge built around the legacy presheaf foundations.

\begin{problem}[Localization presheaf beyond nested products]
Suppose $D(g)\subseteq D(f)$ in $\Spec A$.  Using the radical criterion, construct the canonical restriction map $A_f\to A_g$ without fixing a power relation permanently, and prove independence of auxiliary choices and functoriality under further inclusions.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this challenge matters.}
It pushes the presheaf formalism beyond routine restriction calculations and exposes the
categorical or affine structure that later sheaf theory will exploit.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Translate topology into a poset of opens and use functoriality or the universal property
suggested in the statement.

\begin{solution}
The inclusion implies $f\in\sqrt{(g)}$, so $f^n=gh$ for some $n$ and $h\in A$.  In $A_g$, the element $g$ is a unit, hence $f^n$ is a unit and therefore $f$ is a unit.  The universal property gives a unique map $A_f\to A_g$.  Uniqueness makes it independent of the chosen $n,h$ and forces composition for chains of principal-open inclusions.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{Extension.}
Formulate the same construction in an arbitrary category rather than only
$\operatorname{Open}(X)$.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical challenge built around the legacy presheaf foundations.

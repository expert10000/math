\begin{problem}[Restriction is functorial]
For inclusions $W\subseteq V\subseteq U\subseteq X$, compare the presheaves $\mathcal F|_U$, $(\mathcal F|_U)|_V$, and $\mathcal F|_V$.  Prove all natural identifications are compatible under further restriction to $W$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Repeated shrinking should not create ambiguity.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
All three assignments evaluate an open $T\subseteq V$ as $\mathcal F(T)$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
All three assignments evaluate an open $T\subseteq V$ as $\mathcal F(T)$.

\begin{solution}
For every $T\subseteq V$, both $(\mathcal F|_U)|_V(T)$ and $\mathcal F|_V(T)$ equal $\mathcal F(T)$ with exactly the same restriction maps.  Hence they are canonically identical.  Restricting further to $W$ repeats the same observation.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Restriction of presheaves behaves transparently under nested open subsets.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Package open restriction as a functor between presheaf categories.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical synthesis problem.

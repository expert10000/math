\begin{problem}[The naive constant presheaf]
Let $A$ be a nonzero abelian group and let $\underline A^{pre}$ be the naive constant presheaf.  Prove it is a presheaf.  If $U=U_1\sqcup U_2$ with $U_i$ nonempty, choose $a_1\ne a_2$ and show the compatible local sections $a_i$ cannot be glued inside $\underline A^{pre}(U)$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
The constant-presheaf/constant-sheaf distinction is a standard first test of local versus global behavior.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Restrictions between nonempty opens are identities; on a disjoint overlap there is no compatibility condition.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Restrictions between nonempty opens are identities; on a disjoint overlap there is no compatibility condition.

\begin{solution}
The restriction axioms are immediate from identity maps and the unique map to $0$ on the empty set.  On the cover $U_1,U_2$, the overlap is empty, so $a_1$ and $a_2$ are compatible.  A global section is one element $a\in A$, whose restrictions to both nonempty pieces are the same $a$.  It therefore cannot restrict to two different values.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The presheaf remembers one global constant, while a sheaf must allow constants to vary by connected component.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Identify the sheafification later as locally constant $A$-valued functions.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
Legacy-derived from the AG1 constant-presheaf example; promoted to a canonical problem without claiming it was a numbered legacy Problem.

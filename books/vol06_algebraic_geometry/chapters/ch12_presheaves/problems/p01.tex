\begin{problem}[Contravariant functoriality]
Let $X$ be a topological space and $\mathcal C$ a category.  Prove carefully that giving a $\mathcal C$-valued presheaf on $X$ is equivalent to giving a functor $\operatorname{Open}(X)^{op}\to\mathcal C$.  Translate every functor axiom into restriction notation and back.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
Presheaves are category-theoretic objects; this equivalence is the conceptual definition.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Identify the unique morphism $V\to U$ with the inclusion $V\subseteq U$, then reverse it in the opposite category.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Identify the unique morphism $V\to U$ with the inclusion $V\subseteq U$, then reverse it in the opposite category.

\begin{solution}
From a presheaf, send each open $U$ to $\mathcal F(U)$ and each arrow $U\to V$ in $\operatorname{Open}(X)^{op}$, corresponding to $V\subseteq U$, to $\rho^U_V$.  The restriction identities are exactly preservation of identities and composition.  Conversely a functor supplies these objects and morphisms, and functoriality gives the presheaf axioms.  The two constructions are inverse.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
The notation of restrictions and the language of contravariant functors encode the same structure.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Repeat for a presheaf on an arbitrary poset viewed as a category.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical synthesis from the legacy presheaf definitions in AG1 and the preferred sheaf notes.

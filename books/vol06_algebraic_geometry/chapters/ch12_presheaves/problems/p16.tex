\begin{problem}[Basis data do not determine an arbitrary presheaf]
Construct two nonisomorphic presheaves on a space $X$ whose restrictions to a chosen proper basis $\mathcal B$ are isomorphic.  Explain why this does not contradict later basis-reconstruction results for sheaves.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This prevents a common confusion between presheaves and sheaves.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Choose a finite topology with $X$ not itself in the basis and modify only the value on $X$.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Choose a finite topology with $X$ not itself in the basis and modify only the value on $X$.

\begin{solution}
Take a topology with proper basis opens $U,V$ covering $X$.  Define both presheaves identically on $U,V,U\cap V,\varnothing$.  Let one have $\mathcal F(X)=0$ and the other $\mathcal G(X)=\ZZ$, with all restrictions from $X$ chosen as zero maps into the common basis values.  The restrictions to the basis agree, but the global objects are not isomorphic.  A sheaf's gluing axiom would constrain the global value from compatible basis data; a general presheaf has no such constraint.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Basis reconstruction uses locality and gluing, not functoriality alone.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Formulate the basis sheaf axiom to be proved later.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical synthesis problem.

\begin{problem}[The singular cochain presheaf]
Fix an abelian group $A$.  Prove that $U\mapsto C^q(U;A)$ is a presheaf.  Show the coboundary maps commute with restrictions, so the cochain complexes themselves form a presheaf of complexes.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This links presheaf language to later topology and cohomology.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Inclusions push singular chains forward; dualization reverses the arrow.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Inclusions push singular chains forward; dualization reverses the arrow.

\begin{solution}
For $V\subseteq U$, inclusion gives $i_\#:C_q(V)\to C_q(U)$.  Define restriction on cochains by $\phi\mapsto\phi\circ i_\#$.  Functoriality of inclusions yields the presheaf axioms.  Since singular boundary commutes with inclusion, precomposition shows the coboundary commutes with restriction as well.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Contravariance of cochains is another instance of restriction reversing inclusion.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Compare with the presheaf of differential forms.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical synthesis problem.

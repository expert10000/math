\begin{problem}[Module localization on a basis]
Let $M$ be an $A$-module.  Show $D(f)\mapsto M_f$ with restriction $M_f\to M_{fg}$ is a presheaf on the principal-open basis.  Prove compatibility with a module homomorphism $M\to N$.
\end{problem}

\paragraph{Why this problem matters.}
This is the prototype for quasi-coherent sheaves.

\paragraph{Useful ideas before starting.}
Use functoriality and transitivity of module localization.

\paragraph{Guided hints.}
Use functoriality and transitivity of module localization.

\begin{solution}
The localization map $A_f\to A_{fg}$ induces the canonical module map $M_f\to M_{fg}$.  Localization transitivity gives identity and composition.  A module homomorphism $\alpha:M\to N$ localizes to $\alpha_f:M_f\to N_f$, and the localization squares commute, so the family is a morphism of basis presheaves.
\end{solution}

\paragraph{What happened mathematically.}
Modules inherit the same local algebra as rings under localization.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
Take $M=A/I$ and identify $M_f$ with $A_f/IA_f$.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
New canonical synthesis anticipating VI/38.

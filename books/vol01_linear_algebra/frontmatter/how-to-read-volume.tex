\chapter*{How to Read Volume I}
\phantomsection
\label{sec:i-how-to-read-volume}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{How to Read Volume I}

Volume~I is organized as a three-stage progression from algebraic structure to
operator structure and finally to Euclidean/Hilbert-space geometry.

\section*{Part I — Vector Spaces}

Chapters~1--6 build the invariant language of vectors, subspaces, bases,
coordinates, linear maps, kernels, images, and isomorphisms. The main question
is:

\[
\boxed{\text{What survives when coordinates are removed?}}
\]

A reader new to linear algebra should normally read these chapters in order.
Readers already comfortable with bases and linear maps may use Chapters~1--3 as
a notation check and begin serious review at Chapters~4--6.

\section*{Part II — Matrices and Operators}

Chapters~7--12 turn linear maps into matrices and study operator structure
through determinants, trace, eigenvalues, invariant subspaces, minimal
polynomials, and canonical forms. The organizing question becomes:

\[
\boxed{\text{How much of a linear operator can be exposed by a good basis?}}
\]

The distinction between an operator and one of its matrix representations
remains essential throughout this part.

\section*{Part III — Euclidean and Hilbert-Space Geometry}

Chapters~13--18 add inner products and therefore length, angle, orthogonality,
adjoints, orthonormal bases, spectral structure, quadratic forms, and singular
values. The key transition is:

\[
\boxed{\text{algebraic decomposition}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\text{orthogonal decomposition}.}
\]

Part~III is not a separate subject appended to the book. It reuses the linear
maps, matrices, eigenvalues, and basis changes of Parts~I--II, but adds the
geometry needed to ask whether a basis can be chosen \emph{orthonormally}.

\section*{Suggested routes}

\begin{description}
\item[First course.]
Read Chapters~1--9, then 13--16, and return to 10--12, 17--18 as needed.

\item[Proof-oriented route.]
Read Chapters~1--16 in order, emphasizing coordinate-free formulations before
matrix calculations.

\item[Geometry route.]
Read Chapters~1--7, then 13--16; continue into Volume~VII after Chapters~14--16.

\item[Computation/data route.]
Emphasize Chapters~4, 7--9, 14, 16, and 18, while using the surrounding
chapters for structural justification.
\end{description}

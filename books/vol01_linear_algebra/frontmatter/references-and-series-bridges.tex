\section*{How to use this page}

Volume~I is designed to be self-contained at the level of its stated
prerequisites. The references below are complementary sources for alternate
proofs, broader viewpoints, and additional exercises. The series bridges then
show where the main ideas of linear algebra reappear in later volumes.

\section*{Selected references}

\begin{description}
\item[Axler, \emph{Linear Algebra Done Right}.]
A structural treatment emphasizing linear maps, invariant subspaces,
eigenvalues, inner-product spaces, and the spectral theorem.

\item[Halmos, \emph{Finite-Dimensional Vector Spaces}.]
A concise conceptual account of finite-dimensional linear algebra and operator
language.

\item[Hoffman and Kunze, \emph{Linear Algebra}.]
A classical reference for canonical forms, polynomial methods, determinants,
and the algebraic structure of linear operators.

\item[Horn and Johnson, \emph{Matrix Analysis}.]
A deeper matrix-oriented reference for Hermitian and normal matrices,
spectral inequalities, positive matrices, and advanced matrix structure.

\item[Strang, \emph{Introduction to Linear Algebra}.]
A computational and geometric complement, especially useful for elimination,
orthogonality, least squares, eigenvalue problems, and singular-value
decomposition.
\end{description}

These works use partly different notation and sometimes different
complex-inner-product conventions. Within this volume, the conventions fixed in
\hyperref[sec:i-notation-and-conventions]{Notation and Conventions} are
authoritative.

\section*{Cross-volume bridges}

\begin{description}
\item[I/01 \(\rightarrow\) V/09.]
Vector spaces over fields generalize to modules over rings in
\emph{Volume V, Chapter 9: Modules and Exact Sequences}. Many familiar linear
constructions survive, while basis and dimension become subtler.

\item[I/05 \(\rightarrow\) II/08 and VII/03.]
Linear transformations become the local models for derivatives in
\emph{Volume II, Chapter 8: Differentiability in Several Variables}, and then
for smooth maps between manifolds in
\emph{Volume VII, Chapter 3: Smooth Maps and Diffeomorphisms}.

\item[I/03 \(\rightarrow\) VII/04.]
Bases and dimension become the local linear language of
\emph{Volume VII, Chapter 4: Tangent Spaces}.

\item[I/08 \(\rightarrow\) VII/10.]
Determinants control orientation and Jacobian factors, reappearing in
\emph{Volume VII, Chapter 10: Orientation and Integration}.

\item[I/13 \(\rightarrow\) II/05 and VII/20.]
An inner product induces a norm and hence a metric, linking to
\emph{Volume II, Chapter 5: Metric Spaces and Continuity}. Assigning an inner
product smoothly to every tangent space leads to
\emph{Volume VII, Chapter 20: Riemannian Metrics}.

\item[I/14 \(\rightarrow\) VII/08.]
Orthonormal bases and Gram--Schmidt feed directly into
\emph{Volume VII, Chapter 8: Principal and Frame Bundles}.

\item[I/16--I/18 \(\rightarrow\) later analysis and geometry.]
The spectral theorem, quadratic forms, and singular-value decomposition
provide the finite-dimensional prototype for diagonalization, energy
decompositions, orthogonal modes, and numerical low-rank structure used
throughout Volumes~III and~VII.
\end{description}

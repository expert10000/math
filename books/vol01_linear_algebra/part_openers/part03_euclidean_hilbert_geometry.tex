\chapter*{Part III Roadmap: Euclidean and Hilbert-Space Geometry}
\phantomsection
\label{sec:i-part3-roadmap}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Part III Roadmap}

Parts~I--II treated vector spaces and linear operators using algebraic
structure alone. Part~III adds an inner product. That one extra structure
changes the available questions:

\[
\begin{array}{ccl}
\text{basis} &\longrightarrow& \text{orthonormal basis},\\[0.2em]
\text{direct sum} &\longrightarrow& \text{orthogonal direct sum},\\[0.2em]
\text{transpose/conjugate transpose} &\longrightarrow& \text{adjoint},\\[0.2em]
\text{diagonalizable} &\longrightarrow& \text{unitarily/orthogonally diagonalizable},\\[0.2em]
\text{matrix factorization} &\longrightarrow& \text{SVD and best low-rank geometry}.
\end{array}
\]

\section*{The six-chapter arc}

\begin{description}
\item[Chapter 13 — Inner Products and Orthogonality.]
Introduces the geometry: norms, angles, orthogonality, orthogonal complements,
Cauchy--Schwarz, and polarization.

\item[Chapter 14 — Gram--Schmidt and Orthogonal Projection.]
Turns independent lists into orthonormal ones and makes closest-point
approximation explicit.

\item[Chapter 15 — Orthogonal and Unitary Operators.]
Identifies the linear maps that preserve the inner-product geometry.

\item[Chapter 16 — The Spectral Theorem.]
Shows that self-adjoint/normal structure is exactly what allows the strongest
orthonormal diagonalization statements.

\item[Chapter 17 — Quadratic Forms.]
Uses spectral structure to classify and simplify quadratic energy expressions.

\item[Chapter 18 — Singular Value Decomposition.]
Extends orthogonal spectral ideas to arbitrary rectangular matrices and reveals
their geometric action through orthogonal input/output directions.
\end{description}

Chapters~10--12 deepen the operator theory feeding the spectral theorem, while
Chapter~17 develops the quadratic-form side of the same geometry.

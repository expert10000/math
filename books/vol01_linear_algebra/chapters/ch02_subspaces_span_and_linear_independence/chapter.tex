\\chapter{Subspaces, Span and Linear Independence}
\\label{ch:i02-subspaces-span-and-linear-independence}

Subspaces are the linear subsets of a vector space, span is the smallest subspace generated by specified vectors, and linear independence is the condition that a generating description has no redundancy. These three ideas form the basic grammar of finite-dimensional reasoning.

\\section*{Conceptual roadmap}
The chapter is organized around a repeated pattern:
\[
\\boxed{\\text{definition}\\;\\longrightarrow\\;\\text{structural theorem}\\;\\longrightarrow\\;\\text{coordinate calculation}\\;\\longrightarrow\\;\\text{application}.}
\]
Whenever possible, we separate the invariant mathematical object from a chosen coordinate representation.

\\section{Subspaces}
A subset $W\subseteq V$ is a subspace when it contains $0$ and is closed under addition and scalar multiplication. Equivalently, $au+bv\in W$ for all $u,v\in W$ and $a,b\in\mathbb F$.

\\section{Intersections and sums}
Arbitrary intersections of subspaces are subspaces. The union of two subspaces need not be a subspace, but the sum $U+W=\{u+w\}$ always is.

\\section{Span}
The span of $S\subseteq V$ is the set of all finite linear combinations of elements of $S$. It is the smallest subspace containing $S$.

\\section{Linear dependence}
A finite list $v_1,\ldots,v_r$ is linearly dependent when some nontrivial scalar relation $a_1v_1+\cdots+a_rv_r=0$ exists. Otherwise the list is linearly independent.

\\section{Redundancy and exchange}
If a list is dependent, at least one vector is in the span of the others. This simple observation drives the exchange arguments used to prove uniqueness of dimension.

\\section{Homogeneous systems}
Columns of a matrix are linearly dependent exactly when the corresponding homogeneous system has a nonzero solution. Row reduction converts an abstract dependence question into an algorithm.

\\section{Core structural results}

\\begin{theorem}[Subspace criterion]\\label{thm:i02-01}
A nonempty subset $W\subseteq V$ is a subspace if and only if $au+bv\in W$ for all $u,v\in W$ and all scalars $a,b$.
\\begin{proof}
Taking $a=b=0$ gives $0\in W$; special choices recover closure under addition and scalar multiplication. The converse is immediate.
\\end{proof}
\\end{theorem}

\\begin{theorem}[Dependence lemma]\\label{thm:i02-02}
A list $v_1,\dots,v_r$ is dependent if and only if one vector lies in the span of the preceding vectors after a suitable reordering.
\\begin{proof}
Solve a nontrivial relation for a vector whose coefficient is nonzero. Conversely, any span relation rearranges to a nontrivial dependence relation.
\\end{proof}
\\end{theorem}

\\section{Worked examples}

\\begin{example}[Solution spaces]\\label{ex:i02-worked-01}
The solution set of a homogeneous linear system $Ax=0$ is a subspace of $\mathbb F^n$ because matrix multiplication is linear.
\\end{example}

\\begin{example}[Polynomial constraints]\\label{ex:i02-worked-02}
Polynomials of degree at most $4$ satisfying $p(1)=0$ form a subspace: evaluation at $1$ respects addition and scalar multiplication.
\\end{example}

\\begin{example}[A nonexample]\\label{ex:i02-worked-03}
The set $\{(x,y):xy=0\}$ is the union of the coordinate axes and is not a subspace because $(1,0)+(0,1)=(1,1)$ leaves the set.
\\end{example}

\\end{enumerate}

\\section{Exercises with complete solutions}

\\begin{exercise}\\label{exr:i02-01}
Show that the intersection of any family of subspaces is a subspace.
\\end{exercise}
\\begin{hint}
Identify the definition or structural theorem in this chapter that directly controls the question, then reduce the calculation to that statement.
\\end{hint}
\\begin{solution}
The zero vector lies in every member. If two vectors lie in the intersection, every linear combination lies in each member and hence in the intersection.
\\end{solution}

\\begin{exercise}\\label{exr:i02-02}
Show that $U\cup W$ is a subspace only if $U\subseteq W$ or $W\subseteq U$.
\\end{exercise}
\\begin{hint}
Identify the definition or structural theorem in this chapter that directly controls the question, then reduce the calculation to that statement.
\\end{hint}
\\begin{solution}
If neither contains the other, choose $u\in U\setminus W$ and $w\in W\setminus U$. Then $u+w$ belongs to neither subspace, contradicting closure.
\\end{solution}

\\begin{exercise}\\label{exr:i02-03}
Find the span of $(1,0,1)$ and $(0,1,1)$ in $\mathbb R^3$.
\\end{exercise}
\\begin{hint}
Identify the definition or structural theorem in this chapter that directly controls the question, then reduce the calculation to that statement.
\\end{hint}
\\begin{solution}
It is $\{(a,b,a+b):a,b\in\mathbb R\}$, equivalently the plane $z=x+y$.
\\end{solution}

\\begin{exercise}\\label{exr:i02-04}
Are $1+x$, $x+x^2$, and $1-x^2$ independent?
\\end{exercise}
\\begin{hint}
Identify the definition or structural theorem in this chapter that directly controls the question, then reduce the calculation to that statement.
\\end{hint}
\\begin{solution}
No. The first minus the second minus the third is zero: $(1+x)-(x+x^2)-(1-x^2)=0$.
\\end{solution}

\\begin{exercise}\\label{exr:i02-05}
Prove that a list containing the zero vector is dependent.
\\end{exercise}
\\begin{hint}
Identify the definition or structural theorem in this chapter that directly controls the question, then reduce the calculation to that statement.
\\end{hint}
\\begin{solution}
Give the zero vector coefficient $1$ and all other vectors coefficient $0$.
\\end{solution}

\\begin{exercise}\\label{exr:i02-06}
Determine whether $(1,2)$ and $(2,4)$ are independent.
\\end{exercise}
\\begin{hint}
Identify the definition or structural theorem in this chapter that directly controls the question, then reduce the calculation to that statement.
\\end{hint}
\\begin{solution}
They are dependent because $(2,4)=2(1,2)$.
\\end{solution}

\\begin{exercise}\\label{exr:i02-07}
Show that the span of a set equals the intersection of all subspaces containing it.
\\end{exercise}
\\begin{hint}
Identify the definition or structural theorem in this chapter that directly controls the question, then reduce the calculation to that statement.
\\end{hint}
\\begin{solution}
The span is itself a subspace containing the set, and every subspace containing the set contains all its finite linear combinations.
\\end{solution}

\\begin{exercise}\\label{exr:i02-08}
Relate pivots in a matrix to independence of its columns.
\\end{exercise}
\\begin{hint}
Identify the definition or structural theorem in this chapter that directly controls the question, then reduce the calculation to that statement.
\\end{hint}
\\begin{solution}
A homogeneous system $Ax=0$ has only the zero solution exactly when every column variable is a pivot variable; this is equivalent to column independence.
\\end{solution}

\\section*{Chapter summary}
Subspaces are the linear subsets of a vector space, span is the smallest subspace generated by specified vectors, and linear independence is the condition that a generating description has no redundancy. These three ideas form the basic grammar of finite-dimensional reasoning.
The important habit is to move fluently between invariant statements and concrete coordinates while remembering which conclusions depend on finite dimensionality, the scalar field, or the inner product.

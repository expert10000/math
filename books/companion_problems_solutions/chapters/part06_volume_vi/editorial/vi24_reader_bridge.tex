\noindent\textbf{Theory and worked example: changing the base can change fibres.}
A morphism $S'\to S$ changes an $S$-scheme $X$ into
$X_{S'}=X\times_S S'$. If $X=\operatorname{Spec}B$,
$S=\operatorname{Spec}A$ and $S'=\operatorname{Spec}A'$, then
\[
X_{S'}\cong\operatorname{Spec}(B\otimes_A A').
\]
The construction preserves the fibre-product universal property;
however, one should not expect all properties of the underlying point
set, or all geometric properties, to remain unchanged.

For a concrete example take $S=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb R$ and
$X=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C$. After base change to
$S'=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C$ one obtains
\[
X_{\mathbb C}=\operatorname{Spec}
(\mathbb C\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C)
\cong\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb C\times\mathbb C).
\]
The last isomorphism comes from factoring $z^2+1$ over $\mathbb C$:
one point with residue field $\mathbb C$ over $\mathbb R$ becomes two
points after scalar extension. This does not contradict base-change
stability of open and closed immersions; it demonstrates that base
change can reveal different geometric components.

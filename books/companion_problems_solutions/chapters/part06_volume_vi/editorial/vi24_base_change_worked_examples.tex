\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Worked examples: affine base change and stability.}
For ring maps $A\to B$ and $A\to C$, the base-changed morphism of
$\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ is
\[
\operatorname{Spec}(B\otimes_A C)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}C.
\]
One first computes the tensor product \emph{as a $C$-algebra}; only then
should one infer geometric properties.  The following examples separate
properties that survive arbitrary base change from conclusions that cannot
be read backwards from a single special fiber.

\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Worked example 1 (an explicit finite free base change).}
Let $A=\mathbb Z[u]$ and $B=A[x]/(x^2-u)$.  The map
$A\to B$ makes $B$ a free $A$-module with basis $1,x$:
every polynomial reduces uniquely to $a+bx$ by $x^2=u$.
Choose $C=\mathbb F_5$ via reduction modulo $5$ and $u\mapsto4$.
Tensoring the presentation with $C$ imposes the new coefficients and
the old defining relation:
\[
\begin{aligned}
B\otimes_A C
 &\cong C[x]/(x^2-4)\\
 &\cong\mathbb F_5[x]/((x-2)(x+2))\\
 &\cong\mathbb F_5\times\mathbb F_5.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $2\ne-2$ in $\mathbb F_5$, so the factors are comaximal.
The base change has two reduced points; it is still finite free of
rank two over $C$.  In particular, \emph{finite free rank} is preserved,
although the algebra need not remain a domain.

\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Worked example 2 (pulling back a closed immersion).}
Put $A=k[x,y]$, $I=(x^2,y)$ and $X=\operatorname{Spec}(A/I)$.
The closed immersion $X\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}A$ is induced
by the quotient $A\twoheadrightarrow A/I$.
Take $C=k[t]$ with $x\mapsto t$, $y\mapsto t^2$.
The extended ideal is $IC=(t^2,t^2)=(t^2)$, hence
\[
(A/I)\otimes_A C\cong C/IC\cong k[t]/(t^2).
\]
Thus $X\times_{\operatorname{Spec}A}\operatorname{Spec}C$
embeds as the closed subscheme $\operatorname{Spec}k[t]/(t^2)$ of
$\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$.  It has one point with a square-zero
infinitesimal direction, not just the reduced origin.
The computation explains the general formula
$\operatorname{Spec}(A/I)\times_{\operatorname{Spec}A}
\operatorname{Spec}C\cong\operatorname{Spec}(C/IC)$.

\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Worked example 3 (pulling back a basic open).}
With $A=k[x,y]$, consider the open immersion
$D(x)=\operatorname{Spec}A_x\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}A$.
Base change along $C=A/(xy)$.
Localization commutes with this tensor product:
\[
A_x\otimes_A C\cong C_{\bar x}
\cong k[x,x^{-1},y]/(xy)
\cong k[x,x^{-1}],
\]
because $x$ becomes invertible, forcing $y=0$ from $xy=0$.
Consequently the pulled-back open subscheme is exactly
$D(\bar x)\subset\operatorname{Spec}C$.
This is a concrete reason an open immersion remains an open immersion
under arbitrary base change; the outcome may be a proper open subset
or even empty, depending on $C$.

\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Worked example 4 (flatness survives a non-reduced fiber).}
Let $A=k[t]$ and $B=A[z]/(z^2-t)$.  Since the polynomial is monic,
$B\cong A\oplus Az$ is free of rank two over $A$.
In particular $B$ is flat.  Base change to $C=A/(t)\cong k$ gives
\[
B\otimes_A C\cong k[z]/(z^2).
\]
The latter is a two-dimensional vector space over $k$, hence a flat
$k$-module, as the base-change theorem predicts.  Nevertheless it is
\emph{not reduced}: $z\ne0$ while $z^2=0$.
So flatness of the original family does not mean every fiber is
reduced; these are independent properties.  This example also shows
why inspecting only the underlying points discards information.

\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Worked example 5 (a special fiber cannot certify flatness).}
Take $A=k[t]$ and the $A$-algebra $B=A/(t)\cong k$.
The map $A\to B$ is \emph{not flat}: tensoring the injection
$A\xrightarrow{\cdot t}A$ with $B$ gives the zero endomorphism
$B\xrightarrow{0}B$, which is not injective.
Yet after the non-flat base change $A\to C=A/(t)$,
\[
B\otimes_A C\cong k\otimes_{k[t]}k\cong k,
\]
and the resulting map $C\to k$ is the identity, hence flat.
Thus a base-changed morphism can be flat even when the original
morphism is not.  One cannot infer the original flatness from this
single special fiber.  Flatness \emph{does} descend along faithfully
flat base changes; $k[t]\to k[t]/(t)$ is not faithfully flat.

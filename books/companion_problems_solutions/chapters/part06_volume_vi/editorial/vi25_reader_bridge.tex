\noindent\textbf{Theory and worked example: ordinary and geometric fibres.}
For $f:X\to S$ and a point $s\in S$, the scheme-theoretic fibre is
$X_s=X\times_S\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(s)$. A geometric fibre is
obtained by extending $\kappa(s)$ to an algebraic closure. The two
need not have the same number of irreducible components: properties
such as reducedness and irreducibility may change after a field
extension.

Let $k$ be algebraically closed of characteristic different from $2$,
and consider
\[
X=\operatorname{Spec}k[t,x]/(x^2-t)
\longrightarrow \operatorname{Spec}k[t].
\]
The fibre above $t=a\in k$ has ring $k[x]/(x^2-a)$. If $a\ne0$,
it consists of two distinct reduced $k$-points. At $a=0$ it is
$\operatorname{Spec}k[x]/(x^2)$, one nonreduced point. By contrast,
over the generic point, $x^2-t$ is irreducible in $k(t)[x]$, so the
generic fibre is the spectrum of a quadratic field extension of
$k(t)$. Its geometric generic fibre becomes two reduced points.
A family can therefore have reduced general closed fibres and a
nonreduced special fibre, while its generic and geometric generic
fibres have different irreducibility behaviour.

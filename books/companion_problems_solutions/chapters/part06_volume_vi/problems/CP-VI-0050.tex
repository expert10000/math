\subsection*{CP-VI-0050: Union and intersection of algebraic sets}

Let $k$ be a field and let $S,T\subseteq k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$. Prove
\[
V(S)\cap V(T)=V(S\cup T),\qquad
V(S)\cup V(T)=V(ST),
\]
where $ST=\{fg:f\in S,\ g\in T\}$. Deduce that arbitrary intersections and finite unions of algebraic sets are algebraic.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Solution.}

A point lies in $V(S)\cap V(T)$ exactly when every element of $S\cup T$ vanishes there, proving the first identity. If $a\in V(S)\cup V(T)$, then either every $f\in S$ or every $g\in T$ vanishes at $a$, so all products $fg$ vanish. Conversely, if every $fg$ vanishes and $a\notin V(S)$, choose $f\in S$ with $f(a)\ne0$; then $f(a)g(a)=0$ forces $g(a)=0$ for all $g\in T$, so $a\in V(T)$. The two identities give arbitrary intersections and finite unions by taking unions of defining sets and iterating products.

\subsection*{CP-VI-0051: A parabola as an affine algebraic set}

Over a field $k$, let $X=V(y-x^2)\subseteq \mathbb A_k^2$. Show that projection $(a,b)\mapsto a$ gives a bijection $X\to\mathbb A_k^1$, and determine $I(X)$ when $k$ is infinite.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Solution.}

Every $a\in k$ gives the unique point $(a,a^2)\in X$, so projection is bijective. If $F(x,y)$ vanishes on $X$, divide $F$ by the monic polynomial $y-x^2$ in $k[x][y]$: $F=Q(y-x^2)+R(x)$. Evaluating at $(a,a^2)$ gives $R(a)=0$ for every $a\in k$. Since $k$ is infinite, $R=0$. Thus $I(X)=(y-x^2)$.

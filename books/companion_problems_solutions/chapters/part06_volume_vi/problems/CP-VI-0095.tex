\subsection*{CP-VI-0095: Principal Cartier divisors}

Let $X$ be an integral scheme and $f\in K(X)^\times$. Show that $f$ determines a Cartier divisor and that multiplying $f$ by a global unit does not change its Cartier-divisor class.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Solution.}

A Cartier divisor is locally represented by non-zero-divisor rational functions modulo multiplication by regular units. The single global rational function $f$ therefore gives the local representatives $f|_U$ on every open set. If $u\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X^\times)$, then $uf$ differs from $f$ by the regular unit $u$, so the two representatives define the same Cartier divisor class.

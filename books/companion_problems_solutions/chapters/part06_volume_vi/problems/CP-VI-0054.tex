\subsection*{CP-VI-0054: Coordinate ring of a graph}

Let $X=V(y-x^3)\subseteq\mathbb A_k^2$. Compute $k[X]$ and exhibit an explicit $k$-algebra isomorphism $k[X]\cong k[t]$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Solution.}

By definition $k[X]=k[x,y]/(y-x^3)$ (over an infinite field this is the vanishing ideal, and algebraically the quotient is the natural coordinate algebra). Define $\phi:k[x,y]\to k[t]$ by $x\mapsto t$, $y\mapsto t^3$. It is surjective and its kernel is $(y-x^3)$, because division by $y-x^3$ leaves a polynomial in $x$, which can vanish under $x\mapsto t$ only if it is zero. Hence the first isomorphism theorem gives $k[X]\cong k[t]$.

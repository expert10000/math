\subsection*{Problem 2 Cohomology Computed by an Acyclic Resolution}

Let \(\mathcal F\) be a sheaf of abelian groups on a topological space \(X\), and suppose we are given a long exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow \mathcal F
\longrightarrow \mathcal F^0
\xrightarrow{d^0}
\mathcal F^1
\xrightarrow{d^1}
\mathcal F^2
\xrightarrow{d^2}
\cdots .
\]
Assume further that
\[
H^i(X,\mathcal F^j)=0
\]
for all \(i>0\) and all \(j\geq 0\). We want to prove that
\[
H^i(X,\mathcal F)
=
\frac{
	\ker\left(
	d^i:\Gamma(X,\mathcal F^i)\to \Gamma(X,\mathcal F^{i+1})
	\right)
}{
	\operatorname{im}\left(
	d^{i-1}:\Gamma(X,\mathcal F^{i-1})\to \Gamma(X,\mathcal F^i)
	\right)
}
\]
for all \(i\geq 0\), with the usual interpretation in degree \(0\).

\textbf{Solution.}

Define sheaves
\[
\mathcal Z^0:=\mathcal F
\]
and, for \(i\geq 0\),
\[
\mathcal Z^{i+1}:=\ker(d^i:\mathcal F^i\to \mathcal F^{i+1}).
\]

Because the original sequence is exact, we have short exact sequences
\[
0\longrightarrow \mathcal Z^i
\longrightarrow \mathcal F^i
\longrightarrow \mathcal Z^{i+1}
\longrightarrow 0
\]
for every \(i\geq 0\).

Apply the cohomology functor to this short exact sequence. We obtain the long exact sequence
\[
0\to H^0(X,\mathcal Z^i)
\to H^0(X,\mathcal F^i)
\to H^0(X,\mathcal Z^{i+1})
\to H^1(X,\mathcal Z^i)
\to H^1(X,\mathcal F^i)
\to \cdots .
\]

Since each \(\mathcal F^i\) is acyclic, we have
\[
H^q(X,\mathcal F^i)=0
\qquad\text{for all }q>0.
\]
Therefore the long exact sequence gives
\[
H^q(X,\mathcal Z^{i+1})
\cong
H^{q+1}(X,\mathcal Z^i)
\]
for all \(q>0\).

Repeatedly applying this isomorphism gives
\[
H^i(X,\mathcal F)
=
H^i(X,\mathcal Z^0)
\cong
H^{i-1}(X,\mathcal Z^1)
\cong
\cdots
\cong
H^1(X,\mathcal Z^{i-1}).
\]

Now look at the short exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow \mathcal Z^{i-1}
\longrightarrow \mathcal F^{i-1}
\longrightarrow \mathcal Z^i
\longrightarrow 0.
\]
Its cohomology sequence begins
\[
0\to H^0(X,\mathcal Z^{i-1})
\to H^0(X,\mathcal F^{i-1})
\to H^0(X,\mathcal Z^i)
\to H^1(X,\mathcal Z^{i-1})
\to H^1(X,\mathcal F^{i-1}).
\]

Since
\[
H^1(X,\mathcal F^{i-1})=0,
\]
we get an exact sequence
\[
H^0(X,\mathcal F^{i-1})
\to H^0(X,\mathcal Z^i)
\to H^1(X,\mathcal Z^{i-1})
\to 0.
\]

Hence
\[
H^1(X,\mathcal Z^{i-1})
\cong
\frac{
	H^0(X,\mathcal Z^i)
}{
	\operatorname{im}\left(
	H^0(X,\mathcal F^{i-1})
	\to H^0(X,\mathcal Z^i)
	\right)
}.
\]

Since
\[
H^0(X,\mathcal Z^i)
=
\Gamma(X,\mathcal Z^i),
\]
and
\[
\mathcal Z^i=\ker(d^i:\mathcal F^i\to \mathcal F^{i+1}),
\]
we have
\[
\Gamma(X,\mathcal Z^i)
=
\ker\left(
d^i:\Gamma(X,\mathcal F^i)\to \Gamma(X,\mathcal F^{i+1})
\right).
\]

Also, the image of
\[
\Gamma(X,\mathcal F^{i-1})
\to
\Gamma(X,\mathcal Z^i)
\]
is precisely
\[
\operatorname{im}\left(
d^{i-1}:\Gamma(X,\mathcal F^{i-1})
\to \Gamma(X,\mathcal F^i)
\right).
\]

Therefore
\[
H^i(X,\mathcal F)
\cong
\frac{
	\ker\left(
	d^i:\Gamma(X,\mathcal F^i)\to \Gamma(X,\mathcal F^{i+1})
	\right)
}{
	\operatorname{im}\left(
	d^{i-1}:\Gamma(X,\mathcal F^{i-1})\to \Gamma(X,\mathcal F^i)
	\right)
}.
\]

Thus
\[
\boxed{
	H^i(X,\mathcal F)
	=
	H^i\bigl(\Gamma(X,\mathcal F^\bullet)\bigr).
}
\]

Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
	H^i(X,\mathcal F)
	=
	\frac{
		\ker\left(
		d^i:\Gamma(X,\mathcal F^i)\to \Gamma(X,\mathcal F^{i+1})
		\right)
	}{
		\operatorname{im}\left(
		d^{i-1}:\Gamma(X,\mathcal F^{i-1})\to \Gamma(X,\mathcal F^i)
		\right)
	}.
}
\]

This proves that an acyclic resolution computes sheaf cohomology.

\subsection*{Problem 10. Cartesian Diagrams}

\textbf{Problem.}
A commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
	A \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & B \arrow[d] \\
	C \arrow[r] & D
\end{tikzcd}
\]
is Cartesian if the induced morphism
\[
A\longrightarrow B\times_D C
\]
is an isomorphism.

Show that the following diagrams are Cartesian in any category.

\subsection{First diagram}

Let \(X\to S\to T\) and \(Y\to S\to T\) be the given morphisms. Their fibre products over \(S\) and \(T\) are \(X\times_S Y\) and \(X\times_T Y\), respectively. The diagonal \(\Delta:S\to S\times_T S\) is induced by the two identity maps on \(S\).

The diagram is
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
	X\times_S Y \arrow[r] \arrow[d]
	&
	S \arrow[d,"\Delta"]
	\\
	X\times_T Y \arrow[r]
	&
	S\times_T S.
\end{tikzcd}
\]

We prove that it is Cartesian.

Let \(Q\) be any object. A morphism
\[
Q\longrightarrow
(X\times_T Y)\times_{S\times_T S}S
\]
amounts to morphisms \(x:Q\to X\), \(y:Q\to Y\), and \(s:Q\to S\). Writing \(x_S:Q\to X\to S\) and \(y_S:Q\to Y\to S\) for the induced maps, the conditions are that \(x\) and \(y\) agree after mapping to \(T\) and that
\[
\Delta\circ s=(x_S,y_S).
\]

Since \(\Delta\circ s=(x_S,y_S)\) is equivalent to \(s=x_S=y_S\), the same data amounts precisely to a pair \(x:Q\to X\), \(y:Q\to Y\) with \(x_S=y_S\).
By the universal property of the fibre product, this pair is equivalent to a morphism \(Q\to X\times_S Y\).

Thus for every object \(Q\), there is a natural bijection
\[
\operatorname{Hom}
\left(Q,X\times_S Y\right)
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}
\left(
Q,(X\times_T Y)\times_{S\times_T S}S
\right).
\]
By the Yoneda principle, this gives an isomorphism
\[
X\times_S Y
\cong
(X\times_T Y)\times_{S\times_T S}S.
\]
Therefore the diagram is Cartesian.

\subsection{Second diagram}

Now let \(f:X\to Y\) be a morphism over \(S\). The diagonal of \(Y\) is \(\Delta:Y\to Y\times_S Y\), and the graph of \(f\) is the morphism \(\Gamma_f:X\to X\times_S Y\) defined by \(\Gamma_f=(\operatorname{id}_X,f)\).

The diagram is
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
	X \arrow[r,"f"] \arrow[d,"\Gamma_f"']
	&
	Y \arrow[d,"\Delta"]
	\\
	X\times_S Y \arrow[r,"f\times \operatorname{id}_Y"']
	&
	Y\times_S Y.
\end{tikzcd}
\]

We prove that it is Cartesian.

Let \(Q\) be any object. A morphism
\[
Q\longrightarrow
(X\times_S Y)\times_{Y\times_S Y}Y
\]
amounts to morphisms \(x:Q\to X\), \(y:Q\to Y\), and \(z:Q\to Y\), where \((x,y)\) defines a morphism \(Q\to X\times_S Y\) and
\[
(f\circ x,y)=\Delta\circ z=(z,z).
\]
This forces \(z=f\circ x=y\), so the triple is uniquely determined by the single morphism \(x:Q\to X\).

Conversely, any morphism \(x:Q\to X\) supplies such a triple by taking \(y=z=f\circ x\).
Therefore there is a natural bijection
\[
\operatorname{Hom}(Q,X)
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}
\left(
Q,(X\times_S Y)\times_{Y\times_S Y}Y
\right).
\]
By the Yoneda principle,
\[
X
\cong
(X\times_S Y)\times_{Y\times_S Y}Y.
\]
Therefore the second diagram is Cartesian.

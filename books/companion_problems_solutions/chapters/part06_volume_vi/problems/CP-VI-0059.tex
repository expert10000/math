\subsection*{CP-VI-0059: Prime ideals as irreducible closed sets}

For a ring $A$, prove that $V(\mathfrak p)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}A$ is irreducible whenever $\mathfrak p$ is prime. Conversely, show that every irreducible closed subset of $\operatorname{Spec}A$ has the form $V(\mathfrak p)$ for a unique prime ideal $\mathfrak p$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Solution.}

The closed subset $V(\mathfrak p)$ is naturally homeomorphic to $\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak p)$. Since $A/\mathfrak p$ is a domain, its zero ideal is prime and the generic point $(0)$ has closure the whole spectrum, so the spectrum is irreducible. Conversely, if $Z=V(I)$ is irreducible, then $\sqrt I$ is prime: if $fg\in\sqrt I$, then $Z\subseteq V(fg)=V(f)\cup V(g)$; irreducibility gives $Z\subseteq V(f)$ or $Z\subseteq V(g)$, hence $f\in\sqrt I$ or $g\in\sqrt I$. Thus $Z=V(\sqrt I)$ with $\sqrt I$ prime. Uniqueness follows because $V(\mathfrak p)=V(\mathfrak q)$ implies $\mathfrak p=\sqrt{\mathfrak p}=\sqrt{\mathfrak q}=\mathfrak q$.

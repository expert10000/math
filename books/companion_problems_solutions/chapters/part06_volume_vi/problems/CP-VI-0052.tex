\subsection*{CP-VI-0052: Closed sets in the affine line}

Let $k$ be an infinite field. Prove that the Zariski closed subsets of $\mathbb A_k^1$ are precisely the finite subsets and the whole line. Conclude that every nonempty Zariski open subset is dense.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Solution.}

A proper closed set has the form $V(I)$ with $I\ne(0)$. Choose $0\ne f\in I$; then $V(I)\subseteq V(f)$, and a nonzero one-variable polynomial has only finitely many roots. Conversely, a finite set $\{a_1,\dots,a_r\}$ is $V(\prod_i(x-a_i))$. If $U$ is nonempty open and its closure were proper, the closure would be finite; then $U$ would be finite and open, so its complement would also be finite and the infinite line would be a union of two finite sets, impossible. Hence $\overline U=\mathbb A_k^1$.

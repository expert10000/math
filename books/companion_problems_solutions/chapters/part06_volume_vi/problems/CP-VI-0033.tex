\subsection*{Problem 3 - Integral Schemes, Reduced Schemes, and Irreducible Schemes}

\textbf{Problem.}
We say a scheme \(X\) is \emph{irreducible} if it is irreducible as a topological space, i.e. whenever
\[
X=X_1\cup X_2
\]
with \(X_1,X_2\) closed subsets, then either \(X_1=X\) or \(X_2=X\).

We say a scheme \(X\) is \emph{reduced} if for every open subset \(U\subseteq X\), the ring
\[
\mathcal O_X(U)
\]
has no nilpotents.

We say a nonempty scheme \(X\) is \emph{integral} if for every nonempty open subset \(U\subseteq X\), the ring
\[
\mathcal O_X(U)
\]
is an integral domain.

Show that a scheme is integral if and only if it is reduced and irreducible.

\textbf{Solution.}

We prove both implications.

\subsection*{Integral implies reduced and irreducible}

Assume that \(X\) is integral. By definition, for every nonempty open subset \(U\subseteq X\), the ring
\[
\mathcal O_X(U)
\]
is an integral domain. Every integral domain is reduced. Therefore \(\mathcal O_X(U)\) has no nilpotents for every open \(U\), so \(X\) is reduced.

It remains to prove that \(X\) is irreducible.

Recall that a topological space is irreducible if and only if every two nonempty open subsets intersect. Suppose, for contradiction, that \(X\) is not irreducible. Then there exist nonempty open subsets
\[
U_1,U_2\subseteq X
\]
such that
\[
U_1\cap U_2=\varnothing.
\]
Consider the open subset
\[
W=U_1\cup U_2.
\]
Since \(U_1\) and \(U_2\) are disjoint open subsets, the sheaf property gives
\[
\mathcal O_X(W)
\cong
\mathcal O_X(U_1)\times \mathcal O_X(U_2).
\]
But the product of two nonzero rings has zero divisors. Indeed,
\[
(1,0)(0,1)=(0,0),
\]
while
\[
(1,0)\neq 0,
\qquad
(0,1)\neq 0.
\]
Therefore \(\mathcal O_X(W)\) is not an integral domain. This contradicts the assumption that \(X\) is integral.

Hence every two nonempty open subsets of \(X\) intersect, so \(X\) is irreducible.

Therefore
\[
X\text{ integral}
\implies
X\text{ reduced and irreducible}.
\]

\subsection*{Reduced and irreducible implies integral}

Assume that $X$ is nonempty, reduced, and irreducible. An irreducible
scheme has a unique generic point $\eta$, which belongs to every
nonempty open subset. We prove that the section ring on each
\emph{nonempty} open subset is a domain.

Let $U\subseteq X$ be nonempty, and cover $U$ by affine opens
$W_i=\operatorname{Spec}A_i$. Each $W_i$ is nonempty, reduced, and
irreducible, because it is open in $X$. Therefore the nilradical of
$A_i$ is zero and prime. Thus $A_i$ is an integral domain.

The point $\eta$ lies in each $W_i$ and corresponds to the zero
prime, so restriction to its stalk gives an injective homomorphism
\[
A_i\longrightarrow (A_i)_{(0)}=\mathcal O_{X,\eta}=K(X).
\]
If a section $s\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_X)$ has zero germ at $\eta$,
then its restriction to every $W_i$ maps to zero in $K(X)$; the
preceding injections show that each restriction is zero. By the sheaf
locality axiom, $s=0$. Consequently
\[
\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_X)\hookrightarrow K(X),
\]
so it is a domain, as required.

This avoids the invalid claim that the set of points at which an
arbitrary section has a nonzero germ is open.

\subsection*{Conclusion}

We have proved both directions, so
\[
\boxed{
	X\text{ is integral}
	\iff
	X\text{ is reduced and irreducible}.
}
\]

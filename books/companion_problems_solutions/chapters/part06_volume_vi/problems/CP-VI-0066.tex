\subsection*{CP-VI-0066: Computing a stalk}

Let $F$ be the sheaf of continuous real-valued functions on a topological space $X$. Describe the stalk $F_x$ at $x\in X$ and prove that two functions define the same germ exactly when they agree on some neighborhood of $x$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Solution.}

By definition $F_x$ is the direct limit of $F(U)$ over neighborhoods $U$ of $x$. A representative is a pair $(U,f)$ with $x\in U$. Two representatives $(U,f)$ and $(V,g)$ define the same direct-limit element precisely when there is a neighborhood $W\subseteq U\cap V$ of $x$ on which the restrictions agree. Thus $F_x$ is exactly the set of germs of continuous functions at $x$.

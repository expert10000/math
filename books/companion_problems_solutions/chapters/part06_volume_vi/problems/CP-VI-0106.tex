\subsection*{CP-VI-0106: Why flabby sheaves are acyclic candidates}

Let $F$ be flabby, meaning every restriction map $F(U)\to F(V)$ for $V\subseteq U$ is surjective. Show that every section over an open subset extends to every larger open subset, and explain why this property makes flabby resolutions useful for computing sheaf cohomology.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Solution.}

The first assertion is exactly surjectivity of the restriction map. In a flabby resolution $0\to F\to I^0\to I^1\to\cdots$, the extension property removes local obstruction to lifting sections. Standard sheaf theory proves flabby sheaves are acyclic for global sections, so applying $\Gamma(X,-)$ to such a resolution computes the derived functors $H^i(X,F)$. Thus flabby sheaves provide a convenient class of cohomologically trivial resolution objects.

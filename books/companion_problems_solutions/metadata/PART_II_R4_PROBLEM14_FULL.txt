  1666: \section*{Problem 14$^*$: Two norms on $\mathcal{P}$ giving different limits}
  1667: 
  1668: \paragraph{Statement.}
  1669: Let $\mathcal{P}$ be the vector space of real polynomials on $[0,1]$.  
  1670: For any infinite set $I\subseteq[0,1]$, define $\|p\|_{I}:=\sup_{t\in I}|p(t)|$.
  1671: \begin{enumerate}
  1672: 	\item[(a)] Show that $\|\cdot\|_{I}$ is a norm on $\mathcal{P}$.
  1673: 	\item[(b)] Use this to produce a vector space, a single sequence in it, and two different norms on it
  1674: 	such that the sequence converges to \emph{different} elements of the space with respect to the two norms.
  1675: 	(Hint: Weierstrass approximation.)
  1676: 	\item[(c)] Is such a phenomenon possible in $\ell^1$ or $\ell^2$ when the two norms are chosen
  1677: 	from the standard norms on $\ell^1,\ell^2,\ell^\infty$?  
  1678: 	What about in $C([0,1])$ when the two norms are chosen from $L^1,L^2,L^\infty$?
  1679: \end{enumerate}
  1680: 
  1681: \bigskip
  1682: \paragraph{Theory.}
  1683: \begin{itemize}
  1684: 	\item If a real polynomial vanishes on an infinite subset of $[0,1]$ with a limit point in $[0,1]$,
  1685: 	then it is identically zero (identity theorem for polynomials).
  1686: 	\item On disjoint compact sets $K_1,K_2\subset[0,1]$ there exists $f\in C([0,1])$ with
  1687: 	$f|_{K_1}\equiv0$ and $f|_{K_2}\equiv1$ (Urysohn on $[0,1]$).  
  1688: 	By Weierstrass, there are polynomials $p_n\to f$ uniformly on $[0,1]$.
  1689: 	\item In $\ell^p$ and in $C([0,1])$ (measure $1$),
  1690: 	\[
  1691: 	\|z\|_\infty \ \ge\ \|z\|_2 \ \ge\ \|z\|_1,
  1692: 	\qquad
  1693: 	\|h\|_{L^1}\ \le\ \|h\|_{L^2}\ \le\ \|h\|_{L^\infty}.
  1694: 	\]
  1695: 	Hence convergence in any stronger norm implies convergence in any weaker norm to the \emph{same} limit.
  1696: \end{itemize}
  1697: 
  1698: \bigskip
  1699: \paragraph{Solution.}
  1700: 
  1701: \subparagraph{(a) $\|\cdot\|_{I}$ is a norm on $\mathcal{P}$.}
  1702: Let $p,q\in\mathcal{P}$ and $\alpha\in\mathbb{R}$.
  1703: \begin{itemize}
  1704: 	\item \textbf{Positivity/definiteness:} $\|p\|_{I}\ge0$.
  1705: 	If $\|p\|_{I}=0$ then $p(t)=0$ for all $t\in I$.  
  1706: 	Since $I$ is infinite and has a limit point in $[0,1]$, the identity theorem gives $p\equiv0$.
  1707: 	\item \textbf{Homogeneity:} $\|\alpha p\|_{I}=\sup_{t\in I}|\alpha||p(t)|=|\alpha|\,\|p\|_{I}$.
  1708: 	\item \textbf{Triangle inequality:}
  1709: 	$\|p+q\|_{I}=\sup_{t\in I}|p(t)+q(t)|\le \sup_{t\in I}|p(t)|+\sup_{t\in I}|q(t)|=\|p\|_{I}+\|q\|_{I}$.
  1710: \end{itemize}
  1711: Hence $\|\cdot\|_{I}$ is a norm on $\mathcal{P}$.
  1712: 
  1713: \bigskip
  1714: \subparagraph{(b) Same sequence, two norms, two different limits.}
  1715: Let
  1716: \[
  1717: K_1=[0,\tfrac12],\qquad K_2=[\tfrac12,1],
  1718: \]
  1719: and pick infinite sets $I\subset K_1$, $J\subset K_2$.
  1720: 
  1721: By Urysohn on $[0,1]$ choose $f\in C([0,1])$ with $f|_{K_1}\equiv0$ and $f|_{K_2}\equiv1$.
  1722: By Weierstrass there exist polynomials $p_n\in\mathcal{P}$ such that
  1723: \[
  1724: \|p_n-f\|_{[0,1]}:=\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|p_n(t)-f(t)| \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{} 0.
  1725: \]
  1726: 
  1727: Then
  1728: \[
  1729: \|p_n-0\|_{I}=\sup_{t\in I}|p_n(t)|
  1730: \ \le\ \sup_{t\in K_1}|p_n(t)-f(t)|
  1731: \ \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\ 0,
  1732: \]
  1733: so $p_n\to 0$ in $(\mathcal{P},\|\cdot\|_{I})$.
  1734: 
  1735: Similarly,
  1736: \[
  1737: \|p_n-1\|_{J}=\sup_{t\in J}|p_n(t)-1|
  1738: \ \le\ \sup_{t\in K_2}|p_n(t)-f(t)|
  1739: \ \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\ 0,
  1740: \]
  1741: so $p_n\to 1$ in $(\mathcal{P},\|\cdot\|_{J})$.
  1742: 
  1743: \medskip
  1744: \noindent
  1745: Thus the \emph{same} sequence $(p_n)$ converges to \emph{different} elements (0 and 1) under two different norms on the same vector space $\mathcal{P}$.
  1746: 
  1747: \bigskip
  1748: \subparagraph{(c) Impossibility in $\ell^1,\ell^2$ and in $C([0,1])$ with standard norms.}
  1749: No such example exists.
  1750: \begin{itemize}
  1751: 	\item On $\ell^1,\ell^2,\ell^\infty$, for every $z$ we have
  1752: 	$\|z\|_\infty\ge \|z\|_2\ge \|z\|_1$ (HĂ¶lder).
  1753: 	If $x_n\to x$ in a stronger norm and $x_n\to y$ in a weaker norm,
  1754: 	then $\|x-y\|\le \limsup_n \|x_n-x\|+\|x_n-y\|=0$, hence $x=y$.
  1755: 	\item On $C([0,1])$,
  1756: 	$\|h\|_{L^1}\le \|h\|_{L^2}\le \|h\|_{L^\infty}$ on a set of measure $1$.
  1757: 	The same argument shows any two of these norm limits must coincide.
  1758: \end{itemize}
  1759: 
  1760: \paragraph{Remark. When two different limits are possible.}
  1761: 
  1762: The phenomenon of having \emph{two different limits} under two norms does not rely on the supremum
  1763: norm itself. It arises whenever we choose two \emph{non-equivalent} and \emph{non-comparable} norms
  1764: on the same vector space, so that the identity map between the two normed spaces is not continuous.
  1765:

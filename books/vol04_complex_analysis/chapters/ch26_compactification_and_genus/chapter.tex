\chapter{Compactification and Genus}
\label{ch:iv26}

Compactification adds missing points in a way compatible with the complex structure, while genus records the basic topological type of a compact connected Riemann surface.

\section*{Learning goals}
After this chapter, the reader should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
\item compactify basic noncompact Riemann surfaces by adding points with valid local coordinates;
\item compute or identify genus from polygonal, covering, or Euler-characteristic data;
\item distinguish genus zero, one, and higher-genus compact surfaces in standard examples;
\item relate punctures, compactification points, and meromorphic extension;
\item use Riemann--Hurwitz-type data where available to constrain genus;
\item check that the added points produce a compact Riemann surface rather than only a topological compactification;
\end{itemize}

\section{Core structural results}

\begin{theorem}[Holomorphic functions on compact surfaces]\label{thm:iv26-01}
Every holomorphic function from a compact connected Riemann surface to $\mathbb C$ is constant.
\begin{proof}
The absolute value attains a maximum by compactness. In a local coordinate the maximum-modulus principle forces local constancy, and connectedness propagates it globally.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Riemann--Hurwitz formula]\label{thm:iv26-02}
For a degree-$d$ holomorphic map $f:X\to Y$ of compact Riemann surfaces, $2g_X-2=d(2g_Y-2)+\sum_{p\in X}(e_p-1)$.
\begin{proof}
Triangulate the target with branch values among vertices and lift the triangulation. Faces and edges lift $d$-fold, while vertices lose exactly the total ramification multiplicity. Comparing Euler characteristics yields the formula.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Genus of a double cover]\label{thm:iv26-03}
A degree-two cover of the sphere branched simply at $r$ points has genus $g=(r-2)/2$.
\begin{proof}
Apply Riemann--Hurwitz with $d=2$, target genus zero, and total ramification $r$: $2g-2=-4+r$.
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}

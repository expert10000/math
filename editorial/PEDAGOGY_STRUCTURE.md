# Pedagogical content architecture

Status: adopted for canonical reconstruction drafts; final global layout may still be refined.

## Four levels of learning material

1. **Worked Examples** - primarily new, fully solved demonstrations placed after or near the
   theory they illustrate.
2. **Exercises** - primarily new, short-to-medium reinforcement designed specifically for the
   canonical chapter.
3. **Problems** - harder synthesis material.  All substantive legacy problems are retained
   exactly once: INCLUDE, MERGE, or MOVE to the mathematically correct canonical chapter.
4. **Challenges / Extensions** - optional material beyond the normal chapter level.

## Problem dossier rule

Every canonical Problem is a self-contained source unit and contains:

- Problem statement
- Why this problem matters
- Useful ideas / prerequisites
- Guided hints
- Full solution
- What happened mathematically
- Extensions
- Provenance

The same problem source can render in two modes:

- **student mode**: statement only;
- **full/study mode**: complete dossier.

## Exercise rule

Exercise statements are kept together and are followed, in study/complete editions, by
matching hints and full solutions using stable identifiers.

Examples: `VI.05.3`, `VI.05.P2`, `VI.05.C1`.

## Preservation rule

No substantive legacy problem is silently discarded.  Duplicate/versioned copies are merged
into one canonical problem with provenance.  Misplaced problems are moved to the correct
chapter rather than duplicated.

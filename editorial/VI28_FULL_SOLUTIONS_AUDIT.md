## New high-depth challenge examples

### Nonfinite normalization without finiteness hypotheses

Let

`A = k[x_1^2,x_1^3,x_2^2,x_2^3,...]`

inside

`B = k[x_1,x_2,...]`.

Then `Frac(A)=Frac(B)`, every `x_i` is integral over `A`, and `B` is integrally closed, so `B` is the normalization.
The quotient by the maximal ideal generated by all `x_i^2,x_i^3` shows `B` is not a finite `A`-module.

### Purely inseparable base-change failure

For

`k=F_p(u)`, `L=k(t)`, `t^p=u`,

one has

`L tensor_k L = L[epsilon]/(epsilon^p)`,

so a normal point becomes nonreduced after scalar extension.
